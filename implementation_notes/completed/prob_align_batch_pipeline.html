    Probabilistic alignment as a batch pipeline: per-particle draws, keyed random streams, no global table¶

    Planned 2026-11-04 on master a723f4919, ruled done by Hans the same day ("we have reached the point of no return; this refactoring is obvious"), reviewed by Hans against 6b924ccf5 the same day (the review's requirements are folded into sections 3, 4, 5 and 8), and planned as the autonomous run prob_pipeline on the Dell (sections 10-14). Implemented by the run prob_pipeline (2026-11-04/05; the Progress entries below and prob_align_batch_pipeline_report.md beside this note). The frame is the architecture review of 2026-11-04 in speed_architecture_proposals.md; this note is its execution. It supersedes that note's Part 2 rows on the worker fold, the streaming assignment, the read-ahead thread and the 2D table pre-step, and it closes the worker fold implemented and dropped on 2026-11-04.

    1. What is proposed, in one paragraph¶

    Since 2026-10-24 every probabilistic assignment is one draw from one particle's own row; no population balance, no other particle's row, nothing enters a draw that is not fixed before the iteration's particle loop. The global table, the part files, the master's assignment step and the second worker pass exist only to serialise those draws through one random stream. Give every draw its own stream, keyed on the run, the iteration, the particle and the draw, and the table becomes unnecessary: one pipeline prepares a batch of particles, scores its rows, draws each particle's pose and extras, polishes the drawn pose, computes the noise residual and commits the decision, then releases the batch. The same pipeline runs in shared memory on the caller's (params, build) and in every distributed part; the master draws the sample, waits, merges the decisions and assembles, as it does today. The stochastic trajectory changes once, when the draws move to keyed streams; the draws are then independent of thread and partition placement. Byte identity of a complete run across thread or part counts is not promised: the reductions, the FFT plans and the batch sizes keep their rounding (section 8 states what is required instead).

    2. Where the code stands¶

    2.1 The two-phase structure¶

    A probabilistic iteration runs each particle through two programs or phases:

    phase 3D dense (refine=prob) 3D neighbourhood (prob_neigh) 2D dense (prob) 2D sparse (prob_neigh)
    table worker exec_prob_tab → eul_prob_tab%fill_tab_range (process_particle_with_map or refine_best_refs) exec_prob_tab_neigh → eul_prob_tab_neigh%fill_tab_range exec_prob_tab2D → eul_prob_tab2D%fill_tab_range exec_prob_tab2D → fill_tab_prob_neigh_range
    part file dense rows, format -4 neighbourhood candidates dense rows, format -7 candidate stream, format -6
    master prob_align_exec: read_tabs_to_glob, ref_assign, write_assignment (write_insert_table under topk > 1) prob_align_neigh_exec, ref_assign_neigh prob_align2D_exec, ref_assign ref_assign_sparse
    search worker refine3D_exec → strategy3D_prob%srch_prob: read_assignment, extras, continuous polish, commit; sigma2 residual; partial reconstruction the same refine2D_exec → strategy2D_prob%srch_prob: polish, shift state, commit; class sums the same

    The commanders are in src/main/commanders/simple/simple_commanders_prob.f90 (550 lines), the masters in src/main/strategies/parallelization/simple_prob_align_strategy.f90 (522), the tables in src/main/strategies/search/probabilistic/ (simple_eul_prob_tab.f90 1,733, _2D 1,901, _neigh 1,743, _utils 1,592), the matchers in src/main/strategies/search/simple_strategy3D_matcher.f90 (641) and simple_strategy2D_matcher.f90 (516).

    Shared memory runs the same two phases through the file system: the strategy writes the particle field, runs the prob_align commander (which builds a second parameters and builder from the file, reseeds the generator, fills the table in process, writes the part file, reads it back, assigns and writes the assignment file), then rereads the field (simple_refine3D_strategy.f90 lines 1267-1276; the 2D strategy's run_prob_align2D). The matcher then reads the assignment file. The layering policy lists this as the one accepted exception.

    2.2 What couples particles, and what does not¶

    Fixed before the particle loop, the same for every particle (the iteration's model):

    • the sample (sample4update_cnt, _rnd, _fillin, drawn by the master or the shared-memory strategy, written to the project, reproduced by sample4update_reprod);
    • the references (the reprojection model, or the class averages), the halves, the band, the matching low-pass limit, the shift window and map geometry;
    • the committed sigma2 spectra;
    • the shift prior's width (shift_prior_sigma2, from the previous iteration's sampled shincarg); the particle temperatures' formula;
    • the per-state FSC cones of the weighted insertion (insert_cone_from_fsc);
    • the 2D class populations of the previous assignment (prep_strategy2D_glob);
    • prob_beta, the extremal fraction, the refined-reference budget projs_ns (calc_num2sample).

    Two of these are computed inside the fills today, from the live particle field: dense 3D derives projs_ns through calc_num2sample at every fill_tab_range call (simple_eul_prob_tab.f90 line 306, reading the sampled particles' distances and labels through calc_athres), and every shifted fill derives the prior's width through shift_prior_sigma2 (line 340; _2D 324 and 554; _neigh 218 and 470, reading the sampled shincarg). The finalisation writes those same fields. A fill called after an earlier batch's decisions were committed would therefore see a different budget and prior, and the result would depend on the batch size. Section 3 moves both to the iteration's model.

    Row-local, reading only the particle's own row and the model above:

    • the assignment draw: draw_posterior_row over the row, in simple_eul_prob_tab.f90 line 1115 (dense 3D: the closest reference's state, then the direction within its block), simple_eul_prob_tab2D.f90 lines 1083 and 1223, simple_eul_prob_tab_neigh.f90 line 828;
    • the extras (select_extras): the row and the state's cone;
    • the fallbacks: a row without a valid distance, a particle without a temperature;
    • the polish (refine_assignment_continuously, refine_selected_continuously): deterministic L-BFGS from the drawn pose;
    • the sigma2 residual of the committed pose; the 2D shift state (record_shift_state).

    Nothing else. The multi-state proposal (multistate_posterior_state_draw.md, the joint draw of state and direction over the whole row) is row-local as well. The n_eff and row-mode readouts (prob_neff_readout, POSTERIOR DRAW N_EFF, % at the row mode) and the disk-set line (print_disk_set) are diagnostics over particles, not inputs to any draw; n_eff reads a bounded subsample chosen by global row position, which section 5 keeps meaningful across parts.

    2.3 The random draws¶

    Every draw on the probabilistic path, with where it runs today:

    2.4 The reductions¶

    The 3D master adds the parts' partial reconstructions in part order (simple_volassemble_strategy.f90, reduce_partials, do part = 1, params%nparts); 2D adds the parts' class-sum contributions (cavg_sums%accumulate). A map that differs in the last bit feeds the next iteration, where an arbitrarily small score difference can move a draw. None of that changes with keyed streams, which is why section 8 separates draw identity from complete-run identity.

    3. The contract¶

    3.1 Lifetimes¶

    lifetime holds
    the run (refine3D, refine2D command; a distributed part's program) partition identity, the project owner's project, control metadata; nothing of the alignment
    the iteration's model, immutable from before the particle loop to after it references (polar model or class averages, both halves), band and grid, shift-map geometry, committed sigma2 spectra, the sample, the incoming orientations, shifts and widths (the input view), the temperature formula, prob_beta, the shift prior's width, the refined-reference budget, the insertion cones, the 2D class populations
    the alignment phase's workspace compatible FFT plans, the map's setup, the optimisers, per-thread numerical scratch; reused between batches, released before the 3D reconstruction
    one batch (capacity from the memory budget of section 3.5, at most nthr x BATCHTHRSZ to begin with) the canonical observations, the prepared polar particles and CTF terms, the particle temperatures (computed from the prepared particles), the complete candidate rows, the drawn poses and extras, the polished poses, the residuals; released when its decisions are committed
    across the 3D alignment/reconstruction barrier the final poses, shift states, the frozen insertion poses and weights, the sigma2 contributions, the record buffer of cropped observations (as now); no alignment calculator, reference memo or row

    3.2 The iteration's model is an enforced input boundary¶

    Everything in the model row above is computed once, in begin_alignment, from the complete incoming particle field before any decision is published, and passed to every fill and polish consumer. In particular projs_ns (calc_num2sample) and the shift prior's width (shift_prior_sigma2) leave the fills (section 2.2). The fills and the selector read per-particle metadata from the input view and write decisions to an output view; no batch routine derives an iteration statistic from the evolving output. No whole parameters or builder is copied for this: the model holds the scalars and arrays it needs and borrows the domain objects (the calculator, the references) explicitly.

    The regression that guards it (section 12): with the iteration's input fixed, change earlier batches' output distances, states and shift increments; later batches' rows must be identical, at several batch boundaries and part layouts.

    3.3 The operations¶

    One strategy-owned alignment context and one bounded batch workspace, with these operations (ownership boundaries, not seven modules; the existing fills, selection rules, polish and residual routines are reused behind them, not rewritten):

    begin_alignment(input model, incoming metadata, sampling/RNG scope, consumers)
prepare_batch(global particle IDs, canonical observations, workspace)
evaluate_rows(model, incoming metadata, RNG scope, workspace)
select_rows(model, RNG scope, workspace)
finalize_batch(model, workspace, decisions, noise, canonical records)
publish_results(decisions, noise, diagnostics)
end_alignment() -> release alignment resources before 3D reconstruction

    The outer entry runs on (params, build); the inner operations receive the specific inputs they need. Plans, map setup, optimisers and scratch belong to the context and are reused where safe; mutable numerical scratch stays per thread. A new model generation, band, noise, mask, half or preprocessing invalidates the context's caches explicitly, and an input of another generation is rejected at the hand-off. Domain evaluation and selection know neither commanders, file names, queues nor images; only publish_results writes changed project rows or partition artefacts. GPU batching later replaces evaluate_rows through this boundary; SPMD replaces publication and the iteration's transport without touching the science.

    The batch path, with the existing routine behind each step:

    batch of sampled particles (the part's share, in order)
  -> read, or take from the record buffer      (ptcl_read_session)
  -> prepare the alignment batch               (build_batch_particles3D / 2D)
  -> fill the complete rows                    (the route's fill on the batch)
  -> draw each particle's pose and extras      (the route's selection rule over draw_posterior_row)
  -> polish the drawn pose                     (refine_assignment_continuously / refine_selected_continuously)
  -> sigma2 residual of the committed pose     (as now)
  -> commit: orientation, shift state, extras  (as now)
  -> 2D: the batch's class sums; 3D: the record for the reconstruction
  -> release the rows

    The row never leaves the process: the sparse routes keep their candidate lists as the row (an adapter over the candidate stream's records), the dense routes the batch's batchsz x nrefs entries (at 2,500 references over all active states and 300 particles, 6 MB for distances and in-plane indices; refined entries, diagnostics and scratch are additional).

    One selector. draw_posterior_row stays the primitive. The route adapters give it the row (the dense block of the drawn state, the neighbourhood's pooled candidates, the 2D class row or candidate list) and take back the draw, as ref_assign, ref_assign_neigh, ref_assign_sparse and the 2D ref_assign do today; those four become the adapters' selection step, called per particle inside the batch instead of over the global table. The multi-state joint draw lands in the same place.

    The decision schema: canonical particle, state and reference identities; pose and score; absolute and incremental shifts with their unit and frame; shift width and state; fallback status; the frozen insertion poses and weights; validity flags for the optional consumers. Exact-once coverage of the part's share is checked at publication.

    Consumers decide the preparation. Scoring and polish need the polar particles; the sigma2 residual needs the committed pose against the reference; the 3D reconstruction needs the cropped record; the 2D class sums need the cropped (or raw) particle. Nothing is prepared twice for the alignment, and nothing is prepared for a consumer that is off (volrec=no, objfun=cc). The canonical observations stay distinct from the normalised, masked alignment transforms: reusing one as the other needs a proven preparation contract (CTF, band, half, noise, centring, frame), and none is assumed here.

    3.4 What stays as it is¶

    The master's sample draw and project ownership (layering_and_project_ownership_policy.md); merge_algndocs; volume assembly and the sigma2 commit; the 3D partial reconstruction after the loop from the record buffer, one state and one half at a time (separate_alignment_and_reconstruction_for_multistate_peak_mem_reduction.md stays a hard boundary; at the barrier no decision or diagnostic keeps a pointer into a released batch, calculator or reference allocation); the 2D class-sum order (batch, class, half); the pose_cont Cartesian polish pass, which stays a pass of its own and keeps its preparation; the classic modes (shc, snhc_smpl, ...), untouched; the stream's pool and the solve2D chunks, which run refine2D through its commander and are not edited.

    3.5 The memory budget¶

    The guaranteed saving is the master's global table and its transport. A worker already bounds its dense rows by the batch; fusing the table's and the matcher's workspaces can raise its peak if both coexist. The context budgets, in checked 64-bit byte arithmetic, the references of all active states, the per-thread map and optimiser scratch, the canonical buffers, the prepared batch, the complete and refined rows, the optional diagnostics and the result metadata, and chooses the batch capacity inside the remaining budget (ptcl_buffer_budget is the model: half the task memory the queue grants). Worker, master and whole-node peaks are measured, including at the 3D barrier (section 12).

    4. The random-stream contract¶

    4.1 The generator¶

    A keyed, stateless draw rnd_keyed(key) -> real(dp) in [0,1): the key's words are mixed by a multiply-xor-shift hash on 32-bit lanes with 64-bit products (a 32-bit lane times a 32-bit constant fits a 64-bit integer; no step relies on integer overflow, which Fortran leaves undefined), finalised to 64 bits, and the top 53 bits divided by 2^53 (so 0 is possible and 1 is not). The exact constants, the lane order, the finaliser and the float mapping are pinned by fixed test vectors in simple_rnd_tester.f90, checked in the Debug and Release builds of this run and recorded in this note for the Mac build. The generator carries a version in its key; changing any of this changes results and bumps the version. One hash per draw; the kernels dwarf it.

    4.2 The key schema¶

    Every draw's key is the tuple

    field what
    seed the run's 64-bit seed: SIMPLE_SEED when set, else drawn once by the project owner at the run's start and exported to the parts as SIMPLE_SEED through the environment the queue scripts inherit (put_simple_seed, as today), never through a real value or a new key
    version the generator's schema version
    scope the logical pass: the program (refine3D, refine2D), the pass kind (discrete alignment; the pose_cont polish; the final full-population assignment; a diagnostic evaluation), which together with the iteration distinguish repeated commands and solve stages (a solve stage's refine3D continues which_iter, so stage and iteration are one number)
    iteration which_iter within the scope
    particle the particle's row in the run's particle field (global, the same on the master and every part, as fromp..top are); never a batch slot, a part-local index, a thread or a process
    family SAMPLE, INPL, DISK, CAND, ASSIGN, DIAG
    reference the canonical reference (state, direction or class) where the family has one
    ordinal the sub-draw's ordinal within the family for the particle (the screen's ordinal, the pick's ordinal, the sampler's draw count)

    Reuse versus independence is explicit: an evaluation that is repeated on the same reference in the same pass reuses the variate (same key); a distinct pass or a diagnostic gets a distinct scope or family. The sample's draws belong to the project owner (particle 0, the sampler's ordinal). Nothing is keyed on a thread, a part, an image, an arrival order, a path or a process.

    4.4 Restart¶

    The seed, the generator version, the scope and the next iteration are written with the run's restart metadata (the job description and the project's projinfo, where refine3D continue=yes and the stream's restart read their state) as well as the environment. A retry of a logical iteration reuses its keys; a distinct pass gets a distinct scope; inconsistent restart metadata is rejected.

    4.5 What changes, once¶

    The draws' values change (a different generator, keyed), so every probabilistic trajectory changes once, in phase 1, before any structural move. The same logical draw then returns the same bits on any thread, in any batch, in any part; identical prepared inputs and arithmetic give identical rows and decisions wherever they run. A complete run still differs across layouts by the rounding of section 2.4; section 8 says what is accepted.

    5. The pipeline, per route¶

    3D dense. The batch loop of refine3D_exec takes over the fill: for each batch, fill_tab_range on the batch's rows (the map's screen and polish per particle, as now, with projs_ns and the prior's width from the model), then per particle the dense selection (the state by the closest reference, the direction by the draw in its block; or the joint draw of the multi-state proposal), the extras under topk > 1 from the row and the cone (select_extras), then strategy3D_prob%srch_prob as it is (polish, commit, extras resolved), the residual, the record. The eul_prob_tab object shrinks to a per-batch row store plus the assignment map of the part.

    3D neighbourhood. The same with eul_prob_tab_neigh%fill_tab_range and the pooled-candidate selection of ref_assign_neigh.

    2D dense and sparse. The batch loop of refine2D_exec takes over the fill (fill_tab_range or fill_tab_prob_neigh_range on the batch), then the class draw (ref_assign or ref_assign_sparse per particle), then strategy2D_prob%srch_prob as it is, then the batch's class sums in the order of today. The particle buffer's hand-over between the table and the search becomes moot: the batch's prepared particles serve both. The stream's pool runs the same refine2D.

    Empty parts, fractional updates, assign_all, update_missing. A part with no sampled particles runs no batches and writes its empty documents, as now. Sampling is unchanged (the master's draw, keyed). update_missing keeps its refusal under the probabilistic modes.

    The readouts. The rows of the n_eff readout are chosen from the global sampled manifest before partitioning (the same rows whatever nparts); each part publishes sums and counts over its share of them, and the master merges them in part order (so the readout is exactly reproducible), never means of means. Integer counts (row mode, coverage, disk set) are summed. The disk-set line moves to the master's sample draw, where its inputs are. The multi-state readouts (population, mass, normalisation) go through the same result boundary.

    • No master table and no part files: 713 MB and 4 x 199 MB per iteration on trpv1; 15 GB per state at 750,000 particles. A worker's alignment memory is the references, the thread scratch and one batch, within the budget of section 3.5 (measured, not assumed).

    8. Acceptance levels¶

    The run holds every phase to these; none is relaxed.

    1. Draw identity. Identical logical keys give identical random bits whatever the execution order, thread count, batch size, partitioning or restart (fixed test vectors; the permutation test of the sparse picks).
    2. Frozen-input identity. Identical prepared inputs and arithmetic give identical candidate rows, decisions, fallbacks, shift metadata and frozen extras under different schedules (one and eight threads, two batch sizes, one and three parts), compared by canonical particle and reference identities, on the four routes.
    3. Structural identity at a fixed configuration. Each structural phase (2, 3, 4) reproduces the previous phase's outputs byte for byte at each fixed execution configuration (the declared outputs of the registry's entries, test=identity, at one thread and at eight; shared memory and distributed each against itself), including the classic stages that follow a probabilistic stage in a workflow.
    4. Complete-run numerical tolerance across layouts. Runs of one build at different layouts (shared memory; 2, 3 and 4 parts; one and eight threads) agree on fixed-input accumulators (a reconstruction and a class-average set from the same committed poses) within a tolerance frozen in phase 0 from the base code's own layout-to-layout differences (twice the base's largest observed difference, recorded in Progress), and the multi-seed truth gates (the suites' floors) stay unchanged and met. Stochastic trajectories need not match.

    Byte identity of complete runs across part counts is not required by this note. If the maintainer requires it, a reproducible-reduction, FFT and preparation contract is costed separately before phase 1; it cannot be had while those operations are left as they are.

    9. Risks and open points¶

    12. How the work is tested and judged¶

    The ladder. A change goes to a slower rung only when it passes the faster ones: the unit suites of the touched areas and the fast gate (minutes); the frozen-input identity harness and the identity entries at one thread (half an hour); the identity entries at eight threads and the distributed cases (an hour); the truth suites and the real-data readouts (hours).

    Level 1, draw identity: unit_numerics (random draws): the generator's fixed vectors (keys and expected bits, in both builds), the unit-interval mapping's endpoints, independence of order (a permuted key sequence gives the same values), and the keyed partial permutation's distribution (every subset of size k equally likely over many keys, and the previous-class-first rule).

    Level 2, frozen-input identity: a harness on the synthetic references and particles of polar_matching_bench (which already compares the production kernels at one thread against eight): the fill, the selection and the extras of each route on the same prepared batch at one and eight threads, two batch sizes and one and three parts, compared by particle and reference identity; the selectors on hand-built rows with explicit variates against an independent cumulative-posterior oracle (ties, invalid and empty rows, state blocks, sparse eligibility, frozen extras); the input-boundary regression of section 3.2. These are unit tests in the tables' and the bench's testers.

    14. Common rules¶

    • Nothing is committed; git is read-only; the base is a git archive export.
    • Fix as you go: a defect found is fixed when found and recorded with its evidence; a base failure outside this run's files is recorded, not fixed.
    • Thresholds are frozen before the runs they judge, never loosened; no run is repeated until it passes.
    • New or changed OpenMP code gets a tester at 16 threads, repeated, compared bit for bit with one thread; no string temporaries inside parallel regions; work arrays sized by the team in use.
    • No goto; THROW macros never continued with //&; no .and. short circuit on allocatable arrays; use ..., only:; no large type as a plain component (doc/policies/compile_time_policy.md); no new parameter (ruling 2); no generator or hash that relies on integer overflow (section 4.1).
    • Timed work runs with the load recorded; a measurement under load is repeated.
    • Bulky outputs are deleted once their numbers are in Progress; scratch/keep/ holds the base export and build, the identity run directories of phase 1 and the measurements' logs.
    • Write prose for people: Progress entries and the report read without other documents, with SIMPLE terms explained and acronyms spelled out on first use; name things by what they do.

    The generator (item 3; ruling R2 applied here, section 10). rnd_keyed in src/utils/math/simple_rnd.f90 beside seed_rnd: a draw is a pure function of its key, the type rnd_key with the fields of section 4.2 (seed as a 64-bit integer; version, scope, iteration, particle, family, reference, ordinal as default integers). The key's nine 32-bit lanes, in this pinned order: the seed's low word, the seed's high word, the version, the scope, the iteration, the particle, the family, the reference, the ordinal (a negative field contributes its two's-complement low word). The mixer is lowbias32, two multiply-xor-shift rounds on a 32-bit lane held in a signed 64-bit integer: x ^= x >> 16; x = (x * 0x21f0aaad) mod 2^32; x ^= x >> 15; x = (x * 0x735a2d97) mod 2^32; x ^= x >> 15 (the constants 569420461 and 1935289751, both below 2^31, so a lane times a constant stays below 2^63: no step relies on integer overflow, which the Fortran standard leaves undefined). Two lanes a and b start at 0x6a09e667 and 0x510e527f (the fractional parts of the square roots of 2 and 11, 1779033703 and 1359893119); lane i of the key is absorbed as a = mix32(a xor w_i), b = mix32(b xor a xor i); the finaliser is a = mix32(a xor b), b = mix32(b xor a); the 64-bit word is a in the high and b in the low 32 bits; the unit variate is the word's top 53 bits over 2^53 (0 is possible, 1 is not). Beside the variate: rnd_keyed_bits (the word), rnd_keyed_int(key, n) (the uniform integer in 1..n by the variate's equal-width bin, as irnd_uni), and rnd_keyed_partial_shuffle(key, arr, nshuffle), the keyed partial Fisher-Yates permutation whose swap i draws its uniform under the key with ordinal = i (so the permutation depends on the key alone, never on the order of other draws).

    What the numbers say. Wall time. In 3D the fill is 93 per cent of the part's job on both builds and is unchanged (295 against 293 s at the first iteration, the same shift searches, evaluations and memos, the same thread-seconds within 1 per cent); the pipeline saves the second scheduling phase and the second preparation, read and reference load of each part (about 4 s a part, 1.3 per cent, as section 2.5 estimated), and the run is 1.4 per cent faster (1,001 against 1,015 s). In 2D the duplicated preparation was 8 per cent (crop 120) and the full-box preparation 13 per cent (box 256) of the part's job, and the runs are 17 and 15 per cent faster (34.6 against 41.6 s, 81.5 against 96.0 s); the 2D fill's own wall time is 7 to 8 per cent higher in the pipeline at the first iteration (9.1 against 8.5 s, 21.0 against 19.5 s, with the same shift searches and 1 to 9 per cent more objective evaluations and refinement thread-seconds), which the batch structure explains best (the pipeline fills the matcher's batches, smaller than the table worker's, and a batch builds its search objects), and which the preparation saved outweighs by three to four times. The second iterations are not comparable across builds: the keyed draws give another trajectory, and the base's second iteration refined more (its coarse maps 5.3e5 against 2.4e5). Memory. The master's peak falls by 13 per cent in 3D (1.26 to 1.10 GB) with the global table, and stays in 2D (0.32, 0.44 GB); the worker's peak is the search worker's (2.42 GB in 3D: the particle buffer and the reconstruction, the alignment context at 1.38 GB within its budget; 0.49 and 0.84 GB in 2D), where the table worker's 1.56 GB (0.35, 0.62 GB in 2D) is gone from the node; the node's peak is unchanged (16 GB in 3D, 8.3 and 9.7 GB in 2D, over a 6.2 GB baseline), being the four search workers' peaks beside the master's; at the 3D barrier the master assembles while the parts have exited. At 750,000 particles the master's table would have been 15 GB per state (section 2.5): that, not the wall time, is what the pipeline removes. Preparations per particle. One compatible preparation per particle per iteration on the final build (3.3 s a part in 3D, 2.0 and 5.5 s in 2D) against two on the base; the reference loads once a part (0.3 s) against twice; no table bytes written (199 MB a part per iteration before); the map and optimiser work of the fill unchanged.
