    The atomicity prior on the PCG backend, for SINGLE and atomic electron tomography¶

    Implementation note. First version 2026-09-15; revised 2026-10-15 at master e2673a5d7.

    Status (2026-10-20): the core is implemented; the integrations are open. Done: the prior as a module (simple_pcg_atomic_prior, with unit tests), its hook in the PCG reconstructor, and reconstruct3D atm_prior=yes pdbfile= with the plumbing in simple_rec3D_pcg_strategy (section 11 records the choices and the FePt result). Open, and described in ../planned/single_pcg_integration_unfinished.md: the refine_tomo and refine3D_nano / autorefine3D_nano integration, the held-out scan inside the program (atm_cv), the TEM five-Gaussian kernel with the atoms%convolve kernel builder, per-atom amplitudes into species discovery, and the acceptance runs of section 9 that have not been recorded. Sections 0 to 10 below are the original design text (written when nothing was implemented) and are kept as history. Companions: pcg_backend_overview.md (the solver), reconstruct3D_pcg_policy.md, pcg_decision_log.md, pcg_priors_history.md (the priors tried before), aet_codes_and_simple.md (the FePt work) and doc/refactoring_notes/planned/refine_tomo.md. The species work this feeds is species_discovery_completed.md and species_discovery_unfinished.md.

    0. What the revision changes¶

    • No ICM step. The first version began by reproducing the ICM filter of refine3D_nano inside the solver. ICM is little used any more and never on denoised data, so that step and its Laplacian term are gone. Version 1 has one prior and one strength.
    • Tomography first. The prior is tried first on atomic electron tomography (AET) through refine_tomo, then on SINGLE particle data. A tilt series is fixed and small, a run takes minutes, and the FePt data come with a published atomic model; simulated tilt series with exact atoms are planned with Peter Ercius (NCEM).
    • The solver has changed. Unmeasured Fourier voxels are now excluded from the preconditioner (PCG_RHO_SAMPLED_FRAC), a real-space solvent ridge exists with its mean folded into the preconditioner, the data operator's real-space diagonal (realspace_diag) is available, and a density mask can be the solve support (pcg_mskfile). Section 5 builds on these.
    • The lessons of the rejected priors are rules here (section 2), above all how a strength must be set so that it carries across data sets.

    1. Why now: what the FePt tilt series showed¶

    The FePt nanoparticle of Yang et al. (2017), 68 projections from −65° to +64°, reconstructed in SIMPLE and scored against their published model (23,196 atoms, 28% Fe) with the same atom detector:

    Map Level-1 recall Pt above background Fe above background Atom elongation
    SIMPLE gridding 0.25 63% 4% 2.00
    SIMPLE PCG, 5 iterations 0.35 66% 2% 2.03
    refine_tomo (refined poses, density mask, PCG) 0.42 74% 3% 2.07
    Their GENFIRE map 0.41 90% 7% 1.89

    "Above background" is the share of atoms whose smoothed density at the true position exceeds the 99th percentile of the density between atoms; elongation is the ratio of the longest to the shortest axis of the mean Pt atom. What these numbers say:

    • Detection is matched, the background is not. Least squares with a density support plus pose refinement detects as many atoms as GENFIRE. Their map is still cleaner between atoms.
    • Positivity is not the reason. A GENFIRE-style iteration with positivity on our data made the atoms as round as theirs (1.90) and the map free of negative values, but lifted no more atoms above the background, and recall fell from 0.30 to 0.24.
    • Every map smears atoms along the beam. The missing 50° wedge doubles the atom length in that direction, in their map too.
    • Fe is barely visible anywhere. Almost no Fe atom is detected, so species discovery splits the Pt in two instead of finding Fe.

    The atomicity prior addresses all three directly: it pulls the space between atoms to zero, it extrapolates into the missing wedge with atom-shaped density instead of nothing, and it returns a least-squares amplitude for every atom, which is what species discovery needs (section 3.3).

    2. Lessons from the earlier priors¶

    From pcg_priors_history.md, pcg_decision_log.md, the NU-evidence sharpening note and LocScale (~/src/locscale, v2.4.1):

    • Strength anchored to the low-band data scale does not carry over. The biharmonic smoothness prior, the binary solvent prior and the NU-evidence precision all set their strength relative to data_scale, the mean of the data diagonal over the six lowest shells. Their effect then depended on how steeply the data diagonal falls across the band, a property of each data set: the same coefficient was inert on PfCRT and strong on msp1. The NU prior worked (it beat everything on the 1WCM truth phantom) and was removed for that reason, after a controller that steered it by the FSC of non-independent halves wrecked ten runs.
    • A spectral target is not the operative ingredient. The Wilson prior (a shell-diagonal precision from a reference spectrum) helped at both ends of the band but was beaten everywhere by local evidence. The atomic kernels carry the Wilson envelope themselves: a kernel with width B has power |f(s)|² exp(−B s²/2), and the positions add the structure factor. No separate spectrum is needed.
    • The solvent ridge shows the mechanics. It is a real-space diagonal term λ_s (1 − w(r)) with a protein weight built per half from that half's own prior-free map. The closed-form regularized solve cannot represent it, so it needs a real solve, and without its mean in the preconditioner two iterations stalled. The FSC is never computed from a prior'd pair.
    • LocScale. Model-based amplitude references sharpen well. Its hybrid mode fills unmodelled density with pseudo-atoms, the same split as atoms plus a free residual. It smooths refined B factors over 25 Å, which argues for a smooth B field over free per-atom B. Randomly placed pseudo-atoms impose a liquid-like structure factor that real proteins do not have, so a kernel prior needs real atoms or a real model; nanoparticles and AET specimens have them. Its network mode warns about hallucinated density. Its Wilson completion is no longer called in v2.4.1.
    • Rules every prior obeys. A symmetric positive semi-definite term; a linear operator frozen during a solve; nothing accumulated into the raw accumulators; attached only in deapodized kernel mode; strength zero reproduces the prior-free result bit for bit; half independence kept or its loss declared; resolution reported from the prior-free pair only; judged by truth, by held-out data or against a model, never by the FSC of prior'd halves; one variable per experiment, thresholds written down before a run.

    3. The prior¶

    3.1 Definition¶

    The map is modelled as x = G c + e. Column g_i of G is the density kernel of atom i at its model position, c are free per-atom amplitudes, and e is what the atoms do not explain. Only e is penalized:

    Q(x) = λ_A · xᵀ (I − P_G) x,        P_G = G (GᵀG)⁻¹ Gᵀ

    P_G is the orthogonal projector onto the span of the atom kernels, so I − P_G is symmetric and idempotent and the term is already in normal-equation form. The regularized solve is

    (P H P + λ_A (I − P_G)) u = P b

    on the solve support P, solved by the existing CG.

    3.2 Properties¶

    • Scale-free. P_G does not change when a column of G or the map is rescaled. The model contributes positions, species and kernel shape, never amplitudes, so the simulated and reconstructed maps never need a common scale.
    • Vacuum. Where no kernel reaches, P_G x = 0 and the term is λ_A ‖x‖²: the space between atoms is pulled to zero by the same operator. This is the useful half of positivity, kept linear.
    • The missing wedge. The kernels have full Fourier support. Atom amplitudes set by the measured data therefore come with the matching density in the unmeasured wedge, while λ_A suppresses whatever else would fill it. Atoms should come out rounder without a nonlinear constraint.
    • One parameter spans the range. λ_A = 0 is the present solve. As λ_A → ∞ the solution is exactly G ĉ, atoms with least-squares amplitudes. No separate atomic-basis solver is needed.

    3.3 Per-atom amplitudes¶

    Species discovery now classifies an aperture sum on a filtered map. On the synthetic particles that sum underestimates the Pt/Ni ratio by 23% and the Pt/Al ratio by 43%.

    The replacement is the amplitude fitted to the data, ĉ = argmin ‖A G c − b‖². It solves Gᵀ H G c = Gᵀ r with the reconstructor's own normal operator H and right-hand side r, by a small CG in atom space. This is the strong-prior limit of the regularized solve.

    The amplitude measured on a map, (GᵀG)⁻¹ Gᵀ x, does not work. In the numpy prototype on FePt (2026-10-15, ~/agent_runs/runs/atm_prior_mac/numpy_proto/RESULTS.md) it separated atoms from false peaks no better than the peak height. A two-class mixture on it did not split Fe from Pt at all.

    The amplitude fitted to the projections did both:

    • Atoms against false peaks: recall at 90% precision rose from 0.75 to 0.85.
    • Fe against Pt: the unsupervised two-class split called 76 to 80% of the Fe atoms Fe at about 70% precision.

    Refining each atom's position against the data (Gauss-Newton steps, with the amplitudes refitted after each step) raised the Fe recall further. It is tested on its own (section 9, step 4).

    3.4 Half independence¶

    The atom positions come from the merged base map, so they are a shared frozen field. Both halves are then pulled toward the same atoms, and the FSC of the regularized pair is inflated. As for the solvent ridge: resolution is reported from the prior-free base pair only.

    4. Kernels and the one fitted width¶

    • TEM data (SINGLE). The five-Gaussian electron scattering factor of each element, as atoms%convolve uses, broadened by B_i = B_min + B_data.
    • Annular dark-field STEM (AET). Intensity scales roughly as Z^1.7, and less predictably for low-angle detectors. The prior ignores amplitudes, so only the kernel shape matters: a Gaussian per species, whose width comes from the fit below. The FePt refinement of Yang et al. used one B per species, which is a precedent for a per-species width.
    • The width fit. On the base map x_b with the model simulated at B_min, the per-shell regression slope c_s = Re⟨m̂_s, x̂_s⟩ / ‖m̂_s‖² is unbiased under map noise. Fit log c_s = log K − B_data s²/4 over shells where the model correlates with the map at 0.5 or more; discard K; clamp B_data ≥ 0. One B_data in version 1. A smooth B field, the LocScale lesson, comes later; free per-atom B does not.
    • Footprints and Gram matrix. Kernels are evaluated within cutoff (4 to 8 pixels) and clipped to the solve support, so P_G lives on the solve domain. GᵀG is sparse (only overlapping footprints couple) and positive definite as long as atoms are separated. It is solved by an inner CG or a banded Cholesky built once per model. When kernels overlap strongly (wide B at low resolution), the Gram solve gets a small ridge ε I.

    5. Operator and preconditioner¶

    • Operator. apply_normal adds λ_A (p − P_G p) next to add_ridge, after the support mask. P_G p is a gather, a Gram solve and a scatter, all in real space; no FFT is added. Cost per CG iteration: about N_atoms × footprint operations, small next to the FFTs.
    • Preconditioner. The denominator of the measured voxels gains λ_A (in the kernel's units, divided by padsc² as for the ML prior). The unmeasured voxels keep the present zero. The numpy prototype on FePt (2026-10-15, ~/agent_runs/runs/atm_prior_mac/numpy_proto/RESULTS.md) tried both. Giving the unmeasured voxels the gain 1/λ_A stalled CG at small strengths and gave worse maps at every strength. With the zero, CG converged at every strength, and the wedge still filled through the support coupling: Pt atoms went from twice as long along the beam as across to 1.4 to 1.8 times.
    • Iteration budget. Not the production two iterations. The prior acts through the coupled solve, so the budget is set by convergence on FePt and on the synthetic tilt series (an rtol, with maxits as a cap; 20 to 30 expected). The kernel operator's behaviour when over-iterated is watched with the existing indefinite-stop guard and checked against the matrix-free operator on the small fixtures.
    • What is untouched. The raw accumulators, the ML prior and its closed form (not used on these paths), the solvent ridge.

    6. Strength¶

    The earlier priors failed on their anchor, not their form. This one is set so that it does not depend on how steeply the data fall across the band.

    • Anchor. λ_A = λ_A,rel × realspace_diag. realspace_diag is the mean of the data diagonal over all native shells, the data precision per voxel in real space. A real-space prior competes with that, not with the low-band mean.

    • Estimate. λ_A,rel is a Wiener ratio from moments of the base map:

      N = noise variance per voxel S = max(mean(x_b²) over the support − N, ε N) signal power E = mean((P_G x_b)²) over the support power the atoms explain E' = max(E − N · N_atoms / |V|, 0) minus the noise in span(G) C² = min(E'/S, 0.98) explained fraction of the signal λ_A,rel = (N/S) / (1 − C²)

    N comes from the half-map difference where the halves are independent (an AET tilt series split by interleaved tilts: var((x_even − x_odd)/2) inside the support), and from the empty region of the box otherwise. The ratio is the sigma-A weight of crystallographic map combination, built from the map's own moments; no constant is tuned. - Check by held-out data, never by FSC. For a tilt series, each half's regularized map predicts the other half's projections, which are other tilts. For particles, each half's map predicts the other half's images (the cross-validation of the first version). The score is the prediction error over the run's band, on a grid of {0, ¼, ½, 1, 2, 4} × λ_A,rel. - Acceptance. The estimate becomes the default only if, on every test set of section 9, steps 3 and 5, its held-out prediction error is within 1% of the best on the grid. Otherwise lambda_atm stays an explicit input. The test is on the error, not on the strength, because the optimum is flat. In the numpy prototype on FePt (every fourth tilt held out), the estimate came out at λ_rel 0.5 to 0.7 and the held-out optimum at 0.1. The error differed by 1% across that range. Every regularized map predicted the held-out tilts better than the prior-free map at its best iteration count (0.117 against 0.121).

    7. Where it sits¶

    7.1 Tomography (refine_tomo)¶

    Version 1 changes only the final reconstruction:

    1. The masked PCG base map from the refined poses, as now.
    2. detect_atoms on the base map with the element list, in process, giving the model, with the atoms species discovery recovers from the residual. The model errs on inclusion: a spurious kernel costs little (its amplitude goes to zero), a missing one pulls a real atom toward zero. Surface atoms are the ones at risk, being the weakest and partly occupied on real maps.
    3. B_data, the Gram matrix and λ_A from the base map.
    4. The regularized solve per half.

    Products: tomo_final.mrc (regularized), tomo_final_base.mrc, tomo_final_res.mrc (x_b − G ĉ, the residual on the base map) and a sidecar _atmprior.txt (B_data, N, S, C², λ_A,rel, N_atoms, CG statistics). Peaks of the residual above 3√N are missing-atom candidates; atoms with ĉ_i far below their species' median are removal candidates. Using the regularized map as the reference of the pose refinement comes later, after the drift found on 2026-10-15 is understood.

    7.2 SINGLE particles (refine3D_nano, autorefine3D_nano)¶

    The loop is an EM iteration. detect_atoms (the M-step) builds the model after each refinement stage. The next stage's reconstructions use the regularized solve (the E-step) as the alignment reference, with the base solve kept for resolution and for the residual. Stage 1, without a model, runs prior-free. ICM is not combined with the prior.

    9. Order of work and the gates¶

    1. The module. Unit tests on a synthetic box:
    2. P_G is a projector (P_G² = P_G, symmetric, P_G G = G);
    3. apply is symmetric to 1e-6;
    4. P_G does not change when columns are rescaled;
    5. the Gram solve is accurate;
    6. in the reconstructor, strength zero reproduces the prior-free solve bit for bit, and the kernel and matrix-free operators agree with the prior on.
    7. Synthetic tilt series. The atomic phantom of the PCG sparse-sampling test, with a sparse and a dense tilt range.
    8. With the true model: rounder atoms and a cleaner background than prior-free, at the same detection.
    9. With a damaged model (10% of atoms removed, half of them surface atoms, 10% spurious added, positions jittered 0.2 Å, width off by a factor two, model map scaled by 10): the missing atoms are the top residual peaks; the spurious ones get ĉ ≈ 0 and no density; B_data does not depend on the scale; and detection on the regularized map beats the prior-free map.
    10. Scored against the phantom's atoms, surface atoms (fewer than 12 neighbours) apart from the rest, over several noise realizations; not by FSC.
    11. FePt through refine_tomo. The λ_A grid around the estimate, with the thresholds written down before the run:
    12. level-1 recall and precision, Fe recall, recall of surface atoms (fewer than 12 neighbours in the published model);
    13. background separation and atom elongation;
    14. held-out tilt prediction error;
    15. distance to the published model.
    16. Amplitudes for species. ĉ against the aperture intensities on FePt and on the synthetic species cases of species_discovery, by coordination number.
    17. Across data sets. At least these four, so that a calibration that holds only on FePt is caught:
    18. the multislice simulations with Peter Ercius (NCEM), with exact atoms;
    19. a single-species AET set (the tungsten tip of Xu et al., 2015);
    20. an AET Pt/Ni set if obtainable;
    21. the SINGLE Pt particle data.

    The estimate's acceptance rule (section 6) is judged here. 6. SINGLE particles in refine3D_nano and autorefine3D_nano, judged by held-out image prediction and the atom statistics of detect_atoms. 7. Later. A smooth B field; a per-atom trust weight (if surface or low-Z atoms fail the hard inclusion); non-negative amplitudes; the prior in the pose-refinement references.

    10. Related methods¶

    • Crystallography. Atomicity has long been used to extend phases: Sayre's equation (1952) and the squaring methods, and density modification (solvent flattening, histogram matching). In cryo-EM this was taken up by Terwilliger et al. (2020). ARP/wARP (Perrakis et al., 1999) alternates placing free atoms and recomputing the map, the same loop as section 7.2. Charge flipping (Oszlányi & Sütő, 2004) is a dual-space iteration that needs no prior model.
    • Radio interferometry. CLEAN (Högbom, 1974) deconvolves an incompletely sampled Fourier plane by subtracting point sources and keeping a residual map, the same idea as the residual of section 7.1. Self-calibration (Cornwell & Wilkinson, 1981) alternates the source model with the instrument calibration. It cannot recover the absolute position or flux scale, much as our pose refinement drifted in the tilt scale and frame rotation.
    • Materials STEM. Statistical atom counting from annular dark-field intensities (StatSTEM, De Backer et al., 2016) fits a Gaussian per atomic column and counts atoms with a mixture model, using integrated intensities as section 3.3 does. Discrete tomography (DART, Batenburg & Sijbers, 2011) and atom counting gave 3D atomic positions of nanoparticles (Van Aert et al., 2011). De Backer et al. (2022, npj Comput. Mater. 8:216) build a 3D Pt particle from a single projection: per-column atom counts with their mixture-model probabilities, a neighbour-count prior and an embedded-atom energy, searched by a genetic algorithm. They judge the result by the share of surface atoms placed correctly against simulated truth over 30 noise realizations, the score adopted in section 9. Their time series shows surface atoms moving under the beam while the atom count stays fixed, consistent with the partly occupied outer sites seen on the SINGLE Pt maps.
    • Sparse recovery. Off-the-grid point-source recovery (Candès & Fernandez-Granda, 2014) and the atomic norm (Chandrasekaran et al., 2012) formalize when a sum of point sources can be recovered from limited Fourier data.
    • Cryo-EM maps. Gaussian-mixture representations of maps (Chen & Ludtke, 2021) and denoiser priors inside refinement (RELION Blush, Kimanius et al., 2024). The latter show the gain of strong priors and the risk of density the data do not support.

    11. Status (2026-10-16)¶

    Implemented for reconstruct3D (atm_prior=yes pdbfile= [lambda_atm= bfac_data= maxits_atm= rtol_atm=]): simple_pcg_atomic_prior with its unit tests (unit_reconstruction, sub-suite atomic_prior), the hook in simple_reconstructor_pcg (sections 5 and 8) and the plumbing in simple_rec3D_pcg_strategy. Not yet done: refine_tomo and refine3D_nano integration, the held-out scan inside the program (atm_cv), the TEM five-Gaussian kernel and the atoms%convolve kernel builder.

    Choices made in the implementation:

    • Kernel. One Gaussian for all species, B = B_min + B_data, B_min a Gaussian of one voxel.
    • Width fit. It simulates its own model with a Gaussian of 1.5 voxels and a 5-sigma footprint. The narrowest kernel aliases and a truncated footprint adds a high-frequency plateau; both bent the per-shell slope by 6% in the unit test.
    • Noise of the estimate. The solve is per half, so the estimate uses the half-map noise 2 var((xe-xo)/2). The merged ratio of section 6 is logged beside it and is half of it.
    • Gram solve. Jacobi-preconditioned CG in double precision with a ridge of 1e-7 of each diagonal entry.
    • Budget. 30 iterations by default. On FePt the CG residual is 1.5e-3 at 30 and 1.2e-3 at 60, and the atom scores move by 1 to 2% between the two.
    • Half maps. The prior'd halves are no noise reference: species discovery on them overcalls. Use the prior-free _base halves.
