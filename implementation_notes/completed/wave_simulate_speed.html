    Wave mode in simulate_particles, a faster wave operator, and clean-up¶

    Status: plan, 2026-10-24, decisions of the same day applied. Nothing implemented. It follows the wave_operators run (ghostbuster_operators.md and its report) and section 3 of wave_model_summary.md, which explains the terms used here (wave model, flat model, scale link).

    It starts from a tree that holds the wave_operators changes.

    Decisions (Hans, 2026-10-24)¶

    1. simulate_particles gets a wave option; simulate_scattering stays as it is (the route from a PDB model).
    2. Wave mode takes an ordinary SIMPLE map and converts it to a potential with the scale link, from the dose, the voltage, the pixel size and the box.
    3. Noise in wave mode: Poisson shot noise at the dose on the expected electron counts, then Gaussian detector noise in real space as simulate_particles adds it today.

    1. simulate_particles in wave mode¶

    • Keys wave_model (projection|first_born|rytov|multislice) and ewald (neg|pos|no), the keys reconstruct3D_wave uses, with the same defaults and refusals. Without wave_model the program does exactly what it does today, byte for byte.
    • Wave mode needs total_dose (it sets both the scale and the shot noise) and ctf=yes (a phase object in focus has almost no contrast).
    • Input: each state's map, as SIMPLE writes maps, divided by the scale link kappa = 2 g sigma a N (g the square root of total_dose times the pixel area, sigma the interaction constant at kv, a the pixel size, N the box). The scale link gets one home, a function next to the electron wavelength and interaction constant in simple_math_ctf, used by reconstruct3D_wave (which computes it inline today) and by simulate_particles.
    • Image: the wave operator's prediction of each particle at its pose, shift and CTF (wave_projector), as expected electron counts per pixel, E = total_dose a^2 I with I the relative intensity.
    • Noise: shot noise first, each pixel's count drawn from a Poisson distribution of mean E; then detector noise, Gaussian in real space, its standard deviation sqrt(var(E) / snr) from the noiseless image's variance, as add_gauran sets it today, and none for snr of 5 or more.
    • Output: particles in SIMPLE's convention, x = -(counts - D a^2) / sqrt(D a^2) (contrast inverted, normalised as the scale link assumes), with the orientation table as today.
    • Per-particle B-factors (bfac, bfacerr) act on the lens envelope of each particle, so the operator takes a B-factor per particle. Multiple states, recthres, even, parallel parts (part, fromp, top) work as today: only the per-particle image changes. Multislice is far slower than the single-scatter models; the guide says so, with the measured cost.
    • The detector step (shot noise, then detector noise) is one routine shared with simulate_scattering's detector, which keeps its own keys.

    Tests: the weak-signal limit through the program's path (a weak map gives the flat model's particle, up to second order, in the same units); the noise statistics in an empty region (mean D a^2, variance D a^2 + sdev^2 before the normalisation); the scale link round trip (a wave-mode stack of a map, reconstructed by reconstruct3D with PCG, returns the map's scale at low and middle resolution within the second-order terms; with shot noise only, snr of 5 or more, since detector noise raises the background the scale link assumes to be shot noise); the default path unchanged (simulate_particles without wave_model byte-identical to the base).

    2. A faster wave operator¶

    Measured first: per-stage timers for one gradient pass (gather, lens set-up, FFTs, image formation, scatter) at box 128 and 256, for Rytov, first Born, projection and multislice, on the Release build, nothing else running. Then:

    • Lens set-up. wave_imaging%set_optics rebuilds the lens on the whole fine grid for every particle on every pass, with several transcendental calls per point through a type-bound call. The parts that do not change between particles (the angle terms of the astigmatism, the B-factor envelope at a common B, the Rytov propagator and its inverse, which depend only on the wavelength) are computed once; per particle, one phase evaluation inline and one sine and cosine per point remain.
    • Real fields. The intensity, its derivative and the real part taken in the adjoint are real fields transformed with complex FFTs; real-to-complex and complex-to-real transforms take half the work.
    • Work arrays. Arrays allocated per particle inside the parallel loops (six planes per particle, two cubes per particle for multislice) become per-thread buffers allocated once, sized at the point of use.
    • Shift phase. Computed per pixel with a sine and cosine; separable phase vectors along h and k give the same factor with a product.

    Each change keeps the operator's tests passing at their current tolerances (adjoints, finite differences, weak-signal identity, specter's golden images) and the results identical at 1, 11, 16 and 24 threads; outputs may move at float rounding only. The gain is reported per stage and on the ribosome case of the previous run (reconstruct3D_wave against reconstruct3D with PCG, 24 threads, twice each).

    3. Clean-up to SIMPLE's rules¶

    • Comments to the simple-modern-fortran rules: file headers of at most five lines after !@descr:, procedure headers of at most three, no restating of the policy; the type headers of the new modules (up to 20 lines today) shortened, pointing at doc/policies/3D/wave_model_policy.md instead of the plan.
    • No umbrella imports in the new modules (simple_wave_field, simple_nlcg_solver import simple_core_module_api today).
    • The loop in simulate_scattering's commander that writes predicted planes into an image moves into the operator, shared with simulate_particles.

    Execution¶

    Common rules: build in the run's build tree, the fast gate and check_test_registry.py at every phase end; new or changed OpenMP code tested at 1, 11, 16 and 24 threads, twice each; no tolerance relaxed; timings on the Release build, nothing else heavy running, twice each way; bugs found are fixed in the phase that finds them; prose readable on its own.

    1. simulate_particles in wave mode (section 1) with its tests. Exit: the tests pass; the default path is byte-identical to the base; a stack made in wave mode reconstructs with reconstruct3D to its map's scale.
    2. Speed (section 2). Exit: the per-stage profile before and after recorded; the operator's tests pass unchanged; thread identity; the ribosome timing recorded.
    3. Clean-up, documents, report (section 3; the user guide of simulate_particles, the wave-model policy, the test-environment rows, the summary's status). Exit: check_descr.py and check_test_registry.py pass; this plan moved to completed/ with the report beside it.

    Progress¶

    Phase 0 (run star_wave, Phase 1), 2026-10-24: wave mode in simulate_particles done¶

    In plain words. simulate_particles can now draw particles with the wave model: wave_model=projection|first_born|rytov|multislice (and ewald) take an ordinary SIMPLE map, convert it to a potential in volts with the scale link of total_dose, predict each particle's electron counts with the wave operator, add Poisson shot noise at the dose and then Gaussian detector noise set by snr, and write particles in SIMPLE's units. Reconstructed with reconstruct3D, such a stack returns its map's scale to within a few percent at low and middle resolution. Without wave_model the program writes exactly the same files as before.

    Files changed. src/utils/math/simple_math_ctf.f90 (the scale-link function electron_scale_link), src/main/strategies/parallelization/simple_rec3D_wave_strategy.f90 (uses it instead of its inline formula), src/main/volume/simple_wave_projector.f90 (lens B-factor per particle; plane_to_image, the loop that turns a predicted plane into an image, moved here from simulate_scattering's commander), src/main/simple_simulator.f90 (simimg_wave, the detector of wave mode), src/main/commanders/simple/simple_commanders_sim.f90 (the wave branch of simulate_particles; simulate_scattering calls plane_to_image), src/main/ui/simple/simple_ui_sim.f90 (the keys wave_model, ewald, total_dose and the help text), the new tester src/main/simple_simulator_tester.f90 with its registration as the "wave simulation" sub-suite of unit_reconstruction (simple_commanders_test_class.f90, simple_test_ui_class.f90), this plan and the run plan (file table).

    How it works. Wave mode is chosen by the presence of wave_model; it needs total_dose and ctf=yes, takes ewald's default and refusals from reconstruct3D_wave, and refuses ewald or total_dose without wave_model. Each state's map is masked as in the flat mode and divided by the scale link kappa = 2 g sigma a N (g = sqrt(total_dose) a, sigma the interaction constant at kv, a the pixel size, N the box); one wave operator per state predicts its particles in batches, in the output order of the flat mode (the particles of a state that fall in a batch are consecutive in that state's selection). Each predicted plane becomes the relative intensity I of the native box; simimg_wave draws the counts from a Poisson distribution of mean D a^2 I (D the dose), adds Gaussian noise of standard deviation sqrt(var(E) / snr) for snr below 5 (the rule of add_gauran, E the noiseless counts), and writes x = -(counts - D a^2) / sqrt(D a^2). The detector step is detect_intensity, shared with simulate_scattering. bfac and bfacerr are drawn per particle as in the flat mode and act on each particle's lens envelope. Multiple states, recthres, even and parallel parts work as in the flat mode.

    Decisions taken during the phase.

    • The flat mode's particles are at half the scale the wave model refers to. The flat chain projects the volume padded to twice the box and normalises over the padded box, without Kaiser-Bessel deapodisation: its particle is the sum of the map along the beam divided by 2N (measured: the image sum is 1/64 x 0.959 of the map sum at box 32). The wave model's linear part is the PCG (preconditioned conjugate gradients) reconstruction's forward model, the sum divided by N, which the scale link and reconstruct3D use. The flat mode is not changed (its files stay byte-identical, and its absolute scale never matters: its noise is set relative to the image and reconstruction normalises particles to unit noise). The weak-signal test therefore compares the wave particle's linear part with twice the flat particle of the deapodised map.
    • Second order is checked by how it scales. The even part's share of the odd part doubles when the map doubles; at phases below 0.01 radian the single-precision rounding of the unit intensity (about 1e-5) hides the second-order term, so the check runs at phases 0.015 and 0.030. A first bound, "the even part is at most twice the phase of the odd part", was wrong: the CTF suppresses the linear part at low frequency but not the second-order part (measured share: 0.13 to 0.14 times the phase).
    • The round trip needs a background without signal. reconstruct3D normalises each particle by its background outside the mask. With the myoglobin map in a box of 64 at 1.5 micrometres of defocus the CTF spreads signal into that background (standard deviation 1.108 instead of 1), and the reconstruction came back at 0.84 of the map's scale at every resolution. With the map in a box of 128 at 0.6 micrometres (background 1.003) it comes back at the map's scale (below). This is the same effect the policy notes for the fitted gain of real data.
    • The program-level bookkeeping is checked by hand rather than in a unit test: a commander ends the process, which a unit test may not.

    In plain words. One gradient pass of the single-scatter wave models (projection, first Born, Rytov) now takes 15 to 20% less time on this machine. The lens is set up about six times faster, the Fourier transforms of the real fields cost a quarter less, and nothing else changed measurably. Multislice is as fast as before: its time goes into the transforms of its slice-by-slice propagation, which none of the planned changes touch. On the ribosome case of the previous run the whole reconstruct3D_wave is about as fast as before (within the run-to-run spread), because the operator's passes are only a fifth of that program's time; the largest single cost there, the preparation of the particle images at every pass, is outside this plan's files and is left as a finding below.

    What was changed, and what each change bought (box 128, projection, unless said otherwise; thread-seconds per pass, base then run):

    • Lens set-up (wave_imaging%set_optics): the angle terms of the astigmatism are precomputed on the fine grid, the B-factor envelope is kept until the B-factor or pixel size changes, the Rytov propagator and its inverse until the wavelength or depth changes; per particle one phase (ctf%eval_phase with the angle terms expanded) and one sine and cosine per point remain. 1.41 to 0.22; box 256 3.50 to 0.42. The inverse propagator stays a separate factor: folded into the phase it no longer cancelled the propagator to single precision, and the test that first Born is Rytov's linearisation caught it.
    • Real fields (wave_field, wave_imaging): the intensity, its derivative and the real part taken in the adjoint use real-to-complex and complex-to-real transforms out of place, the native disk read through the Hermitian symmetry; the transform of the adjoint's real part adds each disk point and its conjugate mirror into the half spectrum, which also covers the grid without oversampling, where the disk's two Nyquist points share a bin (a first version missed that, and the operator's transpose test without oversampling caught it). Transforms 6.39 to 4.76; box 256 17.7 to 13.3.
    • Work arrays (wave_projector): the six planes per particle (and the cubes of multislice) allocated inside the parallel loops became per-thread buffers allocated once, sized by the team before each parallel region; the cubes are sized only when a pass needs them. Shift phase: separable vectors along h and k replace a sine and cosine per point. Together with the above, the forward chain 4.17 to 3.43 and the adjoint chain 3.56 to 3.03.
    • Scatter (pcg_lattice, which the plan allows when the profile points at the gather or scatter): its serial pass over the wrapping rim scanned every plane point, and now visits only each row's outer segments, the same points in the same order (bitwise identical results, for the PCG reconstruction as well); 0.20 to 0.18 s. Two larger designs were tried and removed: all planes of a batch in one parallel region, and an owner-computes scatter (each thread adds, in the original order, only the parts of the windows in the lattice blocks it owns, so no barriers and identical results at any team). Neither was faster: one thread needs 2.5 ms per plane at box 128 (350 000 scattered additions into a 135 MB accumulator), so the scatter is limited by memory, and both designs reach about 13 times on 24 threads, as the existing one does.
