    Probability-table scoring as matrix products¶

    Status, 2026-11-21: run table_gemm complete on the Dell (Phases 0-7), awaiting the maintainer's review: the dense table and the neighbourhood table's state representatives score with the block kernel; the members and the geom cones stay per pair. Report: prob_table_matrix_scoring_report.md beside this note. Planned and ruled 2026-11-20; rulings R1-R8 in Progress and the decisions. Base: 7a679f424. Supersedes: none. Depends on: the bundled OpenBLAS staying on the calling thread inside an OpenMP team (SIMPLE_BLAS_TEAM_SAFE, defined for every build since 4f7b5dcf0; validated: yes, probed 2026-11-19 at up to 232 concurrent in-team callers); the measure-only benchmark polar_matching_bench and its report (validated: yes, Mac 2026-10-21, Dell 2026-11-20, with the caveat of section 2 on its objective).     1. Why¶

    SIMPLE's probabilistic refinement scores every particle of a batch against a set of references, one pair at a time (gen_posterior_val: the two Euclidean sums over the shells, one inverse FFT over the rotations, the distances, the keyed in-plane draw). Two tables do it in a single-state solve3D: the dense table (refine=prob) in stages 3-5, every reference; the neighbourhood table (refine=prob_neigh) in stages 1-2 (prob_neigh_mode=shc) and 6-8 (state), and refine3D_local (geom). On beta-galactosidase (/data/bgal/tst, 4,967 particles, four parts of six threads, box 256, point group D2):

    • the dense table at 2,500 directions and shells to 65 (stage 5 of the run of 2026-11-15, 4_solve3D/BENCH/REFINE3D_BENCH_PART1.txt, iteration 67): a table fill of 22.6 s per iteration and part, 79 thread-seconds of it in the score loop at 25.5 µs per pair and thread, 54 in the shift refinement, beside 27 s of partial reconstruction;
    • the neighbourhood table in state mode at 5,000 directions and shells to 81 (stage 8 of the run of 2026-11-06, prev2/4_solve3D, iteration 17): 309 references scored per particle, 252 of them the subspace representatives every particle scores (126·5,000/2,500, NSPACE_SUB), the rest the members of its three peak cells; a fill of 7.1 s, 18 thread-seconds of score loop at 42.8 µs per pair and 19 of refinement.

    The benchmark's matrix_t layout computes the two sums of every angular frequency bin as one cgemm over a tile of references and a block of particles, transposes, and runs the inverse FFTs batched. Measured against production's per-pair routine on 2,500 directions and two states (5,000 references, pftsz 182, shells 2-62; the Mac: 2026-10-21; the Dell, this machine: 2026-11-20, ~/tmp/blas_review/pmb/polar_matching_bench.tsv), µs per pair and thread:

    machine, threads per_pair matrix_t (best B, R) speedup
    Mac M3 Pro, 11 31.1 8.3 (32, 64) 3.7
    Mac, 1 18.7 5.7 (32, 64) 3.3
    Dell, 24 33.4 9.6 (16, 32) 3.5
    Dell, 12 24.0 8.1 (16, 32) 3.0
    Dell, 1 15.7 6.7 (32, 64) 2.4

    Smaller blocks cost little at the team: on the Dell at 24 threads B 4, 8, 16 and 32 give 11.3, 10.3, 9.6 and 10.1 µs at R 32; R 64 and 128 are no better. The sums shrink 3-4 times; the distances and the draw (2.9 µs at one thread, the same code in every layout) stay. The dense table's references are shared by every particle of a batch, and so are the neighbourhood table's subspace representatives (about four fifths of its pairs in state mode): both are matrix products as they stand. The members of each particle's peak cells, and geom's cones, differ per particle: blocks of particles that share cells must be formed first, and whether that pays is measured before it is adopted (Phase 4).

    2. Contract¶

    • A row entry keeps its meaning: for every (particle, reference) the Euclidean distances over all in-plane rotations from the sums sum_k w_k [FT(CTF2) FT(REF2) - 2 FT(X.CTF) FT(REF)*] over the angular bins 1..pftsz+1 (the Nyquist bin included, as production has summed it since 9933db06c, 2026-11-03), the particle's norm and temperature, and the keyed draw u(scope, particle, INPL, reference) (gen_posterior_val). The block kernel changes only the order of the arithmetic of the sums. The distance conversion and the draw are the same compiled procedures for both kernels (decision 14). The draws are keyed on the particle and the reference since prob_align_batch_pipeline, so the block order changes no draw given equal distances.
    • The neighbourhood table scores the same references per particle as today, in the same order of record: the representatives first, in subspace order, then the members of the pooled cells; the peak selection and the pooling read the same distances in the same order. Only the arithmetic of the sums changes.
    • Precision: single, as the per-pair kernel already sums (sigma2_wk, euclid_shell_sum).
    • What changes: the last bits of the distances. Measured on both machines, matrix_t against the benchmark's replica of the per-pair sums: at most 4.0e-7 relative, the best rotation differs in 2 of 320,000 pairs. The benchmark's replica and matrix layouts still drop the angular Nyquist bin, which production sums since 2026-11-03: that is why on the Dell every benchmark vector differs from production's by up to 2.7e-3 absolute. It is a formula difference in the harness, not rounding. Phase 1 brings the harness to production's objective; the gate on the new kernel is its tester against production's own per-pair routine (decision 11), never the harness's replica. Each adoption is result-changing in the last bits, judged by profile, never by byte identity against the base.
    • Thread identity holds as today: the new fills at one thread and at any team give the same rows, batches and parts included (polar_matching_bench suite=fills).
    • Off-state: there is no switch. The per-pair routines stay for their other callers (the shift refinement, the shc/snhc neighbourhood search, the 2D tables, the cross-half diagnostics, the cc objective) and for any neighbourhood path Phase 4 does not adopt; their arithmetic does not change, and Phase 1 keeps every production output byte-identical.
    • No new parameter. No environment variable: the BLAS inside the team is single-threaded by the build's contract.

    3. Decisions¶

    Ruled 2026-11-20 by Hans: decisions 1 (re-ruled the same day on corrected facts: the first menu said only the dense table had a cost record), 3, 4, 6, 10, 11, 12, 13 by explicit choice, 16 and 17 by the re-ruling of decision 1 ("dense, then neighbourhood, adopted only past a bar fixed before the measurement"); the others follow from them or are fixed by measurement. Never renumbered.

    1. Scope: first the dense 3D table's all-reference score loop in fill_tab_range, in its three paths (process_particle_with_shift, process_particle_with_map, process_particle_without_shift); then the neighbourhood table as decisions 16-20 say. objfun=euclid (both tables require it). The 2D tables and the shc/snhc neighbourhood search stay on the per-pair routines.
    2. Layout: the benchmark's matrix_t: per block of B particles of one half and tile of R references, one cgemm('C','N') per angular bin 1..pftsz+1, depth 2·nk (both sums fused), the products transposed so that each pair's inverse FFT reads contiguous memory, then R·B transforms in one batched c2r. A tile of consecutive references is read in place from the bin-major memo; a tile given as a list of references (the representatives, a cell's members) is gathered per bin into the thread's tile buffer. Revised in Phase 1 (rulings R2): the product has a fixed shape, 16 particles by 32 references, whatever B and the tile (an absent particle is a zero row, a partial tile's missing references are zero columns), with the particle terms as the conjugated operand and each reference in the column of its place in the list (tiles aligned to the list's start). The products are the conjugated sums; the transpose conjugates them back, exactly. The inverse FFTs run as one batched c2r of the 32 transforms of a particle's tile, once per particle of the block. A full tile of consecutive references is read in place; any other tile is gathered bin by bin. Reason: the bundled OpenBLAS gives an element of cgemm other last bits in another column or in a call of another shape (probed on the Dell: up to 72 % of the elements), but not in another row of a full call; decision 3's B depends on the team and the batch, so in the benchmark's layout a pair's last bits would depend on its block-mates and the fills could not be identical across threads, batches and parts (section 2).
    3. Tiles: R = 32. B = min(16, max(4, nb_half / (2·nthr))), nb_half the batch's particles of the half, so that a batch gives the team at least two blocks per thread. Origin: the Dell sweep of section 1 (B 8 keeps 3.2 of the 3.5 at the team; B 4 is the floor, 3.0); frozen.
    4. The unit of work is a block. Each fill keeps one parallel region per batch, dynamic over blocks; the batch's particles are ordered by half and cut into blocks that never mix halves (as the benchmark's make_blocks). A task processes its block whole: each particle's seed, the block's scores into the particles' rows or candidate buffers, then each particle's remaining steps in the order its path uses today. Per-thread state stays per thread; the dense fill's comment "a particle is processed whole" becomes "a block is processed whole". The dense fill's per-thread copies of a row (dists_refs, inpl_refs) are replaced by reads of rows_batch(:,i).
    5. The seed bank (the particle shifted once by its seed, memoize_shifted_ptcl) holds the block's particles per thread, seed_bank(:,:,B,nthr); a particle without a seed shift uses its memo (ft_ptcl_ctf, ft_ctf2). The block kernel takes, per particle, which of the two it reads.
    6. The bin-major memo (2·nk, nrefs, pftsz+1, parity), conj(FT(REF2)) and FT(REF) per bin, is derived from the finished ft_ref/ft_ref2 by one layout transform (parallel over references, outside any region, as every memo), only when a table that uses the kernel asks for it: its new_pipeline calls it after the matcher's memoize_refs. It has no logic of its own: the mirror rule of memoize_refs is already in its source. It is released with the other reference memos and at kill. Memory: one more term of 2·nk·(pftsz+1)·nrefs·2·8 bytes (about 355 KB per reference at the benchmark's sizes, 0.9 GB at 2,500 directions and one state), counted by matcher_memory_terms only when such a table runs.
    7. Per-thread work, in the calculator's existing per-thread pattern: the particle terms (2·nk, B, pftsz+1), the tile (2·nk, R, pftsz+1) for gathered tiles, the products (R, B, pftsz+1), the transposed products (pftsz+1, R·B) and the FFT output (nrots, R·B), sized at pftc%new by the team and the largest B and R; a partial block or tile runs the full-size plan on zero-filled rows. One batched c2r plan of R·B transforms per calculator, made and destroyed with its other plans. Revised in Phase 1 (rulings R2, with decision 2's revision): the tile buffer holds one bin, (2·nk, R), gathered just before its product; the products are (16, 32, pftsz+1); the plan has R transforms and runs once per particle of the block, so an absent particle costs no transform. The work and the plan are made in allocate_memo_workspace (called by new) with the calculator's other plans, under the same planning flags and wisdom, and released in kill_memo_workspace.

    7. Common rules and stop conditions¶

    No new parameter (ruled). No floor or tolerance relaxed; a failed tolerance is a diagnosis. Fix as you go, with a file-table row before the edit. Every parallel region added or changed follows the rules of the Fortran guide (per-thread arrays sized by the team at the region, no strings inside, a per-task status raised after) and of the pftc guide (new scratch in the calculator's per-thread buffers, never a per-call allocation in the score loop). One thread for every identity comparison. Builds for before/after timings from one snapshot of the tree, interleaved, two repeats each way, with the load recorded. Rulings at launch, applied first in Phase 0 (2026-11-21): the run never uses the maintainer's checkout ~/src/SIMPLE or its build and puts the machine's load beside every timing; no speed verdict is taken on a loaded machine without the load beside it (R1). /data/bgal, the earlier runs' directories and ~/tmp/blas_review are read only; a bgal project is copied into the run's scratch before a run writes it (R3). The base is the copy's HEAD, 7a679f424 as pinned above (R4).

    Stops: decision 12's premise check; the kernel tolerance of decision 11 not met after diagnosis; a bgal map outside the tolerance of section 6 (rulings R5) after an adoption; Phase 1 not byte-identical after diagnosis (the shared tail must not change arithmetic); a file outside the table; the block order or the ordering of decision 18 found to change a draw or a particle's candidates (a keyed draw depends on no order: if one does, stop). A neighbourhood path failing its bar is a recorded negative result, not a stop. Gates no ruling may waive: the fast gate after every edit, with the tester of decision 11 in it; suite=fills identity; Phase 1's 6vxx identity; the 6vxx profiles of Phases 3 and 6.
