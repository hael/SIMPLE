    In-plane quadrature insertion in 3D¶

    Status, 2026-11-14: planned, not started; both design reviews incorporated, including the per-view window-prior centre correction (section 10). No design findings remain open; implementation validation is pending. Base: 06d3a4de9 (first written at b2a9d544f; the three commits between them touch no file of the file table except an unrelated paragraph of the solve2D policy). Supersedes: none. Depends on: the 2D in-plane quadrature (inpl_quadrature, prob_insert_inpl, INPL_POSTERIOR_BUDGET; d6cf4ae25 to b2a9d544f; validated: yes, Hans's beta-galactosidase and TRPV1 class averages, 2026-11-13); the 3D weighted insertion (topk, top_posterior_extras, prob_insert_cone, prob_insert_poses; validated: yes, production, default topk=3 since 2026-11-01); the continuous in-plane solve of the probabilistic route (refine_assignment_continuously, refine_extra_pose_continuously; validated: yes, production). Companion: the 2D plan. Its rulings on the row, the temperature, the phase and the continuous angle are applied here to every pose a particle is inserted at; they are restated below so that this note reads on its own.

    Terms¶

    • Row (table row). A particle's losses against every reference of the probability table (eul_prob_tab, eul_prob_tab_neigh), one in-plane index and one shift per reference.
    • Committed pose. The pose the assignment draws from the particle's row at prob_beta times the physical temperature and the search commits to the project after its continuous solve. It is the hypothesis the next iteration starts from.
    • Extras (weighted insertion, topk). Up to topk - 1 other refined entries of the committed state's block, within the insertion cone of the committed pose, of highest posterior at the physical temperature (top_posterior_extras). The particle enters the iteration's reconstruction at the committed pose and at its extras, with weights summing to 1. The final and bootstrap reconstructions use the committed pose alone.
    • Insertion cone. The poses whose rotation from the committed pose (smallest over the point group) times the mask radius, plus the shift difference, move the rim by at most the finer of the map's FSC=0.5 resolution and the matching low-pass limit (insert_cone_within).
    • In-plane row. The Euclidean losses L_r, r = 1..nrots, of one reference against the particle at one shift over every grid rotation (gen_euclid_dists, gen_euclid_dists_bank).
    • Physical temperature T. posterior_temperature(wsqsum, pftsz) = pi wsqsum / (2 pftsz): the posterior over a row is p_r proportional to exp(-T (L_r - L_min)). The table's draws run at prob_beta times T (ptcl_posterior_temp). The extras' weights and the 2D quadrature use T itself.
    • Partition sum Z. Z = sum_r exp(-T (L_r - L_min)) over the valid rotations of an in-plane row. Z = 1 for a row with one plausible angle, about n for a row with n equally plausible angles.
    • View weight m_k, m_c. The share of a particle that inserted pose k (its direction, all angles together) receives under the key: the in-plane marginal of decision 4, normalised over the particle's poses. m_c is the committed pose's.
    • Quadrature. The K rotations holding the points u_j = (j - 1 + v) / K, j = 1..K, of the row's cumulative posterior, each at weight 1/K, coincident points merged (inpl_quadrature). v is a keyed per-particle phase. A row whose mass sits in one rotation collapses to one entry.
    • Solved pose. A pose after its continuous joint solve of shift and fractional in-plane angle.
    • n_eff. exp(entropy) of a distribution: the effective number of entries it spreads over.
    • m_max. ceiling(pi mskdiam / lp_band), the highest angular harmonic the band resolves at the rim; the 2D readout prints it beside K.

    1. Why¶

    The 2D quadrature was built against radial artifacts in top-view class averages of small membrane proteins. With K = 64, Hans called the beta-galactosidase averages (5,513 particles, 90 classes) almost perfect and the TRPV1 averages perfect (2026-11-13), and the next day made it a fixed part of every probabilistic 2D pass (b2a9d544f). The run's readout showed 1.1 to 1.8 entries per particle, 40 to 90 % of rows collapsed and mean n_eff 1.0 to 1.4 (2D report, section 8). The loop it acts on exists in 3D. A particle whose in-plane angle the data do not decide enters its half-map at an angle its own noise chose, and the half then favours that angle for that particle. This self-reinforcement was measured on PfCRT with prob_post_diag=yes (pose-marginalization note, section 12, 2026-10-22, at an older base). The own half's advantage at the inserted pose averaged +0.81 nats in stage 3, +2.30 in stage 4 and +4.71 in stage 5, against a control of 0.00 ± 0.03. It was largest on the sparsely populated views: in stage 5, 5.5 nats in the lowest population quartile and 3.3 in the highest. Those are the views a small membrane protein is short of. The 3D weighted insertion does not spread the angle. Each direction of a row carries one in-plane angle, the extras are other directions, and the cone tests the whole pose, angle included. This plan carries the 2D quadrature to every pose a 3D particle is inserted at. Whether it improves 3D maps is Hans's test after delivery (decision 2). The history of the weighted insertion before the cone (section 2, last bullet) is why the feature arrives behind a key that is off by default.

    2. What the code check changed¶

    Checked at b2a9d544f with rg, the review's points re-checked at 06d3a4de9. These premises are fixed by the code.

    • A refined entry's in-plane index is its row's best angle, not a draw. The fill scores every reference at a hot-drawn rotation (score_ref, gen_posterior_val at ptcl_posterior_temp). It then refines the shift of the projs_ns best references with alternating search objects (new_alternating, opt_angle = .true.), and grad_shsrch_minimize re-selects the in-plane argmax over every rotation at each shift. A refined entry therefore holds the minimum of its in-plane row at its refined shift. Only unrefined entries hold the hot draw. The extras are refined entries (select_extras only considers them), so an extra's table weight already ranks its direction by its in-plane minimum. This corrects the design discussion of 2026-11-14, which assumed the extras' angles were random draws.
    • The cone includes the angle. insert_cone_within composes the in-plane and direction matrices of both poses and bounds the geodesic angle, smallest over the point group.
    • Extras take the continuous route before the committed pose. strategy3D_prob%srch calls resolve_extra_poses (each extra through refine_extra_pose_continuously from its own cell and shift, then candidate_pose and resolve_inplane_e3) and then refine_assignment_continuously for the committed pose. The extras keep the table's weights after their solve; the policy records Hans's reason (2026-11-01): the solve is local and bounded, so the losses move little and alike.
    • The extras' weights carry a shift prior (found by the review). Under the polar shift map (shift_sigma > 0, l_centring), a refined entry's loss for the insertion is insert_pose_loss: the data term plus prior_w |du|^2 / T, prior_w = map_prior_weight(shift_sigma, smpd) = 1 / (2 (shift_sigma/smpd)^2), du the increment from the stored shift (dense table, process_particle_with_map; neighbourhood table, select_extras). Under the window search with shift_prior=yes (the default; off under the map), the table's shift refinement minimises the data term times exp(-w |u - u0|^2), w = 1 / (2 sigma2_prior T) with sigma2_prior the iteration's sigma2_prior_model (shift_prior_sigma2) and u0 the actual start of that reference's refinement, converted back to the stored frame by set_prior_centre. Both tables rotate the particle's seed into the reference's starting-angle frame, then clip each component to [-trs, trs] before passing it as xy_in to minimize. Thus the centre is R(irot_start)^T clip(R(irot_start) seed, -trs, trs), which can differ by view; it is zero without the single-state seed. The refined loss the extras are weighed by holds this penalty. An in-plane row at a fixed shift has the prior constant across its rotations: it cancels within the row and in Z, but not between poses at different shifts.
    • Shift frames. The table's refinement returns the shift in the stored frame (minimize with sh_rot: matmul(x, R), the shift_ref2ptcl conversion). The strategy hands the joint solve R(irot) times that increment (matmul(sh, transpose(rotmat))), the optimizer's frame at the cell. minimize_joint returns matmul(x, R) again (sh_rot=.true.). The fill memoizes the particle at the stored-frame increment (memoize_shifted_ptcl after shift_ref2ptcl). The row of a solved pose is therefore memoize_shifted_ptcl(iptcl, sh) with sh the solve's returned increment. The 2D plan proved the bank row's frame (check_bank_particle_frame). The 3D strategy's conversion is not covered by that check, so Phase 3 asserts it.
    • Insertion. calc_3Drec, through update_state_half_rec, inserts the committed plane at wbase * wc, then each extra (insert_extra_poses) from the committed plane re-shifted by the shift difference (fplane_shift, one phase ramp per extra), skipping slots of weight 0. insert_plane_oversamp writes every point-group copy of each insertion.
    • PCG. pcg_extra_entries lays every extra of positive weight out as one more operator entry after the committed ones. accumulate_pcg_extras copies each extra's observation into a scratch plane, and accumulate_pcg_state_half sizes that scratch by the most extras any 500-particle batch holds (MAXIMGBATCHSZ = 500). At the base that is at most 500 * (topk - 1) planes. With K entries per pose it would be unbounded in practice. Capping the scratch is not enough: pcg_extra_entries builds one oris record per entry for the whole half, and reconstructor_pcg%prep_particles allocates a rotation matrix, a shift, CTF parameters and two weights per entry, all before the first batch accumulates. Confirmed before development (2026-11-14, at 06d3a4de9): the production path reads none of that metadata after accumulation. In refine3D and solve3D, shared memory and distributed alike, the matcher's write_partial_recs runs execute_rec3D_pcg_worker. Each (state, half) gets its own reconstructor_pcg in accumulate_worker_state_half: prep_particles, begin_accum, accumulate_batch per batch, then write_raw_accum and kill. write_raw_accum writes only the B and D accumulators, with the particle count and mass passed by the caller. The solve is the master's (assemble_refine3D_pcg → execute_rec3D_pcg_distributed_master, in both modes). It builds its own object from the raw files (begin_reduction, no prep_particles), finalises with end_accum and solves with set_op_mode(PCG_OP_KERNEL). The only routines that read the per-entry metadata after accumulation are apply_normal_matrixfree and apply_adjoint_all. Both stop on an object without prep_particles, and production reaches neither: they serve the matrix-free reference operator and the one-shot solve(y_planes), which only the PCG testers call (FLEX's solve is another type, flex_pcg_t). So the metadata can be prepared per chunk of entries. Repeated prep_particles calls leave the open accumulators untouched. accumulate_rhs_density adds a batch entry by entry, and each lattice cell is written by one thread at a time in entry order, so accumulating the same entries in chunks gives B and D byte for byte equal to one call.
    • Layout. prob_insert_poses%setup_range allocates nptcls * (kmax - 1) slots at the first batch. It takes each batch's extra counts and weights from the table. The matcher calls it only when topk > 1 (pipeline_batch), and the strategy sees the structure only when it has particles.
    • Readout. prob_readout already holds the 2D fields inpl_kmax, inpl_mmax, inpl_nptcls, inpl_npose, inpl_nsingle and inpl_neff_sum. They are written, read and summed for every route. Only the 2D branch of report_prob_readout prints them (inpl_report_counts). The 3D tables close their assignment after the last batch's search (close_assignment in refine3D_exec), so the search's counts can reach the record.
    • Gate inputs. l_prob_align_mode is true for refine=prob|prob_neigh. The neighbourhood table turns the extras off for prob_neigh_mode=shc|snhc (open_assignment of eul_prob_tab_neigh). solve3D copies its command line into every refine3D stage (prep_class_command_lines), so a solve3D key reaches each stage. The multi-state final assignment sets topk=1, maxits=1 and volrec=no (run_multistate_final_assignment). Single-state solve3D stages 1-2 are prob_neigh shc, stages 3-5 prob and stages 6-8 prob_neigh state (PROB_REFINE_STAGE = 3, PROB_NEIGH_REFINE_STAGE = 6). Multi-state runs shc before stage 3.
    • Reusable pieces. inpl_quadrature returns the entries, weights and n_eff but not Z or L_min. RND_FAM_INPLQ is the quadrature's draw family, and rnd_draw_key takes an optional reference. 2D allocates its per-thread bank in prep_strategy2D_inpl_bank; s3D has no bank. The search's per-thread work counts already flow through strategy3D_cont%add_work(cont_work(ithr)) in choose_and_run_strategy.
    • Exits and memory. The weighted insertion's margin on the resolution exits is l_margin = l_prob_align_mode .and. topk > 1 (simple_convergence). The matcher's memory estimate counts 64 * max(1, topk) bytes per particle for the insertion (simple_matcher_memory).
    • History that bounds the design (refine3D policy, "The weighted insertion"). Before the cone, extras over the whole row at the physical temperature made the map the posterior expectation from a stage's first iteration: every stage-3 map was bad on PorF and PfCRT, while the late stages gained. The quadrature is also a physical-temperature expectation over a whole row, but over one coordinate and conditional on the drawn direction. In 2D that was enough. In 3D it is untested, and stage 3 is the first thing Hans's runs read.

    3. Contract¶

    • Off state. With inpl_posterior=no, the default, the run is the base's byte for byte at one thread. No in-plane row is evaluated, no bank or reservation is allocated, the layout is setup_range's at the base, gridding and PCG run the base's insertion code, l_margin is as at the base, and the readout's new fields are zero. This is the gate no ruling may waive.
    • The committed pose is the hypothesis. For identical incoming references, the table, its draws and keyed streams, the extras' selection, every continuous solve, the project's pose, corr, shift, sigma2 residual and convergence statistics are unchanged at either key value. Under the key, the references change from the second iteration on, so "unchanged" holds for one pass, not for a run.
    • Concentrated limit. A particle at topk = 1 whose committed row collapses enters at its solved pose at weight 1, as with the key off. With extras, a particle whose rows all collapse enters at the base's poses with the marginal weights of decision 4. It differs from the key-off run in its weights only.
    • Mass and determinism. The weights of a particle sum to 1, so the reconstruction's mass and the trailing chain's population accounting are unchanged. The phase is keyed on the particle and the reference, and each particle writes its own reserved block. The entries therefore do not depend on the thread count, the batch or the part.
    • Modes equivalent. Shared memory and distributed parts apply the same rule to the same particles. Nothing in it depends on the execution mode.
    • Final maps from committed poses. The final and bootstrap reconstructions, reconstruct3D from the project and the multi-state final assignment use committed poses, as with topk > 1 today.

    1. (2026-11-14, Hans) The switch. A temporary key inpl_posterior=yes|no, default no, on refine3D and solve3D. solve3D forwards it to every stage through its copied command line (section 2). After Hans's read it becomes the shared constant or is removed, as topk_inpl went in 2D (b2a9d544f).
    2. (2026-11-14, Hans) Testing. The plan carries the off-state byte identity, unit assertions with planted violations, and smoke runs. The scale test is Hans's, after delivery. The report names the runs and the readout to read. The plan closes on delivery.
    3. (2026-11-14, Hans) Where it runs. Every pass with a posterior table except the hill-climb neighbourhoods: refine=prob, and refine=prob_neigh with prob_neigh_mode=state|geom. In solve3D that is single-state stages 3 to 8 and the multi-state stages from 3. It does not run in prob_neigh_mode=shc|snhc (stages 1-2), in refine=shc, in the multi-state final assignment (which reconstructs nothing; the commander sets inpl_posterior=no there beside topk=1), or in the final and bootstrap reconstructions. It is independent of topk and of the cone: it runs at topk=1 and before the first FSC exists. Derived once as l_inpl_posterior in the parameter phases, as l_shift_prior is.
    4. (2026-11-14, Hans; the shift-prior term added after the review, section 10) View weights: the in-plane marginal. Under the key, the weight of each inserted pose k (the committed pose and its extras) is recomputed from the rows at the solved shifts, keeping the shift prior the insertion applies today (section 2, the shift-prior bullet):

    text log m_k = log Z_k - T L_min,k - P_k (then minus the largest log m_k, exponentiated, normalised) P_k = prior_w * |du_k - c_k|^2

    Under the polar shift map, prior_w = map_prior_weight(shift_sigma, smpd), du_k is the pose's solved increment from the stored shift and c_k = 0, which is T times insert_pose_loss's prior term. Under the window search with shift_prior=yes, prior_w = 1 / (2 sigma2_prior_model) and c_k is that pose's actual stored-frame prior centre, after rotating and clipping the seed as described in section 2 (zero without the single-state seed). The table retains that centre with each refined entry and exposes it, with the width, to the strategy for the committed pose and extras. Capture it at the refinement's start: its final in-plane index may differ, so neither the unchanged particle seed nor clipping at the final angle reproduces it. The subsequent continuous solve leaves this retained centre unchanged for the marginal weight; the search, selection and solves themselves are unchanged. Otherwise P_k = 0. The conditions are the existing insertion path's, and one helper computes P_k for both routes. The product with the quadrature, m_k / K per point, is then the joint posterior over view and angle at the solved shifts under the same prior, restricted to the kept poses. With the prior retained, the table's weight differs from m_k by the factor Z_k and the solve's small moves only (section 2, first bullet), so it favours poses whose in-plane row is sharp. The selection of the extras stays the table's (top_posterior_extras); only the kept set is reweighted. After reweighting, an extra under PROB_INSERT_WMIN = 0.01 is dropped and the rest renormalised; the committed pose is always kept. With the key on, this replaces the 2026-11-01 rule that "the weights stay the table's". With the key off, that rule stands. What it does to the amount of averaging: it moves weight toward poses whose angle is uncertain, and every inserted entry carries its own share of the joint posterior. Usually that lowers the largest single entry, but not always. Example: a pose with two equally plausible angles fits best, and a one-angle pose has half its likelihood (a loss higher by ln 2 / T). Marginal gives 0.4, 0.4, 0.2 (n_eff 2.87); table weights give 1/3 each (n_eff 3.0). With equal likelihoods the order reverses: marginal 1/3 each, table 1/4, 1/4, 1/2 (n_eff 2.83). The readout compares the committed view's marginal share m_c with the table's committed weight (decision 10). 5. (proposed; the 2D plan's rulings on the row, the temperature, the selection, the phase and the continuous angle, per pose) The product rule. For each inserted pose, after its continuous solve: - the whole in-plane row at the pose's solved shift, at the physical temperature temperature_or_argmin(posterior_temperature(wsqsum_ptcl_now(iptcl), pftsz)); - the K = INPL_POSTERIOR_BUDGET = 64 point quadrature, with the phase v = rnd_keyed(rnd_draw_key(prob_rnd_scope(which_iter, .false.), iptcl, RND_FAM_INPLQ, reference=iref)), so that two poses of one particle take independent phases; - the point or points in the solved bin placed at the solved continuous angle (resolve_inplane_e3) and merged into one entry; - a row collapsed to one entry inserts the pose at its solved angle at its weight, whichever bin that is; - the committed pose not inserted beside its own quadrature (it is one sample of the same posterior), so the committed angle's weight is m_c times the solved bin's point count over K, which can be 0; - every entry of pose k at weight m_k times its point count over K, inserted at the pose's direction, shift and the entry's angle. 6. (proposed) Where the rows are evaluated. In strategy3D_prob%srch, on the particle's thread: - each extra's row in resolve_extra_poses after refine_extra_pose_continuously; - the committed row after refine_assignment_continuously; - the weights (decision 4) and the fill of the particle's reserved block (decision 8) after all rows. A row is memoize_shifted_ptcl(iptcl, sh, s3D%inpl_bank(:,:,ithr)) then gen_euclid_dists_bank(iref, iptcl, bank, dists), with sh the stored-frame increment the solve returns (section 2). It falls back to gen_euclid_dists(iref, iptcl, sh, dists) when |sh|^2 <= SHERRSQ, as in 2D. Without continuous refinement (no shifts, or an invalid solve), it is taken at the table's increment. If any pose of a particle has no valid row, the whole particle enters as with the key off (table weights, one angle per pose), so marginal and table weights are never mixed within a particle. A particle needs only its per-pose results (entries, L_min, ln Z, n_eff), not the rows themselves. inpl_quadrature gains optional lnz and dmin outputs; the 2D callers do not pass them. The cone is unchanged. 7. Withdrawn 2026-11-14 after the review (section 10): no point-group merge, because the search already removes the symmetric duplicates. The directions are a spiral over the asymmetric unit (build_refspiral), so a symmetry mate of a pose never appears as another direction. Each direction's in-plane row covers the full 360 degrees, though. The only poses that row duplicates are those of a direction lying exactly on a symmetry axis: the top view along a Cn axis, where in-plane angles 360/n apart are the same pose, and the views along the 2-fold axes of Dn. Every other direction's row peaks are different poses. Merging at one in-plane step (the first version) would also have merged distinct poses near an axis, which the review showed the rim resolves (a C4 view 0.4 degrees off the axis, dang = 1 degree: 0.57 degrees, about one pixel at a 100-pixel rim). Merging only the exact on-axis duplicates would change no map, because every insertion writes all point-group copies anyway, and would save only extra insertions for those few directions. A pose has at most K entries either way, so the worst case is the same. Every entry is inserted as it is, and the REC3D bench's poses inserted shows the cost. 8. (proposed; revised after the review) Layout: a worst-case reservation. Under the key, prob_insert_poses reserves, at the first batch and for the whole part, one block per pose of every particle: the committed pose and up to topk - 1 extras, so max(1, topk) blocks per particle. A block holds its entry count, the solved bin, and up to K packed 32-bit entries (the rotation index in the low 16 bits, the point count in the high bits; nrots < 2^16 and the count is at most K). That is max(1, topk) * (8 + 4 K) bytes per particle, 792 bytes at topk = 3 and K = 64, 79.2 MB per 100,000 particles of a part, on top of today's extras slots. The reservation is never exceeded: a quadrature gives at most K entries. It does not grow, copy or compact. The pose weights live where they live today: an extra's w holds its m_k, and the committed pose's wc holds m_c, the committed view's weight (decision 10). An entry's weight is its pose's weight times its count over K. The solved bin's entry is inserted at the pose's continuous angle (the project's e3 for the committed pose, the extra's euler(3)), every other entry at its grid angle 360 - rot(irot). A block with one entry is inserted at the pose's continuous angle whatever its bin (decision 5). A block with count 0 (a particle that fell back, decision 6) leaves the particle to the key-off insertion. The table's extra count stays apart from the blocks. With the key off nothing is reserved and setup_range is the base's. 9. (proposed; revised after the review) Insertion paths and bounds. With the key off, gridding and PCG run the base's code (insert_extra_poses, pcg_extra_entries, accumulate_pcg_extras) unchanged. Under the key: - gridding: one new loop per particle, over its poses, then over each pose's entries. The committed plane is re-shifted once per pose by the pose's shift minus the committed one (none for the committed pose), and each entry is one insert_plane_oversamp at its angle and weight; - PCG: the entries (the committed pose's and the extras') are expanded in chunks of at most MAXIMGBATCHSZ entries. Each chunk gets its own oris records and weights, its prep_particles, its observation copies and one accumulate_batch from index 1. The per-entry metadata, the records and the plane scratch are bounded by one chunk whatever the part's size, topk or K, which is less than the base holds for a half of more than MAXIMGBATCHSZ particles. The B and D accumulators span the half as today and come out byte for byte as one call over the same entries would make them (section 2). The contributor count and mass published with them stay the particles'. Section 2 records the check made before development: nothing on the production path reads per-entry metadata after accumulation. The REC3D bench file gains the poses inserted next to the particle images inserted. 10. (proposed; revised after the review) Readout. The 3D route fills the existing inpl_* fields (inpl_kmax = K, inpl_mmax, inpl_nptcls particles with a row, inpl_npose entries inserted, inpl_nsingle rows collapsed, inpl_neff_sum over rows) and two new ones: - inpl_nrows, the rows evaluated; - inpl_committed_view_weight_sum: the weight the committed view (its direction, all angles together) receives after the in-plane-marginal reweighting of decision 4, m_c, summed over the table's particles, with 1 for a particle without extras. It is the counterpart of ins_wc_sum, the table's committed weight that PROB_TAB_INSERT prints, so the two lines show how far the reweighting moves the particle's mass between its committed direction and its extras. It is a view weight, not the committed angle's inserted weight (m_c times the solved bin's count over K, which at topk = 1 with a flat row is 1/64 or 0 while m_c is 1). The angular spreading shows in the collapsed share and n_eff, so the committed angle's weight is not reported. The new fields are written, read and summed with the others. PROB_TAB_INPL (budget K, m_max M) follows PROB_TAB_INSERT in the 3D branch. It prints rows and entries per particle, the collapsed share of rows, the mean n_eff, and the committed view weight beside PROB_TAB_INSERT's table value. The 2D line is unchanged. The counts are accumulated per thread (add_inpl_counts, the add_work pattern) and set on the table before close_assignment (set_inpl_counts, on eul_prob_tab and copied into both tables' records). 11. (proposed) Exits. l_margin = l_prob_align_mode .and. (topk > 1 .or. l_inpl_posterior). The quadrature averages the draw's noise out of each half, as the extras do, so the halves agree a shell early for the same reason. 12. (proposed; revised after the review) Memory estimate. Under the key, the matcher's estimate adds the reservation of decision 8 exactly, max(1, topk) * (8 + 4 K) bytes per particle of the part. It is a worst case, not an expectation, so the first iteration cannot exceed it. The alignment context's share of the budget then grows by that amount, and the pass buffer takes the remainder, as it does today. Over budget, the existing warning is printed. The PCG chunk of decision 9 is bounded by MAXIMGBATCHSZ entries, the order of one observation batch the reconstruction already holds, so it adds no estimate term. 13. (proposed) Objective. The key requires objfun=euclid, as the probability tables already do (gen_posterior_val). The parameter phases refuse inpl_posterior=yes with another objective. 14. (2026-11-14, Hans) PCG is supported and exercised. The PCG reconstruction takes the quadrature's entries as it takes the extras (decision 9), and the plan runs it. solve3D reconstructs by gridding unless rec_backend=pcg is given, and then from PCG_START_STAGE = 6 (set_refine3D_backend_policy), so the gridding smoke runs never reach pcg_extra_entries and accumulate_pcg_extras. The smoke therefore includes a solve3D run with rec_backend=pcg, in shared memory and with nparts=2, key on. The same command with the key off is compared byte for byte against the base at one thread, which proves that the PCG path is unchanged in the off state. 15. (2026-11-14, Hans) Multi-state is implemented here and tested separately afterwards. The enabled path covers every state count. The rows take the multi-state reference index ((istate - 1) * nspace + iproj), the extras stay in the committed state's block, and the marginal weights run over the committed state's poses only, so the particle never enters another state's map. The multi-state stages from 3 run it (decision 3). This plan's smoke runs are single-state. Multi-state is covered here by the unit assertions and the off-state identity only; its scale test is a separate step after delivery.

    Defaults taken without a question: the key name inpl_posterior; l_inpl_posterior; the field names inpl_nrows, inpl_committed_view_weight_sum; add_inpl_counts, set_inpl_counts, prep_strategy3D_inpl_bank; reuse of RND_FAM_INPLQ (its comment widened to both routes); K shared with 2D.

    8. Common rules and stop conditions¶

    • No parameter but inpl_posterior (decision 1). It is registered in simple_parameters.f90, parsed, checked and derived in the phases, and present in the UI of refine3D and solve3D. simple_args.f90 and the UI default values are generated.
    • No floor, tolerance or assertion relaxed unless this plan or a ruling says so. Fix as you go, with a file-table row before the edit. Every file changed outside the table is listed in the report. Dead code found on the way is deleted.
    • Thread policy: per-thread work is sized by the team in use (omp_get_max_threads() read before allocation). There is no allocation per particle in the search loop. Byte identity is judged at one thread; the thread gate of section 7 covers the parallel region.
    • Compile-time policy: the new components are allocatable; imports are lean use ..., only:; no new CMake targets or per-file options.
    • Where builds and runs happen (the agent kit on the Mac or arun on the Dell) is Hans's decision before launch, after further review (2026-11-14); the phases and gates are the same either way. Never into the checkout's build/. Long runs go in the background. Nothing is committed.
    • Sync: no other run is known to edit these files on 2026-11-14. If master moves past 06d3a4de9 in a file of the table before launch, re-check section 2 against the new commit and record the differences in Progress.
    • Stops. The off-state identity fails and cannot be restored. The frame assertion fails (the row disagrees with the kernel at the solved pose): stop, do not adjust the tolerance. A file outside the table needs a change. A fixed decision contradicts the code. Decision 4 turns out to need the table's selection changed, or the table cannot retain and expose each refined pose's actual prior centre and the prior width to the strategy. Section 2's PCG premise (nothing on the production path reads per-entry metadata after accumulation) no longer holds at the launch commit.
    • Gates no ruling may waive. The off-state identity; the fast gate and the unit suites; the Debug smoke at key on.

    10. Review (2026-11-14) and what it changed¶

    Two design reviews checked this proposal against the code at 06d3a4de9. The first made four findings; the second confirmed the symmetry, memory and readout revisions and identified a remaining detail in the window-prior centre. All findings are incorporated below and in the contract, phases and acceptance checks. This section is the review record; the separate review document has been removed. No build or runtime validation was performed for either review; the implementation gates remain pending.

    1. The marginal weights dropped the shift prior. The extras are weighed today with a prior on the shift: insert_pose_loss under the polar shift map, and the prior folded into the refined loss under the window search with shift_prior=yes (section 2). An in-plane row at one shift has the prior constant across rotations, so it cancels in Z but not between poses at different shifts. Without it, equal rows at increments 0 and two prior sigmas would weigh 0.5 each instead of 0.881 and 0.119. Changed: decision 4's formula carries P_k under the existing path's conditions, centres and widths, and section 7 asserts the equal-row, unequal-shift case under both routes. The second review found that the window prior is centred on the rotated, component-clipped seed converted back to the stored frame, not always the original particle seed. A seed (0.9*trs, 0.9*trs) is unchanged at zero degrees but becomes approximately (0.707*trs, 0.707*trs) at a 45-degree starting angle after clipping. Changed: decision 4 retains c_k per refined pose at its refinement's start, and section 7 checks the clipped case and a changed final angle. Evidence: refine_best_refs in simple_eul_prob_tab.f90 (lines 887–893 at the base), refine_best_neighbors in simple_eul_prob_tab_neigh.f90 (1514–1519), and set_prior_centre and its call in minimize in simple_pftc_shsrch_grad.f90 (283–299 and 572–588). The map prior and zero-seed case are unaffected; search and selection are unchanged.
    2. A one-step tolerance merges poses that are not equivalent. Below one in-plane step does not mean equivalent under the point group: a near-axis view keeps a separation the rim resolves. Changed: decision 7 is withdrawn. There is no merge. Hans pointed out that the search covers only the asymmetric unit, so the only duplicates left are the in-plane angles of directions exactly on a symmetry axis. Merging those would change no map and would not lower the worst case.
    3. The memory budget was a guess where the feature makes the worst case likely. A broad posterior fills all K points of every pose, so the estimate of 4 entries per pose could be 16 times low. Doubling and copying added peaks, and capping the PCG plane scratch left the per-entry PCG records and metadata unbounded. Changed: decision 8 is a worst-case reservation in a compact layout (max(1, topk) * (8 + 4 K) bytes per particle, with no growth or compaction). Decision 12 counts it exactly. Decision 9 expands the PCG entries in chunks of at most MAXIMGBATCHSZ, with metadata per chunk. At Hans's request, that the production path reads no per-entry metadata after accumulation was confirmed before development rather than left to Phase 1 (section 2). The same reading showed that chunked accumulation is byte-identical to one call. The key-off paths are the base's code, untouched. Section 7 fills the reservation with a broad C1 posterior.
    4. The readout mixed the committed view's weight with the committed angle's. m_c is a view weight; the committed angle's inserted weight is m_c times its bin's share and can be 0 at topk = 1 with a flat row. Changed: decision 10's field is inpl_committed_view_weight_sum, the view weight compared with the table's committed weight (first named inpl_mc_sum; renamed because the abbreviation did not say what it holds). The angle's weight is not reported, and section 7 asserts the topk = 1 broad-row case.
