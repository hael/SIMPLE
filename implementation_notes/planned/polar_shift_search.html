    Polar shift map: a global shift search on the polar Fourier transform¶

    Planned 2026-10-30. One living note: the contract (the mechanics and what is fixed), the plan (phases, exit criteria, file table), the decisions still open, and the progress record. The mechanics and its unit test are written; nothing is wired into a search yet.

    Why¶

    Benchmarks against cryoSPARC on the same particles (Hans, 2026-10-30) found origin shifts after 2D and solve3D of up to 30 Å relative to the picked coordinates, averaging up to 20 Å on some projects. cryoSPARC finds them by a discrete search over shifts in 0.5 Å bins. SIMPLE cannot: the in-plane search of a pair is a 5 x 5 coarse grid over the window [-trs, trs]^2 (coarse_search_opt_angle, gen_objfun_vals at each point) followed by L-BFGS-B in the window, and the window is the stage's trslim = min(8, max(2, AHELIX_WIDTH / smpd_crop)) pixels, about +-10 Å. A particle picked 30 Å off centre is outside the window at every iteration, the L-BFGS basin is about lp/2 wide, and the shift prior is centred on the start, so the pose never recovers and the particle is scored wrongly against every reference for the whole run.

    The Cartesian remedy is to inverse-FFT the cross spectrum and read all shifts at once (the structure of phase correlation and of cryoSPARC's search). The polar representation has an exact analogue in which the inverse FFT over shifts becomes a Fourier-Bessel (Hankel) transform; this note develops it on the calculator's own conventions and plans its use as the coarse initializer of the windowed L-BFGS search.

    The contract¶

    The identity¶

    The calculator shifts the reference in its own frame and then rotates it (gen_corr_cc_for_rot_8_2, gen_corrs: shmat * pfts_refs, then the rotation). On the half ring p = 1..pftsz, theta_p = (p-1) 2 pi / nrots, the shift matrix of s = (sx, sy) (pixels of the box the calculator's argtransf was built on, ldim(1)) is exp(i (ax sx + ay sy)) with ax = k sin(theta_p) 2 pi / box and ay = -k cos(theta_p) 2 pi / box (simple_polarft_core.f90). For a fixed grid rotation irot the shift-dependent part of every objective is the cross term

    C(s) = sum_k w_k sum_p Re[ Q_k(p) exp(i (ax(p,k) sx + ay(p,k) sy)) ],

    with Q_k(p) the pair's cross spectrum in the reference's own indexing: the shift matrix multiplies entry rp of the reference and the rotation lands that entry on ring position q = rp + irot - 1 (modulo the turn) of the particle, so Q_k(rp) = REF(rp,k) CTF(q,k) conj(X~(q,k)), X~ the Friedel-extended particle (its second half the conjugate of the first). For objfun=cc, w_k = k and cc = C / sqrt(sum_k k |CTF REF_k|^2 sum_k k |X_k|^2), the denominator shift-independent; for objfun=euclid, w_k = k / sigma2_k and the loss is const(irot) - 2 C / wsqsum. In polar shift coordinates s = rho (cos a, sin a) the argument is ax sx + ay sy = z_k sin(theta_p - a) with z_k = 2 pi k rho / box, and the Jacobi-Anger expansion exp(i z sin phi) = sum_n J_n(z) exp(i n phi) separates the sum over the ring:

    C(rho, a) = Re sum_n exp(-i n a) a_n(rho),   a_n(rho) = sum_k w_k J_n(z_k(rho)) H_k[n],
H_k[n]    = sum_p Q_k(p) exp(i n theta_p)      (the half ring's harmonics; J_{-n} = (-1)^n J_n).

    Three consequences:

    1. It is exact. It is the same discrete sum the L-BFGS evaluates, with no regridding or interpolation; the only truncation is in n. The hand-off to the L-BFGS is therefore from the global maximum of the function it then refines.
    2. The direction is exact. At fixed rho, C is a trigonometric polynomial in a of degree nharm(rho): the inverse DFT over n samples it without error, and any direction can be evaluated. Only rho is gridded.
    3. rho = 0 keeps the order n = 0 alone and a_0(0) = sum_k w_k sum_p Re Q_k(p) is the present correlation: the existing code is the zeroth term, and the unit test.

    The truncation rule¶

    Past n = z, J_n(z) is positive and decreasing in n (checked numerically for z up to 120). The orders kept at a magnitude rho are 0 .. nharm(rho), nharm(rho) the last order whose Bessel value at the largest argument z_max = 2 pi kmax rho / box is at least SHIFTMAP_JTOL = 1e-8 (simple_polar_shiftmap). In scale-free form z_max = 2 pi rho / lp with rho and lp in the same units. Orders needed (the usual Wiscombe-type estimate z + 4 z^(1/3) + 2 for comparison):

    z_max example kept at 1e-4 1e-6 1e-8 1e-10 z + 4 z^(1/3) + 2
    2.4 30 Å at lp 80 Å 8 11 13 15 10
    9.4 30 Å at lp 20 Å 18 21 24 27 20
    23.6 30 Å at lp 8 Å 35 39 43 47 37
    47.1 30 Å at lp 4 Å 61 66 71 76 64
    94.2 30 Å at Nyquist, 1 Å/px 111 118 124 130 114

    The grid¶

    Magnitudes rho_i (pixels, zero included) and nalpha directions uniform over the turn. The L-BFGS basin is about lp/2 wide, so a magnitude step of lp/4 (in Å, converted at the search sampling) puts the nearest grid point inside the basin: nrho = 4 rho_max / lp, i.e. 6 magnitudes at lp 20 Å and 15 at lp 8 Å for a 30 Å radius. The direction count matters only for the readout (the polynomial is exact); 2 nharm + 1 samples represent it completely, fewer are fine for a peak search followed by L-BFGS.

    Sampling of the ring¶

    The ring must resolve the phase ramp of the largest shift at the outer shell: nrots >= 4 pi kmax rho / box, i.e. rho <= pftsz box / (2 pi kmax) pixels. With pftsz = pi msk (magic_pftsz) that is rho <= msk lp / (2 smpd), always true for picking errors; it is a condition on the discrete objective itself, not on the expansion, which is an identity of the discrete sum.

    Cost¶

    Per pair at one rotation: the harmonics (a DFT of the half ring per shell and order, 2 K pftsz (N+1), or one FFT of the zero-padded half ring per shell, K nrots log nrots), then the Bessel sums nrho (2N+1) K and the evaluation nrho nalpha (2N+1). Against a brute-force grid of pi nrho^2 points at the same step, each a K pftsz sum, that is one to two orders of magnitude less.

    Over all rotations the production form works from the memoised spectra. On the Friedel-extended full ring the expansion holds with a_{-n} = conj(a_n) (only n >= 0 is needed, and the full-ring sum is twice the half-ring objective), the memoised half spectra determine the full ones (ft_ref, ft_ptcl_ctf keep bins 0..pftsz of the extended ring's FFT, and the extension gives F^[-m] = (-1)^m conj(F^[m])), and for every order n the rotational correlation of the reference's spectrum displaced by n bins with the particle's is one inverse transform of length nrots after the Bessel-weighted shell sum:

    a_n(irot, rho) = (1/nrots) sum_m exp(-2 pi i m (irot-1)/nrots) sum_k w_k J_n(z_k(rho)) R^_k[m-n] conj(X^_k[m]).

    That costs (N+1) nrho (K nrots multiply-adds + one FFT) per pair and no forward transforms. The present coarse grid pays, per grid point, K forward FFTs of the shifted reference plus K nrots products and one inverse FFT; at equal coverage (nalpha ~ 2N+1 directions) the map is roughly 10-20 times cheaper and exact in the direction. Both forms are verified numerically (Progress, 2026-10-30).

    What is built¶

    src/main/pftc/simple_polar_shiftmap.f90: the class polar_shiftmap. new(pftsz, kfromto, box, rho(:), nalpha) builds the tables (Bessel values per order, shell and magnitude; the ring's and the directions' twiddles; the orders kept); cross_spectrum(ref, ptcl, ctf, irot, Q) forms Q of a pair at a grid rotation in the calculator's convention; coeffs(Q, w, A) the coefficients a_n(rho) for any shell weights; eval(A, irho, alpha) the cross term at a magnitude and any direction; map(A, cmap) the grid; peak(cmap, irho, ialpha, val) its maximum; get_shift(irho, ialpha) the shift of a grid point in the frame the shift matrix acts in. The harmonics are a direct DFT with a twiddle table (the mechanics, not the kernel); all arithmetic is double precision; the object is read-only after new, so one instance serves every thread.

    src/main/pftc/simple_polar_shiftmap_tester.f90, sub-suite polar shift map of unit_pftc_align2D3D (simple_test_exec test=unit_pftc_align2D3D suite=polar_shift_map): the map against the closed-form cosine sum of a pure phase ramp on and off the grid, the peak of that ramp, the zero-magnitude row and the truncation rule; the map's normalised cross term against gen_corr_for_rot_8 (objfun=cc, the reference shifted and rotated by the calculator) at the four rotations that exercise every branch of rotate_ref_8 and at 40 random poses, to 1e-9; and peak recovery of a particle that is the reference shifted and rotated by a known grid pose, exact and at unit SNR, where the calculator's cc of 1 at the true pose certifies that the tester's construction follows the calculator's conventions.

    Decisions fixed¶

    • The map scores the same objective the L-BFGS refines: the shell weights are the objective's (k for cc, k/sigma2 for euclid), no per-coefficient whitening. Per-coefficient phase normalisation amplifies every shell whose per-coefficient SNR is below one, which for a single particle is most of them; the peak width is set by the band limit, not by whitening.
    • The shift prior is added on the grid (it is quadratic in s and a per-rotation factor, prior_factors), so the grid maximum is the MAP of the same posterior the L-BFGS polishes; that is also what keeps a disk of several hundred hypotheses honest at low SNR.
    • Where the map runs (ruling 12). On the refined candidates of a particle, where the L-BFGS runs today (<= prob_athres references in 3D, the 2D refined set), in the centring stage as in every other: the global search of their shifts before the windowed L-BFGS, never against every class. A picking offset is a property of the particle, not of the (reference, rotation) hypothesis: the shift found on the committed candidate is the stored shift every entry of the next iteration is scored at, so a mis-centred particle is scored wrongly for one iteration and then correctly. Phase 1's timing table decided it: the map at the disk costs about 150 times one per-class score of the fill.
    • The L-BFGS window after the map is the magnitude step about the grid maximum (the basin), not the search radius.
    • Same mechanics for 2D and 3D (one calculator).

    Rulings (2026-10-30)¶

    Hans's rulings on the maintainer's decisions, from the design discussion of 2026-10-30, with the schedule facts read from the code the same day.

    1. The radius is the prior's: radius = 3 sigma, one quantity, no separate switch. sigma = 10 Å from the benchmark (a Gaussian whose 3 sigma is the 30 Å maximum recovered), so the disk is 30 Å; the grid step is lp/4 (5 Å at lp 20 Å, six magnitudes); the L-BFGS limits are +-one magnitude step about the grid start, the basin, never the disk.
    2. The prior is absolute: a Gaussian centred on the pick with sigma 10 Å, applied on the grid as the per-rotation factor so grid and L-BFGS score one posterior. The present random-walk prior on the increments (shift_prior_sigma2) stays in the stages the map does not run in.
    3. One objective for both levels (Decisions fixed). The grid is joint in rotation and shift: the map gives (irot, rho, alpha) for every rotation at once, no alternation and no rotation at the wrong shift. The direction is a trigonometric polynomial, so the sub-grid start is Newton in alpha and a three-point parabola in rho at no objective evaluation. Where the map runs, this replaces grid_fit and the particle-frame seed bank.
    4. Superseded by ruling 12. The first form, the map inside the fill against every class at the cheap stage, was a cost claim; Phase 1's timing table (Progress, 2026-10-30) put the map at the disk at about 150 times one per-class score of today's fill, above 30 after the obvious optimisations.
    5. The in-plane choice is a draw over the three in-plane degrees of freedom (ruled 2026-10-30). The first ruling, the greedy best (irot, shift) per class with the draw over classes only, was reopened the same day: the tempered in-plane draw has been the mechanism that diversifies the search and converges the hard particles, and a greedy shift would make that draw conditional on one shift hypothesis. Today gen_posterior_val draws the rotation from p_r proportional to exp(-T (L_r - L_min)) over all nrots rotations at the stored shift, with T = prob_beta x pi wsqsum / (2 pftsz) (posterior_temperature, draw_posterior_row), the class is drawn from the row posterior, and the shift is local and deterministic. The map gives the same normalised loss on the whole (irot, rho, alpha) grid, L(irot, s) = const(irot) - 2 C(irot, s) / wsqsum (a shift preserves the reference's power per shell), so one draw_posterior_row over the flattened grid, prior included, is the joint draw; its cost is the map's, the draw itself one cumulative sum. Two conditions: the prior is not tempered, p proportional to exp(-beta T (L - L_min)) x prior(s), because tempering it at beta 0.1 widens sigma by 1/sqrt(beta) and the disk becomes uniform; and the anneal tail (l_anneal_tail, prob_beta_end = 1) ends at the physical posterior, where a sharpened class average narrows the shift posterior below the grid step and the draw is deterministic in effect. Early draws then spread at the 10 Å scale, inside the band of lp 20-30 Å; this is the single-sample form of RELION's marginalisation over psi and the translations under its Gaussian translational prior. The greedy choice is the same draw at infinite temperature on the shift degrees and stays as the comparison arm of Phase 3 behind the switch (ruling 11). To measure: the entropy of a grid of some 45,000 points against today's 300 (more diffuse draws at equal beta; the untempered prior bounds it), and the fill cost (ruling 4).
    6. The class-centring rule and the prior agree on the frame: cavger's centre-of-mass recentring of the class averages (members shifted accordingly) and the absolute prior on the particle are expressed in one frame, and the automask mass-centre shift and the cavg centring are one thing, not two. Settled after reading cavger, before Phase 2.
    7. Masks. The centring stage runs with the widest mask, the mskdiam = 0 effect of derive_image_settings ((box - COSMSKHALFWIDTH) smpd), which also puts the 2D limits at lpstart 20, lpstop 8 and lpcen 30 Å and grows pftsz; a user mskdiam is ignored in that stage. At its exit a mask diameter is estimated from the class averages (automask2D's diameters, population-weighted, empty and junk classes excluded, a high quantile rather than the maximum) and becomes the next stage's parameter through automask2D_mskdiam; a user mskdiam is honoured from that stage on. No mid-run mask change: the stage boundary builds a new calculator anyway.
    8. The centring stage exits on the shift statistics in addition to its usual criteria: the median per-iteration shift change over the sampled particles below lp/4 for two iterations.
    9. Re-extraction (reextract with the recovered centres, shifts reset, the prior re-anchored) and Joe's data are later questions, after a working implementation.

    10. Where the centring stage sits. Shifts are never searched against random or noise-initialised references. Today solve2D (six stages) sets trs = 0, center = no and Gaussian-regularised references in stage 1 and opens its window at stage 2 under snhc_smpl, one stage before prob at stage 3 (simple_solve2D_controller.f90, set_refine2D_stage_reference_policy; the window min(5, max(2, AHELIX_WIDTH / smpd_crop)) cropped pixels, about 12 Å, set in simple_commanders_solve2D.f90). solve3D (eight stages) sets trs = 0 in stages 1-2 and opens its window at stage 3 together with prob (simple_solve3D_controller.f90, build_refine3D_stage_cfg; min(8, ...) in simple_estimate_ssnr.f90). Neither window reaches the 20-30 Å the benchmark found. The centring stage is therefore solve2D's stage 2 and solve3D's stage 3: the map opens the 30 Å disk where the 12 Å window opens today, against references one stage old.

    11. Under a switch (Hans, 2026-10-30), so the integration point is tested, not guessed. The map is off by default and every existing path is unchanged when it is off (identity on the simulated_workflow entries). One key, the prior's sigma in Å (working name shift_sigma; 0 or absent = the present window search), turns it on in whatever program carries it: radius 3 sigma, the absolute prior, the joint draw in the fill, the map as the coarse initializer at the window's radius elsewhere. refine2D and refine3D honour it on their command lines first (Phase 2a), so a stage is tested by handing the key to that stage alone; the solve2D/solve3D controllers adopt it as a stage parameter (Phase 2b) once Phase 3's synthetic recovery says which stage. The greedy arm of the comparison is a second, test-level key, not a user setting.

    12. The map runs as the L-BFGS runs today, on the sampled subset of classes or projections (the refined candidates), in the centring stage too (Hans, 2026-10-30, on Phase 1's timing). The kernel is optimised first, as fast as it can be made, before Phase 2a builds on it; its exactness is kept (the identity tests), its speed measured against the Phase 1 kernel built from its commit (tree) on the same fixture.

    What Phase 3 measures, in order: the fraction of particles that leave rejected classes for good ones; the shift histogram against the Gaussian cryoSPARC measured, sigma included; class quality; after re-extraction, the downstream solve3D. The synthetic recovery test against magnitude and SNR runs alongside.

    Open decisions¶

    1. Closed (Hans, 2026-10-30): the 20 Å average is a mean magnitude, so a spread of picking errors, the case this fixes, not a systematic origin offset. Not every particle has a large shift: the large ones are probably the side views of the membrane protein, which the picker places with a systematic bias. Phase 3 therefore reports the shift histogram per class as well as overall, since a view-dependent bias shows as a per-class offset and the frame question of ruling 6 then matters for those classes.
    2. The centring frame (ruling 6), after reading cavger.

    Common rules¶

    • Nothing is committed; the maintainer reviews the diff.
    • The contract above is fixed: a compile fix may not change the identity, the truncation rule or the API; a test tolerance is not relaxed to pass (the mechanics are exact to double precision; a larger error is a bug).
    • Fortran rules of .github/skills/simple-modern-fortran/SKILL.md: use ..., only:; THROW macros on one line; no goto; no .and. short-circuit on allocatable arrays; large locals class(T), allocatable; comments short.
    • New OpenMP code is stress-tested at the machine's full thread count against one thread.
