    Dose-series accumulation under a temporal attenuation model¶

    Initial design, 3 November 2026, against SIMPLE 69c6c9d78 plus the working-tree refine_motion_model changes. Producer integration is implemented and committed (section 10). The consumer, dose_filter3D, is implemented in the working tree: its implementation and corrections are in section 11. The re-extraction of particles under the measured curve (reextract dose_weighting=measured) is in section 12, its uncertainty and comparison corrections in 12.1, and the validation to redo after these changes in section 13. This note supersedes the dose_filter3D_implementation_note.md draft and incorporates the contract decisions of section 9.

    Working name: dose_filter3D. The voltage, box and doses come from the manifest, not the command line.

    simple_exec prg=dose_filter3D filetab=dose_series.txt smpd=<Å> mskdiam=<Å> \
    [hp=<Å>] [lp=<Å>] [bfac_model=line|free] [zerodose=yes|no] [outvol=dose_series.mrc] nthr=16

    1 Scientific purpose and aim¶

    A cryo-EM movie's later frames image a progressively more damaged specimen. The standard remedy is the Grant & Grigorieff critical-dose curve — in SIMPLE calc_dose_weights (src/utils/filter/simple_estimate_ssnr.f90), applied frame by frame in motion correction — which weights a frame's amplitudes by $\exp!\big(-D / 2N_e(k)\big)$ with

    $$ N_e(k) = 0.245\,k^{-1.665} + 2.81 \quad \mathrm{e}/\text{Å}^2 . \tag{1} $$

    One curve, with three constants, for every specimen ever imaged. It is a prior, not a measurement: the specimen, its support and its ice decide how fast the structure actually fades.

    Aim. Measure that decay on the data itself, from a series of per-dose-window reconstructions, and use the measured curve in place of the generic constants — first to accumulate the windows optimally into one map, and later to weight frames in motion correction.

    Three products, in increasing order of what they commit to:

    1. A measurement with no model — the relative attenuation of every window at every resolution shell, written before any premise is applied (section 4.3). A Guinier plot per window.
    2. A model — one B-factor per window, those B-factors on a smooth curve of dose (sections 4.4-4.5), and the accumulation that curve implies (section 4.6).
    3. A comparison — the model against the Grant & Grigorieff curve in the same estimator, as a cross-half prediction error band by band (section 5). It is descriptive: the attenuations are fitted on both halves, so it shows where the premise fails on these data but is not an independent test of it.

    1.1 Relation to uniform_filter3D¶

    The closest kin in SIMPLE is uniform_filter3D (exec_uniform_filter3D in simple_commanders_resolest.f90, estimate_lplim in src/utils/filter/simple_opt_filter.f90): it filters the odd half with a Butterworth low-pass at every candidate cutoff and keeps the cutoff whose filtered odd half lies closest to the even half inside the mask, the squared distance being the cross-validation cost. estimate_lplim3D runs the same search on refine3D's reference halves to set the low-pass limit; nu_filt3D runs it per voxel over a bank of cutoffs with Potts smoothing of the labels.

    The dose series is that search with $W$ half-map pairs instead of one, and the attenuation model in place of the Butterworth cutoff as the filter family: the prediction of a window's half from the other halves stands where the filtered odd half stood, the even/odd squared distance is the cost, and a window predicted from its own even half alone recovers uniform_filter3D's cost with the all-pass filter. Per shell the cost is a quadratic form in the Gram matrix of the $2W$ halves (section 4.2), so a candidate costs a few numbers where uniform_filter3D passes over the volume once per candidate.

    One difference of purpose: uniform_filter3D delivers the filtered half (per shell its cost is minimised by the FSC itself, the Wiener filter of one half); the dose series delivers the unfiltered accumulation and leaves the filtering to postprocess or enhance.

    2 Notation¶

    Every symbol used in this note. Where a symbol collides with a conventional one, the clash is named.

    2.1 Indices and geometry¶

    Symbol Meaning
    $w, v$ Window index, $1 \le w,v \le W$; windows are ordered by increasing dose
    $W$ Number of dose windows in the series
    $h$ Half index, $h \in {\mathrm{e}, \mathrm{o}}$ for even and odd
    $t$ The target window in a cross-validation fold (section 5)
    $\mathbf q$ Integer Fourier coordinate on the half-volume lattice: $q_1 \ge 0$, and on the $q_1 = 0$ plane only $q_2 > 0$, or $q_2 = 0$ and $q_3 > 0$; the mates are conjugates
    $N$ Box width in pixels (not a noise symbol; noise is $\varepsilon$)
    $d$ Pixel size in Å
    $s$ Radial shell index, $s(\mathbf q) = \mathrm{nint}(\lVert\mathbf q\rVert)$, running $1 \le s \le N/2$
    $k_s$ Spatial frequency of shell $s$, $k_s = s/(Nd)$ in Å$^{-1}$
    $n_s$ Number of listed coefficients in shell $s$
    $n_{\mathrm{sh}}$ Number of shells in the fitted band
    $M(\mathbf x)$ Real-space soft mask at the mskdiam radius
    $f$ Fraction of a shell's coefficients the mask leaves independent (section 4.2)
    $n_s^{\mathrm{eff}}$ Independent coefficient count of shell $s$, $n_s^{\mathrm{eff}} = f\,n_s$

    2.2 Data and model¶

    Symbol Meaning
    $E_w(\mathbf q),\ O_w(\mathbf q)$ Fourier coefficients of window $w$'s even and odd half maps
    $Y_w^h(\mathbf q)$ Either half of window $w$, when the distinction does not matter
    $S(\mathbf q)$ The structure, as window 1 sees it
    $\varepsilon_w^h(\mathbf q)$ Noise in half $h$ of window $w$
    $\sigma_w^2(s)$ Variance of one half's noise coefficient in shell $s$
    $p_w(s)$ Precision, $p_w(s) = 1/\sigma_w^2(s)$
    $P(s)$ Signal power of window 1 in shell $s$, $P(s) = n_s^{-1}\sum_{\mathbf q\in s}\lvert S(\mathbf q)\rvert^2$
    $a_w(s)$ Attenuation of window $w$ at shell $s$, relative to window 1
    $c_w$ Scale of window $w$ (dimensionless); $c_1 \equiv 1$
    $B_w$ B-factor of window $w$ in Å$^2$, relative to window 1; $B_1 \equiv 0$
    $D_w$ Accumulated dose at window $w$'s centre, e/Å$^2$, in the scaled coordinate of section 3.3
    $B(D)$ The dose curve, $B(D) = B_0 + \beta D$
    $B_0$ Intercept of the dose curve, Å$^2$
    $\beta$ Slope of the dose curve, Å$^2$ per e/Å$^2$ — the headline measurement
    $\delta_w$ Window $w$'s excess over the curve, $B_w = B(D_w) + \delta_w$
    $\tau$ Scatter of $\delta_w$ across the windows, Å$^2$
    $B_w^\star$ The B-factor actually used for window $w$, after shrinkage
    $a_w^\star(s)$ Attenuation from the shrunk parameters, $a_w^\star = c_w \exp(-B_w^\star k_s^2/4)$
    $N_e(k)$ Critical exposure of equation (1), e/Å$^2$ (not the box width $N$)

    3 The premise¶

    3.1 The model¶

    Between windows the structure does not change, it fades. Window $w$ sees the structure through one scale and one B-factor:

    $$ Y_w^h(\mathbf q) = a_w(s)\,S(\mathbf q) + \varepsilon_w^h(\mathbf q), \qquad a_w(s) = c_w \exp!\left(-\frac{B_w\,k_s^2}{4}\right), \qquad h \in {\mathrm e, \mathrm o} \tag{2} $$

    and those B-factors lie on a smooth curve of the accumulated dose:

    $$ B_w = B(D_w) + \delta_w, \qquad B(D) = B_0 + \beta D, \qquad \delta_w \sim \big(0,\ \tau^2\big). \tag{3} $$

    The B-factor convention is the usual one on amplitudes, $\exp(-Bk^2/4)$ at $k = 1/\text{resolution}$. The structure $S$ absorbs an overall scale and B-factor, so the gauge is

    $$ c_1 = 1, \qquad B_1 = 0 : \tag{4} $$

    $S$ is the structure as window 1 sees it, every $a_w$, $c_w$ and $B_w$ is relative to window 1, and the dose curve extrapolates below it.

    Noise. $\mathbb E[\varepsilon] = 0$ and $\mathrm{Var}(\varepsilon_w^h(\mathbf q)) = \sigma_w^2(s)$. Detector noise is independent across windows, halves and coefficients: different windows are different movie frames, so their shot noise is independent. What is shared between windows, within a half, is the structure and the alignment error — the same particles at the same poses. That asymmetry is the whole basis of section 4.2.

    3.2 Why this model¶

    • Why a B-factor. A Gaussian in $k$ is the one-parameter description of a resolution-dependent amplitude loss, and the quantity postprocess and enhance already speak in.
    • Why a line in dose. If damage accumulates smoothly, $B$ should be a smooth function of $D$. A line is the simplest thing the data can refute, and the residual scatter $\tau$ measures the refutation.
    • Why bother. The model has $2W + 2$ parameters fitted from of order $10^8$ Fourier coefficients. Its weights are smooth in resolution by construction, where a free per-shell weight set would fit noise.
    • Closed form. Under the model the optimal accumulation is the matched filter (section 4.6): no target window, no weight to choose per shell.
    • It extrapolates. The structure at zero dose is a per-shell rescaling of the accumulated map.
    • It transfers. The dose curve gives per-frame weights for motion correction, replacing the generic constants — a reconstruction from all frames at full time resolution with measured weights.

    3.3 Orientation against Grant & Grigorieff¶

    Their amplitude factor $\exp!\big(-D/2N_e(k)\big)$ is equation (2) with $B = \beta D$ when

    $$ \frac{D}{2N_e(k)} = \frac{\beta D\,k^2}{4} \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \beta = \frac{2}{N_e(k)\,k^2} . \tag{5} $$

    Evaluating (5) at the constants of (1) gives $\beta = 2.2$ Å$^2$ per e/Å$^2$ at 2 Å, $4.2$ at 3 Å and $14$ at 10 Å. Their curve is therefore not one B-factor: it fades the low shells faster than a B-factor extrapolated from the high shells would. Which description fits a given specimen is what sections 4.4 and 5 measure.

    Dose coordinate. calc_dose_weights divides the accumulated dose by $1$ at 300 kV, $0.8$ at 200 kV and $0.64$ at 100 kV — it divides, raising the effective dose at lower voltage. $D_w$ in this note is the dose in that same scaled coordinate, so the fitted $\beta$ drops into calc_dose_weights unchanged and is directly comparable to the equivalents of (5). The price is that $\beta$ is then not a physical specimen constant and is not comparable across voltages; that is recorded as a limitation (section 8). At 300 kV the two coordinates coincide. The input manifest records physical dose; the program applies the scaling.

    4 Method¶

    4.1 Inputs, window selection, noise and band¶

    Inputs. $W$ raw half pairs from refine_motion_model's interval reconstructions — recvol_state01_{even,odd}_unfil.mrc in each rec_frames_aa-bb/, unfiltered and unmasked, state 1 only — at one box $N$, one sampling $d$, one pose convention, with the same even/odd particle partition in every window. A manifest written by the producer carries, per window: the two half-map paths, the inclusive fraction range $[a,b]$, the physical dose bounds derived from the saved motion model's dose_per_frame, and a fingerprint of the eo partition.

    4.1.1 The manifest¶

    filetab names the manifest. It is not a bare read_filetable list: that helper reads every line verbatim with no comment handling, so dose_filter3D parses the file itself. Comment lines start with #; the first line is the version and is required. Half-map paths are relative to the manifest's own directory, so a run directory can be moved. Doses are physical e/Å$^2$; the consumer applies the voltage scaling of section 3.3.

    # SIMPLE dose series 1
# program            refine_motion_model
# state              1
# box                288
# smpd               1.06
# kv                 300.0
# nptcls             12043
# neven              6021
# nodd               6022
# eo_digest          6C4DD423214CC38D
# dose_per_fraction  1.2500000000E+00
# columns  from to dose_lo dose_hi even odd

    dose_lo is the accumulated dose at the start of fraction from, dose_hi at the end of fraction to; the producer takes both from the saved motion model's dose_per_frame and the original fraction IDs. The window centre of section 3.3 is then $D_w = \tfrac12(\texttt{dose_lo} + \texttt{dose_hi})$ after voltage scaling, and $\Delta D_w = \texttt{dose_hi} - \texttt{dose_lo}$ is what section 8's deferred window-integrated attenuation would need. eo_digest is a hash of the selected particles' eo vector, constant across windows by construction; it is recorded once and only lets the consumer confirm that every window came from one partition.

    The consumer rejects: a missing or unknown version line; fewer than two windows after the pruning below; non-increasing from; a dose_hi not greater than its dose_lo; non-increasing dose across windows; a kv outside {300, 200, 100}; a missing half map; and any volume whose box, sampling or cubic shape disagrees with the header.

    Window selection. refine_motion_model's last interval keeps its full stepf width, so when $(\texttt{tof} - \texttt{fromf} + 1)$ is not a multiple of stepf it overlaps its predecessor and the two share particle images — which would break the independence of (2). Drop the predecessor, keeping the full-width highest-dose window. The survivors are disjoint but need not tile the exposure. Fewer than two surviving windows is a hard error: below $W = 2$ the model is not identifiable.

    Lattice. For the statistics each half is read, soft-masked by $M$ at the mskdiam radius about its own solvent level, Fourier transformed, and the listed coefficients gathered into an $(n_{\mathrm{coeff}}, W)$ complex single-precision array per half. Removing the global mean instead would leave an offset under the soft edge, which rings into every shell. For the accumulation of section 4.6 the halves are read again as given, with only their mean removed; the two sets are never held at once.

    Noise. Per window and shell, from the half difference:

    $$ \hat\sigma_w^2(s) = \frac{1}{2\,n_s}\sum_{\mathbf q\in s}\big\lvert E_w(\mathbf q) - O_w(\mathbf q)\big\rvert^2, \qquad p_w(s) = \frac{1}{\hat\sigma_w^2(s)} . \tag{6} $$

    Why (6) measures noise and nothing else. Both halves see the same structure under the same attenuation, so the signal cancels exactly: $$E_w - O_w = \big(a_w S + \varepsilon_w^{\mathrm e}\big) - \big(a_w S + \varepsilon_w^{\mathrm o}\big) = \varepsilon_w^{\mathrm e} - \varepsilon_w^{\mathrm o}.$$ With independent, equal-variance half noise $\mathrm{Var}(\varepsilon^{\mathrm e} - \varepsilon^{\mathrm o}) = 2\sigma_w^2$, hence the factor 2 in the denominator. This assumes the two halves have equal noise, which holds to the extent the even/odd particle split is balanced.

    One spectrum suffices, not two. The statistics run on masked halves and the accumulation on the halves as given, and those have different noise levels. But a soft mask multiplies in real space, so it convolves in Fourier, and where the noise power is locally smooth across the kernel width it scales every window's noise power by the same scalar $\langle M^2\rangle$. The weights of section 4.6 are normalised to sum one, so a factor common to all $p_w$ cancels: $\omega$ is invariant under $p_w \mapsto \gamma(s)\,p_w$. The masked spectrum therefore serves both roles — absolutely for the whitening of section 5, which is computed on the same masked quantities, and up to the cancelling factor for the weights. The approximation degrades only at the lowest few shells, where the noise power varies fast across the kernel.

    Band. From the high-pass shell $s(\texttt{hp})$, hp defaulting to 20 Å, to $s(\texttt{lp})$ when lp is given; otherwise up to the last shell where any window's own FSC, $C_{ww}/\sqrt{G^{\mathrm{ee}}{ww}G^{\mathrm{oo}}$, stays above $0.1$ — gaps allowed, at least six shells. B-factors carry no information at the lowest shells, where a handful of coefficients is dominated by the mask edge and the solvent: on a synthetic series a band starting at shell 1 let the 96 Å shell, 13 coefficients, drive every $B_w$ negative.}

    Why "any window" and not the sum. The early windows reach further in resolution than the full-dose sum does, because the sum's late windows add only noise there. Taking the band from the sum would cut it short exactly where this method has the most to gain.

    4.2 The Gram matrices: sufficient statistics¶

    One pass over the coefficients. With $\mathbf y(\mathbf q) = (E_1,\dots,E_W, O_1,\dots,O_W)$,

    $$ G(s) = \frac{1}{n_s}\sum_{\mathbf q\in s}\mathrm{Re}\big[\mathbf y(\mathbf q)\,\mathbf y(\mathbf q)^{\mathsf H}\big] \qquad (2W \times 2W,\ \text{real symmetric}), \tag{7} $$

    with within-half blocks $G^{\mathrm{ee}}$, $G^{\mathrm{oo}}$ and cross-half block

    $$ X_{wv}(s) = \frac{1}{n_s}\sum_{\mathbf q\in s}\mathrm{Re}\big[E_w(\mathbf q)\,\overline{O_v(\mathbf q)}\big], \qquad C = \tfrac12\big(X + X^{\mathsf T}\big), \qquad \mathbb E\big[C_{wv}(s)\big] = a_w(s)\,a_v(s)\,P(s). \tag{8} $$

    Why the model is fitted on the cross-half block alone. $E_w$ and $O_v$ are built from disjoint particle halves, so their noise and their alignment errors are independent and $\mathbb E[E_w\overline{O_v}]$ is unbiased for the signal product $a_w a_v P$. By contrast $\mathbb E[E_w\overline{E_v}]$ carries the half's shared error on top and is biased upward. Note that this also makes the diagonal $C_{ww}$ usable — it is the window's own FSC numerator — unlike the diagonal of an ordinary Gram matrix, which is pure power.

    The variance $V_{wv}(s)$ is the noise of $C_{wv}(s)$ alone, given the structure. Per coefficient, $\mathrm{Var}\,\mathrm{Re}[E_w\overline{O_v}] = \tfrac12\big(\lvert a_wS\rvert^2\sigma_v^2 + \lvert a_vS\rvert^2\sigma_w^2 + \sigma_w^2\sigma_v^2\big)$, and with $a_w^2P = G^{\mathrm{ee}}_{ww} - \sigma_w^2$ every term is observable:

    $$ \mathrm{Var}(X_{wv}) = \frac{G^{\mathrm{ee}}{ww}\,\sigma_v^2 + G^{\mathrm{oo}}, \qquad V_{wv} = \tfrac14\big(\mathrm{Var}(X_{wv}) + \mathrm{Var}(X_{vw})\big),\quad V_{ww} = \mathrm{Var}(X_{ww}). \tag{8a} $$}\,\sigma_w^2 - \sigma_w^2\sigma_v^2}{2\,n_s^{\mathrm{eff}}

    Why not the spread of the products. The sample variance of $\mathrm{Re}[E_w\overline{O_v}]$ over a shell also holds $a_wa_v(\lvert S_{\mathbf q}\rvert^2 - P)$, the structure's own power fluctuating from coefficient to coefficient. That is no error: $P(s)$ is fitted per shell as the shell's realized power, and the fluctuation is common to every pair, so it cancels in the attenuations. Counting it inflates every error by about the amplitude SNR — 35-fold at high SNR on synthetic series. The off-diagonal $V_{wv}$ is a quarter of the summed variances because $C$ averages $X_{wv}$ and $X_{vw}$, two estimates with independent noise.

    Why this independent count. The mask correlates neighbouring Fourier coefficients, so a shell holds fewer independent samples than it holds coefficients. Masked noise is correlated by a kernel $\rho$, the transform of $M^2$, which is three-dimensional; a shell, one coefficient thick, cuts only a plane of it. By the projection-slice theorem that plane's $\sum\lvert\rho\rvert^2$ is $N^2\sum_{xy}\big(\sum_z M^2\big)^2 / \big(\sum M^2\big)^2$, so $$f = \frac{3\,\big(\sum_{\mathbf x} M^2\big)^2}{N^2\sum_{\text{axes}}\sum_{\text{plane}}\big(\sum_{\perp} M^2\big)^2},$$ averaged over the three axes; for a ball of volume fraction $f_V$ it is $1.075\,f_V^{2/3}$, not $f_V$. The volume fraction is the count for a volume, not a shell: with SIMPLE's soft mask at mskdiam 66 Å in a 96 Å box, $\langle M^2\rangle = 0.142$ where a simulation of the masked noise products gives 0.314 and the formula 0.311.

    Why one pass is enough. Everything downstream is a quadratic form in $\mathbf y$. The cross-half prediction error of any weight set $\boldsymbol\omega$ predicting window $t$'s odd half from the even halves is $$\frac{1}{n_s}\sum_{\mathbf q\in s}\Big\lvert O_t(\mathbf q) - \sum_v \omega_v E_v(\mathbf q)\Big\rvert^2 = G^{\mathrm{oo}}{tt} - 2\sum_v \omega_v X \boldsymbol\omega ,$$ and the FSC of any accumulation is $$\mathrm{FSC}(s) = \frac{\boldsymbol\omega^{\mathsf T} C\, \boldsymbol\omega}{\sqrt{\big(\boldsymbol\omega^{\mathsf T} G^{\mathrm{ee}}\boldsymbol\omega\big)\big(\boldsymbol\omega^{\mathsf T} G^{\mathrm{oo}}\boldsymbol\omega\big)}} .$$ So a candidate costs $O(W^2)$ arithmetic on $n_{\mathrm{sh}}$ small matrices, where } + \boldsymbol\omega^{\mathsf T} G^{\mathrm{ee}uniform_filter3D passes over the volume once per candidate. Only section 4.6 touches the coefficients again.

    4.3 Relative attenuation per shell, with no model¶

    Per shell, the rank-one weighted least-squares fit of (8) with the gauge $a_1(s) = 1$:

    $$ \min_{\,a_2 \dots a_W,\ P}\ \sum_{w \le v} \frac{\big[C_{wv}(s) - P(s)\,a_w(s)\,a_v(s)\big]^2}{V_{wv}(s)} . \tag{9} $$

    Start from the leading eigenvector of the symmetric $C(s)$ scaled to $a_1 = 1$, then three alternating passes (each $a_w$ given the others and $P$, then $P$ given all $a_w$). Output $a_w(s) \pm \mathrm{se}_w(s)$, the standard errors from the sandwich covariance $H^{-1}\big(J^{\mathsf T}D\,\Sigma\,DJ\big)H^{-1}$ of (9), with $J$ the Jacobian of $Pa_wa_v$, $D = \mathrm{diag}(1/V)$, $H = J^{\mathsf T}DJ$ and, to first order,

    $$ C_{wv} - P a_w a_v = a_w\gamma_v + a_v\gamma_w + \varrho_{wv}, \qquad \mathrm{Var}(\gamma_k) = \frac{P\sigma_k^2}{4\,n_s^{\mathrm{eff}}}, \qquad \mathrm{Var}(\varrho_{wv}) = \frac{\sigma_w^2\sigma_v^2}{4\,n_s^{\mathrm{eff}}}\ \Big(\frac{\sigma_w^4}{2\,n_s^{\mathrm{eff}}}\ \text{for}\ w=v\Big). \tag{9a} $$

    Why a sandwich. $\gamma_k$, the projection of window $k$'s half noise onto the structure, enters every pair that holds $k$, so it does not average down over those pairs as the weights alone would assume: their curvature understates the error by about $\sqrt{W+1}$ at high SNR. $\Sigma$ is fixed by the quantities already estimated, and its diagonal is (8a).

    What this is. The Guinier diagnostic, written before any premise is applied. It asks only whether the windows differ by one multiplicative factor per shell — whether $C(s)$ is rank one. If they differ in more than one direction, which is structural change rather than fading, the rank-one fit will not describe $C$ and the residual shows it here, rather than being absorbed silently into a B-factor downstream.

    4.4 One B-factor per window¶

    Per window, over the band:

    $$ \min_{c_w,\,B_w}\ \sum_s \frac{\Big[a_w(s) - c_w\exp!\big(-B_w k_s^2/4\big)\Big]^2}{\mathrm{se}_w(s)^2} . \tag{10} $$

    Start from the weighted regression of $\log a_w(s)$ on $k_s^2$ over the shells where $a_w(s)$ exceeds twice its error, then refine (10) in the linear domain by Levenberg-Marquardt, keeping a step only when it lowers (10), with standard errors $\mathrm{se}_w$ from the curvature, inflated by the mask's correlation across shells (section 11.8). A window whose fit fails — no curvature, a non-finite objective, or a scale $c_w$ within five of its own errors of zero — is rejected with a warning: it is left out of the dose line and takes no weight in the model's accumulation (13), while the control and the flat mean keep it. A window with no signal must take no weight, which the line's prediction at its dose would give it.

    Why step acceptance. Undamped Gauss-Newton steps diverged on a window carrying no signal: the objective rose from 10.67 to $3.5\times10^{45}$ with $B_w = -1927$, and under bfac_model=free that window took 99% of the weight at the band edge while the run reported normal completion. A window that is genuinely sharper than window 1 fits well and keeps its weight; the gate is on the fit, not on how much a window amplifies.

    Why refine in $a$ and not in $\log a$. Shells where the window has faded to noise have $a_w(s)$ near zero or negative. The logarithm is undefined or wildly weighted there. Fitting the residual in $a$ lets those shells contribute with the error they actually have.

    Readout per window: the slope $-B_w/4$, the scale $c_w$, and the rms deviation of the shells from the line in units of their errors. A straight line in $k^2$ is the premise holding for that window. A curvature consistent across windows is the exponent question of section 8.

    4.5 The dose curve¶

    The weighted line $B(D) = B_0 + \beta D$ through the points $\big(D_w,\,B_w,\,\mathrm{se}_w\big)$ for $w = 2 \dots W$, with the excess scatter $\tau^2$ by the usual random-effects (DerSimonian-Laird) estimate, its trace that of a weighted line (section 11.3) — zero when the windows sit on the line within their errors — and per window the residual

    $$ z_w = \frac{B_w - B(D_w)}{\sqrt{\mathrm{se}_w^2 + \tau^2}} . \tag{11} $$

    Why window 1 is excluded from the fit. $B_1 = 0$ is exact by the gauge (4), not measured: its standard error is identically zero, and both the weighted line and the shrinkage below divide by $\mathrm{se}^2$. It carries no information beyond fixing the origin. Fit on the $W-1$ real measurements and report the predicted $B(D_1)$, which should come out near zero, as the diagnostic in place of $z_1$.

    With bfac_model=line the B-factor used for window $w$ is the shrinkage estimate

    $$ B_w^\star = \frac{B_w/\mathrm{se}_w^2 + B(D_w)/\tau^2}{1/\mathrm{se}_w^2 + 1/\tau^2}, \qquad\text{with the limit}\quad \tau \to 0 \ \Longrightarrow\ B_w^\star = B(D_w). \tag{12} $$

    Why shrink. (12) is the posterior mean under $B_w \sim \mathcal N\big(B(D_w), \tau^2\big)$. It gives the line when the windows lie on it, and the window's own value when they do not: a motion-blurred first window keeps its own blur and shows up as a residual $z_1$, while the line is not pulled by it so long as $\tau$ is small. The $\tau\to0$ limit must be written explicitly — (12) is $0/0$ there, and that is precisely the case the premise predicts.

    With bfac_model=free, $B_w^\star = B_w$. The scale is used as fitted, $c_w^\star = c_w$, since it is set by the low shells where the signal is strong; its trend with dose is reported and should be flat.

    4.6 The accumulation¶

    With $a_w^\star(s) = c_w^\star \exp!\big(-B_w^\star k_s^2/4\big)$, the weights and the accumulated halves are

    $$ \omega_w(s) = \frac{a_w^\star(s)\,p_w(s)}{\sum_v a_v^\star(s)\,p_v(s)}, \qquad A_{\mathrm e}(\mathbf q) = \sum_w \omega_w(s)\,E_w(\mathbf q), \qquad A = \tfrac12\big(A_{\mathrm e} + A_{\mathrm o}\big), \tag{13} $$

    with $A_{\mathrm o}$ formed from the odd halves in the same way.

    Why these weights. Minimising $\sum_w \lvert Y_w - a_w S\rvert^2/\sigma_w^2$ over $S$ gives the matched filter under the model, $$\hat S = \frac{\sum_w a_w\,Y_w/\sigma_w^2}{\sum_w a_w^2/\sigma_w^2} ,$$ and writing the delivered map as $A = \bar a\,\hat S$ with $\boldsymbol\omega$ normalised to sum one recovers (13) exactly. Note the weight goes as $a/\sigma^2$, not $a^2/\sigma^2$: weighting each window by its own SSNR — which is what a per-window FSC weighting would do — over-weights the early windows and is not the matched filter. Normalising to sum one rather than to the minimum-variance estimator keeps the output on the scale of a weighted mean of its inputs, with no per-shell amplification, and leaves the residual attenuation explicit and reported.

    Per-shell diagnostics: Kish's effective number of windows (kish_ess in src/utils/math/simple_stat.f90), and the attenuation of the accumulated map relative to window 1,

    $$ n_{\mathrm{eff}}(s) = \frac{\big(\sum_w \omega_w(s)\big)^2}{\sum_w \omega_w(s)^2} = \frac{1}{\sum_w \omega_w(s)^2}, \qquad \bar a(s) = \sum_w \omega_w(s)\,a_w^\star(s) . \tag{14} $$

    Beyond the band, the weights of the last in-band shell continue. On request, the zero-dose extrapolation, applied up to the band:

    $$ S_0(\mathbf q) = \frac{A(\mathbf q)}{\bar a(s)\,\exp!\big(B_0 k_s^2/4\big)} , \tag{15} $$

    where $B_0 < 0$ whenever the line reaches $B_1 = 0$ at $D_1 > 0$.

    Why the halves stay separate. The even output is a mean of even inputs only and the odd of odd inputs only, so the two share no noise and $\mathrm{FSC}(A_{\mathrm e}, A_{\mathrm o})$ is honest: it goes to postprocess or enhance like any half-map pair.

    Why nothing is sharpened here. A coefficient of the output is a weighted mean of the same coefficient over the windows; $\bar a(s)$ is reported so the residual attenuation is known; sharpening stays postprocess's job. The zero-dose map (15) is a per-shell rescaling of $A$, so its FSC is unchanged by it — an interpretation product like enhance's outputs, not a resolution claim, and postprocess would sharpen it a second time.

    5 The control, and what would refute the premise¶

    The standard answer on the same windows, in the same estimator, so the comparison is like for like: the attenuation model of (2) with its parameters fixed by Grant & Grigorieff instead of measured,

    $$ a_w^{\mathrm{GG}}(s) = \exp!\left(-\frac{D_w - D_1}{2\,N_e(k_s)}\right), \qquad c_w = 1, \tag{16} $$

    with $N_e$ from (1) and the doses in the scaled coordinate of section 3.3. The difference $D_w - D_1$ appears because the model's $a$ is relative to window 1 under the gauge (4) and the control must use the same gauge. Its accumulation weights are (13) with $a^{\mathrm{GG}}$ in place of $a^\star$; calc_dose_weights' own normalisation to unit sum of squares differs from (13) by a per-shell scalar, which changes no FSC.

    For every target window $t$ and shell, the cross-half prediction error of predicting its odd half from the even halves and vice versa, whitened by the target's own noise:

    $$ e_t(s) = \frac{p_t(s)}{2\,n_s}\sum_{\mathbf q\in s} \Big( \big\lvert O_t(\mathbf q) - \hat O_t(\mathbf q)\big\rvert^2 + \big\lvert E_t(\mathbf q) - \hat E_t(\mathbf q)\big\rvert^2 \Big), \tag{17} $$

    $$ \hat O_t(\mathbf q) = a_t(s)\,\hat S_{\mathrm e}(\mathbf q), \qquad \hat S_{\mathrm e}(\mathbf q) = \frac{\sum_v a_v(s)\,p_v(s)\,E_v(\mathbf q)}{\sum_v a_v(s)^2\,p_v(s)} , \tag{18} $$

    and $\hat E_t$ symmetrically from the odd halves. The attenuation $a$ in (17)-(18) is $a^\star$ for the model, $a^{\mathrm{GG}}$ for the control, and $1$ for the flat mean — which is the full-dose reconstruction, the model at $\beta = 0$. All three are quadratic forms in the Gram matrices of (7).

    Why the target's own even half may be used to predict its odd half. The two halves are built from disjoint particles, so there is no leakage; excluding it would only discard information.

    Why the whitening. A noise-free prediction leaves only the target's own noise, so $\mathbb E[e_t] = 1$; predicting a window from itself alone gives $\hat O_t = E_t$ and $\mathbb E[e_t] = 2$. The number is therefore comparable across shells, windows and specimens.

    On honesty. The model's error is mildly optimistic, since its $2W+2$ parameters were fitted on the same halves — a handful of numbers from of order $10^8$ coefficients. The error is therefore not held out, and section 6 reports its model-minus-control difference without a significance test. The control's is not optimistic at all: it has no fitted parameter.

    Per resolution band, report the mean paired model-minus-control prediction-error difference. Negative values indicate lower error for the measured model, positive values lower error for the control. This is a descriptive comparison, not an independent validation or a calibrated significance test: attenuation parameters use both halves, and masking correlates coefficients across shells. No z-score, significance threshold or categorical winner is reported. Independent calibration data and reconstruction comparisons are needed to establish a gain.

    The model's weights are delivered either way; the control's accumulation and FSC are written beside them.

    Four independent ways the premise can fail, each with its own readout. Guinier lines curving consistently (the exponent is not 2); the control winning the low shells (the loss has their shape there); $\tau$ large and unstructured (the windows do not lie on a smooth dose curve); rank above one in (9) (the windows differ in more than attenuation).

    7 Cost¶

    The Gram pass of (7) is $2W^2 n_{\mathrm{coeff}}$ complex multiply-adds: at $W = 20$ and box 256 that is $7\times10^9$, seconds with OpenMP over the coefficients. The fits of (9), (10) and (12), the control and every FSC of section 5 are arithmetic on $n_{\mathrm{sh}}$ matrices of $2W \times 2W$. The accumulation (13) is $W\,n_{\mathrm{coeff}}$.

    Memory: the halves as complex single precision, $2W n_{\mathrm{coeff}} \times 8$ B $=$ 2.7 GB at $W = 20$, box 256. The masked halves are held for the Gram pass only and the halves as given for the accumulation only, so the peak is one set; Fourier-crop the halves to the box the band needs when memory is tight.

    8 Standing assumptions and limitations¶

    • Shared poses. The windows are reconstructed with the poses of the full-dose refinement, so the halves of different windows share the alignment errors of their half. Sections 4.2-4.5 and 5 use cross-half products only and are unaffected; the accumulated map is bounded by that refinement's alignment, like any reconstruction from it.
    • $\beta$ is in the scaled dose coordinate of section 3.3: a drop-in for calc_dose_weights, but not a physical specimen constant and not comparable across voltages.
    • Window-centre dose. $D_w$ is the dose at the window's centre, for both the model and the control. A window spans a dose range and its reconstruction averages frames whose attenuation differs; under the line the window's true attenuation is the mean of $\exp(-\beta D k^2/4)$ over its range, $$\exp!\left(-\frac{\beta D_{\mathrm{lo}} k^2}{4}\right)\frac{1 - \exp!\big(-\beta \Delta D k^2/4\big)}{\beta \Delta D k^2/4},$$ not the value at the centre. This matters once $\beta\,\Delta D\,k^2/4$ passes about $0.1$ — a 3 e/Å$^2$ window at 3 Å with $\beta = 4$ gives $0.33$ — and curves a window's Guinier line slightly. Section 4.5 can fit the line with the window-integrated attenuation at no extra cost; section 4.4's per-window B-factor stays the model-free diagnostic. Deferred, and recorded here as a known bias in $\beta$.
    • float16 particle stacks. Stacks are now written unconditionally in MRC mode 12, so every window is quantized identically and the attenuation ratios survive, but the high-resolution noise floor in (6) rises for all of them. To be checked once against a float32 control series, band by band.
    • One state (state 1), disjoint windows, one box and sampling, one even/odd particle partition. The consumer sees only volumes, so it cannot verify the partition itself; the guarantee is the in-run check in reconstruct_motion_fractions, which throws if the selection or the eo labels change between intervals, plus the partition fingerprint in the manifest.
    • Everything is relative to window 1 under the gauge (4). Reported B-factors and attenuations are not absolute.
    • Deterministic.

    Settled with the maintainer, 3 November 2026: raw _unfil halves as the input (with refine_motion_model adjusted to make that pair genuinely unfiltered and unmasked, leaving _even/_odd and therefore the exported FSC matrix untouched); a producer-written manifest for the dose; dropping the window before an overlapping last one; state 1 only; the band rule of section 4.1; detector noise independent across windows with structure error shared; $\beta$ in the scaled dose coordinate; window-centre dose; window 1 excluded from the line fit.

    • The exponent. $a_w = c_w\exp!\big(-B_w k^\gamma/4\big)$ with $\gamma$ a third global parameter ($\gamma = 2$ is the B-factor, $1.665$ the Grant & Grigorieff shape).     • The scale. With equal windows and one reconstruction normalisation, $c_w$ should be 1 throughout. If the real series confirms it, fix it and drop a parameter per window.
    • automsk. Whether the first release supports an automask, and if so which map it is built from.
    • The independent count. Resolved as neither $\langle M\rangle$ nor $\langle M^2\rangle$: the plane formula of section 4.2, with a residual of about 10% recorded in section 11.1.
    • bfac_model. Whether it is a CLI key at all, and whether the name reads too much like the existing bfac sharpening parameter.

    • Per-window doses for unequal windows and EER fractions, beyond what the manifest already carries.
    • Pre-exposure. Dose received before the first frame shifts every $D_w$ by a constant: the slope $\beta$ is unaffected, but the zero-dose extrapolation (15) then refers to the start of the movie, not to the undamaged specimen.

    11 Consumer implementation¶

    simple_dose_series (src/utils/filter/) is one encapsulated class holding the statistics, the fits, the three accumulation schemes (model, control, flat), the paired cross-half comparison and the zero-dose rescaling.     11.1 Error model¶

    The errors of $a_w(s)$ use the noise-only pair variance (8a), not the sample variance of the cross products, which holds the structure's own power fluctuation; the sandwich covariance (9a), since the projection of each window's noise onto the structure is shared by every pair holding it; and the independent count of section 4.2. Masked errors remain about 10% small, uniformly over shells and noise levels; about half is the coherence of the noise's projection onto the structure, the rest is unexplained.

    11.2 Guinier fits¶

    Levenberg-Marquardt with step acceptance replaces undamped Gauss-Newton, which diverged on a window carrying no signal. A fit that fails, or whose scale does not clear the detection gate of section 11.8, is rejected: its window is left out of the line and of the model's weights, the model table marks it used 0 and the log says so.

    11.3 Excess scatter about the dose line¶

    $\tau^2$ is the DerSimonian-Laird estimate with the trace of a weighted line, $\mathbb E[Q] = (k-2) + \tau^2\,\mathrm{tr}(P)$, $\mathrm{tr}(P) = \sum w - \mathrm{tr}\big[(X^{\mathsf T}WX)^{-1}X^{\mathsf T}W^2X\big]$, $X = [\mathbf 1, D]$ (line_tau2). An intercept-only trace left $\tau^2$ low by about $(k-2)/(k-1)$. The untruncated estimate is unbiased under the variance model; clamping it at zero and taking the root bias $\tau$ with few windows.

    11.4 Coefficient count¶

    The count behind the unit-mean-power rescaling is formed in double precision: as a default integer it overflowed past about box 590 with 20 windows.

    11.5 Input guards¶

    dose_filter3D stops when the band's noise is below $10^{-6}$ of the half-map power (one map given as both halves) and when no window after the first can be fitted. scripts/make_dose_series.py refuses the same file, identical data, and halves whose difference is below $10^{-6}$ of their power.

    11.8 Window errors across shells, and the detection gate¶

    The mask correlates coefficients across neighbouring shells as well as within one, so a Guinier fit smooth over many shells has a variance inflated by the sum of the correlations between shells: by Parseval and the projection-slice theorem, per axis $a$,

    $$ F = \frac{1}{3}\sum_a \frac{n_a \sum_{\mathbf x} M^4}{\sum_{\perp a}\big(\sum_a M^2\big)^2}, $$

    1 without a mask, $(N/t + 2)/3$ for a slab of thickness $t$, about $4N/(3D)$ for a ball of diameter $D$. shell_correlation computes it beside shell_independence, and both variances of each window's Guinier fit are multiplied by it, which carries into the shrinkage, the residuals $z_w$ and the rejection gate. A window's scale must clear five of its errors to count as measured (C_DETECT); the shells entering the log-domain start of the fit keep twice their error.

    11.9 Reports against the sharpest window¶

    Window 1 is the gauge of the fit ($a_1 = 1$, $c_1 = 1$, $B_1 = 0$), but early beam-induced motion can leave it blurrier than the next window, whose $B$ then reads negative against it. The model table and the log report against the fitted window of least $B^\star$: $c_w/c_{\mathrm{ref}}$, $B_w - B^\star_{\mathrm{ref}}$, $B(D_w) - B^\star_{\mathrm{ref}}$, $B^\star_w - B^\star_{\mathrm{ref}}$, $B_0 - B^\star_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and $\ln c_0 - \ln c_{\mathrm{ref}}$, with the errors of section 12.1; the header names the reference. The shift leaves every ratio between windows, $\beta$, $\gamma$, $\tau$, the weights, the maps and the dose curve as they were; the fit, the attenuation and decay tables and the curve file keep window 1 as their gauge. The line's prediction $B(D)$ can still be negative for early windows when the fading accelerates.

    12 Re-extraction under the measured curve¶

    reextract with extractfrommov=yes dose_weighting=measured dose_curve=<outvol>_curve.txt weights the movie fractions of every particle by the measured curve instead of Grant and Grigorieff's.

    The contrast line.     The model's critical exposure. Under both lines the model fades as $\exp(-(\gamma + \beta k^2/4)D)$, so its critical exposure is $N_e = 1/(2\gamma + \beta k^2/2)$.     Per fraction. Fraction $f$ of a movie with physical dose $d$ per fraction sits at the scaled centre dose $x_f = (f-\tfrac12)\,d/s_{\mathrm{kV}}$; a window's centre is the mean of its fractions'. Between fitted windows $\ln c$ and $B$ are linear in dose, before the first window the first segment continues, and past the last the two lines' slopes continue from it, clamped at zero so that an unmeasured fraction never gains on the last measured one. Under the lines, equal-width windows need no correction for averaging over their fractions: each carries the factor $\sinh(n\lambda\delta/2)/(n\sinh(\lambda\delta/2))$, $\lambda = \gamma + \beta k^2/4$, which cancels against window 1, so the slopes measured at window centres are the per-fraction ones. With curvature, interpolating between centres errs by $\tfrac12 B''\,\mathrm{Var}(x)$.

    Weights. $\omega_f(k) = c_f e^{-B_f k^2/4} / \sqrt{\sum_g c_g^2 e^{-B_g k^2/2}}$, of unit squared sum at every frequency like the Grant and Grigorieff mode, so the summed noise spectrum stays flat (image%apply_frame_bfactor_weights, beside apply_dose_weighing). The curve was measured on plain sums of fractions, so it also replaces the motion-correction frame weights: the weighted frames are summed equally.

    Checks. The curve holds for one voltage and one dose rate, the producer's rule: a micrograph whose motion model differs in either stops the run. A micrograph without a motion model stops it too, instead of falling back to the integrated micrograph, which carries Grant and Grigorieff's weights. dose_curve is made absolute before the run directory is entered, and the distributed master reads the file before it schedules a job.

    12.1 Uncertainty and comparison corrections¶

    The joint sandwich covariance of the shell attenuations is retained. For the through-origin critical-exposure regression, with fixed fitted weights, the slope error is propagated as

    $$ \operatorname{Var}(\hat m)=g^T\Sigma_a g,\qquad g_w=\frac{w_w(D_w-D_1)}{a_w\sum_v w_v(D_v-D_1)^2}. $$

    This includes the common noisy reference. The neighboring-window log-ratio error includes the corresponding negative twice-covariance term. First-order errors still assume the retained fitting observations and their weights are fixed; the two-error decay gate now uses the propagated error.

    Guinier B-factor covariance follows from each fit's sensitivity to its shell attenuations, with the existing cross-shell variance inflation. The reporting shift uses the sensitivity of the reference's shrunk B-factor to the measured B-factors, including the dose-line fit. The reported variance is that of $B_w-B_{\mathrm{ref}}^\star$, with line weights and excess scatter held fixed. Only a genuine identical-estimator difference has exactly zero error; the reference's measured B residual generally does not.

    The comparison table's final column is now error_model_minus_control, the paired mean prediction-error difference in whitened units. The log reports the same quantity per band and makes no significance-based model/control/tie verdict. These comparisons remain descriptive until independent validation and a calibrated covariance model are available.

    Both stored curve voltage and movie voltage must be finite; movie dose per fraction must be finite and positive before compatibility comparisons.
