    The Pose Student¶

    Problem¶

    Given a set of particle images whose poses have been estimated by the probabilistic 3D search (refine3D, refine=prob), predict for a particle the search has never seen a projection direction, a cone about it that holds most of the posterior, and a confidence, so that the search can align that particle inside the cone instead of over the whole sphere. The student proposes; the search decides. It never writes an in-plane rotation or a shift: those come from the neighbourhood search it hands the cone to, with the search's own machinery (the polar shift map, the refinement, the local refinement).

    The quantity estimated is a distribution over the mirror pairs of the student's grid, modulo the point group, for one state. The grid is the search's reference spiral of nspace (1,000) directions, whose first half holds the unmirrored directions of the asymmetric unit and whose second half their mirrors (build_refspiral; the pairs and their frame angles are what oris%get_mirror_pairs returns, the pairing the opt-in mirr_proj=yes reference extraction uses). A direction and its mirror view are related by the Euler angles (euler_mirror), and the projection of one is the reflected image of the other: the student classifies the 500 pairs and leaves the choice within a pair to the search, which resolves it with a cross-correlation. The pairing is the three-dimensional Friedel symmetry seen in projection: a real volume's transform obeys F(-k) = conj F(k), so the central section for the antipodal direction is the same plane in a reflected in-plane frame. A reflection of an image is itself a reflection of its Fourier plane, θ → -θ, not a conjugation; conjugation enters only because the half ring stores [0, π), and Friedel brings -θ back as F(k, -θ) = conj F(k, π - θ), so on the half ring a reflection is an index reversal with a conjugation, and in the angular spectrum of the full ring the reflection θ → π - θ maps F(n) to conj F(n). For the cyclic point groups, c1 included, the unmirrored half is the northern hemisphere (polar angle below 90°); for the dihedral and platonic groups it is cut in the azimuth instead. Everything continuous in the pose (the in-plane angle, the shift, the direction within the cone) is left to the search as well.

    Model¶

    The input is the particle's polar Fourier half ring exactly as the search holds it: pftsz angles over half a turn, the rings of the band kfromto(1) to box smpd / lp (about 25 rings at a 15 Å band for a box of 128 pixels at 3 Å), the other half of the ring being the Friedel mate. The ring is multiplied by the particle's CTF (the cross term of a CTF-free model with the observation, as the search's objective and flex's Gram both correlate), each shell scaled by the square root of the shell weight k / sigma2(k) the search scores with, and the band scaled to unit weighted power; the ring mean of the CTF squared travels beside the tensor. Nothing is restored (no phase flip, no Wiener filter) and the phases are kept. A shift is applied, when the training needs it, through the search's own phase ramps; an in-plane rotation by one grid step is a cyclic shift of the angular axis whose wrap conjugates what passes the ring's end.

    The network's half ring is the calculator's capped at the band. A ring of Fourier radius k holds angular frequencies up to about k, so a half ring needs π k_max angles. The calculator's pftsz follows the mask radius instead and oversamples a low-pass band: 220 angles on bgal (256 pixels at 1.275 Å) where its 15 Å band of 22 rings needs 69. The network takes at most NET_ANGLE_OVERSAMPLING (2) times π k_max angles, even (polar_feats_net_pftsz). Each ring is resampled exactly onto them: the Friedel-extended ring's angular harmonics |j| < pftsz_net are kept, as two precomputed real matrices on the real and imaginary channels. The scale keeps the band's unit weighted norm. The teacher, the calculator and the model's pftsz stay the calculator's, so predict_poses checks the geometry as before. Where the cap is not below pftsz, as on the medium assembly (128 pixels at 3 Å, 160 angles, cap 164), nothing changes.

    Measured on 2026-11-11 against six base seeds, three seeds per cap, at 24 threads on the Dell: - bgal at twice π k_max (140 angles): within 6° 0.876 against the base's 0.869 (range 0.853 to 0.880), truth in the cone 0.959 against 0.957, seeded 0.426 against 0.417. The designs train in 15.8 s against 32.8 s, and prediction's forward pass takes 1.33 s against 2.37 s. - 1.5 times (104 angles): the reads hold on bgal, but T1000's seeded fraction falls to the edge of its range (0.216 against 0.215 to 0.272). - 1 time (bgal 70, T1000 82): the student loses on both sets (T1000 within 6° 0.756 against 0.784, seeded 0.191 against 0.242). This is the loss the low-pass crop of box_crop showed (doc/implementation_notes/rejected/pose_student_working_grid.md), so it came from the angles.

    The network is HHCNN, the Harmonic Hermitian convolutional neural network: Hermitian because every layer commutes with conjugation on the half ring with the conjugate wrap, so the network is a model of the Fourier transform of a real image; harmonic because the readout's class templates live in the angular harmonic domain of the polar transform and the rotation search is the cross-correlation over the cyclic shifts through the angular Fourier transform; convolutional on the polar Fourier representation. It is not Worrall's Harmonic Networks (circular-harmonic steerable filters): the convolution kernels here are ordinary real kernels in the ring-and-angle domain. Three blocks of a 3 by 3 circular convolution along the angle and the ring with real kernels applied alike to the real and imaginary channels (the only linear map of a complex signal that commutes with conjugation), a bias on the modulus and the gate z relu(|z| + b) / |z| that mixes the two parts only through the conjugation-invariant modulus, and an average over ring pairs. The kernels are symmetric along the angle (their two angle offsets share one weight), so a reflected input gives reflected feature maps; training keeps them symmetric bit for bit, the gradients of the two offsets being summed onto the shared weight. The first block's filters are CTF-free and each response is divided by the filter's energy under the particle's ring-averaged CTF squared and the shell weights, so that the CTF's envelope never reaches the deeper blocks as structure and a shell near a zero enters at no weight.

    The readout is a learned matched-filter bank with a rotation search. A pointwise map with real weights reduces the last block's channels to eight, an average over ring bands reduces the rings to eight, and the band-limited angular spectrum of the full ring is taken to order 16: its even orders from the real part of the half ring and its odd orders from the imaginary part, which is what the Hermitian extension gives. Every direction class owns a template in that domain (eight by eight by sixteen complex values, the order-0 term real), initialised as the spectrum of the reprojection of the pair's unmirrored direction through the untrained network and then trained with the rest. The score of a class is the maximum, over the ring's rotation grid and over the map and its reflection, of the real Hermitian inner product of the particle's spectrum with the template,

    S_c(r) = (1/N) [ Re P_c(0) + 2 sum_{m=1..16} Re( P_c(m) e^{2 pi i m r / N} ) ],
P_c(m) = sum_{channels, bands} F(m) conj(T_c(m)),

    with N the rotation grid's length, and for the reflected map F(m) replaced by its conjugate (the reflection of the ring in the angular-harmonic domain); the logits are a learned scale times the maxima, the posterior is their softmax, and the gradient flows through the maximising rotation and, where the reflection won, through the conjugate spectrum. Rotation equivariance is exact on the search's rotation grid: rotating the particle by one step leaves every class score unchanged and moves the maximising rotation by one step (backwards for a class the reflection scored). With the symmetric kernels the network is invariant to the mirror on that grid: a particle and its reflection, mirrored by the calculator's mirror_ref_bank at any frame angle on the rotation grid, score alike against every class. The readout keeps the angular structure that tells views apart; an earlier head that pooled rotation-invariant statistics (the angular mean of the real part, the mean modulus, harmonic magnitudes) discarded it, which is why it was replaced.

    Training¶

    The teacher is the probabilistic search itself: for every training particle, the row of the dense probability table at the student's grid and band, filled as the production search fills it against the training set's map, each direction scored at its drawn in-plane rotation with the best candidates refined in shift. The soft target is the row's tempered posterior t_c = exp[-tau (L_c - L_min)] / sum_j exp[-tau (L_j - L_min)], tau the search's tempering factor times the particle's own temperature; the whole row is kept, so a broad posterior stays broad, and it is folded onto the mirror pairs (a pair's target is the sum of its two directions') before the loss. The teacher scores all 1,000 directions; only the student's classes are pairs. The shell weights the rows were whitened with are frozen in the model.

    The loss is the cross-entropy against those soft rows, minimised with Adam (rate 1e-3, decoupled weight decay 1e-5) on mini-batches of at most 256, every row shifted each epoch by a keyed draw in a disk of three times the search's centring width (the training's shift tolerance), for at most 30 epochs and stopped after 3 epochs without a held-out improvement (the best epoch comes at 3 to 7 on the fixed sets, its improvements at most 2 epochs apart); the best epoch's weights are kept.

    The training step is built so that the same weights come out at any thread count, bit for bit. The mini-batch is cut into chunks set by its size alone (about 96 chunks), computed in parallel and reduced in chunk order over slices of the weights, also in parallel, with the Adam update in the same pass. The blocks' convolutions are im2col and a matrix product; blocks 2 and 3 build their im2col matrix in tiles of 128 columns, which stay in a core's cache. The readout's class products, its rotation scores and their gradients are matrix products too; the rotation search scores every rotation of the grid (a coarse-to-fine search was measured and changed the winning rotation too often for its gain), and the templates' gradient is one product over the whole batch. Every matrix product takes the same arithmetic whatever the team: sgemm where the build's BLAS stays on the calling thread inside OpenMP teams (SIMPLE_BLAS_TEAM_SAFE). Otherwise the build and the shape choose between explicit loops down the columns of the result and the matmul intrinsic, gfortran's single-threaded product, which is compiled into its runtime and so runs optimised even in a Debug build. On the Dell's Arrow Lake core, one thread, the optimised loops ran at 30 to 54 GFLOP/s on the network's products against matmul's 6 to 43. The exception is a transposed product of fewer than 16 rows (the readout's four): there the loops ran at 6 and matmul at 37. In a Debug build the loops ran at 4 to 5 and matmul at 9 to 63. So a Release build takes the loops except on those few-row products, and a Debug build takes matmul everywhere, transposing an operand first where matmul of a transposed section would run at a sixth of the speed.

    Held-out micrographs (every tenth of the selected particles' stacks in order of their numbers when they lie on ten or more, otherwise every tenth selected particle; fewer than ten selected particles are refused before training, so that neither cohort is empty) serve three decisions, and the test half is never touched before the model is frozen: early stopping on the held-out loss; the choice of the design, where the cheapest (16 channels) is kept unless 32 or then 64 channels recover clearly more held-out seeds (the paired difference over micrographs, resampled a thousand times by micrograph; kept when the 5% quantile of the gain is above zero; 64 channels are tried only from 10,000 training rows: below, they rarely beat 32 and cost two and a half times as much); and the confidence threshold.

    A seed is recovered when the teacher's direction, or its mirror partner, lies inside the student's cone, the cone being within the cap of 64 directions and within the route's bound (below). The threshold is the confidence of the longest confidence-sorted prefix of held-out particles in which at least 95% of the seeds are recovered, a prefix ending only where the confidence drops (prediction takes every particle at or above the threshold, so a prefix inside a group of equal confidences would seed the whole group); above every confidence when no prefix qualifies, so that a student that cannot be trusted seeds nobody. The threshold and the bound are written so that they read back bit for bit, and learn_pose_student reads the model back and checks that it seeds the calibrated held-out particles.

    Prediction and the hand-off¶

    The readout's pose. The readout already estimates the in-plane rotation and the mirror: it scores each class at every rotation of the polar grid, against the template and its reflection, and only the best score enters the softmax. Read out for the mode's pair, the best rotation ang gives a full pose with no search: the unmirrored direction with e3 = 360 - ang (the search's convention for its rotation index), or, when the reflection scored, the mirror partner with e3 = ang + psi, psi the pair's frame angle (the reflection mirror_ref_bank applies; the HHCNN tester pins it at 0, 75 and 180 degrees). The template's frame is the unmirrored direction's reprojection at e3 = 0, where its training starts. The network does not estimate the shift: it is trained to tolerate it. Given the map (vol1), predict_poses registers each particle's shift against the map's reprojection at the read-out pose, its direction and rotation fixed: a 5 x 5 grid over 25 Å about the stored shift, then L-BFGS-B, the search's own fixed-rotation shift search (pftc_shsrch_grad), with the references and sigma2 prepared as learn_pose_student prepares them. The table's last five columns are the pose and the shift (the stored shift without vol1); the route and the records do not use them.

    The posterior is over mirror pairs: each pair's mass is placed on its unmirrored direction of the grid, the mode and the smallest set holding 99% of the mass (the cone) are taken over the pairs, and the cone's radius is that set's spread about the mode, a member's distance being the smaller of its own and its mirror's. The direction written is the mode pair's unmirrored direction; which of the pair the particle shows is the search's to decide.

    The route sends a particle to the neighbourhood search when its cone is within the cap, its radius is within the bound (the dense search's cell: prob_athres or the grid's spacing, whichever is larger, recorded in the model with the threshold calibrated against it) and its confidence is at or above the threshold; every other particle takes the dense search. The seeded particle's record carries the direction as its orientation and, in numbered slots of the particle's record, the cone's radius, the confidence and the route. With nparts, predict_poses is the master of that many parts: given vol1 it makes the even/odd halves, sigma2 and the reprojection model first, which the parts only read; it merges the parts' records, joins their tables in part order and writes the project once, the shared-memory run's result byte for byte (the pose_student workflow test checks it at two parts, registering). A part is given its range as every master hands it (part, nparts, fromp, top, with outfile); its tables carry its number, it writes its range's records to outfile, never the project, and a range without a selected particle writes its records unchanged. Training stays in one process. Prediction refuses a model of another geometry than the project's: the half ring, the box, the crop (box_crop) and the CTF treatment exactly, the sampling (smpd) within 1e-4 Å and the mask (mskdiam) within 0.01 Å, the header's decimals; it does not resample.

    The search (refine3D refine=prob_neigh prob_neigh_mode=geom) scores, for a particle with a cone, every reference within the cone about the stored direction and every reference within the same cone about its mirror (euler_mirror), finds the in-plane rotation and the shift as it does for any neighbourhood, and takes the better pose as it takes any; a particle without a cone runs the base geom search, the directions within athres_cont of its stored direction (the coarse cell until 2026-11-09), so a project without student records runs the base code path byte for byte.

    What the reads found¶

    The split-half protocol (view-balanced halves, the training half's map as the references, the student trained on one half and judged on the other against the standard machinery's alignment and, on simulated sets, the truth) gave these results on the Dell in November 2026; the measurements are in the implementation note's Progress record.

    • On centred particles of the simulated Ribosembly medium assembly at 15 Å the student seeds 46% of the test half with cones about 8° wide, those particles end where the dense search puts them (99% within 6° of the truth), the prediction costs 0.56 ms per particle against 2.2 ms for the coarse fill it is trained on, and the cone search costs 0.6 of the dense search. On the easy simulated set (vcrnapol, SNR 0.1) it seeds 86%.
    • The student is not shift-invariant: a 2 px shift changes its posterior by a total variation near 0.7, and on uncentred particles (no prepared shift) it seeds about 1%. The shift is the open question of the design.
    • On the real sets at 15 Å (trpv1, beta-galactosidase, streptavidin, the flagellar export gate FlhB, two CESPED entries) one dense search from nothing is itself a broad teacher (6 to 69% of its directions within 6° of the reference alignment), the student's confident cones are few (0 to 4% seeded), but reconstructing from its directions alone, with the cone search for the in-plane rotation and shift and one local-refinement iteration, gives maps as good as or better than that dense search's on centred or large sets.

    • A tenth of the training rows can recover more held-out seeds than the whole half: the training budget's early stopping is tuned for small sets.

    • Classifying the 500 mirror pairs instead of the 1,000 directions, with the mirror left to the search, costs nothing and gains with more training data. On the centred test half of the medium assembly at 15 Å, the student trained on the whole training half puts 94.0% of the particles within 6° of the truth against 91.5% for the 1,000-class student, with 99.5% within 12°; its cones are tighter (a 90th percentile of 14 directions against 42), and 97% of the particles clear the confidence of 0.99 with the truth inside the cone for 99.7% of them. From 1,000 training particles it puts 85.5% within 6° against 81.7%; from 200 the two are level (about 70%). One seed per point.

    Rationale¶

    The student is a classifier over a discrete grid because the posterior over directions is multimodal at low signal-to-noise and the references are discrete; a softmax over the grid holds competing directions with their masses, which a regressed rotation cannot. Its classes are mirror pairs because the mirror is a relation of the Euler angles that the search resolves for free, with a cross-correlation of the two candidates: a network that had to tell a view from its mirror would spend its capacity on a distinction the search makes exactly, and would learn half as much data per class. It hands over a cone rather than a pose because the search already has the better continuous estimator for the in-plane angle, the shift and the direction within the cone, with the references, the shift map and the refinement. It is trained on the search's own posterior rows rather than on hard labels so that it inherits the search's uncertainty and never claims more than the teacher resolves: where the teacher is broad, the student seeds nobody and the search runs as it would have.

    Implementation¶

    src/main/learn/ holds four modules: - simple_polar_feats: the input tensor, the augmentation, a disk cache of the rows' tensors. - simple_hhcnn: HHCNN, that is the Hermitian blocks, the template readout, the trainer and the weights file. - simple_polar_student: the student's core, shared with the 2D class student. - It holds the network, the features, the band, the training on soft class targets, the prediction, the threshold and the training record. - It also owns the model file's common part: a text header with the weights beside it. - The header names the weights by their file name alone, resolved against the header's directory and never the working directory. - The reader refuses weights whose shape, seed or weight count differ from the header's. - The header's records may be of any length, as the shell weights of a wide band need. - simple_pose_student: the 3D student around one core. - It holds the grid, its mirror pairs as the core's classes, the folded cone and the read-out pose. - It writes the grid's keys after the core's (model version 4).

    The strategy helpers both students use (the held-out cohort, the resampled design comparison, the confidence threshold, the geometry check, a part's range, a file's hash) are in simple_strategy_student_common. The constants both students' programs set (the grid, the caps, the training designs, the calibration, the shift registration, the validation's settings and file names) are in src/defs/simple_defs_hhcnn.f90. The programs learn_pose_student and predict_poses are strategy routines on the caller's (params, build) with thin private commanders; the band is lp, the grid nspace, the model infile and fname; no other key. The per-particle cone of the neighbourhood search lives in simple_eul_prob_tab_neigh. The design, the rulings that shaped it and every measurement are in doc/implementation_notes/completed/learned_pose_state_assignment.md.

    Validation and benchmarks¶

    validate_pose_student (simple_exec, developer visibility) takes solve3D's inputs on a project with a 2D solution; its nsample is the training set: a class-balanced greedy sample of the selected classes, best 2D scores first. It runs solve3D on that sample with nsample at the sample's size, so every iteration searches every training particle (no fractional update, no trailing reconstruction). It trains the student on solve3D's poses and map at 15 Å, with a shift disk of three times 2 Å since every particle is centred by the 2D or 3D solution, and predicts every selected particle: the training rows at the teacher's poses, the others at their 2D shifts. The report (validate_pose_student_report.txt, also in the log) gives:

    • the model's threshold and held-out recovery;
    • the seeded and capped fractions, the confidence and the cone radius of all, training and unseen particles;
    • the student's mode against the teacher on the training particles;
    • class consistency: the distance of every particle's direction to its 2D class's teacher medoid, the teacher's own spread being the baseline;
    • the training rows' shift change from the 2D solution to the teacher's, against the training's shift disk;
    • the readout's full pose against the teacher's on the training particles (the direction with the readout's mirror, and the in-plane angle);
    • the registered shifts against the teacher's, beside the 2D shifts;
    • six maps by reconstruct3D from poses with no orientation search: the unseen particles at the student's poses with their 2D shifts and with the registered shifts (their FSC against the teacher's map is cross-validated: the two share no particle), the training particles likewise, the training particles at the student's poses and the teacher's shifts, and the teacher's own poses as the control; each with its FSC against the teacher's map and between its halves;
    • the phases' wall times with the per-particle costs.

    The phases' own records are BENCH/VALIDATE_POSE_STUDENT_BENCH.txt, solve3D's BENCH/ in its directory, learn/BENCH/POSE_STUDENT_LEARN_BENCH.txt and predict/BENCH/POSE_STUDENT_PREDICT_BENCH.txt.

    POSE_STUDENT_LEARN splits learn_pose_student into the rows (setup, particles, the teacher's table fill with its fill profile, the features) and, per design, the training into batch assembly, gradients (the parallel region), reduction with the Adam steps and the held-out evaluation, with the steps, the samples per second, the held-out prediction and the design choice. POSE_STUDENT_PREDICT splits predict_poses into its phases and the per-particle steps in thread-seconds (features, forward pass, cone, the reference error, ruling R2's shift check) with the team's utilisation and load balance. test=pose_student_bench (simple_test_exec, no CTest entry) times the HHCNN kernels on synthetic tensors per ring count and width: the forward pass split into the blocks and the readout, the backward pass, a training step of 256 samples at one thread and at the team, the team's prediction throughput and the step's identity across threads; the arithmetic rates of the blocks' and the readout's products, the chunks per step and the threads they keep busy; and the blocks' other kernels (im2col, the gate, the pooling, their backward passes) at one thread (BENCH/POSE_STUDENT_KERNELS_BENCH.txt).
