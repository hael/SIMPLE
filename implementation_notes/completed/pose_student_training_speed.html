    Training speed of the pose student, and the review's corrections¶

    Status: completed, 2026-11-10 (the run student_speed, phases 0 to 6, uncommitted for review; the report is pose_student_training_speed_report.md beside this note). Planned 2026-11-09. Hans set the priority ("everything we can to speed up training of the student; priority number one") and asked for an autonomous run; this note is its plan. The same day a code review of the pose student (learned_pose_state_assignment_review.md, at 519161ff3) found seven open problems; Hans asked for them to be addressed in the same run. The run is student_speed on the Dell (section 9). Nothing in it is committed; the run hands over one working-tree diff.

    The pose student (doc/algorithms/pose_student.md) is a small network trained per dataset on the posterior rows of the probabilistic search. It proposes a direction, a cone, an in-plane angle and a mirror for particles the search has not seen. Its training is now the slowest part of every use: on bgal, 1,500 training particles take 180 to 540 s to train against 4 to 15 s to predict 2,756 particles, and on the review's macOS build the registered workflow test pose_student timed out at 2,400 s at eight threads while still training. The run first corrects what the review found, then makes training as fast as it can be made on this machine without changing what the student learns beyond the run-to-run spread of its own reads.

    1. What is measured, and the yardsticks¶

    Speed is the wall time of learn_pose_student on fixed inputs at 24 threads, split as its bench record POSE_STUDENT_LEARN splits it (rows, then per design: batch assembly, gradients, reduction and optimiser steps, held-out evaluation), and the kernel benchmark test=pose_student_bench (per ring count and width: forward, readout, backward, a training step at one thread and at the team, prediction throughput, the training step's identity across threads).

    Quality is read on fixed teachers, so that a change in the student is not drowned by a change in the teacher (solve3D is unseeded and takes a different stage path from run to run):

    • The truth sets. The medium assembly of the Ribosembly benchmark, kept by the pose_student run under /data2/agent_runs/pose_student/scratch/keep/medium: the training half T (24,000 particles) with its map from the truth poses, its subsets T200, T1000, T5000, and the centred test half S with the truth poses. The student trains on a subset at 15 Å and shift_sigma=2, predicts S, and pred_stats.awk reads the folded mode against the truth: within 6° and 12°, the truth in the cone, the cone sizes, the confidence. This is the generalisation read: S was never seen.
    • The bgal fixed teacher. The training project and the teacher's map of validation run 5 (/data2/agent_runs/validate_ps/run5/1_validate_pose_student): the held-out recovery from the model, and the training rows' agreement with the teacher from a prediction on a copy of the training project (its rows carry the teacher's poses); the seeded fraction of the unseen particles from a prediction on a copy of the run's all-particles project (predict_poses writes into the project it predicts, so the run's own copy no longer holds pure teacher poses).
    • The spread. Training draws its batches and augmentations from keys of the run's seed (SIMPLE_SEED); three seeds per set measure the spread of every read. A change is judged against that spread, never against one pair of runs. The review's corrections change what is calibrated (the threshold over confidence ties, the held-out split), so the spread is measured twice: at the base (phase 0) and after the corrections (phase 1), and the speed phases are judged against the corrected one.
    • End to end. validate_pose_student on bgal at the end (phase 6), with the timings table and the maps from the student's poses.

    The training step must be bit-identical between one thread and any team, on every supported BLAS configuration (the network's contract; the review's first finding shows it is not yet). Results may change against the base where a change regroups floating-point sums; they are then judged on the reads.

    3. Where the time goes¶

    • The schedule trains too much. Three widths are trained to the end one after the other, and the widest costs as much as the other two together, or more; early stopping waits 250 steps (15 epochs at 17 batches per epoch) after the best epoch.
    • Small batches starve the team. A mini-batch is cut into chunks of 8 samples (CNN_CHUNK), computed in parallel and reduced in chunk order (the reduction that makes the step identical on any thread count). bgal's batch of 84 (a sixteenth of the training rows) gives 11 chunks: at most 11 of 24 threads work. A batch of 256 gives 32 chunks, two rounds of which the second keeps 8 threads busy. The kernel benchmark's one-thread to team ratio of 8 to 13 on 24 cores is the symptom. The chunk loop is already scheduled dynamically, so the eight performance cores take more chunks than the sixteen efficiency cores.
    • The reduction and the Adam step are serial. Every chunk's gradient (the templates are most of the 160,000 to 920,000 weights) is summed in double on one thread, then one Adam update: 5% of a step now, a larger share once the gradients are faster.
    • The readout's rotation search. Per sample, each of the 500 classes is scored against the template and against its reflection: the product of 17 angular harmonics over the readout's channels and ring bands, then the score at every rotation of the half ring's grid (2 x pftsz rotations, 440 at bgal's 220 angles). The rotation scores are about 30 million floating-point operations per sample, in hand-written loops at roughly 8 GFLOP/s (one core's single-precision peak is about 20 times that); they are 50 to 70% of the forward pass at 16 channels. Each class's score is a trigonometric polynomial of order 16 in the rotation angle, so it is smooth on the grid's scale.
    • The blocks are im2col plus sgemm (OpenBLAS on the calling thread) where the build's BLAS stays on the calling thread inside OpenMP teams (SIMPLE_BLAS_TEAM_SAFE, set by cmake/Dependencies.cmake; on for the Dell's bundled OpenMP OpenBLAS), and plain triple loops inside a team otherwise (the review's macOS build with the system OpenBLAS). Their arithmetic rate is unmeasured. They dominate at 64 channels.

    4. The changes, in the order of the phases¶

    4.1 The review's findings (phase 1)¶

    Each finding is corrected with the regression coverage the review asks for; its section in the review states the evidence and the expected behaviour. The run records each finding's resolution (the change, the test, the evidence) in a closing section of the review file.

    • PS01, the training arithmetic changes with the team (P1). blas_ok() lets sgemm run outside an active team and selects the explicit loops inside one when the BLAS is not known to be team-safe; a one-thread region is not an active team, so one thread and a team of four take different arithmetic. The arithmetic path must depend on the build only: with a team-safe BLAS the products always go to sgemm, without one always to the same loops, whatever the team. The HHCNN tester's identity check keeps an actual team of four (it must not pass by running serially), and the run checks it on two builds: the Dell's default (team-safe) and a variant configured without the definition (section 9.1). A build without a team-safe BLAS then trains on the loops everywhere: their speed is part of phase 5.
    • PS02, a model can load another network (P2). The weights file is resolved against the header's directory, never the working directory; the reader checks the binary's shape (and the seed and version the header records) against the header and stops on a mismatch. Tests: two model directories with the same default names, a foreign weights file in the working directory, a relocated model loading its own network.
    • PS03, prediction workers ignore their part (P2). A worker of predict_poses derives its range from part and nparts through the machinery the distributed workers already use (how the qsys job descriptions hand fromp and top to a part), or the program's contract requires the range explicitly; a part whose range holds no selected particle writes the empty contribution the merge expects instead of stopping. Tests: disjoint coverage by the parts, uneven ranges, a part of only unselected rows.
    • PS04, prediction accepts other physical geometry (P2). Prediction checks the model's recorded sampling (smpd), crop (box_crop) and mask (mskdiam) against the project's within stated serialisation tolerances, besides the half ring, the box and the CTF treatment it checks now; no resampling is implied. Tests: differing sampling, crop and mask with equal box and pftsz.
    • PS05, calibration and prediction select different particles (P2). The threshold is taken only at the end of a group of equal confidences, so that conf >= threshold at prediction selects exactly the calibrated prefix; it is written with enough digits to round-trip (the F8.5 form does not), and the accepted population is recomputed after reading the model back. Tests: tied confidences (the review's 18-and-2 example), values next to the serialisation's boundary, the round trip.
    • PS06, a training set can have no held-out cohort (P2). The held-out split is a deterministic choice over the selected particles' groups (stacks, or particles of a single stack) that leaves both cohorts non-empty, not stkind modulo 10; a selection too small to split is refused before training with a clear message; the resampled design comparison handles zero groups. Tests: two to nine stacks, selections without a tenth stack, small single-stack selections.
    • PS07, wide-band models cannot be read back (P2). The reader takes records of any length (or the shell weights move to a form without the limit, such as one value per line or the weights file); tests: exact round trips on both sides of 227 and 228 shells.

    The model file may change for PS02, PS05 and PS07 (its version goes up); release 4 keeps no compatibility with earlier models. After the corrections the base's three seeds are repeated on every fixed set (the corrected spread of section 1).

    4.2 Occupancy and the step (phase 2)¶

    • The chunk size follows the batch alone, never the team (the step must not depend on the thread count): chunks of max(1, ceiling(batch / CNN_NCHUNK)) samples with a named target of 48 to 96 chunks, the target and the chunk buffers' memory measured (the buffers hold one gradient per chunk: at 920,000 weights and 64 chunks, 240 MB).
    • The reduction in parallel over slices of the weights: each slice sums the chunks in chunk order, so the sums are bit-identical to the serial ones; the Adam update in the same pass.
    • The chunk buffers zeroed in parallel, or not at all where the first sample writes them.

    4.3 The readout (phase 3)¶

    • As matrix products. The class products for all classes and both reflections as one complex product of the templates with the sample's harmonic spectrum, the rotation scores as one real matrix product with the rotation basis (cos(m theta_r), sin(m theta_r)), both through sgemm (through the same build-only rule as PS01); the backward through the winning rotation likewise.
    • Coarse to fine. The score's order-16 polynomial sampled on a coarse grid of a named size (32 to 64 rotations), the best few coarse cells refined on the full grid around them. Measured: the speed, and how often the coarse search's maximum differs from the full grid's on real tensors (the bgal and truth-set features), per class and for the mode class. Kept if the reads stay within the spread; the full-grid maximum stays the reference in the tester.
    • The backward over negligible classes. Most classes have a probability and a target near zero; their template gradients are near zero. Skipping classes with |p - t| below a named bound is an approximation: measured for speed and reads, kept only with a gain above 10% and the reads within the spread.

    4.4 The schedule (phase 4)¶

    • Early stopping in epochs, with a named patience of a few epochs, and the budget in epochs.
    • A learning-rate schedule (a short warm-up and a cosine or step decay; named constants) so that training converges in fewer epochs; Adam's rate and decay as now unless a measurement says otherwise.
    • The batch size larger where the training set allows it (the rate scaled with it), measured for epochs to the best held-out loss and for wall time.
    • The design choice. Today every width trains to the end and the wider one is kept when its held-out recovery gain is resampled clearly above zero. Two candidates: successive halving (every width trained a short named budget, only the leader by held-out loss continued, the recovery test kept between the finalists), and a rule from the training-set size (the size curve: 64 channels never won below some thousands of rows). Each is measured on every set and seed against the full search: the width it picks, the reads, the time. The tree keeps one.

    4.5 The blocks and the rest (phase 5)¶

    What phase 0's arithmetic rates and phases 2 to 4's profiles leave as the largest share: the blocks' sgemm sizes (several samples' positions in one product), im2col reuse between forward and backward, the gating and pooling loops vectorised (!$omp simd where the compiler does not), memory layout; the feature tensor and the batch assembly if they reach 5% of training. The explicit loops of a build without a team-safe BLAS (PS01) are measured on the variant build; blocked and vectorised if they leave training more than three times slower than the team-safe build. If nothing remaining exceeds 10% of training, phase 5 records that and ends.

    4.6 Not in this run¶

    The teacher's rows (the table fill), the network's architecture, the prediction table's columns and the route, the shift registration, warm starts from an earlier model (the streaming use; recorded as outlook), a GPU, reduced precision, a macOS build (the run has none; the maintainer checks the review's macOS configuration after the run).

    5. Decisions already taken¶

    • The review's findings are corrected first (phase 1), each with its regression coverage, before any speed change is measured.
    • No new command-line key of any kind; every new number is a named constant with its reason in a comment.
    • No second code path stays in the tree: alternatives are built and measured as variants in the run's scratch, and only the kept one is in the diff. The two arithmetic paths of PS01 are the build's BLAS configuration, not a choice at run time.
    • The training step stays bit-identical between one thread and any team, on both BLAS configurations.
    • The student may change against the base; it is judged on the reads of section 1 against the seed spread, never on one pair of runs.

    6. Risks¶

    • A correction that changes the reads (PS05's threshold, PS06's split): expected; the corrected spread of phase 1 is the reference for the speed phases.
    • A faster schedule that stops too early: the reads at T200 and T1000 are the first to show it.
    • The coarse rotation search that misses a maximum: measured directly, and visible in the in-plane read-out of the validation.
    • Load from the maintainer's work on the Dell distorting timings: every timing records uptime's load and is repeated under load.

    7. How the run is judged¶

    Every review finding closed with its test, the tests passing on both BLAS configurations. Per fixed set and seed, the training wall time and the reads at the base, after the corrections and after each speed phase; the end-to-end validation; the registered workflow test's wall time at its registered thread count. Hans rules on whether the reads are equal; the run sets no floor. Its working target is training at least five times faster on every fixed set at equal reads, the kernel benchmark's training samples per second at least three times the base's.

    Phase 3: the readout (2026-11-10, done)¶

    What changed (src/main/learn/simple_hhcnn.f90, its tester and the kernel benchmark). The readout scores every direction class against the sample at every in-plane rotation of the polar grid (and against the sample's reflection) and keeps the best; it was the larger part of the forward pass at 16 and 32 channels.

    • The class products as matrix products. Per angular order the sample's spectrum enters as four columns (the spectrum, the spectrum turned by a quarter, and the two of its reflection, the conjugate spectrum), and one product with the templates of all classes gives every class's real and imaginary products for the map and its reflection (readout_products).
    • The rotation scores as one matrix product. Every class's score at every rotation is the product of a fixed rotation basis (the grid's cos(m theta) and sin(m theta), scaled, held by the network) with the classes' coefficients (readout_rotations); the maximum over the rotations and the reflection is then a scan, the map's columns first as the ties went before.
    • The backward through the winning rotation likewise. Each class's gradient at its maximising rotation becomes four coefficients per order; the spectrum's gradient is one product of the templates with them per order. The templates' gradient leaves the per-chunk buffers: each sample keeps its spectrum columns and coefficients by its place in the batch, and after the batch one product per order and block of 64 classes (CNN_TCOLS) sums them over the batch (template_grads), each block one call on fixed shapes, so the sums are the same at any team. The chunk buffers no longer hold a template gradient (1.09 million weights at 500 classes, 4.4 MB per chunk, 86 chunks at a batch of 256).
    • All products go through the build's rule of the first finding: sgemm with a team-safe BLAS, the explicit loops otherwise. The accumulations are now single precision (they were double in the loops); the scores and gradients agree to single-precision rounding.
