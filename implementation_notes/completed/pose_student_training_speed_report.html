    Pose student: the review's corrections and training speed, run report¶

    The autonomous run student_speed (2026-11-09 to 2026-11-10, on the Dell: Oracle Linux 8.10, Core Ultra 9 285, 24 cores, GCC 15.2) worked on the pose student. The pose student is a small network trained per dataset on the posterior rows of the probabilistic 3D search. For particles the search has not seen, it proposes a direction, a cone about it and a confidence (doc/algorithms/pose_student.md). The network is HHCNN, the Harmonic Hermitian convolutional neural network. The run first corrected the seven findings of the code review (learned_pose_state_assignment_review.md, PS01 to PS07). It then made the training faster without changing what the student learns beyond the run-to-run spread of its reads. The plan and its full record are in pose_student_training_speed.md beside this report; its Progress section holds every phase's detail. Nothing is committed. The change is one working-tree diff against 6541633df, and the driver wrote each phase's part to /home/elmlundho/agent_runs/student_speed/review/phase_N.diff (N = 0 to 5, and 6 when this phase ends).

    Terms. - The fixed teachers are training inputs kept constant for the whole run, so that a change in the student is not hidden by a change in its teacher. T200, T1000, T5000 and T are 200, 1,000, 5,000 and 24,000 particles of a simulated ribosome assembly with known poses; the student predicts an unseen test half and is read against the truth. bgal is the 1,500 training particles of beta-galactosidase from an earlier validation run, read against that run's solve3D poses (the teacher). - The reads: within 6° (the student's most probable direction within 6° of the truth or the teacher), in the cone (the truth or teacher inside the student's cone), and seeded (the fraction of particles whose confidence clears the calibrated threshold and is handed to the neighbourhood search). Held-out recovery is the share of held-out particles whose teacher direction falls in the cone. - The spread is the range of a read over three training seeds. The corrected spread is that range after the review's corrections (phase 1), the reference for the speed phases. - Load is the machine's one-minute load average during a timing; the maintainer used the machine during the run. - The blocks are the network's three convolution blocks. Each is im2col (the 3 by 3 neighbourhoods laid out as columns of a matrix) followed by a matrix product, a gate on the modulus, and pooling. - BLAS is the matrix-product library (OpenBLAS here). A team-safe BLAS stays on the calling thread inside an OpenMP team (the build definition SIMPLE_BLAS_TEAM_SAFE). The variant build is a build of the same source without a team-safe BLAS, as on the review's macOS machine. FSC is the Fourier shell correlation, the resolution of a map.

    What was corrected¶

    Each finding has a regression test. Unless stated otherwise the tests are in the pose student's tester or the strategy's new tester (suite pose_student_strategy of unit_numerics). The review file's closing section, "Resolution in student_speed", holds each change, its test and its evidence.

    • The training arithmetic changed with the thread team (PS01). The network chose between the BLAS product and explicit loops at run time, by whether an OpenMP team was active. So on a build without a team-safe BLAS, one thread and a team took different arithmetic. The choice is now the build's alone. Test: the HHCNN tester's identity check asserts that its team actually formed (a serial run now fails instead of passing) and compares teams of 4 and 16 threads with one thread, bit for bit, on both builds.
    • A model could load another network (PS02). The weights file is resolved against the model header's directory, never the working directory. The reader refuses weights whose shape, seed or weight count differ from the header's. Tests: two model directories with the same default names, a foreign weights file in the working directory, a relocated model. The workflow test predicts with a relocated model.
    • Prediction workers ignored their part (PS03). A part of predict_poses now requires its range: fromp and top, with part, nparts and outfile. These are existing keys, handed to every part as the distributed masters already hand them. A range without a selected particle writes its records unchanged, and part tables carry the part's number. Tests: uneven ranges cover a selection exactly once; a part of only unselected rows; refused ranges. The workflow test runs four uneven parts and merges their documents.
    • Prediction accepted another physical geometry (PS04). Prediction refuses a model whose sampling (within 1e-4 Å), mask (within 0.01 Å) or crop differ from the project's. Test: each is refused with the same box and half ring; differences inside the header's decimals pass.
    • Calibration and prediction could select different particles (PS05). The confidence threshold ends only where the confidence drops, never inside a group of equal confidences. It is written so that it reads back bit for bit, and the trained model is read back and checked to seed exactly the calibrated held-out particles. Tests: the review's tie of eighteen recovered and two missed rows, thresholds next to the old five-decimal boundary, the round trip.
    • A training set could have no held-out cohort (PS06). The held-out split takes every tenth of the selected particles' stacks, in order, when there are ten or more stacks; otherwise every tenth selected particle. Fewer than ten selected particles are refused before training. Tests: two to nine stacks, fifteen stacks none numbered a multiple of ten, small single-stack selections.
    • Wide-band models could not be read back (PS07). The header reader takes records of any length. Test: models of 227 and 228 shells round-trip bit for bit.

    The model file's version is now 4; release 4 keeps no compatibility with earlier models. The corrections changed no trained network on the fixed teachers: the held-out tables were the base's on all fifteen runs. The seeded fractions moved by at most 0.0013, because the threshold now keeps its full precision.

    What was gained, and at what cost to the reads¶

    Training wall time of learn_pose_student at 24 threads on the fixed teachers (mean of three seeds in seconds; the base is the code before the run):

    set (training particles) base after the run factor
    T200 (200) 88.9 8.5 10.5
    T1000 (1,000) 192.5 18.3 10.5
    bgal (1,500) 351.3 34.7 10.1
    T5000 (5,000) 468.4 86.1 5.4
    T (24,000) 606.1 369.3 1.6

    Where the gain came from: - The schedule gave most of it on the small and middle sets. Training now counts in epochs, with a patience of three epochs; the base waited 250 optimiser steps after the best epoch, which is 14 to 21 epochs on the small sets. The 64-channel design is tried only from 10,000 training rows. - The step itself got faster: - the readout as matrix products; - the batch cut into chunks by its size alone (a target of 96; 15 to 86 chunks in practice), so that more threads work on small batches, with the reduction in parallel; - the blocks' im2col built in tiles that stay in a core's cache. - On T the schedule changed little, because the base already stopped after 7 to 9 epochs. Of the 369 s, 56 s are the teacher's table fill, which the run kept out of scope.

    The kernel benchmark (test=pose_student_bench, a training step of 256 samples at 24 threads) reaches 1.4 to 2.4 times the base's samples per second: 2.0 to 2.4 times at 16 channels, 1.4 to 1.5 at 64.

    The registered workflow test pose_student (2,000 simulated particles, eight threads, deadline 2,400 s) passes through CTest on every build. It takes 31.9 s on the default build and 51.5 s on the variant build, against 307.2 s for the base build the same evening (313.9 s at the run's start). On the review's macOS build the base had timed out at 2,400 s.

    End to end. validate_pose_student ran on bgal twice per build: 1,500 training particles of 2,756. Its teacher is a fresh, unseeded solve3D in every run, so only the learning time compares directly. The map column is the unseen particles reconstructed at the student's poses with the shifts registered there, FSC 0.143 against the teacher's map, in Å.

    run solve3D, the teacher (s) training (s) widths trained; kept training rows seeded within 6° of the teacher unseen seeded map (Å) total (s)
    base 1 321.2 474.8 16, 32, 64; 32 0.412 0.859 0.044 4.80 855
    base 2 320.6 185.9 16, 32; 16 0.625 0.915 0.043 5.63 564
    run 1 655.3 50.8 16, 32; 32 0.545 0.843 0.143 6.40 761
    run 2 288.3 36.4 16, 32; 32 0.484 0.890 0.048 4.87 381
    • Learning time: 44 s on average against 330 s, 7.6 times faster. The plan's baseline runs had taken 178 and 537 s. Every held-out recovery is 1.000.
    • Run 2 lies between the two base runs on the table's reads, apart from its unseen seeded fraction (0.048 against 0.043 and 0.044).
    • Run 1 had the weakest teacher of the four. Its solve3D took twice as long as the others', and its control map (the training particles at the teacher's poses) reached 4.47 Å between its halves, against 4.08 Å in the other three runs. Its student is below both base runs within 6° (0.843) and in the unseen map (6.40 Å), above them in unseen seeded (0.143), and between them in training rows seeded.

    Two runs per build cannot separate the student from the teacher; the fixed-teacher reads are the comparison.

    The cost to the reads, on the fixed teachers: - The means. Every read's three-seed mean lies inside the corrected spread, except two on bgal. Training rows seeded is 0.414, against the range 0.415 to 0.437 (0.396 after phase 2, 0.407 after phases 3 and 4). Seeded rows in the cone is 0.998, above the range 0.995 to 0.997. - Per seed, two runs move by more than rounding: - bgal seed 1 seeds 0.386 of its training rows against 0.419 at the base. Those confidences cluster just above 0.99, and the threshold is a quantile of 150 held-out rows, so any change that regroups a floating-point sum moves it this much. - T5000 seed 2 keeps 32 channels where the base kept 64, under the rule that tries 64 channels only from 10,000 rows: within 6° 0.883 against 0.913, seeded 0.481 against 0.509. This seed sets T5000's mean apart from the base's in the second decimal (0.870 against 0.881 within 6°). - Every other read of every set and seed differs from the base's in the third or fourth decimal.

    The plan's working targets were five times faster training on every fixed set and three times the base's kernel samples per second. The first is met on four sets of five (T: 1.6 times); the second is not (1.4 to 2.4 times).

    Decisions taken under delegation¶

    Each is recorded with its numbers in the plan's Progress entry for the phase named.

    • The variant build uses the system pthreads OpenBLAS (-DSIMPLE_BLAS_OPENMP=OFF, with BLAS_LIBRARIES and LAPACK_LIBRARIES set to /usr/lib64/libopenblasp.so), because this machine's plain libopenblas.so is single-threaded (phase 0).
    • The kernel benchmark's one-thread passes had run with the team's thread count set, so the OpenMP BLAS spread them over the team. They now run at one thread (phase 0).
    • The truth sets' test half is the copy that carries the truth shifts. The copy the plan named had the shifts removed; on it the student found the truth within 6° for only 3 to 5% of particles (phase 0; the plan's data section corrected).
    • The review's open choices:
    • PS03: the part's range is required rather than derived.
    • PS04: the tolerances are the header's decimals.
    • PS06: every tenth stack in order of number (phase 1).
    • The chunk target is 96 chunks per batch, against 64 by three alternating benchmark runs and against 48 by one. Reduction slices are 4,096 weights (phase 2).
    • The templates' gradient moved out of the per-chunk buffers into one product over the batch per block of 64 classes, an extension the plan did not list (phase 3).
    • The coarse-to-fine rotation search was built, measured and dropped. It was 1.7% faster at 24 threads and 8% slower at one thread, and it changed the winning rotation in 0.14 to 0.22% of the class searches. The backward skip of negligible classes was not built, because it could gain under 5% (phase 3).
    • The schedule is at most 30 epochs with a patience of 3, and the 64-channel design only from 10,000 training rows. A warm-up with rate halving, a doubled batch and successive halving were each measured on every set and seed, and dropped for their reads or their time (phase 4).
    • The blocks (phase 5):
    • the explicit loops reordered to run down columns: the variant build trained 14 times slower than the default, now 1.6 to 1.75 times;
    • the gate without branches;
    • im2col in tiles of 128 columns.

    Deviations from the plan¶

    • The variant build's thread identity in the kernel benchmark at 24 threads was checked on three of its twelve shapes; the loops would have taken hours for the rest. The HHCNN tester checks the identity on that build at 4 and 16 threads (phase 2).
    • The readout's sums are now single precision, as matrix products; the explicit loops had summed in double (phase 3). Scores and gradients agree to single-precision rounding.
    • The tile sizes 64 and 256 were measured once each, not three times; both ran within 2% of 128 (phase 5).
    • The default build's BLAS is the system OpenMP OpenBLAS, not a bundled one as the plan assumed.
    • T200 after the corrections: its training took 13% longer than the base's on average, with all three seeds slower. The corrections did not change its networks, and the phase did not find the cause; the later phases made the question moot.

    Left open for the maintainer¶

    • Whether the reads are equal, which the plan leaves to the maintainer. In particular: bgal's seeded fraction at the edge of the corrected spread, and the threshold's sensitivity behind it; and T5000 seed 2's narrower model.
    • The review's macOS configuration (the system OpenBLAS without the team-safe definition) was checked only through the Linux variant build. The workflow test's timeout at eight threads there is the case to rerun.
    • T, the 24,000-particle set, trains 1.6 times faster, short of the five-times target. What remains there:
    • the designs, about 300 s; at 16 and 32 channels the blocks' matrix products, already near the core's peak, are half to two-thirds of a sample;
    • the teacher's rows, 56 s, out of scope.
    • The readout is the largest share left outside the blocks: 14 to 18% of a sample at 16 channels.

    Proposed skill text¶

    The maintainer applies skill edits. For simple-modern-fortran, section OpenMP:

    • A tester's thread-identity check asserts that its team formed (omp_get_num_threads() inside a region against the count asked for): under OMP_THREAD_LIMIT=1 or a serial runtime the comparison passes trivially. Check teams of 4 (the fast gate's cap) and, by hand, 16.
    • Whether the BLAS runs inside a team is the build's property (SIMPLE_BLAS_TEAM_SAFE), never a run-time test of omp_in_parallel(): a one-thread region is not an active team, so a run-time test gives one thread and a team different arithmetic.

    For the guide of src/main/learn (or simple-main-strategies-parallelization), section Tests:

    • The pose student: unit_numerics suite=hhcnn, suite=pose_student, suite=pose_student_strategy; the workflow test=pose_student (CTest, eight threads); the kernels test=pose_student_bench (no CTest entry; run with OMP_PLACES=cores OMP_PROC_BIND=close).
