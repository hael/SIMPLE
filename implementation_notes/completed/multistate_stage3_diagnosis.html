    Why the anchor's early stages end with better maps: a multi-state diagnosis¶

    Planned 2026-10-21 for a short Dell run (arun run ms_stage3diag), base f8195504b. It follows the regression work in multistate_solve3D_regression.md, whose runs on the Dell (ms_regression, stopped, and ms_fixcheck) hold the data, the tools and the evidence this note starts from.

    What is known¶

    Cyril's simulated set: 30 000 particles of two RNA polymerase conformations (17 000 of EMD-51275, 13 000 of EMD-51273), box 180 at 2.055 Å, SNR 0.1, CTF, random poses. The two true maps agree closely, FSC about 0.8 out to 10 Å after docking, so the information that tells the states apart lies at high resolution. Particles classified against the two true maps keep 99.5 % of their states, so the set is separable in principle.

    Runs of solve3D nstates=2 from the same 2D project, distributed (nparts=3 nthr=8):

    • The anchor build (1bbbdcc24, 2026-10-15) separated the states (accuracy 0.971). At the end of stage 3 one state's half-maps agreed at FSC=0.5 out to the crop's edge (5.6 Å) while the states were still unseparated (accuracy 0.51). Because of that, the stage-boundary FSC=0.5 rule ran stage 4 at 6.0 Å instead of its planned 7.0 Å, and the states split during stage 4 (iterations 54-61), finishing in stage 5.
    • The fixed build (stage skipping removed for multi-state, f8195504b): every run so far ended stage 3 with maps whose FSC=0.5 stayed coarser than stage 4's planned 7.0 Å (11.2 Å in the first run, with FSC=0.143 at the 7.4 Å matching limit), so stage 4 stayed at 7.0 Å. The states began to split only in stage 5, the first stage at 6 Å, and either ran out of iterations while splitting (accuracy 0.674) or one state drained (0.572). Times: about 87 minutes per run against 4.3 hours for the anchor.

    The FSC code is the same in both builds (checked in ms_fixcheck). While the states are unseparated each state's map is a mixture of two nearly identical structures, which both half-sets reconstruct alike, so a state's half-map FSC then says how consistent the poses are, not whether the states are separated. The anchor's stage-3 maps therefore point to better poses after stage 3, which this run measures directly against the true poses.

    The maintainer will not change resolution policy on the evidence of simulated data. This run diagnoses what in the code makes stages 1-3 end with worse poses; it changes no ladder, promotion, exit or iteration-budget rule.

    Terms¶

    • Stage: one of the five refine3D executions of a multi-state solve3D run: stages 1-2 shc, stages 3-4 prob, stage 5 prob_neigh in state mode, each with a planned low-pass limit.
    • Accuracy: the fraction of particles whose state equals the true state, under the better of the two matchings of found to true states (0.567 if all particles are in one state).
    • Pairwise pose error: for pairs of particles in the same true state and the same found state, the difference between the angle of their relative rotation in the found poses and in the true poses; free of the frame and hand each found map chose (pair_pose_error in src/main/commanders/test/simple_test_truth_metrics.f90). Reported as the median in degrees and the fraction of pairs within 5°.
    • Stage snapshot: the project file copied the moment solve3D writes states_stage<N>.txt after stage N, which holds the poses and states of that moment.

    Rulings that hold¶

    From 2026-10-21: the speed changes stay (a diagnostic variant may undo one, a fix may not); NU filtering at stage 5 stays; resolution-limit rules stay as they are (this run varies none); distributed and shared memory must behave the same; stages 1-2 keep shc with sticky sampling. No commits; bugs found are fixed in the run when they are code defects, as the phases say.

    From 2026-10-21 ~16:20 (the maintainer's ruling during Phase 0): the fixed build had failed in all four full runs of ms_fixcheck (0.572, 0.674, 0.515, 0.589), and its fourth run ended stage 3 the way the anchor's first run did (one state's half-maps to 5.6 Å, stages 3 and 4 promoted, stage 4 at 6 Å) and still failed; only one anchor run had ever finished. So Phase 0 first asks whether the anchor separates reliably. The three anchor runs of Phase 0 go to the end (snapshots after stages 2 and 3 still taken; final labels, final maps and per-stage reprojection images kept); the three fixed-build runs stop after stage 3 as planned, and the fixed build's separation count comes from the five full runs of ms_fixcheck (four on P2, one on P2H). Order FS, AS, FS, AS, FS, AS. The anchor counts as clearly better when at least three of its four full runs (its first run and the three new ones) separated (accuracy 0.90 or more). Phase 1 then depends on both answers: anchor clearly better and the pose rule holds, Phase 1 as written; anchor clearly better but the pose rule does not hold, probes and variants run to the end and are judged by separation (at most four probes, two runs each, each probe's time recorded); anchor not clearly better, no probes: the report says there is no clear regression on this set and describes, from the per-stage labels, poses and images of all runs, where and how runs of both builds fail (including the fixed build's third run, which never found the structure).

    How runs are made and judged¶

    The command is the one of the earlier runs: solve3D pgrp=c1 mskdiam=270 nparts=3 nthr=8 nstates=2 print_states=yes on a copy of the 2D project made by the anchor (P2), one run at a time on the whole machine, without SIMPLE_SEED. Runs here stop after stage 3: the run's process group is ended once states_stage3.txt exists. Stage snapshots are taken after stages 2 and 3; each snapshot's states must equal the states_stage<N>.txt it goes with.

    Measured after stages 2 and 3 of every run: the pairwise pose error per true state; accuracy and populations; each state's half-map FSC=0.5 and FSC=0.143 (from the log); the stage's snapshot maps (recvol_state0<s>_stage0<N>_lp.mrc) docked onto the true maps in both hands, correlation; the iterations and wall time of each stage.

    A build ends stage 3 better when its pairwise pose error after stage 3 is lower in every one of its runs than in every run of the other build (three against three); anything less is not reproducible and is reported as such.

    Phases¶

    Phase 0: is the anchor's stage 3 reproducible?¶

    1. Build f8195504b (the run's tree). The anchor's build from ms_regression may be used in place (read only).
    2. Three anchor runs and three fixed-build runs through stage 3, alternating, with snapshots.
    3. The same measurements for the stage-2 and stage-3 snapshots, plus, for reference, what the logs of the full runs already made say about stages 2 and 3 (the anchor's first run, the fixed runs).

    Exit: the table of both builds after stages 2 and 3, and the decision: the anchor ends stage 3 better (Phase 1 localises it) or not reproducible (Phase 1 is skipped: write the report). Under the ruling of 2026-10-21 (see "Rulings that hold") the anchor runs go to the end and the exit also answers whether the anchor separates reliably; what Phase 1 does follows both answers.

    Phase 1: which change makes the difference¶

    1. Diagnostic variants first, on the fixed build, two runs each through stage 3, only for the suspects the Phase 0 table points at:
    2. stage 2 without the scan exit (a scratch patch restoring stage 2's earlier convergence rule): the anchor's stage 2 ran 20 iterations, the fixed build's 13-15. Diagnostic only; the scan exit is a speed change and stays.
    3. the prob table before 2026-10-15: the commit pair e2673a5d7 against its parent (stage 3 is the first prob stage).
    4. If neither explains the difference: at most five bisection probes, two runs each through stage 3, over the first-parent commits between 1bbbdcc24 and f8195504b that touch the path, in order: b57538b7b, e2673a5d7, 65bee3073, 57a447151, 13795c1d9, 04e520e78, 189e0a355, fb4e653ac, cd13708eb, 8c8d8b427 (these five built with 43de9b340's fix of the table-read race applied), 43de9b340, 9531dee62, f4d6accff, aea5b1846, af13bc828, 0c9542e50, f078cf683, dafd4d046, 24d50aabf, 497cc974b, 68278fa9a, 72930a3a4, f8195504b. Builds from cd13708eb up to 72930a3a4 can omit stage 3 or end it early (stage omission, the mid-stage resolution exit); they get f8195504b's stage-skipping fix applied, so that every probe runs stage 3 in full and only the other changes differ.
    5. For the change found: what it does to stages 1-3 (iterations, scan, pose error per iteration where the logs allow).
    6. If it is a code defect (the code does not do what its commit intended), fix it at its cause in the run's tree, add the files to the file table, and verify: three runs through stage 3 (pose error as good as the anchor's) and two full runs (accuracy). If it is a speed change or a resolution rule, describe it with the evidence and stop there: the maintainer decides.

    Exit: the change named and its effect shown (or "no single change" with the evidence), any defect fixed and verified, the report written.

    Common rules¶

    • Nothing is committed; git is read-only; other commits are built from git archive exports.
    • One SIMPLE job at a time on the whole machine; record the load when timing.
    • Never rerun a run to change a verdict; the decision rule above is fixed before the runs.
    • New or changed OpenMP code: a tester at 24 threads, repeated, compared bit for bit with one thread. THROW macros never continued with //&; no .and. short-circuit on allocatable arrays; no goto.
    • Bulky outputs are deleted once their numbers are in the journal; snapshots, logs and the stage maps used go to scratch/keep/.

    Progress¶

    Phase 0 (done 2026-10-22)¶

    Answer. Neither build is reliably better on this set. The anchor separated the two states in two of its four full runs (accuracy 0.971 and 0.931; then 0.768, partly separated, and 0.512, failed); the fixed build separated in one of five (0.912 on the anchor's 2D project rebuilt by the current 2D code, called P2H; its four runs on P2 failed at 0.572, 0.674, 0.515 and 0.589). Under the maintainer's ruling two of four is not clearly better. The pose question is not reproducible: two anchor runs ended stage 3 with far better poses than any fixed-build run, but the third ended it with worse poses than all three. Under the ruling Phase 1 therefore makes no probes and no variants: it writes the report, which describes where and how the runs of both builds fail.

    What decides a run, as far as these runs show. In every run, both builds, stages 1 and 2 end with poor poses (pairwise pose error after stage 2 18-32°, stage-2 maps docking onto the true maps at correlation 0.1-0.6) and no state separation (accuracy 0.50-0.52). Stage 3 is where runs part ways. In the two anchor runs that went on to separate, one found state's map became right during stage 3 (docked correlation 0.93-0.97 onto the truth, its particles' pose error 2-6°, its half-maps agreeing to the edge of the 132-pixel crop, 5.6 Å), which promoted stage 4 to 6 Å; the states then began to split late in stage 4 and finished splitting in stage 5 (AS 2 ran out of iterations before it was done). The other found state still had near-random poses (pose error 18-34°). In the three fixed-build runs and the third anchor run no map became right in stage 3 (correlation 0.75 at best, pose error 13-29°), stage 4 stayed at 7 Å, and the third anchor run failed with one map matching neither true structure. So a run's fate is largely settled by whether one state's map locks onto the structure during stage 3, and both builds sometimes do and sometimes don't.     Decisions taken before the first run, so that no number of this phase can move them:

    • How the decision rule reads with two true states. Pairwise pose error is measured per true state, so each run gives two medians after stage 3. A build ends stage 3 better only when, for each of the two true states, the median of every one of its three runs is lower than the median of every run of the other build. If the two true states disagree, or any run of one build falls inside the range of the other, the result is not reproducible. The fraction of pairs within 5° and the other measurements are reported beside it but do not enter the verdict.
    • Pairs. For a true state, a pair is two different particles of that true state in the same found state (found state 0 excluded): the first particle is drawn uniformly over the true state, the second uniformly over the particles of the same true state in the first one's found state; 200 000 draws with a fixed seed, so the same snapshot always gives the same number. The rotation matrices are SIMPLE's (euler2m). The tool's self-test gives zero error for the true poses, for the true poses in a rotated frame and in the other hand, about 1.8° for 2° of noise on each Euler angle and about 36° for random poses.
    • Where a run ends. After states_stage3.txt appears the run is let go on until its log shows the start of stage 4 (seconds later), so that the low-pass limit chosen for stage 4 (planned, or promoted by the FSC=0.5 rule) is in the log; then the run's processes are ended.
    • Stage maps. The snapshot maps are at the stage's crop box (112 px at stage 2, 132 px at stage 3). Before docking they are padded in Fourier space to the truth's 180 px box (scale newbox=180), then docked onto each true map in both hands (dock_volpair hp=40 lp=10, the band the earlier runs used); the better hand's correlation is reported.
    • Builds. The fixed build is this run's tree (f8195504b plus this plan), built Release with compile_clean.sh like the builds of the earlier runs (fast gate 15 of 15 passed); the anchor is the earlier run's Release build of 1bbbdcc24, used in place.
    • Order. First planned as AS 1, FS 1, AS 2, FS 2, AS 3, FS 3; under the maintainer's ruling of 2026-10-21 it is FS 1, AS 1, FS 2, AS 2, FS 3, AS 3, with the anchor runs going to the end. One run at a time on the whole machine, starting only once the ms_fixcheck run that was using the machine at the start of this phase had ended (16:12). Two starts of the first anchor run do not count and gave no numbers: the first ran the wrong binaries for a minute (the anchor's environment file was missing; the run script now refuses to start unless the build's own simple_exec is the one found), the second was ended in stage 2 together with the session that had started it.

    Phase 1 (done 2026-10-22)¶

    What this phase did. Phase 0 found the anchor not clearly better (two of four runs separated) and the pose rule not reproducible. That is the third case of the maintainer's ruling, so this phase made no probes and no diagnostic variants, and ran no SIMPLE job and no build. It describes, from the kept per-stage labels, poses, maps, logs and images of all twelve runs, where and how runs of both builds fail. The twelve runs are the anchor's four (A_1 from ms_regression, AS 1 to 3), the fixed build's five full runs from ms_fixcheck (HF_1 to HF_4 on P2, EF_1 on P2H) and its three runs through stage 3 (FS 1 to 3).     Where runs part ways: whether one state locks on during stage 3. In every run stages 1 and 2 end alike: no state separation (accuracy 0.50-0.52), poor poses (pairwise pose error 18-32° in the six runs with snapshots), and stage-2 maps that dock onto the true maps at correlation 0.6 at best. The images of the stage-1 maps of a run that separated (AS 1) and one that failed (AS 3) look alike. During stage 3 a run either locks on or it does not. Locking on means that one found state's map becomes right: in the runs with snapshots it docks onto the truth at 0.93-0.97, its particles' pose error falls to 2-6°, and its population grows (to 18 000-25 000 of the 30 000 particles) while the other state keeps near-random poses. In the log it shows as that state's half-maps agreeing out to the edge of the 132-pixel crop (5.61 Å at FSC=0.5), which in turn makes the stage-boundary rule promote stage 4 to 6 Å. A run that does not lock on ends stage 3 with neither map right (correlation 0.75 at best, pose error 13-29°), populations between 12 500 and 17 500, FSC=0.5 of the better state at 7-11 Å, and stage 4 at its planned 7 Å.     What this suggests, for the maintainer (no change made: these are speed and schedule rules the ruling keeps): - The one systematic difference visible before stage 3 is stage 2's length. The anchor ran stage 2 to its 20-iteration budget in all four runs. In the fixed build the scan exit ended it after 11-19 iterations in six of its eight runs. Lock-on is a yes/no marker visible about 35 minutes into a fixed-build run, so the plan's diagnostic variant (stage 2 without the scan exit) could be judged cheaply on lock-on. Telling a three-in-four rate from a one-in-seven rate reliably would take about eight to ten runs per arm, roughly five to six hours for the variant. - Because the anchor itself fails one run in two, the regression question needs more than four runs per build to settle, whatever variant is tried. - The second failure point, the minority state not rebuilding after lock-on, is not tied to either build in these runs.
