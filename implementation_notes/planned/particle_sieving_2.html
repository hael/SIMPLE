    particle_sieving_2: a seed solve2D, the class-average CNN's labels and the class student for the rest¶

    Status, 2026-11-25: delivered, uncommitted, for review (decisions 25 to 29 added 2026-11-24, 30 to 40 on 2026-11-25; the key retrain of decision 27 approved by Joe on 2026-11-24); report particle_sieving_2_report.md. Base: 638d99ae3 (the working tree also holds untracked scripts under scripts/, which this plan does not touch). Supersedes: none. Owner: Joe. Depends on: - the class student (hhcnn) and its programs learn_class_student and predict_classes (doc/algorithms/class_student.md; validated: yes, per data set, on bgal, trpv1 and streptavidin); - the class-average CNN (sscnn) in Fortran, simple_cavg_cnn and simple_cavg_cnn_predict (completed/cavg_cnn_fortran_inference_report.md; validated: yes as a copy of the Python network, to 2e-7; as a rejection criterion: no, the real-data read of its predictions is still open, report section 6); - solve2D and refine2D with refine=greedy and update_frac (validated: yes, in production).

    1. Why¶

    particle_sieving runs a solve2D job on every chunk of the data set, in two tiers. The class student's run measured solve2D at about 13 ms per particle on trpv1, and the student's prediction with its alignment at 0.54 to 1.16 ms per particle (doc/algorithms/class_student.md, "What the reads found"). particle_sieving_2 pays for the search once, on the particles of at most 100 micrographs or 10,000 particles (decision 25), and sorts the rest of the data set into those classes with the student, with a greedy refine2D iteration every 50,000 particles to keep the classes current. Classes are rejected by the class-average CNN instead of the sieve preset of model_cavgs_rejection. Whether this selects as well as the existing sieve is not judged in this plan (decision 14); the plan delivers the program and shows that it runs and is reproducible.

    2. Contract¶

    • particle_sieving does not change. ptcl_sieve, its policy, the stream's p03 and p05 and their defaults are out of bounds. No line of the new module runs unless the program particle_sieving_2 is called.
    • One project owner. The ptcl_sieve2 object owns the work project. The child programs (solve2D, learn_class_student, predict_classes, refine2D) run on copies in their own folders; the owner reads their records back and writes the user's project once, at the end, as particle_sieving does (a temporary file, renamed).
    • The children are whole programs run as stages, through their commanders, as validate_class_student runs them. The module calls no part of a stage and copies no frame of one.
    • Nothing is a default. The program is registered at developer visibility until a real-data comparison with particle_sieving has been read by the maintainer (a later plan).
    • Reproducible: at one thread and a fixed seed two runs give the same accepted set, class labels and class averages (section 6 says where this is gated).

    3. Decisions and rulings¶

    All dated 2026-11-23. 1 to 14 are Joe's; the request gave 1 to 6, the questions put to him gave 7 to 14.

    1. The seed. solve2D on the selected particles of the first nmics (default 200) selected micrographs of the project, in project order, into ncls (default 100) classes. (Its size revised by decision 25.)
    2. The mask is the box's default everywhere: mskdiam = (box - COSMSKHALFWIDTH) * smpd (the stream's mskdiam_box), passed to every child. (Replaced by decisions 23 and 28: mskdiam given, and required.)
    3. Bad classes. After the seed, cavg_cnn scores every class average; a class is bad when p_junk > 0.9 (strictly). A class with no particles is neither scored nor bad.
    4. The rest of the data set comes in batches of 10,000 particles, cut at stack boundaries in project order: stacks are added until the batch holds at least 10,000 selected particles; the tail is the last batch. Each batch is classified by predict_classes (the student's prediction and its alignment against the candidate classes).
    5. Every 5 batches a greedy round: one refine2D iteration with refine=greedy on the particles added by those batches and the same number of earlier particles (decision 10).
    6. A new commander beside the existing one, the logic in one new module (decision 11).
    7. No search after a batch. The "local refinement" of a batch is predict_classes' own alignment after prediction. No refine2D iteration follows a batch.
    8. Bad classes stay classes and are relabelled after every greedy round (revised the same day, Joe: rejected particles are not left out of refinement). Every ingested particle stays active in the work project and takes part in the greedy rounds whatever its class's label, so a bad class keeps its particles and its average, and a particle can move between bad and good classes. After each round cavg_cnn scores the averages again; a class may turn bad or good. Rejection is a label during the run: only the final project sets a particle's state, 0 when its class's last label is bad.
    9. The class averages are rebuilt only by the greedy rounds. Between rounds the batches are predicted against the averages and the model of the last round.
    10. (Its share replaced by decision 31.) The earlier particles of a greedy round are those of lowest updatecnt, by the existing sample4update_cnt through update_frac = min(1, 2 * n_new / n_active), where n_new is the number of particles added since the last round and n_active all ingested particles. The new particles have updatecnt 0 and fill the first tier; the lowest remaining tiers give the other half. No new bookkeeping field. With no search between rounds, updatecnt counts greedy rounds only.
    11. Code home. All workflow logic in src/main/sieve/simple_ptcl_sieve2.f90 (type ptcl_sieve2, new, run, kill) with its tester. A thin commander_sieve_ptcls2 in simple_commanders_sieve.f90. Elsewhere only registration (exec router, UI), the constants, and one extraction in simple_cavg_cnn_predict.f90 (Phase 1).
    12. The student is trained after the seed and again after every greedy round, on a random sample of 10,000 ingested particles against the current class averages. (Replaced by decision 26: once, after the seed; again after the rounds retrain lists, decision 27.)
    13. Command-line keys: nmics (200; 100 since decision 25) and ncls (100) from the existing keys; the class-average CNN's weights file through the existing infile, required. The batch size (10,000), the round interval (5), the junk threshold (0.9) and the training sample (10,000) are named constants in src/defs/simple_defs_sieve2.f90. No new key (until decision 27's retrain).
    14. Acceptance is mechanical: a miniature workflow test, a run to completion on a real set, and identical results twice at one thread. No comparison with particle_sieving and no judgement of the selection in this plan.
    15. (2026-11-23, Joe, on stop condition 1) The greedy rounds run at the full box (autoscale=no). solve2D leaves full-box class averages and FRCs (its final make_cavgs overwrites the last stage's cropped ones) beside carried class sums at its crop (cavg_state.bin; MotAB: 320 px averages, 160 px sums), and refine2D neither crops references nor blends sums of another box. Round 1 therefore runs as a full update over every ingested particle and publishes full-box carried sums; decision 10 applies from round 2. Replaces default f's "startit above 1" with decision 18.
    16. (2026-11-23, Joe, on stop condition 3) The workflow test's junk threshold is frozen after the Phase 0 dry run, between the miniature's p_junk values, so that both labels occur. The fixture network has random weights (p_junk 0.48 to 0.52 on MotAB's 100 averages); the CNN is not judged, the mechanics are. If a later change moves the split, the test reports which label is missing.
    17. (2026-11-23, Joe) Phase 4 runs on thunder with the trained cavg_cnn_v3.bin (~/Data/ss_sim/fortran_ref/ here). The project is to be found there; Phases 0 to 3 and 5 do not wait for it.
    18.
