    Multi-state posterior state draw: report of the run ms_posterior2¶

    Report, 2026-11-06. The run carried out the plan doc/implementation_notes/completed/multistate_posterior_state_draw.md on dell2: the home build machine, with 16 cores of three kinds, Ubuntu 26.04 and gfortran 15.2. It ran unattended, as one uncommitted diff against HEAD ea4c1c20a, the tree the batch alignment pipeline refactoring left. Each phase's diff is /home/hael/agent_runs/ms_posterior2/review/phase_N.diff (written by the run's driver at the end of each phase); R below is /home/hael/agent_runs/ms_posterior2. The plan's Progress section holds the detail and the evidence paths; this report is the summary.

    The change concerns multi-state solve3D (de novo 3D reconstruction with nstates > 1) and the probabilistic refinement it runs: - A particle's state is now one draw from its tempered posterior over state and direction together, in place of the closest state followed by a draw within it. - The labels stay hard, and the weighted insertion's extra poses come from the drawn state. - The table fill splits its shift-refinement budget over the states. - The neighbourhood table draws over every state's candidates. - The annealing tail waits until no state gains resolution. - A final assignment at the physical posterior labels every particle against the final maps.

    No parameter was added.

    What we learned¶

    • The change works as designed, and single-state runs are untouched. Single-state results are bit for bit identical to the base tree: at one and eight threads, at two parts, and on a full single-state workflow. The unit suites, the fill harness at 16 threads, the multi-state test routes and the nightly suite (19 of 19 entries) pass.
    • On small simulated data it behaves like the rule it replaced. On true poses with random labels (the new labels test route, four minutes) the joint draw recovers the three beta-galactosidase states along the same path as the closest-state rule. The state posterior there is almost as sharp as an argmin from the second iteration. Over 20 random starts it resolves every state 13 times. In the other 7 a state drains as the maps of a random start fall behind. At the three draining seeds checked the old rule drains too, and so do hotter draws (prob_beta 0.1 at all three, 0.03 at two). The de novo shc route resolves every state in 15 of 20 seeds, against the floor of 6 (13 of 20 in October, on older code).
    • On the three-state benchmark subset it does not separate the states. The subset is 20,000 simulated ribosome-assembly particles: 30 % the smallest assembly, 60 % the medium, 10 % the largest.
    • With the true maps and poses every particle goes to its true state (the state signal is there at 10 Å).
    • From one shared random labelling on true poses, two runs end at accuracy 0.69 and 0.65 (accuracy: the share of particles in the found state matched to their true state). The smallest assembly separates; the medium is split over two states; the largest never gets a state and merges into one of them.
    • Three de novo runs end at accuracy 0.59, 0.42 and 0.58, all failed by the thresholds fixed beforehand. Two give a clean map of the medium assembly (correlation 0.97 to the truth) holding half to 60 % of its particles. The largest assembly is never isolated.
    • The readout places the failure in the low-resolution probabilistic stages. In stage 3 (22.6 Å) the state posterior at the search's factor 0.3 spreads over 2.2-2.7 of the three states (1.6-2.3 in stage 4, at 20.2 Å). Accuracy stays near chance while about half the labels change each iteration, and a quarter to over half of each state's posterior mass rests on entries the fill did not shift-refine. The labels start to organise at the end of stage 4 (it adds 0.09-0.10 of accuracy in two runs) and through the last stage at 6 Å, which adds 0.03-0.10.
    • Stage 3 meets the plan's test for "too hot".
    • But even at the physical factor the state posterior is broad (1.3-1.7 states). That fits maps that cannot yet tell the states apart at that resolution as well as it fits a draw that is too hot.
    • The existing annealing tail at the end of the last stage moved accuracy by less than 0.01.
    • So the plan's fallback, an annealing tail at the end of every probabilistic stage, is the named next step, but these runs do not show it would help.
    • The final assignment works and is cheap. It labels all 20,000 particles at the physical posterior against the final maps in 8-12.5 minutes, changes 1-3 % of the labels, and the coverage check after it finds nothing missing.

    What changed, phase by phase¶

    • Phase 0 (2026-11-04; review/phase_0.diff), the builds, the test route and the tools:
    • the new labels route of multistate_workflows, in the default route list, with its variants labels_identical and hard_labels;
    • the registry comment and the test policy;
    • the subset's 2D solution, P2S;
    • the tools, copied and adapted.

    The phase ran before the batch alignment pipeline refactoring, which the maintainer ruled should go first (ruling 12 of the plan's section 10); its diff was re-applied on the tree that refactoring left (ruling 13). - Phase 1 (2026-11-05; review/phase_1.diff), the joint draw and its readout: - the dense table's joint state-and-direction draw in the per-row selection assign_rows; - the state readout: PROB_TAB_STATE_DRAW, PROB_TAB_STATE_MASS and four statistics-file keys, carried in the parts' readout records and stored on the convergence object in both execution modes; - the unit tests, the single-state identity, and the labels reports. - Phase 2 (2026-11-05; review/phase_2.diff), the rest of the design: - the per-state split of the fill's refined-reference budget on both shift paths; - the neighbourhood table's pooled draw, with the extras gathered from the committed state; - the per-state stall test of the annealing tail; - the final assignment, replacing the greedy missing-update pass; - the fill harness's per-state polish check; - the identity again.

    An independent review found two defects, both fixed. First, the distributed master re-seeded and checked the multi-state labels at every refine3D start; a state drained in one stage would then stop the next stage, and the final assignment. It now does that only at a fresh start, as the shared-memory strategy does. Second, a neighbourhood particle whose candidates all carry a non-finite distance got no reference. That case crashed the single-state simulated_workflow_6vxx on the binary before the fix; such a particle is kept again, as before. - Phase 3 (2026-11-05; review/phase_3.diff), robustness on small data: - the labels profile and its floors, written in the suite; - the shc profile; - the single routes, the full entry and the nightly suite; - the entry's new time in the registry comment and the test policy. - Phase 4 (2026-11-05/06; review/phase_4.diff, the plan only), validation on the subset: - the truth-frame check and the known-reference control; - two label-recovery runs and three de novo runs, judged against the truth. - Phase 5 (2026-11-06; review/phase_5.diff), documents and report: - solve3D_policy.md, refine3D_policy.md and refine3D_states_policy.md updated; - two path references follow the plan's move; - this report, and the plan moved to completed/.

    Decisions taken under delegation¶

    Each is recorded in the plan's Progress section of its phase and in that phase's journal (R/state/journal_phase_N.md); the journal holds the reasons in more detail.

    1. How labels starts (Phase 0). Shared-memory refine3D stops without starting maps, so the route draws the uniform labelling itself, reconstructs the maps, and names them as the distributed master's fresh start does. Its settings mirror solve3D's multi-state probabilistic stages.
    2. The consensus route's failure in Phase 0's default entry (a state merged and emptied) was recorded, not fixed. It is independent of Phase 0's diff, which changed only test code: at one thread the metrics are byte-identical on base and tree, and other eight-thread runs pass with a partial merge. On the state draw's code the route passed in Phases 2 and 3.
    3. No per-stage tail in Phases 1-2. On labels the readout read "too cold" (the state posterior near-sharp from the second iteration), not "too hot", so the fallback was not indicated.
    4. The single-state identity on simulated_workflow_6vxx instead of _1jxy: at this HEAD _1jxy stops before any probabilistic stage (ruling 13).
    5. The polish rule kept (Phase 2). The polar shift map records a polish only where it beats the stored shift's distance. Changing that would change single-state results, so the harness checks each state's share and the budget instead (a deviation, below).
    6. The file table followed the work:
    7. the 3D matcher added under ruling 13 (the readout's route to the caller now that the pre-step is gone);
    8. the refine3D strategy's row extended to Phase 2 for the distributed label start;
    9. the fill harness and its driver added (Phase 2);
    10. the speed-week closeout added for its link (Phase 5).

    The rows of the retired pre-step files were removed. 7. The labels drains are the plan's risk 4, not a stop (Phase 3). Risk 4 is a state that drains because its map is garbage at any temperature. At the three draining seeds checked the closest-state rule drains too, and so do hotter draws (prob_beta 0.1 at all three, 0.03 at two). No allowed setting cures it, and reseeding is outside the plan. The route's floors were therefore set from its profile by the floor rule. For a measured value the floor sits no higher than the worst run less one robust spread, and no higher than the median less three. For a success count it is the count less three binomial standard deviations: - every state resolved in 6 of 20 seeds; - adjusted Rand index 0.70, accuracy 0.75, population error 0.30; - rotation error 2.0°, shift error 0.10 px. 8. hard_shc's collapse checked against the base build (Phase 3): the base collapses the same way at the same seed. 9. The entry's time updated (Phase 3), and the build reinstalled before the nightly ran, so the nightly ran on the final tree. 10. The subset runs' settings (Phase 4). The label-recovery start was drawn once (seed 20261106) and applied with selection. The refine3D runs added iterstats=yes and mkdir=no, which change no result. The verdict thresholds kept their defaults: the known-reference ceiling was 1.0000, and they were fixed before the first de novo run. 11. The per-stage tail not tried in Phase 4, although stage 3 meets the "too hot" test: - ruling 10: the subset validates and does not drive development; - the fast tests do not call for it; - a controller change would reopen the shc profile and the nightly; - the evidence does not show it would help.

    Deviations from the plan¶

    • The design landed in the batch pipeline. Sections 2-4 of the plan describe the pre-step ref_assign / prob_align_exec; under ruling 13 the draw went into the per-row selections assign_rows and assign_rows_neigh, every draw keyed, and the readout into the parts' records. Ruling 13 also widened the identity to eight threads and two parts.
    • The fill's polish coverage. The plan asks that "every active block carries a polished entry per particle". The code guarantees each block its share of screen and polish attempts; 1 of 64 harness particles ended without a recorded polish in one state.
    • Phase 1's fixture known-reference run was not the known-label control it was meant to be: the particle import resets states, so it started from random labels. Corrected in Phase 2's Progress.
    • The shc route was run in Phase 2 beyond the plan's list, because it is the one route that reaches the final assignment.

    Evidence against the baseline¶

    Ruling 10 forbids a comparison arm of the current code on the subset, so the comparisons are on small data and are named as such. - Single-state identity, base build (HEAD exported with git archive) against the tree, same seed and layout: every map, FSC, sigma2 store, statistics file and particle record is identical. That holds for a small refine3D at one thread, at eight, and at two parts, and for simulated_workflow_6vxx at one thread (181 of 181 declared outputs), in Phases 1 and 2. - labels on the unchanged pipeline tree against the joint draw (Phase 1): final accuracy 0.999 against 0.999, along the same per-iteration path. The conformational pair (hard_labels) stays unseparated (accuracy 0.67 before, 0.69 after, below the 0.80 of one state for all), and identical states still drain a state. - The budget split on labels (Phase 2): the unrefined share of each state block's mass, 0.64 / 0.40 / 0.25 at the last iteration of Phase 1's annealing tail, now stays at or below 0.061, with the labels unchanged. - The fill's cost (Phase 2): +1.7 % table-fill time against Phase 1's binary, within the tenth the plan allows. - shc profile: 15 of 20 against 13 of 20 on 2026-10-19 (older code, not paired). The new labels profile: 13 of 20. - Drains and collapses found in Phase 3 occur on the base build too: the closest-state rule drains at the three of the seven draining labels seeds checked, and hard_shc collapses at its seed. - The benchmark: the first run's de novo run of the code before this work on the full 80,000 particles (the plan's section 9) failed in the shape of two of Phase 4's runs: one medium-locked state and mixtures, though without the smallest-rich state those two had. It is not paired (other data size, older code).

    Open for the maintainer¶

    1. The per-stage annealing tail (the plan's section 6 fallback; the controller would set the existing key prob_beta_end=1 on stages 3-5). Stage 3 on the benchmark meets the test that calls for it; the evidence does not show it would help. Trying it means a controller change, then the labels and shc routes, the shc profile and the nightly, then new de novo runs. If it does not suffice, a separate state factor would be a new parameter, which ruling 4 leaves to you.
    2. The polish budget per state. In stages 3-4 a quarter to over half of each state block's posterior mass rests on stored-shift losses. One polish count for every block (the plan's section 4.2) would cost up to 2.5-2.8 times the table time.
    3. The rare state on true poses. From random labels, the partition that forms at the flat factor splits the dominant class. Whether the row's in-plane bias plays a part is not settled by these runs. That is the plan's risk 3: each row entry holds the loss at one drawn in-plane angle, not the loss marginalised over the angle. Its remedy, the in-plane marginal, changes what the row holds, which is your decision.
    4. The labels drains (7 of 20 random starts lose a state). Reseeding an emptied state is outside the plan, and nothing map-driven is allowed (ruling 7).
    5. consensus is borderline at eight threads. It failed once in Phase 0 (a merged and emptied state) and passed in Phases 2 and 3; a profile of the current route is pending (test policy).
    6. The stream and the GUI need no edit. The stream's multi-state solve3D stage runs solve3D and inherits the change, and nothing there reads the deleted pass or the new statistics keys. The GUI could show the state readout; nothing was done.
    7. The skill text below.

    multistate solve3D: draw the state from the posterior, final assignment at beta 1

direction together (dense and neighbourhood tables); labels stay hard and the
weighted insertion's extras come from the drawn state. The fill splits its
refined-reference budget over the states, the annealing tail waits until no
state gains, and a final assignment at prob_beta=1 replaces the greedy
missing-update pass. A state readout (PROB_TAB_STATE_DRAW/_MASS, STATS_FILE
keys) shows the draw's sharpness. New multistate_workflows route `labels`
