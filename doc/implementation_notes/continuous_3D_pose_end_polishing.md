## Mathematical and numerical contract

### Pose, prepared reference, and weighted objective

For an input pose $(R_0,t_0)$,

$$
q=(\omega_x,\omega_y,\omega_z,t_x,t_y),
\qquad
R=R_0\exp([\omega]_\times).
$$

Rotations are right tangent-space increments in radians and shifts are image-plane pixels of the reference box. A pose-reference constructor accepts the physical volume $X$, applies the inverse Kaiser--Bessel envelope internally,

$$
X_{\mathrm{prep}}=E^{-1}X,
$$

and retains one immutable Cartesian Fourier reference until the physical volume changes. Callers do not pass an ambiguously pre-corrected volume.

For detector frequency $(h,k)$, define

$$
S(h,k;t)=\exp\left[i\frac{2\pi}{N}(ht_x+kt_y)\right],
\qquad
T_{\mathrm{pose}}(h,k)=\frac{C(h,k)}{\sqrt{\sigma^2_{s(h,k)}}}.
$$

$T_{\mathrm{pose}}$ contains the CTF and noise amplitude only; it never contains a shift phase. The pose evaluator alone applies $S(t)$, so a shift cannot be counted twice. The whitened prediction, observation, residual, and objective are

$$
\widehat Y_w=S(t)T_{\mathrm{pose}}G_R(X_{\mathrm{prep}}),
\qquad
Y_w=\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\sigma^2_s}},
\qquad
r=\widehat Y_w-Y_w,
\qquad
\Phi(q)=\frac12\sum_{(h,k)\in\Omega}|r(h,k)|^2.
$$

The CTF is applied to the prediction, not divided out of the observation. `CTFFLAG_NO` gives $C=1$, the normal mode uses the signed CTF, and `CTFFLAG_FLIP` uses $|C|$. A physical CTF zero is valid. This is not the PFTC matcher objective and requires no polar-coordinate radial/Jacobian scaling factor.

### Executed forward-model conventions

- $N$ is even. The spatial origin is zero-based $j=N/2$, or one-based Fortran $i=N/2+1$.
- $(h,k)$ are native integer Fourier coordinates. The gather samples the padded lattice at $\mathrm{padf}[h,k,0]R$.
- The forward transform uses the negative exponential. The padded FFT contributes $1/N_{\mathrm{pd}}^3$ and the gather contributes $\mathrm{padf}^3$, giving native normalization $1/N^3$.
- The rotation update is right-sided. For Cartesian unit axis $e_a$, $\partial\operatorname{loc}/\partial\omega_a=\operatorname{loc}\times e_a$.
- The positive shift phase above gives $\partial M/\partial t_x=i(2\pi h/N)M$ and $\partial M/\partial t_y=i(2\pi k/N)M$.
- The active domain is the full redundant Fourier disk

$$
\Omega=\left\{(h,k)\in[-N/2,N/2]^2:
k_{\min}^2\le h^2+k^2\le k_{\max,\mathrm{effective}}^2\right\}.
$$

Conjugate mates and logical positive and negative Nyquist endpoints remain explicitly counted. Validation, the later standalone mode, and the first complete `refine3D` integration retain this representation and log `fourier_domain=full_redundant_disk`, requested and effective shell limits, and active sample count. A weighted half-plane requires a separate post-integration review, conjugate/DC/Nyquist weighting, equivalence tests, and real-dataset A/B evidence.

The variance input contains one noise-power value per shell, with $s(h,k)=\operatorname{nint}\sqrt{h^2+k^2}$, and the pose path sets

$$
k_{\max,\mathrm{effective}}=
\min\left(k_{\max,\mathrm{requested}},\operatorname{ubound}(\sigma^2),N/2\right).
$$

Every used variance must be positive and finite. No usable shell overlap or an invalid used value returns a nonfatal invalid-noise-range outcome without changing the pose or stopping SIMPLE. The pose path does not extend the final variance into unknown shells. The existing PCG helper retains its last-shell fallback unchanged; implementation comments and diagnostics must make the difference visible for later dataset testing.

The fast normalized Kaiser--Bessel derivative holds $i_0=\operatorname{nint}(\operatorname{loc})-\mathrm{iwinsz}$ fixed. Half-integer stencil boundaries are non-differentiable, and switches are measured separately rather than included in centered finite-difference acceptance.

### Local derivatives and LM

The five residual-Jacobian columns differentiate the executed fast Kaiser--Bessel polynomial and normalized stencil inside one fixed interpolation cell. The LM step is

$$
(J^H J+\mu D)\delta=-J^H r.
$$

Independent evidence covers the unscaled and scaled normal terms, damping diagonal, proposed step, predicted reduction, recomputed objective, gain ratio, decision, and rollback.

### Tolerance-calibration protocol

Use separate algebraic, LM, derivative, and forward-model tolerance families. Existing finite-difference tolerances do not qualify as algebraic or forward-model tolerances. Scalar, vector, matrix, and complex comparisons use the componentwise combined rule

$$
|a-b|\le\tau_{\mathrm{abs}}+\tau_{\mathrm{rel}}\max(|a|,|b|).
$$

For complex values, $|\cdot|$ is complex magnitude. Relative comparison applies to every gradient component as well as values, residuals, and normal terms.

Calibration fixtures use boxes $8^3$ and $12^3$; acceptance fixtures use boxes $10^3$ and $16^3$. The sets use different deterministic asymmetric volumes and poses, and both cover constant and varying sigma, ordinary and attenuated CTF, and exact and nonstationary poses. Before calibration, each family records its absolute floor, relative floor, and relative-scale floor. Calibration records maximum absolute and scaled-relative errors and sets

$$
\mathrm{atol}=8E_{\mathrm{abs,max}},
\qquad
\mathrm{rtol}=8E_{\mathrm{rel,max}},
$$

subject to the predeclared floors. A scientifically loose result stops for diagnosis rather than becoming an acceptance threshold.

For the scaled LM system $Az=b$, compare every solution component, the physical step after coordinate rescaling, predicted and actual reductions, gain ratio, and backward error

$$
\eta=\frac{\lVert Az-b\rVert_\infty}
{\lVert A\rVert_\infty\lVert z\rVert_\infty+\lVert b\rVert_\infty}.
$$

Tight step agreement applies only to reliable systems; singular or poorly conditioned fixtures verify their terminal outcome instead. Ordinary decision fixtures remain away from the LM ratio threshold: for the current $0.25$ threshold, use ratios below approximately $0.20$ or above $0.30$ and test the boundary separately.

Terminal status, sample counts, active masks, invalid-input classification, symmetry, and zero contracts remain exact. Rejected-trial rollback is bitwise identical where the representation permits it. The evidence package records fixtures, raw error tables, derived tolerances, safety factor, source commit, compiler, precision, and a pre-acceptance freeze marker. A failed acceptance test reopens the protocol or implementation; it never changes a threshold in place.

### Independent forward evidence and comparison contracts

Independence comes from four paths: the executed fast gather, a structurally separate slow interpolator, a tiny-box brute-force DFT, and a finite-box real-space projection followed by FFT. The direct oracle uses

$$
F(\boldsymbol{\xi})=\frac{1}{N^3}\sum_{\mathbf{x}}X(\mathbf{x})
\exp\left(-2\pi i\boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot\mathbf{x}\right),
\qquad
\boldsymbol{\xi}_R=N^{-1}[h,k,0]R,
$$

with spatial-frequency derivative

$$
\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}F(\boldsymbol{\xi})
=-\frac{2\pi i}{N^3}\sum_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{x}X(\mathbf{x})
\exp\left(-2\pi i\boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot\mathbf{x}\right).
$$

The four comparison contracts are:

1. **Analytic object versus brute-force DFT:** a tight double-precision convention test for origin, sign, frequency, and normalization.
2. **Brute-force DFT of $X$ versus executed $G(E^{-1}X)$:** an interpolation-accuracy contract, not machine equality.
3. **Independent slow interpolation versus executed gather:** a tighter source-fidelity contract using the same polynomial Kaiser--Bessel function, normalization, padding, packed/Friedel rules, and periodic wrapping without calling the executed gather or stencil helper.
4. **Finite-box projection versus direct DFT:** a separate discrete-model contract that declares whether projection rays are summed or averaged. Line sums followed by a normalized two-dimensional FFT require an explicit $1/N$ factor to match the normalized three-dimensional DFT. Arbitrary rotations report additional clipping and real-space interpolation effects separately.

Analytic fixtures include centered and off-center deltas, separated points, and a non-collinear unequal-amplitude point set. Sampled Gaussian and sphere fixtures test robustness, not exact continuous-transform identities; a sphere cannot test rotational identifiability and a two-point object is degenerate about its connecting axis.

Preserve the existing evidence distinction. `simple_continuous_3D_pcg_refinement_rotation_test.f90` remains a matched-model derivative-consistency test and is not independent evidence for CTF or whitening order. New independent CTF/whitening tests follow `simple_continuous_3D_pcg_refinement_shift_test.f90`: form the raw CTF-distorted observation first, whiten it separately, and never generate oracle observations with the evaluator under test.
