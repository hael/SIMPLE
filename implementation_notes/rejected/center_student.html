    The center student: a real-space network that centres raw picks before the class student¶

    Status, 2026-11-18: rejected by Hans before launch, on the measured ceiling (section "Rejected" at the end; the measurement: rejected/center_student_ceiling.md). Not to be revisited for the class student. Base: 8a690cc19 (clean master; the review read this commit). Supersedes: none. Depends on: solve2D's class-average centring (validated: yes, the production default); center_ptcls (validated: yes, doc/algorithms/center_ptcls.md); the class student on raw picks with the alignment after prediction (validated: yes, completed/class_student_raw_picks.md, adopted 2026-11-16); the students' shared strategy helpers (validated: yes). Nothing here depends on an unvalidated component.

    In one paragraph¶

    Every measurement on the polar students says the same thing: at good shifts they assign classes and poses well, at the picking error they do not. The HHCNN is equivariant to rotation about the box centre, so a shift (a phase ramp spread over every ring) is exactly what it cannot absorb. A real-space convolutional network with stride-1, circularly padded convolutions is equivariant to whole-pixel circular translations by construction. The center student is such a network, trained per data set on the shifts of a solve2D teacher, which are already centred on each class average's support (section 3, decision 1). It reads the particle's box as extracted. It outputs a heatmap over a disk of candidate centres, plus one "outside" bin. From that it commits an absolute shift, replacing the stored one, or falls back to the stored shift when the centre is outside the disk or at its edge. By default the class student then searches its usual disk, under the pick prior, about the new start. A narrower search for confident particles is a separate option: it is adopted only if it earns it, and only through an explicit record of which particles the network centred (decision 9). The questions are pre-registered and are answered first in the PyTorch prototype, through the existing class-student validation, before any Fortran is written: - Does the network centre unseen particles as well as center_ptcls within a declared margin? - Does the class student from the network's centres, over the usual disk, beat raw picks over the disk? - Does the narrower search add anything on top?

    1. Why¶

    • The students fail on raw picks and succeed on centred ones. On raw picks the class student's most likely class agrees with the full search for 23 to 35% of particles, while its candidate list holds that class for 90 to 96%. Dropping centring alone cost 5.6, 15.7 and 19.3 points of mode agreement on streptavidin, bgal and trpv1 (completed/class_student_raw_picks_report.md, Phase 0). The pose student's cone holds its answer for 96% of particles at 6.4 Å of shift and for 28% at 12.8 Å (doc/algorithms/center_ptcls.md).
    • But the old centred route lost downstream. The class student on raw picks, aligned over the disk against its candidate list, beat the route through center_ptcls on every set: by 2.6 to 17.4 points of committed-class agreement and 0.75 to 4.80 Å at FRC 0.5. A centring step is therefore judged by what it does to the class student, not by how many particles it moves within 5 Å (verdict 13 (c)).
    • The picking error is much larger than the students' tolerance: a median of 8 to 10 Å, a 90th percentile of 16 to 22 Å and a 99th of about 40 Å on the real sets.
    • What exists does not close the gap cheaply. center_ptcls needs references and a pass over the data. It puts 0.45 of trpv1's picks within 3 Å and 0.67 within 5 Å, but only 0.22 to 0.25 and 0.45 on bgal. A rotational average centres 0.18 within 3 Å. A power-spectrum student is shift-invariant but learns almost nothing. The amplitude channel was rejected (2026-11-16). polar_student_future_directions.md section 2 already names the remaining candidate: "a translation-equivariant real-space network supervised by converged shifts".
    • Two reference points are measured. A single pass of bgal's picks against the full run's 50 class averages puts 0.57 within 3 Å, 0.66 within 5 Å and 0.70 within 6.4 Å. That is a strong measured baseline, not a proven ceiling. Two converged solve2D runs agree within 3 Å for 69% (trpv1) and 76% (bgal) of the same particles. That is the labels' reproducibility, not an accuracy floor.

    The prize: once a solve2D teacher and the network's training have been paid for, prediction needs no references and no further 2D. It costs a few milliseconds per particle-thread, could later serve the stream's chunks, and gives each particle a width that may, once calibrated, narrow the search. Its accuracy gain over center_ptcls may be modest.

    2. Contract¶

    • New programs only, until Phase 4. learn_center_student and predict_centers (private) and validate_center_student (simple_exec) are new. Until Phase 4 no existing program, default or record changes.
    • Off state. Without the new programs, refine2D, refine3D, solve2D, solve3D, center_ptcls, the class student and the pose student are byte-identical to the base at one thread with a fixed seed (simulated_workflow 1jxy and 6vxx, test=identity). The pose student never changes in this plan.
    • The raw-pick route is untouched. predict_classes on a project the center student has not centred behaves exactly as at the base, byte for byte. That includes fresh imports, whose records hold shsig = 0 (decision 9). The default centred route needs no change to predict_classes at all.
    • The record. predict_centers writes the shift, shsig and shincr to ptcl2D, and to ptcl3D when it has rows, and nothing else. The shift is replaced by the network's absolute estimate (decision 5). shincr is the length of the actual change of the stored shift. shsig is the sentinel −1 in the default route, so the next search takes the disk under the pick prior about the new start. The project has one owner: parts write part files, and the master merges in memory and writes the segments once.
    • Same answer at any thread count. Training is bit-identical at a fixed thread count, as the HHCNN's is: chunks whose size follows from the batch alone, and gradients reduced in chunk order. Prediction is the same at any thread count and, with nparts, equal byte for byte to shared memory.
    • One copy of everything shared. The part ranges, the distributed prediction master and the file hash stay in simple_strategy_student_common. The HHCNN, polar_student, polar_feats and src/main/pftc are not edited.
    • No new command-line parameter unless ruled. The center student's settings are named constants in src/defs/simple_defs_hhcnn.f90. Existing keys keep their meaning: nsample, mskdiam, nthr, nparts, projfile, fname, infile.
    • No new CTest entry. The center student's workflow checks run inside the existing class_student high-level entry. Its fixture has planted shifts. SIMPLE_CTEST_BUDGET stays unchanged (doc/policies/test_environment_policy.md: a new entry needs the owner's agreement).
    • Layering. The commanders are thin execute(cline) wrappers. The learn and predict steps are strategy routines, run_<x>(cline) and <x>_exec(params, build, ...), as the class student's are. The network and the student are encapsulated types with new and kill. Large components are allocatable (doc/policies/compile_time_policy.md).

    3. Decisions and rulings¶

    What the code check found (at cfbad5a26, re-read at 8a690cc19)¶

    • The teacher's shifts are already centred on each class's support. solve2D sets center=yes and center_type=seg (simple_commanders_solve2D.f90; solve2D_chunks and the stream pool do the same). The parameters default mass is overridden. From refine2D iteration 3 on, every class with more than MINCLSPOPLIM (5) members is centred at reference preparation. calc_2Dref_offset calls calc_cavg_offset (simple_strategy2D_utils.f90), which:
    • band-passes the average from cenlp up to a high-pass of max(1.5 cenlp, the mask diameter in Å);
    • applies the hard mask and zeroes the negative values;
    • thresholds with Otsu and keeps the largest connected component;
    • takes that component's unweighted centroid (masscen_cc).

    An offset of at least CENTHRESH (0.5 px) is applied to the reference and added to the class's members (prep2Dref, add_shift2class). The carried even/odd sums get it through cavger_set_center_offset. solve2D's first stage runs with center=no. Chunk runs inside solve2D_chunks set center=no. Classes of 5 or fewer members are never centred. - The label is therefore the centroid of the thresholded support of the particle's class average, not an intensity-weighted centre of mass. It does not depend on the class beyond the class's own projection, so it can be learned, and it is the frame the class student's teachers were trained in. - The shift's sign. A particle image is centred by shifting it by minus its stored shift: the class averager calls shift2Dserial(-shift) (simple_classaverager_restore.f90). The stored shift is therefore the centre's displacement from the box's origin, pixel N/2+1 on SIMPLE's even grid, in native pixels. - A fresh particle record holds shsig = 0, not the sentinel. Particle parameters initialise to zero (simple_ori.f90, pparms). shift_needs_disk(0, trs) is false, so an untouched record would be searched over the window. predict_classes therefore resets every particle's state to the sentinel before its alignment (simple_strategy_class_student.f90). shiftmap_srch%prep gives the pick prior's weight only to a particle with shsig < 0, so a finite width also switches the prior off, even when the disk is searched. - record_shift_state is not a unit conversion. Its next_shift_width inflates the width when the move reached the searched radius: a posterior truncated at a bound says nothing of the mass beyond it. - The held-out splitter (student_holdout_split) holds out stacks, and falls back to every tenth selected particle when there are fewer than ten stacks. It does not guarantee that whole micrographs are held out. - The side-artifact pattern exists. simple_class_candidates is a file registered in the project's out segment and tied to the particle rows by ptcl_layout_digest. It is refused when the rows or the project differ, and the consumer then runs as without it. - validate_class_student already has the read at the reference's shifts (its predict_dense step: the network at the full search's shifts, no alignment). That is the σ = 0 point of the tolerance curve in Phase 0. - The phase flip exists (image%apply_ctf(tfun, 'flip', ctfparms), used by the class averager). lpstages_setlims is not the crop policy here: it targets max(2 Å, lp/3) but imposes an 88-pixel floor and FFT rounding. Decision 2 sets the center student's own crop. - No real-space network exists in SIMPLE. The HHCNN's machinery is the pattern to copy, not a framework to generalise: im2col plus sgemm (team-safe BLAS, explicit loops otherwise), the chunked deterministic reduction, Adam and the weights file with a magic number and a version.

    Decisions¶

    1. (2026-11-18, Hans and Claude; R3) The label is the teacher's final shift, as stored, from solve2D on nsample. No correction for each class's offset is needed: solve2D's seg centring already puts the members in the class-free support-centroid frame. Training rows are the teacher's particles in classes of more than MINCLSPOPLIM members (the others were never centred). The label is the centre's displacement from the origin pixel N/2+1 of the box as extracted, in Å (native pixels times smpd), with the stored shift's sign. Phase 0 checks the frame on the final class averages (decision 10 (d)).
    2. The input frame. The particle's box as extracted, with no stored shift applied. It is phase-flipped and low-passed at lp_in = 12 Å (the pass band of center_ptcls, measured best there). It is Fourier-cropped to box_in = the even number nearest box·smpd/4 Å, so that the realised sampling is smpd_in = box·smpd/box_in, recorded per set. It is normalised to zero mean and unit standard deviation over the background outside mskdiam. Phase 1 compares a 3 Å target and freezes one. The preprocessing is written once, in Fortran:
    3. as Phase 1's exporter, a variant patch that writes the network's input tensors;
    4. moved into the tree unchanged in Phase 3.

    So the prototype and the port read identical inputs. The label is converted to the heatmap's pixels with the realised smpd_in, never with the target 4 Å. 3. The output: a heatmap over a support disk, plus an outside bin. One logit per pixel inside the support disk of radius R_s = min(60 Å, the box's half width − 2 px), about the origin pixel. One more logit means "the centre lies outside the support". The softmax runs over all of them. 60 Å is 1.5 times the 99th percentile of the picking error (guessed). Phase 0 measures the share of labels beyond R_s per set. - A training label within R_s − 2σ_target gets the Gaussian target of decision 4. A label beyond it gets all its target mass on the outside bin. - Nothing is renormalised onto the disk, so a centre beyond the support cannot come out as a narrow peak at the edge. - Regressing (dx, dy) directly was considered and not chosen: under noise it shrinks the estimate toward the pick and has no width. 4. The target is a normalised Gaussian over the support's pixels about the label, of width σ_target = max(σ_label, half a pixel of smpd_in). The loss is the cross-entropy of the softmax against it. σ_label starts at 1.5 Å, from the reproducibility of two converged solve2D runs. That figure assumes the two runs' errors are independent, isotropic, Gaussian, unbiased and of equal variance: - P(|d| < 3 Å) = 1 − exp(−9/(2s²)) gives s = 1.96 Å (trpv1, 0.69) and 1.78 Å (bgal, 0.76); - a single run's per-axis noise is then s/√2 = 1.39 and 1.26 Å.

    Phase 0 re-measures it from the full difference distribution, not one radius, and freezes it. The target is a smoothing of hard labels, not the physical posterior. The width's meaning comes from decision 5's calibration, not from the target. 5. The readout and the commit. - Estimate. ŝ is the posterior mean over the 5 × 5 support pixels about the pixel of largest mass, with the outside bin left out. - Width about the estimate. σ_c = sqrt(½ Σ_x p(x) |x − ŝ|²) over the support, after one temperature T scales all the logits. T is fitted by negative log-likelihood of the yardstick's centres on the calibration cohort (decision 8) and frozen with the model. σ_c is the root-mean-square distance of the posterior from the committed estimate, not the spread about the posterior's mean. Two modes 12 Å apart with masses 0.51 and 0.49 give σ_c ≈ 5.9 Å. - Validity. A prediction is invalid when the outside bin's mass is at least 0.5, or when ŝ lies within 2σ_target of the support's edge (guessed, frozen in Phase 1). An invalid prediction leaves the stored shift unchanged and writes the sentinel. It is counted as a fallback and is never dropped from a read. - Commit. Absolute and replacing: the stored shift becomes ŝ in native pixels (divided by smpd), and shincr is the length of the change. The second-pass variant (Phase 1) prepares the input at the first estimate ŝ₁ (shifted by −ŝ₁, as SIMPLE centres an image) and commits ŝ₁ + ŝ₂. The network is the same, since a re-prepared box is a translated box (decision 6). - Width in the record. In the default route the record holds the sentinel, and σ_c goes to the prediction table only. A width is written only under decision 9's adaptive route. 6. The network: a dilated stride-1 trunk with circular padding (guessed sizes; Phase 1 may change them within 2× and freezes them). - Seven 3 × 3 convolutions of 16 channels with ReLU, dilations 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 1, 1, and a 1 × 1 head. - Receptive field 1 + 2·(1+2+4+8+16+1+1) = 67 px, about 270 Å at 4 Å. - About 1.4 × 10⁴ weights and about 6 × 10⁷ multiply-adds per particle at box_in 64. - With circular padding the trunk is exactly equivariant to whole-pixel circular translations: a translated input gives the translated logit map, at any position. - The fixed support and outside bin break equivariance only at the support's edge. That edge is tested separately, as the validity rule. - Real kernels, and the same deterministic im2col products and chunked reduction as simple_hhcnn. - A pooled encoder-decoder (stride-2 pooling with upsampling, cheaper but only approximately equivariant) is a Phase 1 variant. If it is chosen, its equivariance is measured over every pooling phase and over shifts of a quarter and half a pixel, and frozen as a tolerance. 7. Augmentation. The eight elements of D4, exact on the pixel grid and unbiased since the in-plane angles are uniform. Each is an affine index map about the origin pixel N/2+1 with circular wrap (i → N+2−i mod N for a reflection), applied to the image. The label gets the same linear map. A plain array reversal would move the origin by one pixel and is not used. There is no translation augmentation in the base design: the real picks' displacements supply it. A circular translation over 10 Å with a cosine-tapered box edge (so that the seam is smooth) is a Phase 1 variant. 8. Cohorts, frozen in Phase 0 before any training.     9. Review, 2026-11-18¶

    Reviewed against clean master 8a690cc19, including the current shift-state producer and consumer, particle preparation, class centring, held-out splitter and the previous raw-pick experiment. The findings below are review recommendations, not changes to R1 to R6. No implementation or experiment was launched. The per-data-set, prototype-first scope is sound; the proposal needs the following contract corrections before implementation.

    1. P1: removing the reset does not preserve the raw-pick route (decision 9). Fresh particle records initialise their static fields to zero (simple_ori.f90, pparms); import does not generally set shsig=-1. The current predict_classes_exec explicitly sets that sentinel, whereas shift_needs_disk(0, trs) is false. Removing the reset therefore sends an ordinary imported project to the window without any center-student prediction. Keeping the reset in the validation's raw arm hides this regression. Preserve raw-pick behaviour unless valid centring state is established, with an explicit contract for old/imported records and prediction provenance. Test a direct predict_classes call on a fresh import, a student-centred project, a legacy zero-width record and a mixed population. Import-wide sentinel changes would widen R4's scope and must not be assumed by this plan.

    2. P1: absolute labels and additive commits use different frames (decisions 1, 2 and 5). Reading the raw image and targeting the final stored teacher shift predicts an absolute displacement from the extraction origin. Adding that prediction to an existing shift counts the existing displacement twice. Choose one explicit contract: predict absolute shifts from raw input and replace the stored shift, or prepare input at the existing shift and train the residual teacher_shift - input_shift, then add it back. In both cases shincr is the actual change of the stored shift. Define the heatmap origin, axis order, sign and conversion through the actual Fourier-crop sampling. SIMPLE applies minus the stored shift when preparing an image (simple_matcher_2Dprep). Include nonzero input shifts, repeated calls, the second-pass variant and non-integral sampling ratios in the producer/consumer tests. On an even grid SIMPLE's origin is pixel N/2+1; ordinary array reversal moves that pixel by one, so D4 image and label transforms need the same affine pixel mapping, not just a vector rotation.

    3. P1: the output disk censors exactly the errors whose uncertainty must stay large (decisions 3 to 5 and 7). The proposal itself reports roughly 40 A errors at the 99th percentile, outside its 30 A output radius; augmentation can move further labels outside. A Gaussian renormalised on that disk can become narrow at its boundary despite a large true residual. An independent calculation on the proposed 4 A grid, with target sigma 2 A and label (40,0) A, gives mean (28,0) A and sqrt(trace(Cov)/2)=1.31 A: approximately 12 A error with an apparently precise width. Define support for real and augmented labels, an out-of-support policy, and a boundary/invalid-prediction fallback. record_shift_state is not merely a unit conversion: its next_shift_width deliberately inflates the width at a search boundary. The new readout needs equivalent protection tied to its own support. Do not silently drop tail particles from evaluation or score clipped labels as the truth.

    4. P2: the proposed network is not exactly translation-equivariant (decision 6; Phase 2). Stride-two average pooling followed by nearest-neighbour upsampling already fails one-pixel equivariance away from boundaries; additive skips do not guarantee its restoration. Four pools retain, at best, a coarser translation subgroup before boundary effects. The fixed output disk also prevents unrestricted equivariance of the normalised heatmap. Either use an architecture with the required property or describe approximate equivariance and measure centre/width stability over every pooling phase and representative subpixel shifts. Separate a trunk test from masking/readout tests. Also freeze padding and skip alignment for boxes not divisible by 16. Zero-mean background does not remove the seam introduced by a circular translation; test the boundary convention and particle margin.

    5. P2: the width is not yet a calibrated uncertainty about the committed centre (decisions 4, 5, 8 and 12). Cross-entropy against Gaussian-smoothed hard shifts learns a smoothed label distribution, not automatically the physical posterior. The label-noise estimate assumes independent, equal-variance, unbiased Gaussian run errors; agreement at one radius does not establish those assumptions. Moreover, covariance about the whole posterior's mean is not the squared error about the committed local-mode mean. The 0.865 coverage target applies to an isotropic Gaussian centred at the estimate, not an arbitrary multimodal heatmap. For masses 0.51 and 0.49 at centres 12 A apart, this readout commits the first mode, reports sigma 4.24 A, and contains only 0.51 of the mass within twice that sigma. Specify the calibration operation and freeze it on calibration data. Measure actual search-window escape rates conditional on being sent to the window, including anisotropic, multimodal, boundary and low-signal cases. Distinguish report-only heatmap coverage from evidence sufficient to enable window routing. A finite stored width also disables the pick prior even when the disk is selected (shiftmap_srch%prep), so Phase 4 changes both the starting position and search policy, not just disk versus window.

    6. P2: the verdicts do not establish non-inferiority (decision 13). Defining worse as a loss beyond two SE makes a noisy or underpowered comparison count as "not worse"; for example a four-point loss with SE three points passes that rule. Predeclare an acceptable loss and require its one-sided confidence bound to satisfy that margin, or label these outcomes inconclusive rather than equivalent. Make the go/negative/stop branches mutually exclusive: as written, a network indistinguishable from both C and B0 can satisfy both the go and negative clauses of 13(b). This recommendation leaves costs report-only, as ruled.

    7. P2: an aggregate two-SE comparison cannot validate the Fortran port (13(c)). A wrong transpose, padding, target transform or optimiser can stay inside between-seed performance variation. Freeze a prototype model and inputs and compare preprocessing, intermediate activations/logits, probabilities, readouts, loss, gradients and an Adam update against the same weights with justified floating-point tolerances. Then compare independently trained models statistically. Seed spread is an efficacy measure, not an implementation-parity tolerance.

    8. P2: freeze selection and evaluation cohorts before tuning (decisions 8, 11; Phase 1). The proposal judges variants by reads on the unseen particles but does not reserve a final cohort untouched by variant selection. Use separate selection/calibration and final evaluation roles, or label the prototype reads exploratory and confirm the selected design on untouched data. The existing helper holds out stacks, and falls back to every tenth selected particle when fewer than ten stacks occur; it does not guarantee a micrograph holdout. Record the actual grouping and fallback, and use micrograph identities if micrograph generalisation is claimed.

    Before paying for a Fortran port, the off-tree prototype should also export its predictions back to the existing class-student validation. Compare raw+disk, centred+disk and centred+width-selected search on the same teacher and particles. The earlier experiment already found a centred route worse downstream, so matching center_ptcls within 5 A alone does not establish value for the stated consumer. The extra centred+disk arm separates the centres' effect from the width/search-policy effect. Keep this within R4's class-student scope.

    Other corrections before launch: the oracle pass is a strong measured baseline, not a proven information ceiling; two-run agreement is reproducibility, not an accuracy floor. "Before any 2D" and "without references" apply to prediction after a solve2D teacher and training have already been paid for. lpstages_setlims targets 4 A at 12 A but also imposes a minimum 88-pixel working box and FFT-size rounding; it does not yield exactly 4 A or a 48-to-64 pixel box in general. Specify the CNN's crop policy and record its realised sampling. The new high-level test also needs its simple_test_registry.f90 row, CTest registration and corresponding process-budget update, missing from the file table; "the CTest list (if needed)" is insufficient for a new high-level entry.

    Validation of this review: source tracing and independent standard-library Python calculations for the truncated Gaussian, two-mode coverage, pooling translation counterexample and even-grid reflection. These are design probes, not compiled SIMPLE tests or measured model performance. No build, fast gate, training, real-data or Linux/BOX run was performed. All eight findings and their implementation/experimental checks remain open.

    10. Response to the review (2026-11-18, Claude)¶

    Every finding is accepted, and the decisions above are rewritten in place (their numbers kept). Where each now stands:

    1. P1, the reset (decision 9). The default route no longer touches predict_classes: centred + disk keeps the reset, so every particle gets the disk under the pick prior about its new start. Narrowing the search is a separate, earned option (verdict 13 (d)). It reads the width only through a center_state artifact tied to the particle rows, never from a bare record. A fresh import stays byte-identical to the base: this is a contract item, a stop and a gate no ruling may waive. The five tests named in the review are decision 9's. The import defaults stay out of scope (section 4).
    2. P1, the frames (decisions 1, 2 and 5). Absolute prediction from the box as extracted, replacing the stored shift. shincr is the actual change. The origin is pixel N/2+1, and the sign is the stored shift's (an image is centred by minus its shift). The conversion uses the realised smpd_in. The second pass re-prepares the input at the first estimate and commits the sum. The D4 maps are affine about the origin with circular wrap. Phase 1's exporter checks a planted shift, the D4 maps and a non-integral crop before any training.
    3. P1, the support (decisions 3 and 5). The support is 60 Å, 1.5 times the 99th percentile, capped by the box. An outside bin receives the target mass of labels beyond the support, so nothing is renormalised onto the disk. A validity rule (outside mass at least 0.5, or an estimate within 2σ_target of the edge) falls back to the stored shift and the sentinel. This is the counterpart of next_shift_width's inflation at a bound. Fallbacks are counted and scored at their raw position, never dropped. Phase 0 reports the share of labels beyond the support.
    4. P2, equivariance (decision 6, Phase 2). The base design is now a dilated stride-1 trunk with circular padding, exactly equivariant to circular whole-pixel translations, at about four times the pooled network's multiply-adds. The support and outside bin are tested apart from the trunk. Translation augmentation is dropped from the base; the tapered variant is Phase 1's. A pooled encoder-decoder is a variant only, with its equivariance measured over every pooling phase and over shifts of a quarter and half a pixel.
    5. P2, the width (decisions 4, 5 and 12). The width is now taken about the committed estimate (the two-mode example gives 5.9 Å, not 4.2) and calibrated by one temperature fitted on cohort S. It is read as a reliability curve, not against a Gaussian's 0.865. Enabling the window needs the escape rate's upper bound to be at most ε, split by low signal, several modes, anisotropy and edge. The default route writes the sentinel, so the pick prior stays on. The adaptive route writes a width only for particles it sends to the window.
    6. P2, non-inferiority (decisions 13 and 14). The margins are declared (δ5, δ_agree, δ_FRC, ε) and judged by one-sided 95% bounds over the eight paired seeds. Each verdict's branches are ordered, so exactly one applies; anything else is inconclusive and stops.
    7. P2, port parity (13 (e)). Layer-wise parity against a frozen prototype model and frozen inputs: preprocessing, activations, logits, probabilities, readouts, loss, gradients and one Adam step, within decision 14's tolerances. Only then the statistical comparison. The preprocessing is the same Fortran code in both (decision 2).
    8. P2, cohorts (decision 8). Micrograph-level cohorts T, S and E are frozen in Phase 0. Variants are chosen on S, the frozen design is read once on E, and reading E early is a stop. The splitter's actual grouping and fallback are recorded, and the micrograph identity is checked.

The prototype's centres go through the existing class-student validation as raw + disk, centred + disk and centred + adaptive, on the same teacher and particles. Matching center_ptcls within 5 Å (13 (b)) is necessary but not enough. If only the adaptive arm wins, the run stops, because the gain would then belong to the search policy, not the centres.
