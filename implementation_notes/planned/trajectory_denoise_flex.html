    trajectory_denoise on the flex kernel: every frame a pre-image of the frames that look like it¶

    Implementation note, 2026-10-26. Implemented; the default pca_mode of trajectory_denoise since 2026-10-26 (the maintainer's call after a first real trajectory; the previous default was diffusion_maps). Written against 7d8586b88 without a compiler at hand; the maintainer builds. First look on real data: high-resolution detail better preserved than with ppca, ppca stronger at low resolution. Sections 1-3 are the contract and the method as implemented, section 4 the toy behind the choices, section 5 how to run and tune it on real data, sections 6-7 validation and open points. The toy is a numpy script outside the repository (claude.ai project SIMPLE, claude/trajectory_denoise_flex_toy.py).

    single_exec prg=trajectory_denoise stk=traj.mrcs smpd=0.358 nthr=16 [pca_mode=flex] \
    [neigs=32] [halfwin=0] [eo_pool=yes] [lp=<A>] [mskdiam=<A>] [outstk=trajectory_denoised.mrcs]

    1. Why¶

    trajectory_denoise runs commander_ppca_denoise; the visually best mode is pca_mode=ppca: Tipping-Bishop PPCA on the real-space pixels of all frames, neigs=200 by the commander's default, every frame replaced by avg + W E[z|y]. One linear subspace has to describe every pose and state of the whole trajectory, so it cannot hold each frame's own high-resolution detail, and a basis fitted to the noisy frames it then denoises carries noise into every frame (with N frames < D pixels the sample eigenvalues are inflated: more components keep more noise; at 200 components the toy's error is 44% of the raw noise energy).

    pca_mode=flex keeps PPCA's strength, the whole trajectory informs every frame, and drops the subspace as the image: the flex class model is only the metric that says which frames look alike; the image of a frame is a weighted average of the data of those frames.

    2. Contract¶

    • In: stk (rows are frames in time order), smpd; neigs (largest latent rank the CV may choose, default 32), halfwin (0, the default: the whole trajectory pools; > 0: windows), eo_pool (default yes), optional lp (the fit band), optional mskdiam (the similarity mask), nthr. No project, no CTF (SINGLE trajectories are ctf=no).
    • Out: outstk with the input's row count, box, sampling and row order, and trajectory_denoise_flex.txt (per frame: the Kish population of its weights and the CV's rank, tau, h).
    • No alignment. Frames are used exactly as given: no shift, rotation, interpolation or re-centring. Every output row is in its input row's pose. Pose differences between frames stay in the latent (no pose nuisance columns), so the kernel pools only frames that look alike, pose included.
    • Row t is a weighted average of input rows (only rows of t's parity with eo_pool=yes); row t's own weight is the expected weight of a frame with its clean image (section 3, step 8). A frame no other frame resembles comes back as itself: nothing is borrowed that the kernel does not support.
    • eo_pool=yes: even and odd output rows share no input row. The halves of an imported stack are row parity (simple_eo_partition: a stack row without a micrograph row takes the per-index route), so the 3D half-sets of the denoised stack stay independent. eo_pool=no pools across parity (more neighbours, for viewing and 2D only).
    • No output mask. The mask shapes the similarity only: the models, latents, noise spectrum, fit band and CV see soft-masked frames (mskdiam when given, at most the box's inscribed circle; else the inscribed circle), the pre-images average the frames themselves, so every output row is the whole box. Outside the mask the weights are the ones the particle earned, so the background is averaged over frames whose particle looks alike. Each frame's mean is removed before and restored after.
    • Deterministic for a given input (the models are seeded per set).

    3. Method as implemented¶

    Files: src/main/flex/simple_flex_traj_denoise.f90 (new, arrays in and out, outside the FLEX-PCA layering like the cls kernel); exec_trajectory_denoise routes pca_mode=flex to trajectory_denoise_flex in single_commanders_trajectory.f90 (stack I/O); keys halfwin, eo_pool (simple_parameters*.f90, the UI).

    1. Planes. Each frame minus its mean, Fourier transformed on the half-plane lattice of flex_class_planes (shells 1..nyq, wq = 2; c = 1), twice: unmasked (yu, what the pre-images average) and soft-masked (ym, mask2D_soft at radius min(box/2 - 2, mskdiam / 2 smpd), what every similarity step sees).
    2. Noise. Per shell, the median over consecutive frame pairs of |y_t - y_t+1|^2 / 2: model-free and robust to events; it includes the signal change between consecutive frames, so it errs on the high side. One spectrum for the trajectory; noise weights w = 1 / s2(shell), the same for every frame.
    3. Fit band. lp when given; otherwise up to the last shell whose spectral SNR (mean frame power over the noise spectrum, minus one) reaches 0.1, gaps allowed (lattice fringes sit beyond shells of weak signal), at least 6 shells. Split into two coefficient sets A and B, Fourier blocks in a checkerboard (as the cls kernel). The mask correlates each coefficient with its neighbours over about box / radius pixels, so the block side grows with the mask: nint(2 box / radius), between 4 (the inscribed circle) and 16; logged as split block.
    4. Sets. halfwin=0: one set, the whole trajectory, every frame a candidate of every frame. halfwin>0: blocks of 10 frames, each with the frames within halfwin of its centre as its set (the model, CV and choice per block).
    5. Models. flex_cls_fit on set A and on set B of the fit band, rank min(neigs, frames - 2, coefficients/3 - 1), one nuisance column, the set mean (contrast, dose).
    6. Latents. ML latents with the nuisance profiled out. Because c and w are the same for every frame, every frame's latent has the same covariance: whitened by it (unit noise per coordinate) and rotated to decreasing scatter. The kernel is then Euclidean: w_ij = exp(-|zeta_i - zeta_j|^2_r / 2(2 + tau^2)) * exp(-(t_i - t_j)^2 / 2h^2), over the leading r directions; tau < 0: no latent term, h < 0: no temporal term.
    7. Cross-validation. Rank r in {1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128} up to the models' rank, tau in {0, .25, .5, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, none} (noise units), h in {2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, none} frames, jointly. Set A's latents weigh the other frames (the validation frame left out, only its parity with eo_pool=yes) to predict its set-B coefficients, and vice versa; whitened error per held-out coefficient (1 is the noise floor). At most 1000 validation frames and 1000 held-out coefficients per set (strided). The predictions are single precision matrix products (sgemm), each thread its own block of validation frames through the whole grid, all temporal bandwidths of a (rank, tau) in one product. How the products run is decided once per run, with no environment variable: a probe compares the process CPU time of a few serial-region sgemm calls with their wall time. A serial BLAS (e.g. RHEL's libopenblas): blocks of validation frames, one or more per thread, each its own products. A threaded BLAS (an OpenMP OpenBLAS such as the bundled build, MKL, a pthreads OpenBLAS): wide blocks one after the other, the products from the serial region, the kernels around them in parallel; inside a team (halfwin>0) the BLAS when the build knows it stays on the calling thread there (SIMPLE_BLAS_TEAM_SAFE, see blas_openmp_policy.md), else the compiler's matmul. The log line Trajectory flex: BLAS cpu/wall says which. The optimum over the whole grid is the choice, the parity mean (tau and h none) included; z is its paired per-frame gain over that mean.
    8. Pre-images. With the weights the CV scored: the fit band's set-A coefficients averaged with weights from the set-B model's latents and vice versa (no selection bias, so no Stein term), every other coefficient with the mean of the two normalised weight rows. A frame's own weight is not exp(0): a frame with the same clean image would sit at squared distance 2 per whitened direction from it (two frames' noise), so the frame gets that twin's expected weight, ((2 + tau^2) / (4 + tau^2))^(r/2); at weight 1 its own noise dominates at high rank (in the toy a rank-64 choice kept a third of the noise although the CV, which leaves the frame out, looked good). The means are matrix products over chunks of coefficients (serial BLAS: at most 128, one product per thread; threaded BLAS: 512, from the serial region).
    9. Readouts. Trajectory flex: the band, the noise spectrum; Trajectory flex CV by temporal bandwidth, by latent rank, by tau (the curves); Trajectory flex set= the choice, CV, parity-mean CV, z, mean neff; at the end neff min/median/max. Per frame in trajectory_denoise_flex.txt.

    Cost, whole trajectory of N frames: two model fits on half the band; per CV direction about 440 weight settings, each a 1000 x N x 2000 single-precision product (a few minutes for N of several thousand on one node); the pre-images three N x N weight matrices (3 x 4 N^2 bytes) and an N x N x 2 ncoeff product. Memory is dominated by the frames' coefficients (8 bytes x ncoeff x N, three times: masked, unmasked, pre-images; the first two are freed before the output is written).

    4. Toy evidence¶

    An fcc cluster (249 Gaussian atoms, a = 6 px, radius 15 px) in a 64 box; 600 frames in four segments tumbling at 0.1, 0.3, 0.8 and 0.05 deg/frame plus rotational diffusion, a random reorientation at each segment boundary, halfway through the last segment a 25-atom facet cap leaves at once; tracking jitter 0.7 px rms, uncorrected; white noise at SNR 0.2 and 1.0 (signal variance in the particle disc over noise variance). "Revisited": the last segment retraces the first one backwards (each view perturbed by about 1 deg), no facet loss. Frames used as given; same parity pooling; the twin weight of step 8. err = error energy over raw noise energy inside r = 24 px (raw = 1). Global PPCA is the converged closed form of simple_ppca (Tipping-Bishop ML noise, posterior mean).

    err SNR 0.2 SNR 0.2, revisited SNR 1.0 SNR 1.0, revisited
    ppca, 200 components 0.444 0.443 0.455 0.453
    PPCA at its best rank (10, known only from the truth) 0.049 0.047 0.091 0.081
    flex, window +-40 (halfwin=40) 0.048 0.049 0.113 0.117
    flex, whole trajectory (halfwin=0) 0.032 0.032 0.121 0.117
    SNR 1.0, standard trajectory FRC 0.5 against the truth FRC at the lattice fringes (raw 0.435)
    PPCA, best rank 5.3 px 0.40
    flex, window 3.4 px 0.49
    flex, whole trajectory 3.4 px 0.50

    Reading:

    • Against ppca as it runs, 4-14 times less error. Against PPCA at its best rank: a third less at SNR 0.2; at SNR 1.0 more total error (the global subspace is excellent at low resolution) and, on the standard trajectory, clearly more high-resolution detail (FRC 0.5 at 3.4 against 5.3 px); where views recur PPCA's subspace is as sharp (FRC 0.5 at 3.1 px against 3.1 for the window and 4.0 for the whole trajectory).
    • At SNR 0.2 the whole trajectory beats the window by a third; the CV switches the temporal kernel off (h none) and 80% of each frame's weight comes from frames more than 40 frames away. At SNR 1.0 the window is 7% better where the particle tumbles fast (a local model spends its directions on the local differences) and the two tie when views recur. Choosing the bandwidth locally with whole-trajectory candidates did not help (it fits the choice to noise), and ranks up to 128 were offered: the CV took 32 or 8.
    • Same-parity pooling costs 10-35% in error (half the neighbours) and removes all cross-half noise (the leak, the share of an output's noise variance from the other parity, is 0.42-0.50 without it).
    • Without alignment the latent is what keeps the detail: at SNR 1.0 time-only smoothing halves the fringe FRC of the latent kernel. The ceiling is the tracking jitter: correcting the frames' shifts alone (0.25 px error) cut the window variant's error by a third (SNR 0.2) to almost a half (SNR 1.0); not part of this design.

    Caveats: white noise, no CTF, no graphene, a near-spherical particle, 600 frames at box 64, one seed; SIMPLE's PPCA runs 15 EM iterations from a random start where the toy uses the converged solution; the toy's latent bandwidth is in prior units, the code's in noise units.

    5. Running it on real data¶

    Start with the defaults; compare against pca_mode=ppca on the same stack (montage of frames; then analysis2D_nano). Then, guided by the log:

    look at meaning adjust

    7. Open¶

    • The twin weight in the cls kernel route. flex_cls_kernel%preimage and the representatives weigh their own member at exp(0) as well; for a representative's average that keeps its own noise in it. Worth the same correction there.
    • Tracking jitter sets the ceiling without alignment (section 4); the tracker's sub-pixel positions as a phase shift would use no search and no interpolation. The owner's call.
    • Noise calibration. The frame-difference spectrum is conservative; the CV picks the bandwidths regardless.
    • Fit band threshold (0.1), to be settled on real data.
    • Low resolution. ppca's global subspace is strong at low resolution (as in the toy at SNR 1.0). A per-band choice (the CV run per resolution band, low shells with the wider bandwidths) would give each band its own pooling; not tried.
    • Window and whole trajectory together: a local model as the metric with whole-trajectory candidates would take the best of both rows of section 4; not tried.
    • Junk axis. Junk frames only lower their weights through their latents.
    • Cost for long trajectories: a sparse kernel (nearest neighbours in the latent) once N reaches tens of thousands.
