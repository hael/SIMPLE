    Species discovery: where it stands and where to go next¶

    Status note, 2026-10-12 (links updated 2026-10-20 after the note was split into completed and unfinished parts), at master 618d709ec. It is the entry point for picking the work up again after a break and does not repeat the design. Three other documents hold the detail:

    • ../completed/species_discovery_completed.md (the note): the design, every ruling, and the record of phases 0 to 8.
    • species_discovery_unfinished.md: the open work (phase 9, validation, promotion, open questions), self-contained.
    • ../completed/species_discovery_report.md (the report): the reports of the unattended runs.
    • atom_bfactor_model.md: the follow-on work on per-atom B factors and occupancy.

    What exists¶

    detect_atoms (SINGLE) has an opt-in species discovery. A run without the new keys behaves as before.

    • element may be left out. Atoms are then detected with a Gaussian template instead of an element kernel.
    • discover_species=yes adds two steps after the present detection.
    • First, weaker atoms are looked for in the residual map (the map minus the model of the atoms already found). They are kept only if they pass the same contact-score pruning as the present detection. These are the recovered atoms.
    • Second, every atom's aperture intensity is measured: the density summed in a sphere of half the nearest-neighbour distance. The atoms are then sorted into one to three intensity classes, and each class is a candidate species. nspecies fixes the number of classes; 0 lets the data choose.
    • element=Pt,Ni gives the species as a list, brightest first. The number of classes is then fixed, and the names go into the products.
    • A two-class result is admissible when both classes are large enough and their means are at least three pooled spreads apart (D >= 3).
    • Since 618d709ec the class fit has one more term. Intensity rises linearly with the atom's coordination number (its number of neighbours), with one slope shared by all classes. The slope is fitted with the classes and written to _species.txt as coord_slope.
    • Discovery writes four extra files: _species.csv (one row per atom), _species_radial.csv, _species.txt (the summary) and _species.pdb (class in the element column). They are diagnostics only: the five existing products, _ATMS.pdb, _CC.mrc and _SIM.mrc among them, are identical with and without discovery.

    The same work also produced these changes:

    • B factors in the simulator's element kernels (pdb_bfac=yes on simulate_nanoparticle).
    • Compound-crystal lattices for CdSeW, CdSeZ and CdSeR.
    • A fix of the automatic mask of detect_atoms, which covered the whole box on unmasked maps.
    • The test seed fixed inside simple_test_exec.

    The commits:

    • d3480ae79: phases 0 to 3, the first pass.
    • 29808bf3e: phases 4 and 5, species lists, element kernels, compound lattices.
    • 0029ed77e: aperture intensity at one common width; the test seed.
    • c28bfc101: the mask fix.
    • 618d709ec: the coordination term; the core case's label check back; extract_atom copies every field.

    Code:

    • src/main/nano/simple_nano_species.f90: the class fit, fit_species_mixture, on the clustering library's extreme-deconvolution mixture simple_xd_gmm.
    • src/main/nano/simple_nanoparticle.f90: discover_species, which runs the recovery, the intensities and the products.
    • src/main/nano/simple_atoms.f90: pseudo-atoms, species lists, lattices.

    Tests:

    • the species sub-suite of unit_single;
    • the high-level species_discovery;
    • four single_atoms_stats entries, one for Pt and one for each CdSe selector.

    What the tests and the real maps show¶

    Synthetic particles (species_discovery). Each case is a 285-atom particle, rendered with element kernels, filtered as a reconstruction is, with noise shaped like a real map's. The four cases are pure Pt, Pt/Ni mixed at random, a Ni core under a Pt skin, and Pt with a light species (Al).

    On 2026-10-12 every check passed:

    • no false atoms;
    • recall of 0.986 to 1.000 for both species;
    • every atom labelled with its element in all three mixed cases;
    • a two-class fit forced on pure Pt not admissible (D 0.55).

    The fitted intensity ratio of the classes is biased low: by 23% for Pt/Ni mixed, 6% for the Ni core and 43% for Pt/Al. The classes still come out in the right order. This bias comes from measuring on filtered maps and is a documented limitation. Phase 6 tried to remove it by fitting with a map-filtered kernel; that made things worse and was withdrawn on 2026-10-09. Don't try that form again.

    Real maps. The archive holds 66 Pt maps: 15 particles, each with 2 to 8 time points, all one species. The second validation run (2026-10-11, before the coordination term) found:

    • False species are rare. One class was found on 95.5% of the maps with element=Pt discover_species=yes, and on 97.0% with discovery but no element. element=Pt,Ni was rejected as a two-class fit on 62 of 66 maps.
    • The present detection matched the stored coordinates on 56 of 66 maps with element=Pt and on 47 without an element. A map matches when 98% of its stored atoms are found within 1 Å. The ten maps that fall short match 85% to 98%.
    • Sensitivity is poor. A weaker class was planted into 20% of the atoms at 0.6 of the Pt density, about Ni. It was called a second class on 4 maps; at 0.4 it was called on 21. The planted atoms were detected; they did not separate as a class.
    • The reason is the spread of real intensities. Real Pt atoms differ in aperture intensity by about twice the measurement noise. Most of that follows the atom's environment:
    • +3.8% per neighbour at the median map, 26% more at 12 neighbours than at 5;
    • the fitted width flat.

    Missing intensity without broadening is what partial occupancy of the outer sites gives. The synthetic particles show the opposite trend, a halo effect of the filtering, so they do not reproduce this.

    The coordination term was chosen on 2026-10-12 to absorb that trend. The numpy check used one real map (Pt_example, 476 atoms), with classes planted as above and eight seeds per level:

    • the 0.4 class was found on 8 of 8 seeds (recall 0.96, about 3 wrong labels);
    • the 0.6 class was found on 5 of 8 (recall 0.72);
    • no second class was called on the unplanted map or the noise control.

    On the synthetic particles it changed no label. In SIMPLE it has so far passed only the synthetic tests. It has not run on the real maps.

    What Miao's FePt data show (2026-10-14)¶

    Peter sent the material of the FePt nanoparticle of Yang et al., Nature 542, 75 (2017), from Jianwei Miao's group (UCLA). The particle was imaged by atomic electron tomography (AET): a tilt series of annular dark-field STEM images, reconstructed with their GENFIRE algorithm. The folder holds:

    • the raw tilt series: 68 projections of 300 × 300 pixels, from −65.6° to +64.0° about one axis;
    • their MATLAB code for atom tracing, species classification and position refinement;
    • the 29,325 traced peaks, each with its Gaussian fit and the 5 × 5 × 5 voxels around it;
    • the initial classification (23,804 atoms) and the final model (23,196 atoms: 6,569 Fe and 16,627 Pt; 0.3725 Å per voxel);
    • the reconstruction their classification used, FePt_reconstruction_volume.mat (256³, downloaded from the group's FePt page on 2026-10-14).

    The numbers below are measured on that volume, as their classification measures them. The voxels stored around each peak give the same numbers to within 0.1.

    What they show:

    • The same environment trend as our Pt maps. The intensity summed over the 3 × 3 × 3 voxels at each atom (a 1.1 Å cube) rises by 5.2% per neighbour, for Fe and for Pt alike. The fitted width stays almost flat; Fe is a little narrower at low coordination. This is a different microscope mode, in vacuum, with a different reconstruction method, so the trend is not specific to SINGLE or to the liquid cell.
    • The contrast is like ours. Pt over Fe is 1.7 in those sums, and 2.1 in the per-species amplitudes of their refinement. For comparison, Pt over Ni is 1.65 in our element kernels. The two classes overlap: their separation D is 2.6 overall and 2.7 among fully coordinated atoms, so our gate of D >= 3 would reject the split.
    • The surface composition depends on how the environment is modelled. In their model, atoms with 8 or fewer neighbours are 65 to 68% Fe; those with 12 are 22% Fe. A plain two-class fit to the intensities gives 86% Fe at the surface. The same fit with our coordination term gives 20%, about the bulk value. Intensity alone does not decide between these. The environment effect itself is real in their data. Fe-labelled atoms with 4 neighbours are at 0.64 of bulk Fe, and Pt mislabelled as Fe would have raised that value, not lowered it.
    • Their pipeline works as follows:
    • Peaks are traced greedily. Each fitted Gaussian is subtracted before the next peak is found, which is our residual recovery applied to every atom.
    • Species are assigned by iterating global 5 × 5 × 5 templates (L1 distance), starting from an intensity threshold. Labels are then edited by hand.
    • A chemistry prior flags Fe atoms near the missing wedge.
    • Positions are then refined against the projections, with one amplitude and one B per species and an interatomic potential as a restraint.

    Their pipeline has no environment term and no occupancy term. Between the initial classification and the final model, 2.4% of the labels changed, 91% of them from Pt to Fe. The change rate was 5 to 10% among atoms with 5 to 11 neighbours, against 1% at 12. Later papers use k-means with local templates (class means within 10 Å). On this volume that leaves the surface at 87% Fe.

    How their codes relate to ours, reconstruction included, is in aet_codes_and_simple.md.

    What this changes:

    • A real two-species map exists. The FePt reconstruction can be the first real two-species run of detect_atoms, with their model as a reference. It is not ground truth, and the microscope mode differs.
    • Species calls at low coordination are model-dependent. They are to be reported as such. Settling them needs evidence other than map intensity: the projections, or the particle images.
    • Real Pt/Ni data may be within reach. Jeong et al., Nat. Commun. 16, 8026 (2025), has AET tomograms of PtNi nanocatalysts. Peter Ercius (NCEM) is a co-author, so they are worth asking for.

    Next steps, in order¶

    1. Phase 9 on dell: the coordination term on the 66 maps. This is a measurement only, with no code change. The plan is in the note, section 10.11.
    2. The run spec is ~/agent_runs/runs/species_validation_coord/ (PHASES "9"). It reuses the second validation run's scripts, with LOCAL_REPO = ~/src/SIMPLE (now at or after 618d709ec).
    3. It repeats the five detect_atoms runs per map: Pt, Pt with discovery, Pt,Ni, no element, and no element with discovery. It also repeats the planting at 0.6, 0.4 and 0.25 and the noise control, with the same seeds.
    4. The expectations were stated before the run:
      • one class, and Pt,Ni rejected, on at least 95% of the unplanted maps;
      • the 0.4 class found with recall of at least 0.9 on at least 90% of the maps, with false labels on at most 2% of the unplanted atoms;
      • the noise control never called a class.
    5. The products stay in the run's scratch/keep/validation/ on dell. Rsync them back and make the figures on the Mac; dell has no numpy.
    6. The run also compares the species_discovery metrics with phase 7's copy. That copy is on dell and used the same test seeding.
    7. Decide on phase 9's result.

    8. If the expectations hold, the validation before promotion comes next (note, section 10.8). It has three parts:

      • particles passed through a real reconstruction's filtering and masking;
      • label reproducibility across real half maps;
      • an A/B of detect_atoms with and without discovery on a real Pt/Ni map, at matched false-positive rate.

      The archive is all Pt. Real two-species data now come from the AET work above: the FePt reconstruction, and possibly Peter's PtNi tomograms. Run detect_atoms with element=Pt,Fe on the FePt volume, with and without the coordination term, and compare with their model by coordination number. That needs no dell and can come before phase 9. Promotion itself comes after that: recovered atoms into _ATMS.pdb, classes into _SIM.mrc, element optional in the other SINGLE programs, and autorefine3D_nano on a two-species trajectory. - If the expectations don't hold, make occupancy explicit instead. The simple version calls species only on fully coordinated atoms (12 neighbours), where full occupancy can be assumed. It reports every other atom's intensity as an occupancy of its likeliest class. In the numpy check it was as sensitive as the coordination term on the atoms it labels, about half of them. The full version belongs to the B-factor and occupancy work. 3. B factors and occupancy (atom_bfactor_model.md), after species. The fitting method is open. Per-atom Gaussian fits on filtered maps invent a core-to-surface B rise: +4.4 Å² on a particle generated with one B. The preferred route measures a smooth B profile with depth in refine3D_nano. There references are matched to the particle images, so no map filtering is involved. An occupancy profile from that work could take over the role of the coordination slope.

    Smaller open items¶

    • Recovered atoms off the stored model. About 20 per real map sit one bond length from a stored atom, mostly in the outer half of the particle. They may be partly occupied sites or noise. Real half maps would tell, and the archive has none. On these boxes the expected number of noise maxima above the second-stage threshold is 2 to 4 per map. The first-stage threshold meets the target of 0.2.
    • The present detection is short on ten maps. ESC1_time28s depends on the mask. The automatic 40.1 Å (1.2 times the measured particle diameter) matches 85% of its stored atoms; 37.8 Å matched 99.8%. The factor 1.2 may deserve a look on non-spherical particles. On two other maps the measured diameter is 19% and 27% above the coordinate extent.
    • Pt,Ni was accepted on four real maps: EFC4_time03s, ESC4_time01s, ESIP1_time08s and ESIP2_time05s. That is one more than the 5% allowed. Phase 9 shows whether the coordination term removes them.
    • A light species confined to the surface layer is not a target (ruling of 2026-10-09). It cannot be told from weak, partly occupied surface sites.
    • The three CdSe single_atoms_stats entries were not run by hand after the coordination term. The next high-level ctest covers them.
    • single_workflow_wurtzite crashed in refine3D on 2026-10-09 (simple_strategy3D_srch.f90, subspace_inds past its bound). It crashed at the base revision too. It is not species work and has not been rechecked since.
    • The random seed. Production seed_rnd still honours the SIMPLE_SEED environment variable, against policy. The plan for an rnd_seed key is doc/refactoring_notes/planned/rnd_seed_parameter.md. It awaits review and needs no dell run.
    • The open questions of the note's section 13 are still open:
    • the atom ratio against the intensity ratio of the target alloys, and the signal-to-noise of the weakest visible atoms;
    • the class symbols in the PDB;
    • radius against depth as the radial coordinate;
    • the starting volume without an element;
    • the close-packed neighbour cutoff;
    • equal integrals within a class at the surface;
    • the false-atom target and the default of the neighbour gate (min_nbrs).

    The prototype took the recommended defaults. The Pt/Ni A/B is where they get judged.

    Lessons worth keeping¶

    • Compare the synthetic input with a real map before blaming the code on a synthetic failure. Phase 5 stopped when the present detection found 3 of 285 atoms on a fixture, although it works on real maps. The fixture was the defect: its B factors, surface-to-core peak ratio and noise spectrum were off, and it lacked the negative wells between atoms.
    • The contact-score pruning removes real densities on purpose. These are weak, poorly coordinated, high-B surface atoms. Discovery must not put them back, so recovered atoms are pruned against the present detection's atoms only.
    • Don't add fitted machinery to fix a secondary output. Phase 6 did, and made the intensity ratios worse.
    • Count the mixture fits before wrapping a search around them. Each tight fit can run hundreds of iterations, and the first version of the coordination term timed out unit_single.
