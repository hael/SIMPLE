    Learned pose and state assignment: a student distilled from the probabilistic search¶

    Proposal of 2026-11-05, revised on 2026-11-06 with Hans and after a methodology review. SIMPLE's matching machinery supplies per-dataset soft labels over 3D direction and state, and a small model learns to propose candidate poses for particles streaming off the microscope. Whether this saves time against the actual stream route is an acceptance measurement. Nothing is built. Sections 1 and 2 are the idea and what exists, sections 3 to 9 the design, section 10 the validation, section 11 the order of work, section 12 the open decisions for Hans, sections 13 to 15 the risks, the rules and the sources.

    1. What is proposed¶

    A pose student: a small network, trained per dataset on the probabilistic posterior of the dense 3D table over a fixed grid of (state, direction) classes. It reads a particle's CTF-multiplied polar Fourier transform in a low-resolution band and proposes a distribution over the grid. Circular convolutions and pooling provide approximate in-plane rotation invariance; shifts are learned as tolerance from the calculator's own phase ramps and found exactly by the shift map in the candidate search that follows. Two students, one per half-set, each assign the new particles of their own half, against their own half's references and spectrum. Their designs and gates are selected independently within that half, with micrograph-level development, calibration and untouched audit cohorts. A bounded candidate search recovers the full rotation and shift; an empirically calibrated confidence measures whether that seed has the right state and lies inside the local refinement's capture range. A row whose candidate coverage or confidence is insufficient takes the existing route. The stream baseline is sub-class assignment followed by continuous refinement, so an end-to-end speedup must include training, fallback and refinement. No new command-line key: the model file travels through infile and fname as the class-average quality model's does, and every threshold and constant that decided lives in the model file.

    What sets this apart from the published attempts, which learn in real space, is the polar implementation: the representation the search already uses, with the in-plane rotation a cyclic shift of the input, the translation handled exactly by the Fourier-Bessel shift map, the CTF applied as the search applies it, the sigma2 whitening of the objective, and the table's own posterior as the teacher. Everything the student sees and learns from is what the probabilistic search computes.

    The design's constraints: deterministic teacher scores, preserved target uncertainty, same-half production with declared upstream provenance, calibrated candidate coverage and full-seed confidence, and measured complete-stage savings. Sections 4, 7, 8 and 10 define the implementation and acceptance requirements.

    2. Where the idea stands¶

    2.1 Prior work¶

    • Unsupervised amortized pose inference (cryoPoseNet, cryoAI, cryoFIRE): an encoder learns the poses jointly with the map, with no labels. Encoders converge slowly and get stuck; the semi-amortized method of Shekarforoush et al. (NeurIPS 2024) and DRGN-AI both retreat to per-particle optimization after an amortized warm start. That is evidence against learning poses from scratch, not against the supervised setting.
    • Supervised pose assignment, which is this setting: the CESPED benchmark (Sanchez-Garcia et al., arXiv 2311.06194; the package is checked out at ~/src/cesped, section 2.4) and cryoPARES (Sanchez-Garcia et al., bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.03.04.641536). cryoPARES trains on a refined apo dataset and infers on ligand-bound datasets of the same protein; a U-Net feeds an Image2Sphere head that outputs a distribution over an SO(3) grid of 294,912 rotations; the CTF enters as a phase-flipped input channel; in-plane rotation and shift are learned by augmentation. Its numbers: network inference above 30,000 particles per minute per GPU; the raw top-1 pose within 5° of the RELION pose for about 30% of the particles, above 60% after a local refinement; resolutions 0.1 to 0.6 Å worse than RELION on six datasets; a direction-normalised confidence rejects junk as well as 2D classification does; two RELION runs disagree by more than 5° on over 20% of the particles. Trained per protein, up to 100 epochs on GPUs, checkpoints of 40 GB.
    • CESPED's own numbers: eight EMPIAR entries of at least 200,000 particles at 1.5 Å per pixel with poses re-estimated by RELION auto-refine, each split at random into two halves, a model trained on one half and judged on the other; its Image2Sphere baseline (an ImageNet ResNet152 on 256-pixel real-space images, an orthographic projection onto a hemisphere, S2 and SO(3) convolutions at lmax 8, a softmax over 294,912 rotations, phase-flipped input, no shift prediction) reaches mean angular errors of 13° to 45° per entry (28.7° over the benchmark) and maps from its poses 1.3 Å worse than the truth at FSC 0.143 and 3.4 Å at 0.5; a local refinement within 6° of its poses brings one entry's map correlation from 0.969 to 0.997 at a threefold speed-up over auto-refine. The same entry simulated gives 6.0° against 13.3° real, and consensus labels 8.3°: the labels and the noise set the error as much as the model.
    • Lessons taken: the raw pose is coarse and a local refinement supplies the precision; the confidence is worth as much as the pose; the student is judged on soft targets and downstream recovery; the model is dataset-specific, so it is trained per dataset and retrained when the references change.

    2.2 What SIMPLE has¶

    • The dense 3D table (src/main/strategies/search/probabilistic/simple_eul_prob_tab.f90): its grid is build%eulspace at params%nspace times the states; fill_tab_range fills a particle's row over every (state, direction) reference, drawing an in-plane rotation per reference from the row posterior at the particle's temperature (score_ref calls the calculator's gen_posterior_val, which requires objfun=euclid and takes a keyed variate) and polishing a selected subset in shift and rotation through the shift map. posterior_row_stats gives the normalised row's mode, pmax, neff and k99; top_posterior_extras supplies the weighted top-k. Skipping assign_batch therefore does not make the fill a deterministic teacher. The deterministic argmin over the in-plane grid at a given shift exists beside it, as the diagnostic posterior's path (deterministic_best over gen_best_objfun_val and gen_best_euclid_val_cross) under euclid. The registration pass of solve3D_addon (set_refine3D_register_policy in src/main/solve/simple_solve3D_controller.f90) is one full-cohort prob iteration at a fixed NSPACE_REGISTER (1000) against frozen references: a table fill at a chosen grid, as a step.
    • The polar calculator (src/main/pftc/simple_polarft_calc.f90): the particle sections over the Friedel half of the circle (pfts_ptcls), the references per parity (pfts_refs(:,:,:,parity): a particle is matched against its own half's reference, the gold standard), the memoised full-ring angular spectra of the CTF-multiplied particle (ft_ptcl_ctf) and of the references (ft_ref), the CTF matrices (ctfmats), the sigma2 shell weights as two global spectra, one per half (sigma2_noise(shell, half), memoize_sigma2_weights), the shifted particle (memoize_shifted_ptcl), and the correlation over all in-plane rotations with the shift search per reference.
    • The polar shift map (src/main/pftc/simple_polar_shiftmap.f90, the calculator's simple_polarft_shiftmap.f90; doc/implementation_notes/completed/polar_shift_search.md): the cross term of a reference/particle pair over every rotation and a disk of shifts from the Fourier-Bessel expansion, exact in the shift direction, gridded in magnitude (gen_shiftmap, shiftmap_mode, shiftmap_shift). The dynamic centring (polar_shift_search_dynamic_centring.md) keeps per particle the last move shincr and the width shsig, numbered slots of the ori module.
    • The stream's route into 3D (doc/policies/stream/stream_3D_ingestion_policy.md section 10; src/main/stream/stages/simple_stream_stage_solve3D.f90, src/main/solve/simple_state_assignment.f90): no probabilistic search on particles; sub-class averages matched against reprojections of the merged state references (LP_ASSIGN 15 Å, NSPACE_ASSIGN 1000 directions), the particles inherit the sub-class state and pose, then one iteration of refine3D refine=cont from the seeds set_refine_seeds sets (the halves of the partition, one per micrograph by eo_transaction; the nearest grid direction), with the halves' starting volumes reconstructed by refine3D from the mapped poses. refine=cont keeps the state fixed and bounds the rotation by athres_cont (10° on this route) and the shifts by trs. Its recoverable full pose, including the state, is the bar; viewing-direction agreement on a 6° grid alone is insufficient.
    • The logistic core (src/main/quality/simple_quality_logistic.f90), binary and at most sixteen features, is not the student's shape but is the right tool for the confidence calibration of section 8; the learn/evaluate/promote lifecycle and the versioned key=value model file of src/main/cavg_quality/simple_cavg_quality_model.f90 are the pattern.
    • OpenBLAS is linked (cmake/Dependencies.cmake) and sgemm is called from Fortran (src/utils/math/simple_linalg.f90, flex); the OpenMP BLAS policy keeps the library on the calling thread inside a team (doc/implementation_notes/completed/blas_openmp_policy.md). Nothing in the repo does backpropagation: the network is new code.
    • Private programs: add_private_prg in src/main/ui/simple_private_prgs.f90, the case in production/simple_private_exec_driver.f90, a thin commander (assign_cavgs_states in src/main/commanders/simple/simple_commanders_cavgs.f90 is the model), started by the stage as a job with the 3D jobs' resources.
    • Validation data (section 10): the Ribosembly three-state benchmark on dell2 (/data2/multistate_bench: 80,000 simulated particles, box 128 at 3 Å per pixel, SNR 0.01, true poses, shifts uniform to plus or minus 10 pixels, CTFs drawn per particle, labels at 30/60/10 percent, half-sets, truth maps with their FSC at 12 Å, a 20,000 subset); /data/simulated_vcrnapol on the Dell (30,000 particles, two states, SNR 0.1, truth); the real multi-state /data2/trpv1_c2 (its 6_refine3D_states) and /data/bgal; the small membrane protein /data/exp_gate_FlhB and streptavidin /data/10335-streptav; two CESPED entries from Zenodo (section 2.4); /data2/stream_holjunk for the stream route with junk; multistate_workflows (src/main/commanders/test/simple_commanders_test_highlevel_msworkflows.f90) as the pattern for a nightly route on simulated beta-galactosidase states.

    2.3 What XEMA's two-fold method contributes¶

    XEMA (~/src/XEMA, Hans's cross-linking mass-spectrometry engine) motivates label-free partitions, held-out choices, published agreement and replayable model records (the sources in section 15). Those are useful principles, but its cross-scoring arrangement is not a cryo-EM gold-standard guarantee. Here each production half owns its teacher references, spectrum, student, model selection and confidence gate. Inner micrograph splits serve model development; a separate cohort calibrates the gate and an untouched cohort audits it. The two halves may choose different rungs. Their agreement is diagnostic and never selects a common winner or gate. Counts are compared as paired micrograph-clustered outcomes, not independent Poisson observations. One route supplies each row, fixed-chunk reductions make a fit reproducible across thread counts, and the model records every data-dependent choice and all constants. Sections 7 and 8 define these boundaries.

    2.4 What the CESPED checkout teaches¶

    ~/src/cesped (oxpig/cesped) holds the benchmark's loader, the Image2Sphere baseline, its training configuration and its evaluation. It settles several things.

    • Held-out halves, and an evaluation through reconstruction. An entry's half 0 trains (with a 70/30 split inside it for validation and hyperparameters) and half 1 is judged, then the reverse. The evaluation reconstructs from the predicted poses and reports the FSC against the truth map beside the truth halves' own FSC, so that the method's loss is separated from the map's quality. That cross-half judgement is an offline benchmark here (section 8), and tier 4 adopts the same two numbers on real data (section 10).
    • Means hide the tail. CESPED reports mean angular errors, which a bimodal distribution (most particles within a few degrees, a fraction wildly wrong) turns into tens of degrees; cryoPARES later reported the fraction within 5°. The reads here are fractions of recoverable seeds and the size of the set that holds the posterior, never a mean angle.
    • Seed, then refine. The only result of theirs that reaches the truth's quality is the local refinement within 6° of the predicted pose, at a threefold speed-up; the student alone is not the product. That is this design's consumer: a seed, a search at the predicted directions, the local refinement.
    • Labels and confidence. Their label is the nearest grid rotation to one RELION pose, weighted in the loss by RELION's rlnMaxValueProbDistribution; a cleaner label on the same particles cut the error by a third. The teacher's soft score row at the student's band (section 4) is the same idea carried through: the whole row, not one pose and one weight.
    • Translations were the stated gap. Image2Sphere predicts no shift, the evaluation used the truth shifts, and joint rotation and translation estimation heads the authors' list of improvements. The shift map in the candidate search at the predicted directions (sections 5 and 8) is this design's answer.
    • CTF. Their loader offers ctf_multiply beside phase_flip, and the paper used the flip; no one measured the difference. The CTF-multiplied input of section 3 is a declared ablation read on the Ribosembly bench (tier 2): the same rung on the multiplied and on the flipped input.
    • Capacity. Their encoder is an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet152 on 65,000 real-space pixels, trained for up to 400 epochs on four A100s; this design's network has about a million weights on 10,000 band-limited polar values with the rotation built in and the shift found by the map. The lower capacity is plausible on the lower-dimensional input and is a risk the ladder's wider rungs bound (section 6).
    • The head. Their distribution over SO(3) is band-limited at lmax 8, so its peaks cannot be much sharper than 20° whatever the 3.75° readout grid; and it classifies the in-plane angle as a class dimension, which the authors name as not separable from the in-plane translation. The flat softmax over directions with the in-plane angle left to the search is the cheaper factoring.
    • The data. Eight public entries with RELION poses, two membrane proteins in a micelle or a nanodisc among them (the GPCR complexes 10786 and 11120 are the hardest, at 33° and 45°), two symmetric ones (C2, D2), and published baselines. Tier 4 takes two CESPED entries as its real data beside the Dell's own, so that the numbers compare with Image2Sphere's and cryoPARES's on the same particles. No entry is on this box yet; they come from Zenodo, with a small TEST entry for smoke tests.

    3. The student's input¶

    simple_polar_feats (src/main/learn/): one routine builds the input of a particle from the polar calculator, called by training and prediction alike so that the two never drift apart.

    1. The band. Rings kfromto(1) to box*smpd/lp with lp the student's low-pass. The band is a rung of the ladder (section 6), 15 Å first, then 10 and 8 Å; the number of rings depends on box*smpd/lp alone, about 25 at 15 Å for the benchmark's box 128 at 3 Å per pixel.
    2. The CTF-multiplied particle. The tensor is built in the angular-sample domain from pfts_ptcls(:,k,i)*ctfmats(:,k,i), with the CTF itself as a third channel. The calculator's ft_ptcl_ctf is the angular FFT of this Friedel-extended ring: its index is the harmonic order, not the angle, so it is the shift map's input (section 5) and never an angle-axis CNN's. The product follows the search's cross term Q_k = REF CTF conj(X). With X = CTF P + N it has the signal CTF^2 P and the noise CTF N: a shell near a zero carries no signal and enters at no weight, where a phase flip would feed its noise at full amplitude. The multiplication is invertible away from the zeros, so it changes the conditioning of what the first layer has to learn, not the information; it does not by itself prove an optimal representation, and the phase-flipped input is the ablation of tier 2. The CTF channel supplies the varying envelope and the astigmatism; whether the model uses it correctly is tested by defocus and acquisition holdouts. Nothing is restored: no phase flip, Wiener filter or deconvolution in any rung; the output is a class, not an image.
    3. The shell weights. The shells are weighted as the search weights them under objfun=euclid, the objective of the biomolecular workflows this is for: k/sigma2(k), where sigma2 is the half's global spectrum, never a per-particle one: the calculator's weights are two spectra, one per half, and a part's per-particle residual records exist only to be summed into them. An unseen particle therefore needs nothing beyond its half, assigned at import, and its own weighted power (wsqsum_ptcl_band), which scales the band. The weights belong to the model, not to the project: each student's record holds the weights it was trained with, and prediction applies the weights of the student it applies, never the project's current sigma2, so a later refinement that moves the spectrum does not move the student's input distribution and a retrain picks the new spectrum up. A project without a spectrum is given one by the sigma2 bootstrap (calc_pspec) before training or prediction, never scored under another objective; the model records the spectrum it was trained with. Tensor amplitudes use sqrt(w_k) so that a squared norm applies the shell weight once. The logarithm of the weighted power is one scalar side input whose usefulness for the confidence is measured; it does not authorise a particle-rejection decision.
    4. The full circle. The Friedel half (pftsz angles) extended by its conjugate to nrots angles, then angularly low-pass filtered and resampled to NANG (128). Circular convolution is equivariant to integer cyclic shifts on this grid; arbitrary physical in-plane angles require interpolation and are approximate. The resampling and filter convention is recorded, and the error is tested against angular increments finer than the direction grid, with astigmatic CTFs.
    5. The tensor. Three channels (the real and imaginary parts of the CTF-multiplied particle, the CTF) by the rings by NANG, about 9,600 values at 15 Å for the benchmark's box. The phases are kept throughout; nothing is reduced to a power, which at 15 Å would see a particle's size and mass and separate no conformational states of one particle. Augmentation lives in the same module: the calculator's own phase ramp for shifts (memoize_shifted_ptcl), cyclic shifts for integer angular-bin rotations, the declared interpolation for other angles, with the CTF channel rotated consistently with the observation.

    4. The class grid and the teacher¶

    • The grid. oris%spiral at NSPACE_STUDENT (1000) directions, the class-average matching's grid, about 6° apart, over the whole sphere in c1 and the asymmetric unit as build%eulspace is built for a symmetric point group, times the states: nstates*NSPACE_STUDENT classes, 3,000 for three states. The in-plane angle is not a class: the student is invariant to it by construction and the search at the predicted directions recovers it (section 8). The student's output is a distribution over (state, direction) modulo the point group; no rotation or shift is regressed, the candidate search supplies the in-plane angle and the shift, and Euler angles stay the project's interchange.
    • The references are the half's own. The teacher of half A scores half A's particles against the per-state reconstructions of half A, as the matcher's parity references are prepared (prepare_refs_sigmas_and_pftc; refine3D reconstructs them from the project's halves at its first iteration). The stream's merged state volumes serve the sub-class matching and are never a teacher's reference. The model records the references' provenance (the project, the rows, the band, a hash of each volume).
    • The teacher is the table's tempered posterior, made deterministic, at the student's grid and band. A new scoring entry beside the table fill, reusing the matcher's preparation, the calculator's objective grids and the shift map's kernels, with no keyed draw anywhere. For every class c the deterministic best over the complete in-plane grid at the particle's prepared shift (gen_best_objfun_val), then, for the NTEACH_SHIFT (64) classes of lowest loss, the shift map over the declared disk and the polish, L_c = min_(rotation, shift) loss_c; the other classes keep their unshifted loss and the row records which classes were polished. The rule is the same for every particle and every class, the budget declared, the disk, prior, band, objective, discretisation and polish policy recorded. The existing sampled rows do not meet this contract; the diagnostic posterior's deterministic path (deterministic_best) is the step to extend.
    • The soft-label definition. t_c = exp[-tau_i (L_c-L_min)] / sum_j exp[-tau_i (L_j-L_min)]. These are normalised profile scores, not a likelihood marginalised over nuisance poses. tau_i is the recorded prob_beta times the particle's Gaussian-noise temperature (posterior_temperature), the factor the table draws at. Objective, temperature and frozen shell weights are explicit in the model. Integrating likelihoods over rotations and shifts would be a different teacher, requiring the prior and the cell measure.
    • What the earlier search contributes. The halves, the per-state half reconstructions, which particles have a converged pose (the candidate training rows), and the initial shift support. The teacher and the prediction use the same image preparation and initial shift support; converged shifts or centring widths that a new particle will not have cannot restrict the training searches.
    • The targets retain uncertainty. The loss uses the full soft rows in bounded batches. There is no uniform label smoothing, no fixed top-k renormalisation and no inverse-frequency class weighting in the probability head: with 3,000 classes, 5% of uniform smoothing would cap the mass in 64 classes at about 95% even for a perfect fit, so the 99% coverage of section 8 could never be met; and reweighting the classes changes the meaning of the predicted probabilities. A later sparse encoding must preserve the omitted mass and its distribution, or bound its approximation error; a broad row stays broad. A micrograph-stratified draw may improve coverage, but a nonuniform draw is corrected by its known inclusion weights for the intended particle population. Rare-view and per-state performance are reported separately rather than changing the probabilities' meaning.
    • Training and evaluation targets have different roles. Training may use the band-specific soft scores. Every rung is evaluated against one frozen, same-half full-search reference pose (the converged run's, at its band) and the actual downstream refinement, with the truth where it exists. A coarser band does not win by agreeing with its own easier teacher.
    • The teacher's resolvability is read before anything is trained. The deterministic rows give, per row and band, pmax, neff and k99; their distributions over the training particles say whether the band carries a concentrated assignment under that score model, before the ladder runs. These are conditional on the references, the objective and the temperature, not an information-theoretic bound. A band at which the median k99 spans a ring of directions is reported as such, and the student's claim for that dataset is assessed against the common reference and the truth (section 10).
    • The consumer's grid is decoupled from the student's by the local search: the student's directions seed an in-plane and shift search and then refine=cont within athres_cont, and refine3D's own grid (set_projs picks the nearest direction in the job's grid) can be 2,500 or 5,000 as the stage likes.
    • Cost. Per particle, the in-plane best for every class is one correlation over the rotations per reference from the memoised spectra, and the shift map runs for 64 classes, so the teacher is of the order of a billion floating-point operations per particle and band, on at most NTRAIN_MAX (200,000) particles drawn by whole micrographs, plus the common-reference evaluations. It is an estimate: the complete nuisance searches, the per-half reference preparation, the calibration and the audits are charged to training and measured in tier 5 before any time saving is claimed.

    5. Shifts and the Fourier-Bessel shift map¶

    On the half ring the shift matrix of s = rho (cos a, sin a) is exp(i z_k sin(theta_p - a)) with z_k = 2 pi k rho / box, and the Jacobi-Anger expansion turns the cross term of a reference/particle pair over every rotation and a whole disk of shifts into one Bessel-weighted sum over angular orders n:

    a_n(irot, rho) = (1/nrots) sum_m exp(-2 pi i m (irot-1)/nrots) sum_k w_k J_n(z_k(rho)) R^_k[m-n] conj(X^_k[m]),
C(irot, a, rho) = Re sum_n exp(-i n a) a_n(irot, rho),

    R^ and X^ the memoised full-ring spectra of the reference and of the CTF-multiplied particle. It is exact, the direction a is exact, only rho is gridded, and the orders kept follow the truncation rule: about 20 at a 30 Å radius in the 15 Å band, about 40 at 8 Å. Three things in it matter here.

    • The memoised spectra are the Fourier-Bessel domain (ring k, order m). A rotation is the phase exp(i m alpha) per order; a shift is a convolution along m with the kernel J_n(z_k) exp(-i n a), whose width grows with k and rho. No basis is diagonal in both, so an architecture absorbs one nuisance exactly and must search or learn the other. The circular convolution along the angle absorbs the rotation; a convolution along m wide enough to absorb a 30 Å shift at the band's outer ring would span about 40 orders, which is the map's work, not a network's.
    • The map is linear in the reference, so a reference may be a reprojection or a class average in the band.
    • The CTF enters as the search applies it, in the cross spectrum, per particle and exact.

    The shift is handled in two places, and in neither by the network's architecture.

    • Training: tolerance by augmentation. The network of section 6 is trained with shifts drawn uniformly in the disk through the calculator's shift matrices (memoize_shifted_ptcl), the exact phase ramps, and with rotations as cyclic shifts: it learns to put a shifted particle in the right class. Its accuracy against the shift magnitude is a declared read (section 10).
    • Prediction: the map and the polish at the predicted directions. For every candidate (state, direction) of section 8, the reprojection is searched over all in-plane rotations and the declared disk of shifts by the map (gen_shiftmap, shiftmap_mode) and the polish, from the particle's prepared shift: the pool's 2D shift in the stream, the centring record where one exists, zero otherwise. The search keeps the full declared support, with a disk retry or the fallback when the result hits a boundary.

    Only the accepted search pose becomes the centring record. An accepted full search result supplies the shift and shincr in the native-pixel frame. shsig stays at its unknown sentinel unless its value comes from an existing objective-based uncertainty that passes coverage tests.

    Cost. Per candidate direction the map costs (N+1) nrho (K nrots multiply-adds and one FFT) plus the readout: at 15 Å with 20 orders, 8 magnitudes, 25 shells and about 400 rotations, 10 to 15 million floating-point operations, so a candidate set of a few directions costs a few times the network and the set of 64 of section 8 about a billion operations per particle. These are kernel estimates, not evidence of a speedup over p07's sub-class route; candidate counts, retries and fallback are part of section 10's timing.

    6. The ladder and the network¶

    The ladder. The designs, simplest first: cnn16, cnn32 and cnn64 (16, 32 and 64 channels in the first block, doubling per block), each at the bands 15, 10 and 8 Å, visited in cost order: every design at 15 Å, then at 10 Å, then at 8 Å. Each half chooses its rung independently under section 7's recorded development rule and common evaluation target. Measured costs may change the traversal order, but the order is frozen before the selection run. A small membrane protein in a micelle is, at 15 Å, dominated by the micelle, whose outline gives the tilt of the membrane normal and little of the azimuth about it; the azimuth may sit at 8 to 12 Å, in the transmembrane helices, which is why the search's late stages run there, and why the band is a rung.

    The network (simple_circ_cnn, src/main/learn/): an encapsulated class with private components, new/kill, forward, backward, one Adam step, read and write.

    • On the tensor of section 3: three blocks of (a 3 by 3 convolution with circular padding along the angle and zero padding along the rings, bias, ReLU, average pooling along the rings only), with 16, 32 and 64 channels; angular stride one throughout, then the mean over the angle for invariance to integer cyclic shifts (interpolation still limits the physical rotation invariance). Angular decimation is not used without an explicit error and cost comparison: three stride-two pools would leave a 22.5° angular step at NANG 128, coarser than the direction grid. Then a dense layer to 256 with ReLU, the side scalar appended, and a dense layer to the classes with a softmax. About one million weights, most in the last layer; the per-particle cost is counted on the retained layout before it is quoted.
    • The convolution as im2col plus sgemm, the backward pass as the two transposed products; weights, activations and gradients real(sp) and allocatable; one Adam state per weight array.
    • Bit-identical on any thread count. The mini-batch is cut into fixed chunks; the chunks are computed in parallel, each into its own gradient buffer indexed by chunk, not by thread, with per-thread activation buffers sized by omp_get_max_threads() read before the region and the BLAS on the calling thread inside it; the chunks are reduced serially in chunk order after the region. The same sum at one thread and at any team, bit for bit, and the tester asks for identity, not a tolerance.
    • The loss: the cross-entropy against the complete soft targets, with the sampling corrections of section 4; Adam at 1e-3, batches of 256, weight decay 1e-5; at most 30 epochs. Early stopping uses a fixed micrograph holdout inside the fitting cohort, distinct from the model-selection folds, the calibration and the audit. No refit absorbs calibration or audit rows without new holdouts.
    • The model file. A key=value text header (model_version=1) and the weights of the two students as two binary files beside it (decision 7). The header records each half's design and band, the grid with nspace, nstates and pgrp, NANG, the frozen weights, the reference and spectrum hashes and source cohorts, the teacher's definition and temperatures, the training inclusion weights, every split and micrograph fingerprint, the calibration and shortlist rules, the uncertainty limits, the selection statistics, the timings, and each half's promotion or decline reason. The record distinguishes model scores, empirical coverage and calibrated seed confidence; a reader refuses incompatible grids, objectives, references or partition history.

    7. Two halves: development, calibration, audit and decline¶

    • The halves are the project's even-odd partition, one half per micrograph in the stream (eo_transaction). Each student is trained and applied within its own half. The record includes particle, state, direction and micrograph counts and stable stack/particle identities. Prediction verifies the recorded half of every overlapping training row with ensure_field_halves; additional rows are permitted when they follow the same partition rule, so a growing project need not have the identical row fingerprint of its training snapshot. Rows used for fitting, early stopping, design selection or calibration keep the incumbent route: the first delivery applies no out-of-sample calibration to an in-sample prediction. Batch reuse on those rows needs cross-fitted students inside the same reconstruction half.
    • The held-out roles. Whole micrographs are assigned before any fit to development, calibration and audit cohorts. In the stream the audit cohort is the next publication's new rows, which no map, spectrum or fit has seen; in batch validation it is a held-out acquisition block. Development is split into two folds by a recorded hash bit, with early-stopping rows reserved inside each training fold. Calibration and audit particles must not have contributed to the teacher's maps or spectra: earlier frozen artifacts, or artifacts rebuilt without those cohorts; where that cannot be met the validation is reported as conditional and promotion waits for independent audit data. No calibration or audit statistic selects a rung or changes its weights, and no final refit absorbs these cohorts.
    • A common development objective. Each rung predicts both held-out development folds. For every row: shortlist inclusion of the common-reference solution, the correct state, and full-pose recovery after the candidate search and the actual local refinement, plus runtime and fallback. Symmetry-aware full rotations and native-pixel shifts are used, with the capture criteria fixed before the comparison (section 8). Band-specific teacher-mode agreement is a diagnostic, not the selection target.
    • The selection rule. Each half traverses the frozen cost order on its own. Candidates are compared on the same rows by paired differences in successful seeds, with whole micrographs resampled under fixed keys, never individual particles. Before the first benchmark the confidence level, the minimum useful improvement, the multiplicity adjustment for the finite ladder, the resampling count and the cost tie-break are fixed in the model policy (decision 16). The cheaper incumbent stays unless the adjusted interval supports the declared improvement; the incumbent route is an available outcome. The square root of a count is not a paired comparison's standard error. There is no joint winner across the reconstruction halves.
    • Calibration and audit. After choosing and fitting one rung, the calibration cohort sets the distribution calibration, the shortlist coverage and the full-pose confidence gate. The model is frozen and evaluated once on the audit cohort. Promotion requires the predeclared seed-risk, coverage, per-state, reconstruction and timing criteria, with micrograph-clustered intervals. An audit failure declines that half's model; tuning after inspecting it requires a fresh audit cohort, and repeated retraining cannot reuse the same audit observations for an eventual pass.
    • Agreement is diagnostic. An offline comparison may score untouched data with both students and report symmetry-aware direction and state agreement and the symmetric Kullback-Leibler divergence. This neither chooses a rung nor substitutes for the half-map FSC or the truth, and never feeds training, thresholds, production poses or retraining. The selection table (<fname>_selection.txt) records each half's cohort counts, common-reference scores, paired intervals, costs, choice and audit.
    • Decline by counts, before any fit. Fewer than MIN_HALF_PTCLS (5,000) fitting particles or MIN_HALF_STATE (500) fitting modes in an active state declines that half; the development folds must satisfy the same floors; the calibration and audit cohorts need enough independent micrographs and per-state observations for the declared risk limits (decision 16). An unavailable comparison or an unsupported gate declines to the incumbent route; it never promotes the first rung by default. A populated half cannot certify the other half's model.

    8. The two programs, the confidence and the arrangement¶

    • learn_pose_student, a private program: the strategy routine run_learn_pose_student(cline) on the caller's (params, build) with a thin commander. Inputs: projfile, nstates, lp, nspace (the student grid), mskdiam, pgrp, fname, nthr; the references are the project's per-state half reconstructions, read where the project registers them or reconstructed as refine3D does at its first iteration (charged to training). Steps: prepare the parity references and the calculator as refine3D's matcher does; assign and record the cohorts; decline unsupported halves; build the deterministic score rows of section 4 in batches at each band, with their row statistics; build the features; select and fit its rung under the common evaluation target; calibrate coverage and confidence; freeze and audit; write the model, the provenance, the timings and the selection table.
    • predict_poses, a private program: run_predict_poses(cline) with a thin commander. Inputs: projfile, infile (the model), mskdiam, trs, nthr, the same references. Per selected row: the identity and eligibility check, the incumbent kept for development and calibration rows; the features under its own half's band and weights; the forward pass of that half's promoted student; the distribution calibration; the smallest set holding at least 99% of the calibrated mass, at least topk (3). A set that needs more than KMAX_STUDENT (64) classes, or a half or stratum that has not passed the empirical coverage audit, is recorded as a fallback without truncating or renormalising the set. For an eligible set, every candidate state's reprojection is searched over the in-plane rotations and the shifts with the existing objective, the shift map and the polish, under the full shift support of section 5. The confidence is evaluated for the returned full pose; the row is accepted only if both the coverage and the seed-risk gates pass. The row's corr, the calibrated seed confidence (pconf), the route, the candidate count and the retained mass are recorded; pconf and the route are numbered ori slots, the counts may live in the part's diagnostics. The accepted state is hard; an abstention leaves the row to the incumbent. The program writes a part file the caller merges, never the caller's project.
    • The student as a pruner. A broad prediction may still allow a bounded candidate search, but searching 40 directions is useful only when that set has demonstrated inclusion of the relevant solution; high normalised mass alone does not prove inclusion. Inclusion is reported against the common full search and the truth, per state, view and acquisition condition. A broad or overconfident prediction that fails this test takes the fallback, and no speedup claim counts omitted correct directions as saved work.
    • The confidence is for a recoverable full seed. Success is the correct state and a symmetry-aware SO(3) error and native-pixel shift error inside predeclared margins of the consumer's actual athres_cont and trs, followed by recovery in the paired local-refinement test. For p07 the rotation bound is 10°, so a direction-only criterion on the 6° grid cannot certify it, and refine=cont cannot repair a wrong state. On the calibration cohort a small binary calibration is fitted on the returned class's score, the candidate search's margin, the retained mass and the shift-boundary and ambiguity indicators, through the existing binary logistic core unchanged. pconf_min is chosen to bound the accepted-seed error and meet the declared useful accepted fraction, then tested on the untouched audit micrographs, each active state included. The model's mode mass and a per-cone median and MAD score are diagnostics, not calibrated probabilities of success. Insufficient calibration support, unresolved competing states or a failed audit declines the gate. Every limit, coefficient, threshold, coverage interval and decline reason is stored in the model, and a different consumer or capture range requires its own calibration and audit.
    • One source per particle. Every particle's pose comes from exactly one route, the student's or the incumbent's, never a blend; the route is recorded per row and counted per publication in the log with the counts that decided.
    • The arrangement, and cryo-EM's gold standard (decision 11). XEMA scores each half by the other half's model to avoid in-sample scoring. Cryo-EM additionally needs independent reconstruction noise: training on half B's map-derived labels and applying to half A introduces a cross-half dependency that a low-pass cutoff and a later local refinement do not provably remove. The production arrangement is therefore same-half throughout: maps, spectra, the data-dependent design choice and the gate. Any common initial model, frame or state alignment or shared low-resolution reference upstream is declared with its cutoff; same-half training cannot repair a dependence inherited there. Cross-scoring is an offline benchmark only, and its map comparisons and agreement statistics are not gold-standard half-to-half resolution.

    13. Risks and limits¶

    • Low SNR. The student cannot create missing pose information. It must supply a full seed inside the local refinement's capture range or a candidate set with demonstrated inclusion; otherwise the incumbent route supplies the row, and view-only grid agreement does not qualify a seed.
    • Small particles and small membrane proteins. At 15 Å a micelle gives the tilt and not the azimuth; the finer bands are rungs and the teacher's resolvability is a conditional diagnostic. Pruning is allowed only when its candidate-inclusion audit passes. The small-particle datasets and the CESPED membrane protein of tier 4 test whether any useful accepted fraction remains, rather than assuming that a broad ring fits in 64 classes.
    • States. A single image may not distinguish conformations. Soft training targets and competing state and direction hypotheses are kept until the physical candidate evaluation; a hard wrong state is not repaired by refine=cont, and the medium and small Ribosembly states' recall and the accepted-seed error are gates.
    • Preferred orientation. The probabilities keep their meaning rather than being reweighted by inverse frequency; rare-view support and fallback are reported explicitly, since a high global accepted accuracy can conceal missing directions and poor maps.
    • Defocus as a confounder. A classifier over CTF-multiplied inputs can learn the defocus where the training set confounds it with the class: states that sit in different micrographs, views that cluster by acquisition. Stratification and the CTF channel do not prove separation; the excess error and the calibration are compared within defocus bins, and later acquisition blocks are audited.
    • Drift. Frozen maps and spectra stabilise replay, not future-data validity. Retraining requires fresh eligible cohorts and an audit; a new version is never certified on its own accepted predictions. p07's fixed session references and the per-half references of the student's retraining need compatible provenance before integration.
    • Cost on the Dell (24 cores, no GPU): every timing is an estimate until tier 5. The deterministic nuisance searches, the complete targets, the independent development, calibration and audit, the physical candidate search and the preserved fallback support may dominate a small CNN, and a faster replacement for a dense particle search need not accelerate the actual sub-class stream route.
    • Compile time. The three modules are small; no new CMake target; heavy arrays allocatable.
