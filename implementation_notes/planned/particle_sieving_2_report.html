    particle_sieving_2: report¶

    Run of the plan, 2026-11-23, interactive, on the development machine (Ubuntu, gfortran 15.2, 16 cores) with Phase 4 on thunder (gcc-toolset-15, 72 cores); decisions 23 to 26 on 2026-11-23 and 24. Base 638d99ae3. Everything is uncommitted for review.

    1. What was learned¶

    • A greedy round cannot start fractionally from solve2D's output. solve2D runs its stages at a crop and its final make_cavgs overwrites the last averages and the FRCs with full-box ones, while the carried class sums stay at the crop (MotAB: 320 px averages, 160 px sums). refine2D pads smaller references but stops on larger ones and treats carried sums of another box as missing. Ruling 15 runs the rounds at the full box: round 1 is a full update and publishes full-box sums, the later rounds blend them (miniature: fraction 0.706 then 0.300, CLASS SUMS blended ... mass ratio 1.002).
    • Unseeded, the particles ingested by batches never get a valid sigma2 record. A pass rewrites a particle's record over its band only; the batch particles were inactive when the seed's calc_pspec filled every shell, so every round's commit skipped them (canonical sigma2 reduction skipped 600 / 1200 / 1500 active particle records) and the run's noise spectra were the seed particles'; a round without the seed's particles stopped on an empty half (a mutation test). By Joe's decision 21 each batch's records are now seeded from the store's global curves with sigma2_state_extend after its prediction: the warnings are gone, the last round's store holds a valid record for every particle (a new gate, which fails with the seeding removed), and the one-thread runs stay byte-identical. The extension forms each half's mean over the seeded rows, so a set of one half (halves follow micrographs) waits for the next batch. The result still carries no store (decision 20), as particle_sieving's has none.
    • Left to the seed's solve2D, every batch particle was in the even half (decision 22). The even/odd transaction gives each unmarked stack a half by micrograph group, to the half with fewer active particles; with only the seed's particles active, the stacks without active particles add none, so all of them went to the lighter half and were marked for good (real set: 50 601 batch particles even, 0 odd). Every round then summed them into the even class sums, and no batch could be seeded with sigma2 records (decision 21's first real run waited on one half throughout). The program now makes the halves of every selected particle before the seed (real set: 233 micrograph groups, 26 569 / 26 481). The miniature hid it (index labels, no micrograph rows); its fixture now has a micrograph per stack and no halves, and fails two new gates without the fix. The two Phase 4 runs of the defaults (section 5) and the first seeding run had the defect.
    • A step's copy must keep the work project's name. The sigma2 store's layout digest includes projname: a copy named after its step would have the store refused and rebuilt by calc_pspec at every step.
    • A test program named like the program it runs hides it. In simple_test_exec, params%new of the program found the test's descriptor (no project required) and skipped the project copy. The test is particle_sieving_2_mini. Also, simple_test_exec makes no execution directory for a program it runs in process; the program runs in the test's folder.
    • The CNN's threshold must be double precision. In single precision 0.9 is 0.89999998, so p_junk = 0.9 was junk against decision 3's "strictly above"; the unit suite caught it on its first run.
    • The CNN fixture cannot exercise the labels at 0.9: its random weights give p_junk 0.48 to 0.52 (MotAB, and 0.49 to 0.52 on the miniature); the trained cavg_cnn_v3.bin puts 86 of MotAB's 100 classes above 0.9.
    • The miniature is fast and reproducible: 29 to 130 s at eight threads, 92 to 141 s at one, byte-identical at one thread over every declared output with no exclusion.
    • On a real set the program runs and keeps its contract. With decisions 21 and 22 (run4_mics10, a 10-micrograph seed, 10 classes): six batches of about 10 000 and a tail of 656, each with both halves and seeded, no sigma2 record skipped, a full round 1 (92 s) and a fractional round 2 (0.025, blended with the carried sums, mass ratio 1.009); 32 412 of 53 050 accepted, 0 mismatches with the last labels; 18:45 in all. The defaults with both decisions (run5_defaults, 24 threads): 4 509 s, seed 3 733 s, student 401 s, the one batch (7 089, both halves) 15 s, round 1 357 s; 45 359 accepted, 0 mismatches; junk classes 18 after the seed, 25 after the round. Before the decisions (the two runs of the defaults, every batch particle in the even half; thunder, the 233-micrograph mic_series_rbp source, 53 050 particles, box 224, cavg_cnn_v3.bin): 5 355 and 5 800 s, of which the seed's solve2D 4 595 and 4 775 s, the student 359 and 674 s, the one batch 17 and 14 s (prediction 0.67 ms and alignment 0.9 to 1.2 ms per particle), the full-update round at 224 px 381 and 334 s. 45 580 and 45 411 of 53 050 accepted; junk classes 8 and 14 after the seed, 17 and 27 after the round. The accepted sets share 40 088 particles (Jaccard 0.79): the multi-threaded seed differs between runs, and the labels follow it.
    • The seed dominates and was slow here: about 2.5 min per solve2D iteration against about 33 s in the study's run of the same 200 micrographs, which ran alone, at 16 threads, with a mask from its class averages (145 A) where this program uses the box's (283 A, decision 2). Not separated further.
    • Under the box's mask the seed's solve2D turns many classes into rings (decisions 23 and 24, 2026-11-23). On the betagal stream set its stage 2 (the first centring stage) collapses classes into rotationally symmetric ring averages; under the box's mask (283 A), or in the automatic mode, whose stage 2 runs under the box's mask, they persist; under 174 to 180 A they recover. mskdiam is now taken from the command line, and mskdiam=0 runs the automatic mode. Share of the accepted particles in protein-view classes (judged by eye from the final montages; p_junk > 0.9): box 41.5 % of 45 359 (run 5), 174.72 A 50.0 % of 36 720 (run 6), 174 A 48.8 % of 36 614 (run 7), automatic (140 A) 37.9 % of 50 044 (run 8); solve2D alone on all particles at 174.72 A, 46.6 % of 37 688. No run rejected a protein-view particle.
    • A 20-micrograph seed is thin, and retraining the student buys nothing measurable (decisions 25 and 26, 2026-11-24). Run 9 (mskdiam=180, 20 micrographs, 4 177 seed particles, batches of 1 000 by a variant build, a round every 5 batches, the student retrained after each round): 3 918 s, of which training 3 424 s; its first round relabelled 29 classes. Runs 10 and 11 seeded by the new rule (45 micrographs, 10 083 particles), trained once (1 109 s, training 504 s) and after every round (4 008 s, training 3 398 s). The share of new particles a round leaves in the student's class: 45 to 65 % once, 45 to 63 % retrained, on the same batches (0.3 points apart on average); a replay of run 9's batches with its first student lost 1.7 to 5.5 points against the retrained ones. The next round searches every new particle against all classes, so the student only places it until then. Accepted (p_junk > 0.9; protein-view share): 45 062 (50.0 %), 42 918 (47.6 %), 38 266 (53.5 %) in runs 9, 10 and 11; the accepted sets' Jaccard 0.71 to 0.75 between any two, as for independent runs.
    • The CNN's most probable group selects more cleanly than p_junk > 0.9: protein-view share 62.0, 63.4, 63.3 and 68.8 % in runs 7, 9, 10 and 11 against 48.8, 50.0, 47.6 and 53.5 %, still rejecting no protein-view particle. The 0.9 rule keeps blurred blobs, edges and noise discs that the CNN calls most likely junk at a lower p_junk; both rules keep comets and some edges. Decision 3 stands; section 6.
    • The selection moves between rounds by the labels, not the particles. In run 9, from round 4 on 0.8 to 3 % of the earlier particles change class per round (8 to 11 % of those a round searches), while 4 to 11 % change between accepted and rejected: 7 to 29 classes change label per labelling, most with p_junk between 0.8 and 1.0 on both sides of 0.9. The result takes the last round's labels.
    • The first greedy round empties the seed's smallest classes for good: 17, 17 and 23 of 100 classes in runs 9, 10 and 11, the seed's empty or nearly empty ones (up to 21 particles); nothing refills a class, so these runs sort into 77 to 83 classes. Section 6.

    4. Deviations from the plan¶

    • The children's two base runs at one thread (Phase 0) were taken inside the program's own one-thread runs, not on their own: no child changed in this run but cavg_cnn_predict, which was checked byte-identical in Phase 1.
    • The Debug build was a separate tree (build_debug, removed afterwards), not compile_debug.sh, which would have replaced the Release tree.
    • Default f ("the seed's last stage's settings") became the greedy round's own command line: the seed's last stage runs at a crop and uses solve2D's stage keys; the round sets refine=greedy, autoscale=no, center=no, startit, minits=maxits=1 and update_frac from round 2, and takes refine2D's defaults for the rest (objfun=euclid, ml_reg=yes, measured sigma2, trs=MINSHIFT, the band from the class FRCs).
    • Batches of 300 in the miniature, not 250: the fixture's stacks hold 100 particles, so a batch closes at the first stack that reaches 250, and the expected counts are exact at 300.
    • Files outside the table (a row added on 2026-11-23): src/main/exec/simple_test_exec_highlevel.f90, production/CMakeLists.txt, doc/policies/test_environment_policy.md. Not needed: src/main/ui/simple_ui.f90 (its particle_sieving line is the stream's JSON), sieve.inf (sources are globbed).
    • Runs 9 to 11 ran variant builds on thunder (~/Code/SIMPLE_ps2_b1000, not this tree): batches of 1 000 (the constant changed), and for runs 10 and 11 the seed rule and an environment switch for the one training. The module now takes run 10's path (the seed rule, one training) without a switch.

    7. Commit message¶

    particle_sieving_2: a seed solve2D, CNN junk labels and the class student for the rest

A new program beside particle_sieving (simple_ptcl_sieve2, a controller of whole
programs): solve2D on the particles of the first micrographs, up to nmics (100) or
10 000 particles, the class-average CNN's labels (junk above p_junk 0.9), the class
student trained on the seed, the rest predicted and aligned in batches of 10 000,
and a greedy refine2D round at the full box every five batches (the first a full
update, the later ones blended with the carried class sums), each followed by the
labels and, after the rounds the new key retrain lists, the student's retraining.
Particles of junk classes are deselected at the end; the result carries no sigma2
store. mskdiam is required and taken by every step (0: the seed's solve2D estimates
it). lpstop (10 A by default) holds the alignment: the seed's solve2D marches to it
and each round's limit from the class FRCs is held at it. The program makes the
even/odd halves of every selected particle
before the seed (left to the seed's solve2D, every batch particle fell into one half)
and seeds the batch particles' sigma2 records from the store's global curves
(sigma2_state_extend). cavg_cnn_class_probs extracted from cavg_cnn_predict (its table
byte-identical). Unit suite particle_sieve2; CTest entry particle_sieving_2_mini
(SIMPLE_CTEST_BUDGET 46), byte-identical at one thread.
