    The atomic density model: species-agnostic atoms that render like the map¶

    Design note, 2026-10-25. Nothing here is implemented. It is the first step of the species-agnostic rebuild of detect_atoms that the maintainer set as the top priority on 2026-10-25: get the atom detection and the simulated atomic density right on the map alone, before any species call, refinement loop or reconstruction prior is touched.

    Terms. SINGLE is the nanoparticle workflow of SIMPLE; detect_atoms finds atoms in a 3D map and writes _ATMS.pdb (the atoms), _SIM.mrc (the map rendered from them) and other products. The B factor B of an atom sets its width (a Gaussian of variance B / 8 pi^2 per axis). The occupancy of a site is the fraction of the time an atom is there; in a map it scales the atom's integral. The coordination number c of an atom is its number of neighbours within the bond cutoff. d_NN is the median nearest-neighbour distance measured from the first detection. The halo is the negative ring every atom carries in a reconstruction whose low frequencies are suppressed. The noise region is the part of the map far from every atom.

    1. What is asked, and the rulings that shape it¶

    The maintainer's rulings of 2026-10-25:

    • detect_atoms works on a map alone. Half maps are typically not available in this field, and the code must take reconstructions made outside SINGLE (GENFIRE, other tomography codes). Half maps may enter the later species step, as a non-independent split aligned to one reference, never as a requirement.
    • Per-atom intensities are not measured on the images. In liquid-cell EM they are too noisy; for tomographic maps the reconstruction is often all we get. The map is the measurement domain. This overrides the "measure B in the refinement" option of atom_bfactor_model.md, which is withdrawn by this note.
    • Order of work: species-agnostic detection and the simulated atomic density first; the species call (detect_atom_species), autorefine3D_nano and the atomicity prior only once the rendered model fits the experimental maps.
    • The model is the minimal one that carries every parameter species detection needs, and a map rendered from it must show the radial fade of the atomic densities that every experimental map shows.

    Rulings on the first draft, same day:

    • The coordination number is the covariate of the profiles (not the depth below the surface).
    • The gap between the species-free detection and the Pt kernel on the real maps (47 against 56 of 66 maps matched) is tested two ways in the validation: a second detection pass with the fitted kernel, and the mask factor (1.2 times the measured diameter).
    • On a map without an empty region the noise reference is the residual between the atoms.
    • The residual recovery of the species branch joins the present path in this step: this workflow must identify and model every atom. The atoms it adds are labelled abstractly, as weak atoms, with no species meaning.

    Two measured facts from the species work set the bar (species_discovery_status.md, atom_bfactor_model.md): on the 66 real Pt maps an atom's intensity rises by about 4 to 6 percent per neighbour while its fitted width stays flat, and a per-atom Gaussian fit on a filtered map invents a core-to-surface B rise of 4.4 A^2 on a particle generated with one B, because the halos of twelve neighbours overlap in the interior and of five at the surface. A model that ignores the halo cannot fit the map, and one that fits the halo freely per shell absorbs missing atoms (phase 6 of the species work, withdrawn).

    2. The model¶

    The map as read, m(x), is modelled as

    m(x) = b(x) + sum_i I_i [ K(x - r_i; B_i) - a K(x - r_i; B_i + B_h) ] + n(x)

K(x; B) = (4 pi / B)^(3/2) exp(-4 pi^2 |x|^2 / B)        (unit integral)

    Each atom i has a position r_i, an integrated intensity I_i and a width B_i. The bracket is one atom as the reconstruction shows it: its Gaussian less a wider, shallower Gaussian. In Fourier space the atom's transform is I_i exp(-B_i s^2 / 4) H(s) with the transfer

    H(s) = 1 - a exp(-B_h s^2 / 4)

    which is 1 at the lattice frequencies and 1 - a at zero frequency. Two numbers, a and B_h, describe the reconstruction's suppression of the low frequencies and the halo it leaves between atoms. On the Pt map used in the species work the amplitude against simulated atoms was 0.37 beyond 20 A and 0.50 at 10 to 20 A, which this form reproduces with a about 0.6 and B_h about 200 A^2 (a halo of standard deviation 1.6 A, 0.6 d_NN); the interstitial wells at minus two noise standard deviations are the sum of the neighbours' halos. a and B_h are global to the map, and they are estimated on the interior atoms only, where every neighbour is present, so that missing surface atoms cannot leak into them. That is the difference from the withdrawn per-shell transfer.

    b(x) is a smooth background, the residual smoothed at the scale of d_NN. n(x) is noise of standard deviation sigma_n.

    The per-atom parameters have structure across the particle, and that structure is what species detection needs. With one species of intensity S:

    ln I_i = ln S + beta (c_i - 12) + e_i,      e_i ~ N(0, tau_I(c_i)^2)
ln B_i = ln B_p + gamma (12 - c_i) + f_i,   f_i ~ N(0, tau_B^2)

    beta is the environment slope: the relative loss of intensity per missing neighbour, the same for every species (on the FePt tomogram Fe and Pt lose the same 5.2 percent per neighbour; the multiplicative form is therefore the right one, and it replaces the additive term now in fit_species_mixture). exp(beta (c_i - 12) + e_i) is the atom's occupancy relative to a fully coordinated atom of its species. tau_I(c) is the spread of occupancy at coordination c, larger at the surface than in the interior; it is a measured quantity, and it is the error bar every surface species call will carry. gamma is the width slope towards the surface and B_p the width of a fully coordinated atom; B_i includes the map's resolution, so only its variation across the particle is physical. If the data say gamma is zero the model loses nothing.

    With K species the only change is S -> S_k with class fractions pi_k; beta, gamma, tau_I and tau_B stay shared. The species posterior of an atom is then

    P(k | I_i, c_i) ~ pi_k N( ln I_i ; ln S_k + beta (c_i - 12), tau_I(c_i)^2 + v_i )

    with v_i the measurement variance of ln I_i. That belongs to detect_atom_species and is written here only to show that nothing is missing from the table detect_atoms must produce: r_i, I_i, B_i, c_i, v_i, and the particle's beta, gamma, B_p, tau_I(c), tau_B, S.

    Parameter count: five per atom, five per particle (S, beta, gamma, B_p, and the spreads), two for the reconstruction (a, B_h), the background field and sigma_n. Every atom, strong or weak, is one instance of the same five parameters; the label records how it was found and enters no equation.

    3. What is species-agnostic in it¶

    Nothing in section 2 names an element. The kernel is a Gaussian; the length scales come from d_NN (atom radius 0.4 d_NN, bond cutoff 1.267 d_NN, the close-packed contact ceiling of 12), as the species-free path of detect_atoms already does; the intensity scale S is the median I of the interior atoms. The five-Gaussian element kernels of atoms%convolve stay for simulate_nanoparticle and for atoms_stats, which still needs an element for the lattice fit; they are not used in detection or in the model. What an element would add, the absolute Debye-Waller factor and a Z-scaled intensity, is exactly what the map cannot calibrate without one.

    4. The fit¶

    The fit runs on the present path after the level-1 pruning (discard_atoms), with no element, and it now includes the residual recovery, so that every atom in the map is identified and modelled. The order is: level 1 and its pruning as today; a provisional fit of the model to the level-1 atoms; the residual recovery; the pruning of the recovered atoms; the full fit; the products. The atoms of level 1 are the strong atoms; the atoms the recovery adds are the weak atoms. The labels say how an atom was found and nothing about its species.

    The recovery is the species branch's, moved as it is: the residual (map minus background minus the model) is matched-filtered with the kernel and standardised to a z map; candidates are its local maxima above a threshold calibrated from the count of noise maxima in the noise region, so that the expected number of false atoms is 0.2 per particle; a first stage searches the whole mask, a second stage, one unit lower, only where a candidate has at least min_nbrs found atoms within 1.15 d_NN; each new atom is fitted on the residual and the levels repeat until nothing is added. One difference from the species branch is expected to help: with the halo in the kernel, the residual no longer holds the halos of the strong atoms, which were part of the noise the matched filter saw. Weak atoms are pruned by the present policy, by the ruling of 2026-10-09 of the species work: a weak atom's contact score counts strong atoms only, and the outer zone and the threshold are those discard_atoms derived for the level-1 set. Weak atoms have no connected component; _BIN.mrc and _CC.mrc are the level-1 products.

    The fit reuses the routines the species branch of nanoparticle has (fit_gauss_width, the Gauss-Seidel sweeps of fit_atoms_joint, update_background, the noise region, noise_stats, compute_zmap, calibrate, detect_residual_level, prune_recovered), with these changes:

    1. Kernel. The atom rendered and fitted is the difference of Gaussians of section 2, not the plain Gaussian. render, gather and fit_gauss_width take a and B_h.
    2. Globals first. a and B_h are fitted by a two-dimensional search of the squared residual over the union of spheres of radius d_NN around the interior atoms (c_i = 12), the atoms held at their current parameters; once after the first sweep of the provisional fit and once in the full fit. Start: a = 0.5, B_h = 8 pi^2 (0.6 d_NN)^2 (about 215 A^2 for Pt).
    3. Per-atom sweeps. Three sweeps, as now, over strong and weak atoms alike: for atom i the data are the map minus the background minus every other atom's model, in a sphere of radius d_NN / 2; the amplitude is linear, ln B_i is a one-dimensional search, and the position moves to the centroid of the atom's own residual (bounded by 0.35 d_NN). The width prior is the profile of section 2, ln B_p + gamma (12 - c_i) with spread tau_B, in place of the shell median of the species branch. A weak atom's own density rarely fixes its width; the profile does, which is what the species branch borrowed from the strong atoms of the same shell.
    4. Profiles after each sweep. beta, S and tau_I(c) by weighted regression of ln I_i on c_i over the atoms with amplitude above ten times its noise, the spread in bins of c (at least 20 atoms per bin, the interior bin as the floor); gamma, B_p, tau_B the same way from ln B_i. Coordination counts are the contact scores on the current positions of all atoms, strong and weak, so that a strong surface atom next to a recovered weak one is counted as the neighbour it has.
    5. Noise. sigma_n and the measurement noise of I_i and B_i come from the map itself: the standard deviation of the noise region when the map has one, and the same estimators applied at phantom sites in it, as now. When a map has no empty region (a support-masked tomogram that fills its box, the FePt case), the noise reference is the residual between the atoms: the robust spread of the fit residual inside the envelope farther than d_NN / 2 from every atom, and the phantom sites are drawn there. It is reported as such; misfit adds to it, so it is an upper bound, and the recovery thresholds calibrated from it are on the conservative side.

    Cost: a sphere of d_NN / 2 holds about 250 voxels at 0.358 A, so three sweeps over 500 atoms take about a second; the FePt particle (23,000 atoms) stays well under a minute. The global search evaluates the model on the interior union, of the order of a hundred thousand voxels, a few dozen times.

    5. Products¶

    Release 4 keeps no compatibility, so the products change in place:

    • _ATMS.pdb: every atom, strong and weak; occupancy column exp(beta (c_i - 12) + e_i) = I_i / S, B column B_i; chain A for the strong atoms and B for the weak ones, so that a viewer selects either set by chain (the label is also a column of the table). valid_corr leaves the B column; it stays in valid_corr_in_bfac_field.pdb and in the per-atom table.
    • _SIM.mrc: sum_i I_i K(x - r_i; B_i), the atoms without the transfer and without the background. This is the map that shows the fade: fainter and broader atoms towards the surface because I_i and B_i say so. It is the reference autorefine3D_nano will align against later (refinement filters itself).
    • _FIT.mrc: the full model of section 2, b + sum_i I_i [K - a K], to be compared with the input; _RES.mrc = m - _FIT, the residual, where missing atoms show as peaks and false ones as wells.
    • _atoms.csv: one row per atom with r_i, I_i, B_i, c_i, the measurement spreads of ln I_i and ln B_i, the occupancy, valid_corr, the label (strong or weak), and for a weak atom the stage, level and detection z that accepted it. _model.txt: S, beta, gamma, B_p, tau_I(c) by bin, tau_B, a, B_h, sigma_n and where it was measured, the residual-to-noise ratio inside the envelope, d_NN, the counts of strong and weak atoms, the recovery thresholds and the expected false count behind them, the pruning zone and threshold.

    _BIN.mrc, _CC.mrc and _MSK.mrc are the level-1 products, unchanged. The _species files stay with discover_species until detect_atom_species takes them over; the recovery no longer belongs to that key, so discover_species=yes reduces to the class call on the table this step writes.

    12. Second run: the measured transfer, element out of detect_atoms, detect_atomic_species¶

    Ruled 2026-10-26 after the first run and a hand run of the 66 maps on the Mac (the kit run_detect_atoms_mac.sh and the analysis scripts beside the run spec). The yardsticks are those of the first run's second and third rulings: the fit to the map, the residual's peaks and wells, consistency across a particle's time points, the synthetic particles, and the map correlation of rendered atom sets; the stored coordinates are a reproduction reference only.

    The transfer is measured, not parametrised. The shell-by-shell projection of each map onto its fitted atoms rendered without any transfer, T(s) = <F_map, F_S> / <F_S, F_S> per resolution shell, pooled over the 66 maps, is a band-pass and nothing like a Gaussian notch: about 0.2 from 50 to 10 Å, a hole of 0.05 between 10 and 6 Å, rising from 0.15 to 0.4 between 6 and 3.3 Å, a step to about 0.85 at 3.3 Å, unity across the lattice band to 2.0 Å, and a cutoff to 0.2 below 1.9 Å. The shape is the same on every map, stable within a particle's time points to about 0.1, and differs in level between particles. The notch at the fitted values gives 0.4 at 10 Å and 0.7 at 5 Å where the maps have 0.05 and 0.25: that is why the model's wells are a quarter too shallow, a runs to its cap, B_h has no answer, and the cutoff at 1.9 Å, which the notch cannot express, is absorbed into the per-atom widths (37 Å², flat with coordination). The transfer therefore becomes a measured radial curve per map: the smoothed shell projection, measured after the provisional fit and once more after the full fit, held fixed while the atoms are fitted. The atom kernel through it, K_T(r; B) = IFT[exp(-B s^2/4) T(s)], is a one-dimensional radial transform tabulated on the width grid of the fit, so the per-atom fit changes only its kernel table; the model is rendered from those kernels (their tails ring out to about 10 Å, so the sweep's cells widen or the render goes through one FFT per colour, whichever the measurement says is cheaper); _model.txt loses a and B_h, the curve goes to _transfer.tsv, and a key lets a data set or a particle's time points share one curve. This is not the withdrawn phase 6 of the species work: nothing is refitted per shell inside the fit, there is no correlation cut, the curve comes from the complete model (strong and weak atoms) and is smoothed, and its stability across time points is the check that it measures the pipeline, not the particle.     How the transfer is measured and used, and the decisions taken on the way (each with the measurement that forced it; the journal has the logs):

    • Measurement. Per shell of 1/50 Å⁻¹, T = Σ Re(F_map conj(F_S)) / Σ |F_S|² over the atoms as fitted, with the origin left out. It is smoothed by a three-shell running median, which keeps a shell's own value where the window is incomplete. Shells with less than 1 % of the atoms' peak power are held at the last reliable value. The transforms are single-threaded, so the result does not depend on the thread count.
    • The table. Built from the notch on fine shells, it reproduces the notch's closed-form kernel K(B) − a K(B + B_h) within 1e-4 of its peak and its derivative within 8e-4.
    • The gauge, a decision. On the first test run, the measured curve came out as 1.6 exp(−7 s²/4) times the true one, the intensities 38 % low and the widths 21 Å² against 28. That is the exact degeneracy above. After every measurement, ln T = ln c + g s² is now fitted over the reliable shells of 3.1–2.0 Å, weighted by the atoms' power. The curve is divided by c exp(g s²), held beyond the last reliable shell, and the atoms take c and −4g, which leaves the model unchanged.
    • The noise bias, a finding. With the gauge, the intensities still settle 3 % low on the white-noise test particle, even when the alternation starts from the true transfer. On a noise-free version of the particle the bias vanishes (ln S −0.005). The fitted intensities and widths take up the noise under each atom, which biases the projection where noise competes with the atoms. Projecting onto the atoms at their profile values instead would remove that bias on a particle of one species. It fails on the two-species particle, whose weak aluminium the profile makes three times too bright, and was withdrawn.
    • The schedule, a decision. The transfer is measured after every sweep and held during the sweep. Up to six more sweeps follow while it changes by more than 0.01 in a reliable shell.
    • The provisional fit, a decision. A transfer measured on the strong atoms of the light Pt/Al particle takes up the aluminium still missing, which shares the lattice's frequencies. The aluminium recall fell from 0.97 to 0.70. The provisional fit therefore uses the plain Gaussian, and the transfer is measured in the full fit, on the complete model, as section 12 describes.
    • The halo field, measured both ways as the plan asks. On a real map the atom through the measured transfer stays above 1e-4 of its peak out to about 24 Å (5e-4 at 16 Å), so rendering every atom from the table would cost about 1.3 million voxels per atom, three times per sweep. One FFT of the atom field per colour costs about 0.06 s per pair of transforms on a 160-voxel box and takes the tails whole. The transform was chosen, with at most 16 threads (this machine's FFTW is slow above 16, phase 5). Raising the cell side to twice the reach would leave a single cell per colour and no parallelism. The sweep keeps its cells; the halos of the other cells of a colour enter at the colour's refresh.

    Phase 8, detect_atomic_species (2026-10-27): stopped at two checks of species_discovery¶

    What it means, in short: the species call is now its own program, detect_atomic_species, which reads the products of detect_atoms and fits the intensity classes in the multiplicative form of section 2.     What was built

    • detect_atomic_species is a program of single_exec in the category atoms (single_ui_atom.f90), routed in single_exec_atom.f90, with its commander exec_detect_atomic_species next to detect_atoms in simple_commanders_atoms.f90.
    • Inputs:
      • vol1 and smpd;
      • pdbfile: the _ATMS.pdb of detect_atoms, with its _atoms.csv and _model.txt found beside it;
      • optional vol_even/vol_odd, both or neither;
      • optional element: a list such as Pt,Ni, brightest first, its length the class count;
      • optional nspecies, refused together with element;
      • nthr.
    • Its work is in a new domain module, simple_atomic_species.f90. simple_nano_species stays free of images and files.
    • The class fit (fit_species_mixture in simple_nano_species) now takes a variance per atom instead of one spread. It is extreme deconvolution: each class has its own intrinsic variance, on top of the per-atom variance. It returns that intrinsic variance and the class weights.
    • Admissibility and the weights of the shared slope use the class variance plus the median per-atom variance.
    • The program fits x_i = ln I_i with the covariate c_i - 12 and one beta shared by every class.
    • The posterior (class_posterior, new) gives each atom's class probabilities with variance tau_I(c_i)^2 + v_i.
    • v_i = SD_LNINT^2. With half maps, v_i comes instead from half the difference of their aperture sums, scaled to the fitted intensities by the map's own apertures.
    • The standard error of the assigned class's posterior comes from the measurement noise by the delta method: sqrt(v) |P_k (g_k - sum_j P_j g_j)|, where g_j = (m_j - x)/(var_j + s2).
    • The coordination spread is measured within the classes. The tau_I that detect_atoms writes is fitted under one species. On a particle with two species it holds their separation: 0.32 on the Pt/Ni alloy, where ln 0.56 = -0.58. With it, every two-element class call was inadmissible, with separation D = 2.19 against the 3 needed.
    • detect_atomic_species therefore first fits on v_i alone. Then, in each of the model's coordination bins, it sets tau_I^2 to the posterior-weighted mean squared residual about the class lines less the mean v_i.
    • It refits until no bin changes by more than 0.001, at most 10 times.
    • _species.txt gives both values per bin: tau_I_bin_k (within the classes) and tau_I_model_bin_k (from detect_atoms).
    • The products are the _species files of the former discover_species.
    • _species.csv has two columns more per atom: SD_POSTERIOR (the posterior's standard error) and TAU_COORD (the coordination spread it carries).
    • MODEL_INTENSITY is now S_k exp(beta (c - 12)). _species.txt reports beta where it had coord_slope, plus the tau_I lines and the median and largest posterior standard error.
    • detect_atoms no longer carries the species call.
    • discover_species and its writers, the half-map apertures and the species fields of atominfo are deleted from simple_nanoparticle.f90.
    • discover_species is no longer a key: release 4 refuses it as an unknown argument (simple_parameters*.f90). element, nspecies, vol_even and vol_odd stay refused by detect_atoms, with the message naming detect_atomic_species.

    The density fixture made determinate across compilers (2026-10-27, by hand, on the Mac)¶

    What it means, in short: the merged tree's fast gate failed one check of 458 on the maintainer's Mac, the uniform particle's fade with the transfer measured (0.0436 for 0.05, tolerance 0.005), where the Dell had 0.0509 with the same code at one thread. The two machines had fitted different particles: gfortran 15 (the Dell's gcc-toolset) and gfortran 16 (the Mac) seed random_number differently (seed sizes 33 and 8, different streams for the same random_seed(put=)), so every fixture SIMPLE draws through a fixed seed is a different realisation on the two machines. With the kernel known, where the fit is a fixed function of the data, the two draws gave 0.0512 and 0.0464 for the uniform fade and -0.0288 and -0.0363 for the gradient's: the checks sat at about one sigma of their fixtures. The code is not at fault and the tolerances stand; the fixture is made quieter instead, so that a draw's scatter is well inside them.
