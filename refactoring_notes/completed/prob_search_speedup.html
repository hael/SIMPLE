    The probabilistic search: paying less for the same search¶

    Status: completed 14 October 2026 in an interactive session (not the planned unattended run); the report is prob_search_speedup_report.md beside this file. Phases 0-3 landed behind one key, prob_fast (ruling 7, which supersedes the three keys of the plan below); Phase 4 was skipped on its measurements; the subspace-map table of the neighbourhood search was added on the default path (ruling 8). The last batch is written but not yet built or run (report, "Open"). Section 11 is the record. Written 2026-10-13; revised 14 October 2026 after review (section 13).

    The probabilistic pre-step (prob_align and its prob_tab parts) was 46% of a three-state ribosome solve3D run (doc/implementation_notes/rejected/particle_caching_3D.md). This plan times it, then makes it cheaper:

    Phase Change Kind Key
    0 Benchmark files, baseline, thread scaling (3.4) exact none
    1 Particle-frame coarse grid in the multi-state refinement (3.1(a)) approximate prob_grid_ptcl
    2 Particle-frame seed in the single-state table (3.1(b)) approximate prob_seed_ptcl
    3 Quadratic shift step after the coarse grid (3.2) approximate prob_grid_fit
    4 Blocked table loop, if Phase 0 or 3 calls for it (3.3) exact none
    5 Documents and close

    Each approximate change has its own default-off developer key and is validated on its own against the baseline. The keys are temporary: after review the maintainer either promotes a method, removing its key, or removes the method with its key.

    1. Aim¶

    • Faster pre-step, same search. An exact change keeps the table's contents, up to the order of random draws inside a thread (3.3).
    • A breakdown of the pre-step's time. The pre-step gets benchmark files of its own, so its time is split into named parts instead of being one number in the refine3D strategy file.
    • Measurement over argument. The table-loop change is decided by a measurement.

    2. What the pre-step costs today¶

    Measured (the ribosome chunk, consolidated layout, 29,101 particles, three states, nspace 1,000, 5 parts of 16 threads, box 336): the pre-step is 3,033 of 6,572 s over 78 iterations, 37 s per iteration at box_crop 132, 50 s at 160 and 138 s of a 190 s iteration at 182. Those are the run's refine=prob stages. In today's solve3D, refine=prob runs in the middle stages (3–5). Single-state runs use prob_neigh before and after them; multi-state runs stop after stage 5 by default (solve3D_policy.md sections 3 and 6). Inside the pre-step only the whole is timed (rt_prob in simple_refine3D_strategy.f90).

    Counted from the code, per sampled particle, at box_crop 182 (nk 60 shells, pftsz 254, nrots 508):

    • The multi-state table. fill_tab_range in simple_eul_prob_tab.f90 (process_particle_with_shift) uses one thread per particle. The shift seed is single-state only (l_sh_first), so with more than one state every one of the 3,000 references is scored at zero shift by gen_prob_likelihood_euclid_val. That is three steps:
    • the memoised contraction ft_ctf2 · ft_ref2 − 2 ft_ptcl_ctf · conj(ft_ref) over nk × (pftsz+1);
    • one real inverse FFT of length nrots;
    • a bounded heap for the best nsample rotations (sample_likelihood_dist, O(n log nsample)).

    Call the contraction plus the inverse FFT the base kernel: about 0.32 Mflop, reading 248 KB of reference and 248 KB of particle memos. 3,000 base kernels per particle. - The single-state table. The seed comes from estimate_shift_seed: one coarse grid and L-BFGS-B per particle against the particle's previous reference. In alternating mode the search starts from the best rotation at zero shift, not from the incoming one. The seed is a reference-frame shift, and every reference is scored at it, so gen_euclid_crvec takes its shifted-reference branch. That branch builds the shift matrix, multiplies the reference by it, and runs nk complex FFTs of length nrots before the contraction. It costs about 5.3 base kernels per reference, nspace × 5.3 per particle. - The refinement (refine_best_refs). It refines the projs_ns best references, projs_ns = athres/180 × nspace, where athres is the smaller of prob_athres (10°) and the sampled particles' mean angular move: 17 to 55 references. Each gets grad_shsrch_minimize (simple_pftc_shsrch_grad.f90) in alternating mode with coarse_init on. The single-state case turns coarse_init off by passing xy_in. Per refined reference: - one base kernel at zero shift; - the coarse grid: coarse_num_steps² = 25 calls of gen_objfun_vals, each shifting the reference (gen_shmat4aln, then nk complex FFTs on plan_fwd1_many) before the contraction. About 1.7 Mflop each (5.3 base kernels), the FFTs four fifths of it. - up to max_evals = 5 alternations of L-BFGS-B (maxits_sh = 60), each followed by one shifted gen_objfun_vals. Every L-BFGS-B evaluation (gen_corr_grad_for_rot_8 → gen_euclid_grad_for_rot_8) copies the reference to double precision (244 KB) and sums residual and gradient directly over nk × pftsz: about 0.6 Mflop in double. - The search object counts the evaluations per run, E (profile_objective_evals, profile_gradient_evals), and has a getter and a reset (get_profile, reset_profile), but nothing calls them. Alternations are not counted.

    Per refined reference that is 1 + 25 × 5.3 + alternations × (2E + 5.3) base kernels: about 250 to 450, for two to three alternations and E of 20 to 50. Per particle it is 4,000 to 25,000, against 3,000 for the table.

    Consistency check. 5,000 particles × about 13,000 base kernels × 0.32 Mflop is 2 × 10¹³ flop per iteration. Over 138 s on 80 threads that is 1.9 Gflop/s per thread, a plausible rate for these loops. So the pre-step is these two loops plus 10 to 15 s per iteration of process launches, reprojection-model reads and memoisation, the table write and read, and ref_assign.

    Where the grid runs. It runs once per particle in: - the matcher (grad_shsrch_first_obj in simple_strategy3D_srch.f90); - the 2D search; - the 2D table (simple_eul_prob_tab2D.f90); - every single-state seed.

    It runs once per refined reference in the multi-state 3D table, and in the multi-state path of the neighbourhood table (simple_eul_prob_tab_neigh.f90). The neighbourhood table is out of scope.

    3. Target design¶

    3.1 Particle-frame shifts in the table (approximate; two keys)¶

    Two changes, both in the 3D table's fill_tab_range, each under its own key: - (a) the coarse grid of the multi-state refinement (prob_grid_ptcl); - (b) the seeded scoring of the single-state table (prob_seed_ptcl).

    They share the polar calculator's builder of shifted particle memos. They never act on the same particle: (a) needs more than one state, (b) exactly one.

    No exact particle-side form. The kernels shift the reference in its own polar frame (shmat(:,k) * pfts_refs(:,k,iref,ieo) in gen_raw_euclid_vals_impl and gen_euclid_crvec), then correlate over every in-plane rotation by FFT. At rotation r, a reference-frame shift s therefore acts as the particle-frame shift R_r s. The code converts this way itself: grad_shsrch_minimize rotates the shift by get_rot(irot) when sh_rot is set, and materialize_seed_shift rotates the seed by the assigned in-plane angle. A fixed particle shift is a different reference-frame shift at every rotation, so a fixed bank of particle shifts reproduces the reference-frame grid at one rotation only. Restricting the change to fixed-angle searches gains nothing: the table uses the alternating object, and the fixed-angle coarse_search scores single rotations by direct sums with no FFT to save. Caching the grid's 25 shift matrices would be exact, but it is worth about 5% of a grid point and is not pursued.

    Exact pointwise. For one particle shift t and one rotation r, the particle-side value equals the reference-side value at the reference-frame shift that t corresponds to at r, to single-precision rounding: |X·S(t) − CTF·R|² = |X − CTF·R·S(−t)|², with |S| = 1 and a real CTF, so ft_ctf2 and the particle's weighted power are unchanged. Both changes rest on this identity, and a test pins it.

    (a) The multi-state coarse grid (prob_grid_ptcl).

    • Lattice. The grid's points are recorded shifts u, in the particle frame like every recorded shift: today's 5 × 5 lattice (spacing h = 2·trs/5, at 0, ±0.4·trs and ±0.8·trs), 25 points. Converted to the reference frame at a rotation, a corner point (|u| = 1.13·trs) can fall outside the search box; the start is then clamped into the box.
    • Bank. A search object built with the option makes, when its particle changes in set_indices, one bank entry per lattice point. Entry u holds the particle's X·CTF shifted by −u and transformed over rotation, as ft_ptcl_ctf is in memoize_ptcls. It scores as the reference shifted by u in the particle frame. The sign lives in the builder only. Size: 25 × (pftsz+1) × nk complex, 3.1 MB per thread at box_crop 182.
    • Grid. Each entry is scored by a variant of the unshifted branch (Euclid and CC) that takes the entry in place of ft_ptcl_ctf(:,:,i): one base kernel per lattice point, all rotations. The grid keeps the lowest (u*, r*) and every point's loss vector (for 3.2).
    • Start. The search starts at rotation r* from the reference-frame shift that u* corresponds to there (the inverse of the sh_rot conversion), clamped into the box. It then continues as today, with today's acceptance against the zero-shift cost.
    • Scope. fill_tab_range passes the option at construction only when the key is on and the table has more than one state. Every other new_alternating caller is unchanged by construction. A one-grid-per-particle consumer would gain nothing, because its bank costs what its grid costs.
    • What changes. The lattice samples the particle's own shift, the same at every rotation, with the same 25 points as today. Today's grid samples a fixed reference shift, which is a different particle shift at every rotation. So the start of L-BFGS-B, and through it the recorded distances, may change.
    • Cost. Per refined reference: 25 base kernels instead of 25 × 5.3 ≈ 133. Per particle: 25 ramps and FFT sets (about 25 × 4.3 base kernels' worth), shared by the 17 to 55 refined references.

    (b) The single-state seeded table (prob_seed_ptcl). estimate_shift_seed finds a reference-frame shift s at the rotation r_s where its search ends (minimize returns it in irot, or 0 with a zero shift). Today every reference is scored at s as a shift of the reference, and every refined reference starts from s (xy_in). An unrefined assigned candidate gets s rotated by its own in-plane angle (materialize_seed_shift). Away from r_s, s is a different particle shift. With the key on, the seed is used throughout as one particle-frame shift u_s, which is s converted at r_s (what sh_rot gives). Three things change together, so that every recorded shift matches how its candidate was scored:

    • Scoring. The particle's X·CTF is shifted once by −u_s into a one-entry bank (the builder of (a)). Every reference is scored with the unshifted-kernel variant taking that entry, in place of the shifted-reference branch (Euclid and CC). A seed below SHERRSQ uses the plain memo, as today.
    • Refinement start. A refined reference at rotation r starts from u_s converted to the reference frame at r.
    • Assignment. The seed table holds u_s.
    • In ref_assign, assign_current_ref records u_s for an unrefined candidate, whatever its in-plane angle, instead of calling materialize_seed_shift. The rule is a small module procedure of simple_eul_prob_tab.f90, so it can be tested without a project.
    • materialize_seed_shift stays as it is for the 2D and neighbourhood tables.
    • prob_tab and prob_align read the same command line, so the table file format does not change.

    The seed's estimation is unchanged. At r_s, every score, start and recorded shift equals today's; elsewhere they differ. Cost: nspace × 5.3 base kernels per particle become nspace + about 4.3, so with a non-zero seed about a fifth of today's.

    3.2 A quadratic shift step from the grid (approximate; prob_grid_fit)¶

    The table's refinement can be made cheaper, not dropped. ref_assign consumes the refined distances, because refine_best_refs replaces the projs_ns entries of loc_tab: - with more than one state, the state is the argmin distance over all references (assign_greedy_state_labels); - the projection is drawn from the distances within that state (assign_refs_for_state).

    The matcher then takes state, projection and in-plane index as given (srch_prob in simple_strategy3D_prob.f90) and only polishes the pose.

    Scope. The step runs in the multi-state refinement, in the objects 3.1(a) scopes, after the coarse grid. The grid keeps its 25 loss vectors in either frame: the reference-frame grid with prob_grid_ptcl off, the lattice of 3.1(a) with it on.

    The step.

    1. Neighbourhood. Take the grid minimum g*, its rotation r*, and the 3 × 3 stencil around g*, all at r*. If g* is on the edge of the 5 × 5 lattice, there is no fit (9 of the 25 points have a full stencil). A stencil shifted inside the lattice for an edge g* was tried and rejected (section 11).
    2. Fit. Fit the six-parameter least-squares quadratic over the nine values, in units of h: f(a,b) = c + gᵀ(a,b) + ½ (a,b) H (a,b)ᵀ, in closed form on the regular stencil.
    3. Curvature. H must be positive definite, with λmin/λmax ≥ 0.01; otherwise there is no fit.
    4. Step. δ = −H⁻¹g must lie in the centre cell, |δa|, |δb| ≤ ½; otherwise there is no fit. A stationary point outside it contradicts g* being the grid minimum.
    5. Limits. The fitted shift, converted to the reference frame at r*, must lie in the search box; otherwise there is no fit.
    6. Value. Evaluate exactly once at the fitted shift over all rotations (about 5.3 base kernels); the rotation is its argmin. The fit is kept only if its cost is below the grid minimum's; otherwise there is no fit.

    "No fit" falls back to today's L-BFGS-B alternations from the grid minimum. Either way the result passes today's acceptance against the zero-shift cost. The search object counts fits, and fallbacks per reason (steps 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6).

    Second form. It is built only if the fit alone fails the case acceptance of section 10 (Phase 3): the fit, then L-BFGS-B from the fitted shift with maxits 10 and one alternation.

    Cost. On the fitted fraction, about 5.3 base kernels replace alternations × (2E + 5.3) ≈ 100 to 300 per refined reference. The fallback fraction keeps today's cost.

    3.3 The table loop (exact; Phase 4)¶

    The table loop streams, per particle and thread, the whole memoised reference bank of the particle's parity (3,000 × 248 KB = 744 MB), at under one flop per byte.

    Blocking. A thread takes B particles and, for each reference, evaluates the B pairs before moving on. A reference's memos are then loaded once per block. The per-pair work is unchanged.

    Candidate storage comes with it. Today's buffers are append-only, and prob_candidate_buffer%append_or_replace finds a particle's earlier candidate by scanning back while the particle index matches. Reference-major blocking interleaves particles, so refined candidates would be appended as duplicates. read_tab_to_glob would then refuse the table, because every particle must have exactly nrefs candidates. So blocking uses block-local dense storage: - per thread, arrays over (nrefs, B) for distance and in-plane index (as dists_refs and inpl_refs today), plus shift and has_sh for refined entries; - the score loop fills them reference-major; - then, particle by particle, refine_best_refs writes its refined entries in place, and the particle's nrefs candidates are emitted with append in reference order.

    The stream keeps today's layout and contents, and the 3D table no longer calls append_or_replace. Memory: nrefs × B × 24 bytes per thread, 1.2 MB at 3,000 references and B = 16.

    Scope. Every table scored with the unshifted kernel: the multi-state table, doshift=no, and the single-state table with prob_seed_ptcl on, which needs one seed entry per block particle (B entries per thread). The single-state table with that key off shifts the reference per pair, so a block would share no memos there, and it keeps today's loop.

    Determinism (ruled: default path, no key). Within a thread, the order of the ran3 draws that pick the in-plane rotation changes, so the picks are the same in distribution, not draw for draw.

    Product form. Writing the contraction as BLAS matrix products over blocks of particles is not in this run. A later plan takes it up if blocking leaves the score loop the larger part.
