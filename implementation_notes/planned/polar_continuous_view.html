    Continuous view refinement on polar sections¶

    Status, 2026-11-17: planned. Base: e94681175. Depends on: the batch-capacity floor in simple_matcher_memory.f90, in the working tree (validated: no); LM in the optimizer factory, Phases 1-2 of lm_optimizer.md (validated: no). If accepted, replaces sections 6.2-6.5 of refine3d_local_cartesian_priors_todo.md (banner added then).

    1. Why¶

    PorF refine3D_local from 3_solve3D (37,502 particles, C1, 8 iterations): prob_neigh gives 3.85/4.33 Å (FSC 0.143/0.5), cont 4.00/4.59 Å. 77 % of the paired poses that disagree by more than 10° (5,300 of 6,894) include an in-plane error above 20°: the polar in-plane scan reaches them, the Cartesian descent cannot. The polar price is memory, not compute: the 20,000-direction bank takes 17.6 GiB per worker, while in iteration 8 refinement took 950 thread-s against 398 for scoring. Aim: keep the polar search on a coarse bank of 1,000 directions, with nspace_sub = nspace so that the bank holds only the coarse directions, and refine the drawn pose's view continuously.

    The view gradient. A polar sample at (kx, ky) sits at loc = Rᵀ(kx, ky, 0). A tilt (ωx, ωy) about the in-plane axes moves it along n = Rᵀẑ by ωx·ky − ωy·kx, so ∂S/∂ωx = ky·∂nV and ∂S/∂ωy = −kx·∂nV, where ∂nV = ∇V(loc)·n. This is one extra sampled quantity. It enters gen_raw_euclid_grad_at_angle (simple_polarft_corr.f90) like the two shift columns, so the view gradient comes out at every in-plane rotation from the same batched angular FFT.

    Checked at the base.

    • Every drawn 3D pose already gets a shift + in-plane solve (refine_assignment_continuously).
    • The coarse cells of prob_neigh index into the bank (subspace_inds, simple_builder.f90), so the bank holds all nspace directions. With nspace_sub = nspace the bank holds only the coarse ones.
    • cart_refs_needed already writes the Cartesian volumes beside the bank.
    • Only the B-spline gather (gather_packed_bspline3_window_grad) has a gradient.
    • Polar sigma2 is taken against the grid section proj.

    2. Contract¶

    • Pipeline unchanged. The fill, keyed draw, temperatures and weighted insertion stay as they are. The draw sees bank (KB) scores only. The refinement compares B-spline scores only: it commits on improvement over its own B-spline re-score of the drawn pose.
    • Ownership. cartft_calc keeps the 3D B-spline references and gains one public point query, gather_ref (value and gradient). The new polarft_cont_calc (pftc/simple_polarft_cont_calc.f90) owns the polar side: the section and ∂nV at a rotation, the per-thread section cache, the tilt parametrisation and the solver's cost interface. polarft_calc keeps the particles and correlates supplied sections. cartft_calc and polarft_calc never import each other.
    • Committed pose. Composed from matrices, Rz(ψ)·R_tilt·R_ref, never by pairing e1, e2 with a new e3. proj is the nearest grid direction. The sigma2 residual is the final evaluation's.

    3. Decisions (2026-11-17, Hans)¶

    1. Interpolator. Cubic B-spline on cartft_calc's padded references for on-the-fly sections; the KB bank stays for coarse scoring. KB derivatives (the KB window's derivative is closed form) are the fallback if T1/T2 show the two scales disagree.
    2. Owner. The solve of refine_assignment_continuously gains two view variables. No new strategy, route or program.
    3. Bound. Tilts about the drawn reference's in-plane axes, bounded by |ωx|, |ωy| ≤ 2√(4π/nspace) rad, two grid spacings (12.8° at nspace 1,000; ruled "at least 2×").
    4. Objective. Polar Euclidean only. The two view columns use the +conjg Friedel extension; their |S|² derivative columns are 2Re(S̄·∂S).
    5. Poses refined. Only the committed pose; weighted-insertion extras keep the 3-variable solve. Refining several posterior samples is section 6, not this plan.
    6. Switch. One key, cont_view=no|lbfgsb|lm, on refine3D_local refine=prob_neigh with one state only; refused elsewhere before workers launch. It stays no until the real-data read, and it becomes no|yes once decision 7's comparison has a winner.
    7. Optimizer: both, compared (ruled). The joint object of pftc_shsrch_grad gains a 5-variable mode that runs 'lbfgsb' or 'lm' from the factory (lm_optimizer.md).
    8. One cost interface. polarft_cont_calc supplies f, the gradient and the Gauss-Newton matrix (15 extra angular-FFT columns); L-BFGS-B ignores the matrix.
    9. One chart. (ωx, ωy, ωz, tx, ty) is an increment from the drawn pose by the exponential map, with ωz the in-plane angle; derivatives are taken in that chart.
    10. The case for LM, to be tested. cont's LM takes 3.3-9 passes per PorF solve, while the polar shift searches take 9-15 gradient evaluations. Here every evaluation that moves the view costs a section.
    11. Bank = coarse grid (ruled). With cont_view on, nspace_sub is forced to nspace (1,000), and an explicit different value is refused. The coarse search scores the bank itself, and no reprojections are made beyond its 1,000 directions. The continuous view refinement takes the place of the fine neighbourhood step.

    Defaults taken without a question: the key name.

    • LM itself and its other clients (lm_optimizer.md).

    4. Acceptance and stops¶

    Unit tests use the 4-blob phantom of simple_pftc_inplane_tester.f90, whose transform is analytic:

    • T1, section accuracy. The B-spline section's per-shell relative RMS error against the analytic transform is ≤ 2× the KB bank's error at the same 20 rotations. The KB error is measured in Phase 0; the factor 2 is guessed. The setting (unmasked phantom, the nspace = 20 c1 spiral, shells 2-17) and the per-shell floors of T1 and T2 are frozen in Progress, Phase 0 (R2).
    • T2, normal derivative. ∂nV against the analytic ∂F/∂n, with the same rule.
    • T3, identity. With the view frozen and the bank section supplied, f and grad(1:3) equal gen_raw_euclid_grad_at_angle within IDENTITY_ATOL (1e-4, an existing constant).

    6. Forward: refined posterior samples (not this plan)¶

    The direction after this plan (Hans, 2026-11-17):

    • Sample. Draw K samples from the tempered posterior over a bank of 500-1,000 directions, each with its own keyed draw. The draws are made, never an argmin.
    • Refine. Refine each sample with this plan's 5-variable solve. The hook for this already exists: refine_extra_pose_continuously refines the weighted-insertion extras.
    • Merge. Samples that converge within the insertion cone of one another (insert_cone_from_fsc) are one basin: they merge into one pose and their weights add.
    • Weight or draw. For distinct basins, either each enters the reconstruction weighted by its refined score at the particle's own temperature, or the committed pose is a keyed draw among the basins.

    The insertion stays within the drawn state, as the weighted insertion is now. The open question is the weight-or-draw choice: the standing rule marginalises only inside the cone and leaves hypotheses to the draw, so weighting distinct basins widens it and needs its own test. Refined samples would replace the table's top-k extras and may let the bank shrink below 1,000. This plan keeps the path open: the solve takes any drawn cell, and decision 5 only limits which cells it is applied to.
