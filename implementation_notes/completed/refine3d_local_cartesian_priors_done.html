    Continuous local Cartesian refinement — done¶

    Updated 2026-11-11. This records delivered mechanisms and observed validation, not scientific acceptance of a width policy or resolution of the new review. The single active work list is the todo record, containing the remaining technical concerns and their response/acceptance plan.

    Split at the maintainer's request from refine3d_local_cartesian_priors.md. The original feature is f68dab7b0 on parent 4965b3fe8; the architectural refactor was reviewed in the working tree against 13ad4833e. No commit or new runtime validation is implied by this documentation split.

    Delivered implementation¶

    • Single-state Euclidean Cartesian joint refinement accepts the opt-in cont_prior=none|shift|pose controls. Shift penalties use fixed or all-active stored-increment widths; pose mode adds an explicit-width Matrix-Fisher rotation penalty. The current penalty re-centres on each transaction's seed.
    • Numerical terms, native/cropped-pixel scaling, particle-power access, set/clear lifecycle, bounds, rollback and parameter/worker propagation were implemented with existing numerical and workflow regression coverage.
    • Optimization uses the penalized objective; committed scores and sigma2 residuals retain their data-only meaning. Prior potency, convergence effects and statistical calibration remain open in C2-C8 of the todo record.

    Fixed shift-prior command-line units¶

    The maintainer requires the same command-line convention as polar refinement: positive cont_prior_shift_sigma values are per-axis standard deviations in Angstroms. The parameter remains in Angstroms through construction and worker serialization. Pass preparation converts once to native-pixel variance, (cont_prior_shift_sigma/smpd)^2, using native smpd, independently of cropping. The optimizer retains its existing native-to-cropped-pixel conversion.

    Zero still selects the all-active automatic estimator and its native-pixel floor; prior-off behavior is unchanged. Historical native-pixel CLI values in the archive below describe the old snapshots. To reproduce an old fixed width, use sigma_A = old_sigma_native_pixels * smpd; for example, 2 pixels at 1.5 A/pixel is now entered as 3 A. Historical measurements are not rewritten.

    Unit-conversion regression coverage exercises 3 A at native samplings 1.5, 2 and 3 A/pixel, at full and cropped boxes, retaining the raw command-line value across repeated preparation. It also covers automatic-floor preservation, fixed widths below that floor, and clearing the disabled prior. Build/runtime validation of this unit correction is recorded separately from the earlier refactoring evidence below.

    1. Specification: what is proposed¶

    Purpose and scope¶

    Add opt-in update regularization to single-state refine3D_local with objfun=euclid: shift prior first, then Matrix-Fisher rotation prior after separate review. Preserve the Cartesian Levenberg-Marquardt (LM) stage configuration and coupled solve of all five parameters (three rotations, two shifts).

    Prior support is limited to the production joint route. Reject cont_route=shift_then_joint with cont_prior /= none; do not extend refine_shift. Retirement of that internal test route is separate work. Prior-off behavior remains covered on existing routes.

    Assume late-stage refinement with a useful stored pose. Regularization around each transaction's seed can suppress noise-driven motion but impede correction of wrong seeds. It neither escapes local minima nor limits drift from the original poses.

    This living note replaces and corrects cartesian_posterior_bites.md; older neighborhood/cycle plans do not govern it. Polar search, top-k reconstruction, active-state search, posterior sampling and Laplace output remain out of scope.

    Public control¶

    Use the maintainer-accepted cont_prior control below. Hans's review permits shift work once its required design revisions are incorporated; scientific benefit remains to be established by tests.

    Value Meaning and availability

    Mathematical sources and implementation provenance¶

    These references document mathematical foundations; they are not retrospective claims that external implementations were copied or consulted during development.

    • Gaussian shift penalty: the negative log of an isotropic Gaussian is ||delta_s||^2/(2*sigma_s^2) up to a constant; its gradient and Hessian follow by differentiation. Background: C. M. Bishop, Pattern Recognition and Machine Learning (2006), author/publisher resource.
    • Rotation model: T. Lee, Bayesian Attitude Estimation with the Matrix Fisher Distribution on SO(3), IEEE TAC 63(10), 3377-3392 (2018), doi:10.1109/TAC.2018.2797162; author manuscript, section II-A, equation (5). Substituting F=kappa*R_seed into p(R) proportional to exp(trace(F^T R)) gives the isotropic trace penalty. Its matrix-difference form, small-angle width matching, right-increment derivatives and Gauss-Newton curvature are derived for this implementation; the paper does not supply our crop/power scaling.
    • SIMPLE reuse: src/main/strategies/search/probabilistic/simple_eul_prob_tab_utils.f90: existing shift_prior_sigma2 supplies the mean-square formula and floor. The extracted helper preserves polar population and rounding. Existing src/main/cftc/simple_cartft_pose_opt.f90 supplies joint LM, coordinate scaling, Cholesky, bounds and transaction behavior. The all-active Cartesian population is our experimental policy, not a literature-validated estimator.
    • Local derivation: T_cart=P/4, crop conversion, penalty weights and the DC approximation follow SIMPLE's Fourier counting/noise and normalized-loss conventions (Numerical contract below), not a borrowed universal formula. Independent scaling/derivative tests support the implementation; they do not establish scientific reliability or justify the quarter-wavelength floor.

    Numerical contract¶

    Let L be normalized data loss, P whitened particle power, c = box_crop/box, and delta_s = s - s_seed in cropped pixels. Production observations already include the stored shift, so their seed increment is zero. Lower-level seeds may be nonzero. Always subtract the actual seed and freeze it through all stages.

    Here sigma2 is the variance per real/imaginary Fourier-noise component. Each conjugate pair is one independent complex observation counted twice in the full-plane residual sum. Its negative log-likelihood is one quarter of that sum, giving T_cart = P/4. Independently test this convention, normalization and active-band/DC handling.

    Accepted DC exception: when the active band includes the real zero-frequency pixel, P/4 gives its likelihood contribution half the required weight. Retain this approximation and the existing data objective for the first experiment. Test conjugate-pair scaling and the DC exception separately; state whether DC is active.

    The Gaussian shift penalty in loss units is:

    F_s = w_s * dot_product(delta_s, delta_s)
w_s = 1 / (2 * sigma_s^2 * c^2 * T_cart) = 2 / (sigma_s^2 * c^2 * P)
g_s = 2 * w_s * delta_s
H_s = 2 * w_s * I_2

    Here sigma_s is a per-axis width in native pixels. Do not apply prob_beta: T_cart converts units and is not a sampling schedule.

    Experimental shift-width policies¶

    Policy in brief: the shift width is a soft leash around each LM run's starting pose. A smaller width gives a stronger penalty; it is not a hard motion limit. Automatic width uses all active records' latest displacement magnitudes, interpreted as per-axis variance under zero-mean, isotropic 2D motion, with a one-native-pixel or quarter-matching-wavelength minimum. This limits tightening as updates shrink. The width stays fixed within each run and is re-estimated at the next pass; a positive explicit shift sigma instead selects a fixed width. These assumptions and the inherited minimum remain experimental.

    The first adaptive candidate uses all active particles' latest stored shift increments, not the current worker's partition. Production policy will be selected from comparative evidence. For N active records its shared mean-square formula is:

    sigma_floor = max(1 native pixel, lp / (4 * smpd))
sigma_s^2   = max(sigma_floor^2, sum(shincarg_i^2) / (2*N))

    The factor two converts mean squared 2D displacement to per-axis variance. Use the matching low-pass, native sampling and double-precision reduction. Freeze the width before updates. Fractional updates leave increments from different iterations: this is not a strict previous-iteration estimator. Include finite, nonnegative values, including zeros. The record initializes shincarg to zero, so absent history and measured zero motion cannot be distinguished. All-zero history uses the floor; report floor selection without claiming measured stability. A negative, NaN or infinite increment in an active record stops with the particle identified. Do not silently discard invalid records or change the population; inactive records do not contribute.

    Use existing fatal-error and queue-failure handling. Width validation runs serially before particle matching: report particle ID and stop nonzero. The queue controller already detects failed/lost parts and stops the master (src/utils/qsys/simple_qsys_ctrl.f90: stop_on_failed_parts); reuse it without a new failure protocol. Existing simple_qsys_ctrl_tester.f90 covers failed local parts/statuses. After implementation, add/run a focused Linux distributed case asserting that an invalid active increment causes master failure before merge, without waiting for successful completion or accepting partial output. This is future coverage, not a test available to run now.

    The scientific reason for this candidate population is to estimate typical latest motion across the active project, rather than let a changing sample define the width. This trades freshness for population coverage: unsampled particles retain old values, and a polar-to-Cartesian handoff can mix discrete and LM increments. Cartesian and polar priors may therefore have different widths on the same field. This rationale and its scientific benefit need comparative evidence. Preserve polar semantics. Scalar reset/preparation is specified in the ownership table.

    Use fixed-width fixtures to isolate penalty mathematics. Adaptive widths may shrink as the prior suppresses motion; test tightening and premature convergence. The floor alone does not establish safety.

    Agreed experimental comparison: prior off, adaptive width, and fixed standard deviations of 1x, 2x and 4x the initial width floor. Freeze each fixed width throughout its run; match starting poses, references, random seeds and iteration budget. These are experimental settings, not production defaults. Derive numerical tolerances from analytic formulas and finite-difference accuracy, not candidate results.

    Production widths must follow an explicit statistical model and a sampling/resolution rationale, evaluated across datasets. The beta-gal comparison checks behavior; it does not determine a dataset-specific production constant. The mean-square formula has a per-axis displacement interpretation; the one-pixel/quarter-wavelength floor is an inherited safeguard whose production justification remains open.

    A completed-sample median-based width is an additional proposed comparator, not yet a specified estimator. Before testing it, define its conversion to per-axis variance and capture the completed sample before next-sample markers overwrite its identity. Do not substitute median displacement directly into the variance formula. Median limits minority outliers but can hide difficult tails and cannot protect against an iteration where most particles move incorrectly. Compare outlier sensitivity, stale history and bad-iteration behavior; do not choose a production winner before testing. This comparator's definition is a later experiment-design gate, not a blocker for the first shift-penalty and fixed/all-active tests.

    Rotation choice and width policy¶

    Use an isotropic Matrix-Fisher penalty in the later rotation step:

    F_r = (w_r/2) * ||R - R_seed||_F^2
w_r = 1 / (2 * sigma_r^2 * T_cart)

    For rotation matrices this equals w_r * (3 - trace(R_seed^T R)), approaching w_r * theta^2 at small angles. Matrix differences avoid trace cancellation and give zero at rounded seeds. Use consistent right-increment derivatives and residual Gauss-Newton curvature, not an assumed exact Hessian away from the seed.

    Alternative Benefit Cost or risk Decision
    Squared geodesic angle Direct angular-distance interpretation. Requires consistent rotation-log derivatives and curvature. Not selected.
    Squared angle with constant curvature Simple local approximation. Constant 2*w*I is not its exact Hessian away from the seed. Not selected.
    Matrix-Fisher via matrix differences Smooth residual formulation; no rotation logarithm; compatible with joint LM. Width needs a stated convention; Gauss-Newton remains an approximation. Selected.

    Start with fixed experimental widths: sigma_r is small-angle per-axis standard deviation in radians, not RMS rotation magnitude. Compare recovery and stopping against prior-off on known pose errors; values and margins remain open. dist and dist_inpl are symmetry-dependent summaries, not rotation-vector components. Defer adaptation until geometry, symmetry handling and an independent floor are established. Do not reuse same-image posterior covariance as the next prior. Rotation derivatives use physical radians. Apply rotation_scale through the existing LM coordinate transformation after forming prior derivatives; do not encode that scale again in sigma_r or the prior weight.

    Corrected background retained for future work¶

    These ideas are deferred, not prerequisites or approved implementation packages:

    • Laplace diagnostics: Gauss-Newton approximates curvature; it is not the exact Hessian. Posterior covariance needs data plus prior curvature in consistent units, without LM damping or the calculator's heuristic curvature scaling. Stationarity, positive definiteness and active bounds matter. A joint covariance's shift marginal is the shift block of the full inverse, not the inverse of its shift block. Under these assumptions, Sigma ~= inverse(T_cart * H_total_raw); the calculator's unscaled data Gauss-Newton block is 2*H_accum/P. Local refinement currently has no consumer for this covariance, so do not add a getter or persistent output for it.
    • Width feedback: A per-particle covariance inferred from the same image is not automatically an independent prior for the next pass. Keep the population policy above. The separate polar_shift_laplace_width.md proposal uses widths for search support, not as a likelihood term; it is not assumed implemented here.
    • Top-k mixtures: Refining every candidate and committing the heaviest would change the existing random-draw contract. A retained top-k list is not the full posterior. Laplace mode weights require comparable objectives, consistent priors, valid local covariances and duplicate-mode handling; fitting error alone is not mode probability. Cost grows with the number of LM starts and needs measurement. The proposed local evidence factor exp(-E_k)*sqrt(det(Sigma_k)) is meaningful only with comparable negative-log posterior energies and the same integration measure; separate seed-centered penalties are not automatically one common posterior.
    • Latest separate workflow discussion: Keep polar extras, optimize only the selected pose, and initially retain original polar weights. This supersedes the background's all-candidate LM proposal, but is not implemented by this local plan.
    • Future sampling/reweighting: For inverse-temperature density exp(-beta*E), local covariance scales as Sigma/beta, not beta*Sigma. Normalize future mode weights in double-precision log space; this does not add sampling to local LM.
