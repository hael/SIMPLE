    Polar shift search: performance refactoring¶

    Status: implementation and local validation completed; integrated in 69a623cb8 on 2026-11-04. Archived in completed/ on the same date. Target-data performance/memory measurements remain follow-up work, with no production speedup or Linux/BOX validation claimed. The staged/uncommitted handoff statements below describe the recorded development snapshots. Initial baseline source reviewed on master at e13422f3bca0205a808146084de7bf43ef0d6194. This is the living record for its contract, implementation sequence, review findings and validation results. The rebase onto ac9032343 preserves the newer increment-prior and numerical fixes; section 8 records its validation separately from the original baseline's evidence.

    1. Objective and scope¶

    Reduce solve2D and solve3D execution time while preserving the recovery gained from dynamic per-particle re-centring and exhaustive shift/in-plane search. First remove redundant computation and improve reuse. Changes to numerical precision, work ordering or search discretization are separate experiments, accepted only against their own quality and performance evidence.

    There is no per-particle cap on the number of exhaustive searches in this plan. A particle's search history alone does not establish that its next large-domain search is unnecessary. Never-centred particles, movers and particles with broad posteriors retain their existing route to the disk.

    The shared map kernel serves dense and sparse 2D tables, dense and neighbourhood 3D tables, and classic polar strategies. Cover all these callers when changing its interface. Keep the candidate map and its bounded alternating shift/in-plane polish distinct from the final continuous in-plane polish of a committed pose. The Cartesian refine=cont optimizer is outside this refactor.

    Related records:

    • Speed-week closeout: background for score-loop locality, precision and worker-lifetime experiments. Its timings and estimates are historical, not the baseline of this refactor.
    • Polar shift search and dynamic centring: scientific decisions this refactor preserves.
    • Probability-table memory closeout: historical memory/equivalence evidence remains unrecorded. The task was archived by maintainer disposition, without inferring those results from this polar refactor.

    2. Contract¶

    For the first, result-preserving phases, retain:

    • the master's sampled rows, half labels, reference candidates and their order;
    • the disk/window rule, sentinel reset, shincr/shsig updates, bound inflation, and the preparation mask selected from the same particle state;
    • the matching bands, radial/directional grids, Bessel cutoff, folded harmonics, cell areas, increment prior and its first-centring-only application;
    • the tempered greedy cell and the physical-temperature posterior width, with existing normalization, frame conversions, cutoffs and tie-breaking;
    • candidate ranking, polish bounds, acceptance/fallback rules, final continuous polish, assignment, reconstruction and sigma2 ownership;
    • random draws and their order within a particle; no new reseeding;
    • batching and bounded memory; no all-particle or all-reference full-ring cache.

    Target byte identity on fixed-seed one-thread cases for those phases. Verify kernel outputs separately at multiple threads. Dynamic scheduling currently assigns thread-local random streams to particles, so a changed multi-thread trajectory is not by itself evidence of a numerical error or equivalence.

    Keep geometry and numerical caches in the existing PFTC objects, and candidate shortlisting in the search/table objects. Commanders and strategies retain orchestration. Preserve project ownership, particle-buffer reuse and the sample-once-and-reproduce contract. Introduce no public setting or persistent format for the initial work.

    Paths in the following table are relative to src/main/. These findings describe the initial baseline. Upstream subsequently replaced the absolute prior with an increment prior, eliminating particle-dependent prior tables; the rebase keeps that newer contract.

Those older observations motivate measuring window work as well as disk work; they do not establish the current bottleneck or an achievable speedup.

    4. Implementation sequence¶

    Each phase is a small, independently reviewable change. Measure before moving to the next. Phase numbers indicate priority, not a promised delivery schedule.

    Phase 0 — establish the current baseline¶

    Extend the existing benchmark record rather than introduce another reporting system. Separate map setup, shell construction/contraction, FFT, cell selection, physical-width calculation and candidate polish. Nested phase timers remain breakdowns of the existing fill time, not extra terms in the wall-time total. Aggregate per-thread counters; do not time individual cells.

    Report disk and window particle/map counts separately, actual screened/polished reference counts, grid dimensions and peak process memory. The existing screens counter also counts window maps despite its printed disk-map label; correct the reporting and update any consumers together. Record both wall time and thread-seconds without adding them together.

    Benchmark an early disk-heavy pass, a settled window-heavy pass, a high-resolution 3D neighbourhood pass and a mixed pass with newly sampled particles. Include dense/sparse 2D and shared/distributed execution. Use the same input, sample, references, sigma2, compiler, flags, seed, partitions and threads for each pair. Repeat matched runs at least three times and record median and spread. Separate setup/planning from steady-state throughput; report complete iteration time too.

    Exit: commands, source revisions, machine, configuration, counts, memory and logs are recorded here. Attribute time to measured phases before selecting larger kernel or scheduling changes.

    Phase 1 — separate greedy selection from unused evidence¶

    Provide a PFTC greedy-mode operation that does not request evidence or its prior normalizer. Share loss/prior/cell-mass arithmetic with the existing evaluator; do not create divergent implementations of the objective. Migrate every production map caller, including classic strategies, and audit actual readers before removing any argument or state.

    Preserve the prior and cell measure in cell selection. Preserve the width's physical temperature, existing intermediate rounding, exponential cutoff and accumulation order initially. Remove the tempered evidence sum and redundant prior-normalizer traversal from the production path. Retain the evidence/draw operation for its existing numerical contract and tests.

    Exit: identical greedy cells, losses and widths for representative grids, including ties; unchanged one-thread workflow outputs; measured phase and iteration times. Do not claim removal of the physical-width normalization.

    Phase 2 — cache map geometry and half-specific shell tables¶

    Reuse unchanged map tables, workspaces and plans across batch fills. Geometry validity includes box, angular size, shell band, radial grid, direction count and harmonic cutoff. Workspace validity also includes the available thread capacity; plan reuse retains dimensions, strides, alignment and plan mode. Fixed-seed identity runs retain the existing estimated-plan policy.

    Build immutable Bessel-weighted shell tables before the parallel region, once per map/objective/noise generation and half. Use an existing reliable sigma2 refresh boundary or explicit invalidation; pointer identity does not prove that sigma2 values are unchanged. Remove redundant per-thread wj after migration.

    Keep new/kill symmetric and idempotent. Rebuilding one map must not invalidate another or retain stale pair state. Size new thread storage for the actual possible team, and never resize shared caches inside a scoring region.

    Exit: unchanged results across repeated setup, changed bands/grids, sigma2 updates, team-size changes, unequal/short final batches, and repeated teardown. Report allocation/planning counts and memory as well as runtime.

    Phase 3 — reuse particle and posterior geometry work¶

    Prepare the full-ring particle spectrum once per particle/reference loop. Invalidate it on every new batch and observation/CTF preparation, even when batch size or particle slot is unchanged. Include half and band dependencies where relevant. Keep reuse bounded to batch or per-thread storage; do not duplicate every reference's full spectrum.

    Cache direction vectors, radii squared, log cell areas and rotation geometry. The upstream increment prior has no particle-dependent prior terms, so its former per-particle tables are removed during the rebase. A separate zero-prior specialization would need to preserve cell areas and posterior temperatures.

    First preserve the existing cell loop and sum order. Treat arithmetic reassociation or a different route to physical log masses as a numerical experiment rather than silently including it in this phase.

    Exit: geometry/half/frame tests and same-sized batch-remapping tests pass; unchanged physical widths and centring decisions; fewer preparation calls and a measured gain without unbounded cache growth.

    Phase 4 — calculate widths only for candidates that can survive¶

    Where screening maps precede a smaller polish shortlist, keep a running list of its best losses without changing the existing final ranking or tie rules. Pass its current worst loss as a scalar width cutoff to the map evaluator. After selecting the greedy cell, the evaluator skips physical moment reduction only if the cell's ranking loss is strictly above that cutoff. Update the running losses in the search layer afterward. Candidates later displaced may have incurred width work; strictly excluded candidates cannot re-enter as the cutoff improves.

    Preserve final candidate order and widths. Use one selection traversal per map; do not repeat the mode solve to request a width, rebuild selected maps, retain maps for all candidates, or change which references are mapped. Paths already mapping only the final shortlist need no such change.

    Exit: identical shortlist/assignment on ties and adversarial rankings; widths match an independent direct-grid moment calculation; reduced width-call count on representative fills. Keep only if the additional bookkeeping pays for itself.

    Phase 5 — improve shell contraction and reference locality¶

    Use Phase 0 evidence to choose the next experiment. First tile angular bins and radii while preserving each accumulator's shell order. Keep the first Fortran index contiguous in hot loops and inspect vectorization/temporary-array reports. Use plain, non-overlapping array interfaces where needed to expose SIMD, respecting Fortran argument aliasing rules; contiguous is a proven caller contract, not a substitute for fixing strides.

    Benchmark a tuned matrix-multiplication contraction separately if the shapes warrant it. BLAS/reassociated sums and single-precision score weights are numerical changes, even if the mathematical formula is unchanged. Keep the existing normalization precision until its own evidence supports changing it. Do not add per-file flags, new subset-library targets or blanket fast-math.

    For locality experiments, compare bounded particle/reference tiles or grouping particles with overlapping neighbourhoods. Preserve each particle's candidate semantics. Changing work order changes thread-local random-stream assignment; state that explicitly and use kernel comparisons plus multi-seed quality checks.

    On Linux NUMA hosts, compare disjoint process placement and thread layouts, first-touch owning-thread workspaces, and avoid nested FFT/BLAS oversubscription. Retain dynamic particle scheduling unless measurements justify another choice. Record bandwidth/cache counters where available; do not assume more threads help.

    Exit: a repeatable improvement beyond timing spread at representative sizes, acceptable peak memory, and the numerical/quality gates in section 5. Mac measurements do not establish Linux scaling.

    Phase 6 — optional search-domain/discretization experiments¶

    Only after the preceding measurements, consider an intermediate radius between window and disk, selected from the existing uncertainty state with immediate escalation on boundary hits. Retain the full disk for never-centred particles. The proposed Laplace width is separate scientific work; it is not implemented and cannot replace the coarse map's multi-modality information by assumption.

    Fewer directions on inner rings, or evaluating the origin once, may reduce redundancy. Preserve the combined central-cell mass and rotation semantics; different ring grids require new quadrature/transform and recovery validation. These are discretization changes, not identity-preserving refactors.

    Exit: a stated mathematical contract and matched multi-seed recovery evidence before adoption. A search-count cap or tighter origin-ranked candidate pool is not an implicit fallback if these experiments fail.
