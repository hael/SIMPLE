    Ghostbuster's forward and adjoint operators in SIMPLE¶

    Status: implemented by the unattended run on the Dell (section 7), Phases 0 to 5, 2026-10-23 to 2026-10-24, uncommitted against base 5111068bf; the progress and evidence are in section 8, the summary in ghostbuster_operators_report.md beside this plan, the contract of the code in doc/policies/3D/wave_model_policy.md. Written 2026-10-23, rulings of the same day applied (section "Rulings"), from specter (the package that contains Ghostbuster) at 5786651 in ~/src/specter and SIMPLE at 630c2604a.

    Summary¶

    Ghostbuster predicts a cryo-EM image from a 3D electrostatic potential with wave optics: the beam picks up a phase in the specimen, travels through the objective lens, and the detector records the squared modulus of the wave. Its reconstruction runs that prediction backwards with automatic differentiation. This plan brings both directions into SIMPLE as Fortran, first as a standalone program, reconstruct3D_wave, that reconstructs half maps from a project with known poses and CTF. Refinement workflows come later and build on it: Ewald sphere correction, CTF refinement and pose refinement all need this forward operator and its derivative.

    The plan is not a translation. Three facts make a SIMPLE-native design both shorter and faster than the Python original.

    1. For the single-scattering models (projection, first Born, Rytov) the exit wave depends on the volume only through one complex 2D spectrum: the volume's Fourier transform sampled on a paraboloid, the Ewald surface. specter samples it with a non-uniform FFT library. SIMPLE's PCG reconstructor already samples the volume's transform at arbitrary 3D points with a Kaiser-Bessel window and has the exact transpose of that sampling. Adding the paraboloid height to the sampling point is a one-line change to a tested, threaded, deterministic kernel.
    2. Everything after that spectrum is 2D: a pointwise exponential, a complex lens transfer, a squared modulus and a Fourier crop. SIMPLE got a 2D complex FFT engine for this on 2026-10-23 (simulate_scattering, commit f4cac4773). The derivative of each step is short and is written by hand, with dot-product and finite-difference tests in place of autograd.
    3. Ghostbuster's reconstruction (Adam epochs, then a preconditioned nonlinear conjugate-gradient solve with a Wiener prior from the half-map FSC) needs a preconditioner, a prior, a support mask, half sets and an FSC. SIMPLE has all of them in its PCG backend. Only the solver loop is new, and it is written as a generic engine that later refinement can reuse.

    What it buys, stated plainly. For cryo-EM proteins the expected gain is the Ewald-sphere curvature, which limits large particles, low voltages and high resolution. The nonlinear terms (Rytov, multislice) matter for strong scatterers such as the metal nanoparticles of SINGLE. Every claim is tested against simulated data with a known answer.

    Terms¶

    • Potential V: the specimen's electrostatic potential on the native box N^3, voxel size a (SIMPLE's smpd), in volts.
    • Padded lattice: the 2N grid on which SIMPLE's PCG backend does every Fourier operation (pcg_lattice, OSMPL_PAD_FAC = 2). The unknown stays on the native box.
    • Gather and scatter: the Kaiser-Bessel (KB) interpolation that reads the volume's transform at a 3D point, and its exact transpose that deposits a value there (forward_plane and scatter_plane in simple_reconstructor_pcg.f90).
    • Ewald paraboloid: the transform of the volume seen by an image is not the central plane kz = 0 but kz = -lambda |k|^2 / 2 (wavelength lambda), for specter's default "negative" curvature sign.
    • Surface spectrum S_i(h,k): the volume's transform sampled on the posed paraboloid of particle i, on the full disk of native image frequencies (h,k), both signs of h.
    • Exit wave psi: the complex electron wave leaving the specimen.
    • Lens transfer T: the complex objective-lens factor exp(i gamma), gamma the aberration phase.
    • Forward operator: V -> predicted image. Linearisation: the change of the predicted image for a small change dV. Adjoint: the transpose of the linearisation applied to an image residual; summed over particles it is the gradient of the image misfit, the "backprojection".
    • Weak-phase model: SIMPLE's current image model, image = CTF (contrast transfer function) times the central section, which is the first-order limit of every model above.
    • PCG backend: SIMPLE's reconstruction by preconditioned conjugate gradients (rec_backend=pcg), which solves the weak-phase least-squares problem with the gather and scatter above.
    • FSC, SSNR, ML prior: the Fourier shell correlation of the two half maps; the spectral signal-to-noise ratio FSC / (1 - FSC) it implies; the per-shell regularisation 1/tau^2 built from it (maximum-likelihood, as in RELION).
    • SINGLE: SIMPLE's programs for metal nanoparticles imaged by high-resolution TEM.

    1. What Ghostbuster computes¶

    Reconstructor.forward runs the simulator's chain on the volume being reconstructed (ghostbuster/_reconstructor.py):

    1. Scatter (imaging/_specimen.py, scattering/_batch.py). Under rotate_mode="surface" (scattering/_surface.py) the slice sum sum_z F2[V_z](k) F_z(k) (F_z the Fresnel propagator from slice z to the exit plane) is evaluated as the volume's transform on the posed paraboloid, with one type-2 non-uniform FFT of the unrotated volume and a phase. Projection samples the flat plane. With c = i sigma a (sqrt(1 - alpha^2) + i alpha) (sigma the interaction constant, alpha amplitude contrast): projection and Rytov give psi = exp(IFFT[c S]), first Born psi = 1 + IFFT[c S]. Multislice alternates slice transmission exp(i sigma a V_z) and one-slice Fresnel propagation, and needs the rotated volume (rotate_mode="real", trilinear grid_sample).
    2. Oversample: the exponential and the intensity are formed on a grid twice as fine (oversample=2, the default), since both double the bandwidth.
    3. Lens: psi_T = IFFT[T FFT[psi]] with T = exp(-i chi) (defocus, astigmatism, Cs, phase shift, beam tilt, trefoil, B-factor). The defocus refers to the specimen midplane, not the exit face.
    4. Detect: I = |psi_T|^2, Fourier-cropped to the pixel grid, times dose * a^2 for expected counts. No noise, no camera, no coherence envelopes in the inverse.

    The loss is the squared error in counts, per-particle contrast scale applied. The reconstruction default is Rytov (configs/reconstruct.toml), the cheapest model with curvature.

    The backward direction has two levels. The adjoint is PyTorch's autograd; for the surface sampling it is a type-1 non-uniform FFT (nufft.py). The reconstruction (ghostbuster/_converge.py) runs a few Adam epochs per half, then minimises

    1/2 sum_i || y_i - p_i(V) ||^2 + 1/2 sum_k lambda(k) |V(k)|^2,
lambda(k) = W(k) / SSNR(k),   SSNR = FSC / (1 - FSC) of the Adam half maps

    by preconditioned Polak-Ribiere conjugate gradients, preconditioner 1 / (W(k) + lambda(k)), W(k) the data curvature measured per radial band with random probes, step length from a Gauss-Newton estimate refined on 5% of the particles. Four iterations from a five-epoch Adam map converge. The map is then unfiltered by 1 + 1/SSNR. specter also has a tomogram reconstructor (Adam only, multislice through slices of the tilted slab); it is out of scope here.

    2. What SIMPLE already has¶

    • A matched gather/scatter pair on the padded lattice (simple_reconstructor_pcg.f90, simple_pcg_lattice.f90). forward_plane gathers at loc = padf * [h,k,0] R, scatter_plane is its literal transpose, threaded by h-strided colouring with a serial pass over the wrapping rim, bitwise reproducible. test=pcg_recon proves the adjoint identity to 1e-5. Deapodisation, symmetry replication over R S_g, the solve support and the full both-sign-h disk are in place.
    • A density, a preconditioner and priors: accumulate_absT2, finalize_density_accum with the unmeasured-voxel rule (PCG_RHO_SAMPLED_FRAC), the ML prior from the half-map FSC (build_ml_prior_from_density), sigma2 whitening, even/odd halves, automask, half-map diagnostics.
    • A generic linear PCG engine (opt/simple_pcg_solver.f90): an abstract operator with deferred procedures, an adapter per client, the engine owning only its vectors. L-BFGS-B exists but its workspace at n = N^3 is several GB. There is no nonlinear CG.
    • The particle read path of reconstruction: ptcl_read_session, prep_rec_observation and the particle cache (cache=yes), used by the PCG worker's accumulation (accumulate_worker_state_half in simple_rec3D_pcg_strategy.f90).
    • A 2D complex wave engine (image/simple_scattering_simulator.f90, simulate_scattering, Ruben, 2026-10-23): owned c2c FFTW buffers, the interaction constant, the electron wavelength, first Born and multislice, an objective lens with B-factor, Poisson noise.     3. Design¶

    3.1 The surface spectrum¶

    The gather and scatter move from reconstructor_pcg into pcg_lattice and take a curvature kappa:

    loc = padf * [h, k, kz(h,k)] R_i,      kz(h,k) = kappa * wl * (h^2 + k^2) / (2N)

    with wl = lambda / a (SIMPLE's ctfvars%wl) and kappa = -1, +1, 0 for ewald=neg, pos and no. At 300 kV, 1 A pixels and N = 256 the paraboloid leaves the plane by 0.63 Fourier voxels at Nyquist; at 100 kV, 0.8 A and N = 400, by 2.3. With kappa = 0 the code is the current PCG code, bitwise. The colouring argument holds unchanged (the map (h,k) -> loc is a rotation of a graph over (h,k)); the rim prefilter sq_rim must include kz.

    S_i(h,k) = gather(loc) * exp(+2 pi i (h s_x + k s_y) / N), the shift on the model side as build_transfer puts it, so the particle preparation of the PCG backend (prep_rec_observation) is reused unchanged.

    Symmetry is replication over R_i S_g in the gather and the scatter, as in PCG: the model sees the symmetrised volume, which is what specter's solver does with its exact point-group average.

    Defocus reference: sampling the paraboloid about the box centre makes the nominal defocus the midplane's with no extra phase. specter's propagation to the exit face and its midplane defocus shift cancel to exactly this for even boxes (numpy, first Born on a random 16^3 volume: equal to 2e-15).

    Measured in Phase 2: the cancellation holds for specter's negative curvature sign; for its positive sign specter refers the defocus one slice off the box centre (an even box's centre slice is nz/2 + 1 slices from that sign's exit face, nz/2 for the other), so specter's positive-sign images equal SIMPLE's at one voxel less defocus (images within 1e-4 with that shift, 3e-3 to 9e-3 without). SIMPLE keeps the symmetric reference about the box centre for both signs. The direction is pinned by a point scatterer: with ewald=neg a point z0 voxels along +z images as the flat model at defocus df + z0 (the test of section 5.1), and the parity with specter (section 8, Phase 2) confirms kappa = -1 for specter's negative sign: the opposite sign is off by 0.2 to 0.7 in images and gradients.

    3.2 Image formation on the padded grid¶

    The padded lattice is 2N per side, which is specter's default image oversampling, so the fine grid comes for free. For particle i:

    u   = IFFT_2N[ embed(S_i) ]                       complex; S_i zero beyond the native disk
psi = exp(c u)        projection (kappa = 0), Rytov
psi = 1 + c u         first Born
psi_T = IFFT_2N[ T * FFT_2N[psi] ]                T on the fine-grid frequencies
I_hat = crop_N( FFT_2N[ |psi_T|^2 ] )             native disk, the detector's band limit
p_i   = -g * I_hat / N^2  (k /= 0)                in the units of SIMPLE's particles (1/N^2: the unnormalised FFT)

    c = i sigma a (sqrt(1 - alpha^2) + i alpha) with alpha = fraca. The minus sign and the gain g map relative intensity to SIMPLE's contrast-inverted, noise-normalised particles (section 3.4). The residual is formed on the native disk in Fourier space, whitened by the particle's sigma2, exactly where PCG forms its right-hand side.

    Three details measured in Phase 2 belong to this chain. Rytov takes its exponential where the wave leaves the specimen, as specter does and as the Rytov approximation is defined: on the curved models the field is carried half a box downstream to the exit face (Kirkland's propagator P = exp(-i pi lambda (N a / 2) |k|^2) on the native disk), exponentiated there, and carried back to the midplane for the lens (P^-1 folded into T); for first Born the two cancel exactly, and without curvature the model has no depth, so Rytov with kappa = 0 stays projection. Taking the exponential at the midplane instead differed from specter by 2% (a protein of 0.7 rad) to 17% (a Pt particle of 2.6 rad) in images; at the exit face the exit waves agree to 8e-6. The phase shift of a phase plate acts on the scattered waves only: T(0) = 1 for the unscattered beam (a phase common to all frequencies would vanish from the intensity). And SIMPLE's wavelength is the CTF's 12.26 / sqrt(V + 0.9784e-6 V^2), 3.4e-4 short of the exact relativistic value specter uses; the defocus phase pi lambda df |k|^2 reaches some 300 rad at Nyquist for 2 um at 300 kV, so the two packages' lens phases differ by about 0.1 rad there, and by about 0.5 rad at a Pt particle's Nyquist through Cs. Parity is measured with specter given SIMPLE's constants; the constant itself is SIMPLE's, shared with the CTF by design.

    The weak-phase limit fixes every convention. With V -> eps V, p_i / eps -> 2 g sigma a * CTF * S_i(flat) where CTF = sin(evalPhSh + phshift + amp_contr_const) is SIMPLE's CTF: the new operator collapses onto forward_plane times build_transfer (numpy, with amplitude contrast 0.1 and Cs: holds to second order in the phase). This identity is the first test written, and it ties orientation, shift, defocus sign, astigmatism angle, amplitude contrast and scale to code that refinement has used for years. It also shows that the amplitude contrast of the wave models is fraca, the same quantity the project already carries per particle.

    The oversampling factor is a constant (2, as specter) until the Dell run has measured the error and the speed of 1 against 2; a parameter is added only if the measurement makes 1 worth offering.

    3.3 Lens transfer¶

    T = exp(i (evalPhSh + phshift)) * exp(-B s^2 / 4), the phase from the ctf type itself (a public eval_phase that returns what evalPhSh computes, the phase a required argument as phase_shift_ctf_policy.md demands). Amplitude contrast lives in c, not in T. Beam tilt and trefoil are not modelled: SIMPLE's project has no fields for them yet, and they come with CTF refinement.

    3.4 Units and the scale link¶

    The nonlinear models need the potential in volts and the images as relative intensity (vacuum = 1); the weak-phase model does not. Two conversions:

    • Images: SIMPLE particles are contrast-inverted and normalised to unit background noise. Following specter's image_units="normalized", relative intensity is 1 - x / sqrt(D a^2) with D the dose in e/A^2 (SIMPLE's total_dose), so g = sqrt(D a^2). The gain the data prefer (a one-scalar fit of the quadratic term) is reported, not used. a is the particles' native pixel size: the reconstruction normalises each particle to unit background noise at its native box before any Fourier crop, and a crop keeps the values, so g = sqrt(D) smpd also at box_crop (Phase 3). The fit: with l the linear part of the prediction (the chain linearised at zero along the solution) and q = p - l its nonlinear part, the data fix g V through l, so y - l = (g / g_data) q and the fitted gain is g sum w|q|^2 / sum w Re(conj(y - l) q). It is meaningful for Rytov and projection, whose nonlinear terms are those of a phase object; first Born lacks the -phi^2/2 of the exponential, and its fit is not a gain.
    • Maps: by the weak-phase identity, a SIMPLE map at the data-quotient scale is 2 g sigma a times the potential in volts, up to SIMPLE's FFT normalisation, which the test pins. The solver works in volts; the program writes its maps at SIMPLE's scale, so they compare directly with reconstruct3D maps and a later integration sees no amplitude jump (the scale-continuity contract of reconstruct3D_pcg_policy.md).

    3.5 Linearisation and adjoint¶

    Each step above is a small operator with a hand-written linearisation and transpose: the KB gather (transpose: the scatter that exists), the embedding and crop (transposes of each other up to the FFT scale), the c2c FFTs, the pointwise exponential (d psi = c psi du), the lens multiply (conj(T)), the squared modulus (dI = 2 Re(conj(psi_T) d psi_T)). The adjoint of a particle is a 2D chain ending in one scatter of a complex plane at the paraboloid points, the same deposit the PCG right-hand side makes. The gradient is the real part of the accumulated lattice transformed back, as in apply_adjoint_all.

    3.6 Reconstruction: one solver for every model¶

    Minimise, per state and half set,

    L(V) = 1/2 sum_i sum_(h,k) |y_i - p_i(V)|^2 / sigma2_i(shell)  +  1/2 sum_q lambda(q) |V(q)|^2

    on the support, with:

    • Start: the half's flat PCG map, solved in the same process with reconstructor_pcg's public API from the same particle pass, converted to volts.
    • Prior: SIMPLE's ML prior from the FSC of the two start maps, built by the existing PCG code. It is the same quantity as specter's W(k)/SSNR(k).
    • Preconditioner: 1 / (rho + lambda), rho the PCG density of the start solve. It is the diagonal of the linearised data term, which specter measures with random probes.
    • Iteration: preconditioned Polak-Ribiere conjugate gradients. The step length uses the exact data slope and the Gauss-Newton curvature ||J d||^2 from the linearisation on a particle subset, refined by a parabola through the loss on the same subset, as specter does. Default four iterations (maxits).
    • Output: the regularised map and the unfiltered half map (1 + 1/SSNR, exact for a Wiener solve), and the FSC of the unfiltered pair.

    Measured and decided in Phase 3. The PCG backend's normal operator, density and prior carry a factor boxpd^3 (its adjoint folds the scattered lattice without the 1/boxpd^3 of the transform's transpose; its own solution does not see the factor), so the prior in volts is kappa^2 P / boxpd^3 with kappa = 2 g sigma a N the scale of a PCG map in volts; the projector's tester checks the link to 2e-6. The preconditioner is PCG's own (a constant scale of it leaves the iterates unchanged). The start is the half's base PCG solve (maxits_pcg) shrunk by the ML prior in closed form (maxits_ml coupled iterations), the PCG master's regularised map; it is written beside the wave maps as recvol_stateNN_<half>_flat.mrc, the flat model under the same prior and support. The step subset is every k-th particle of the half, 5% of it with at least 256 and at most 1000 particles (its planes stay in memory for the solve). The Wiener filter is undone isotropically, by the PCG operator's own shrink factor averaged over each Fourier shell: undone voxel by voxel it amplified the least-sampled voxels by up to P / rho, and the crop back to the native box spread them over every shell (the first run's unfiltered pair crossed an FSC of 0.143 at 17 A instead of 2 A).

    For first Born with a linearised intensity the objective is quadratic, and this is linear CG on the exact two-cap least-squares problem up to the step length taken from a subset. For Rytov, projection and multislice it is the nonlinear solve. One loop serves all of them.

    3.7 Multislice¶

    Multislice is not one sample of the transform. The rotated cube is formed from the lattice: its spectrum on native plane l is the gather at padf [h,k,l] R, then one 3D inverse FFT. Its transpose is the scatter, so the pair is exactly adjoint, which neither rotvol nor trilinear resampling gives. The recursion runs on the 2N fine grid with Kirkland's signs, transmission exp(c v_j) and propagator exp(-i pi lambda a |k|^2), the exit wave referred to the midplane. The adjoint is the reverse sweep, conj(P) and conj(t_j), with the wave stored at checkpoints every ceil(sqrt(N)) slices and recomputed in between. Cost per particle at N = 256: of order a second per core, which suits nanoparticle boxes and small sets, not 100 000-particle cryo-EM sets. Rytov serves those.

    As built in Phase 4. The cube is the native spectrum of the rotated potential on the Nyquist sphere, C(h,k,l) gathered at padf [h,k,l] R_i S_g (with |l| < N/2: the one Nyquist value a z sampled at N points has lies on the sphere only at the plane's origin), times N^3, the shift phase and the symmetry mean; one transform along l gives the sections' disk spectra s_j = (1/N) sum_l C exp(+2 pi i l z_j / N), z_j = j - (N/2 + 1) from the box centre, so no real-space rotated cube is formed. Each section enters the twice-finer grid by the disk embedding of the single-scatter chain, its transmission is exp(c u_j), the propagator acts between sections, and the exit wave is carried back to the box centre (N/2 - 1 sections for the beam entering through the first section, N/2 through the last) before wave_imaging's lens and detector, which take a given exit wave through the same code as their own models. The wave projector runs it as the model multislice under its one batch frame; its gradient passes go in chunks of one particle per thread, each chunk's cubes scattered in particle order. At box 160 one particle costs about 0.9 s forward, 1.2 s for the adjoint sweep and 0.8 s for its cube gather on one core, and each cube scatter 0.3 s on four threads.

    3.8 Parallel execution and performance¶

    Particles are processed in batches: each thread owns its 2D work fields and c2c plans (made once, in the FFTW planner critical section, as scattering_simulator does) and runs the 2D chain of one particle; the batch's gradient planes are then scattered with the coloured scatter, the accumulate_batch pattern of PCG. Single precision fields, double precision losses and dot products.

    Order of magnitude at N = 256: a Rytov gradient is about eight 512^2 complex FFTs plus a gather and a scatter, some tens of milliseconds per particle per core, so a pass over 100 000 particles on the Dell's 24 cores takes minutes and four iterations per half ten to thirty times a PCG accumulation. First Born with a linearised intensity needs no FFT at all (p = A S(k) + B conj(S(-k)) on the native disk) and costs about two PCG right-hand sides per pass.

    4.2 Rules every piece follows¶

    • Imports point downward. Utilities, then ctf, then image, then volume; opt imports no domain module; the strategy imports all of them; the commander imports the strategy. No domain module imports a strategy module: that is why the particle passes live in the strategy, not in wave_projector, and why this work does not add to the known deviation of domain modules using ptcl_read_session (layering policy, section 8).
    • Kinds of step. The program body is a strategy routine on (params, build); the commander is a thin execute(cline) (layering policy, section 3). Numerics live in domain modules. Nothing calls a commander from a strategy.
    • One copy of each frame. The observation-batch frame is extracted from the PCG worker, not copied; the gather and scatter are moved, not copied; the wavelength has one home. The PCG worker's results are byte-identical after the extraction (simple_test_exec test=identity on a reconstruct3D rec_backend=pcg case).
    • Encapsulated classes. Each new stateful module holds one type named after it, with private components, new/kill (symmetric, idempotent) and behaviour as type-bound procedures (simple-modern-fortran). Large components are allocatable, allocated in new and released in kill. No type holds a copy of parameters; it copies the fields it reads (box_crop, smpd_crop, kv, ...).
    • Threads. Per-thread objects (wave_imaging, its FFT plans) sit in allocatable arrays sized by omp_get_max_threads() at the point of use, never by a stored thread count (thread_budget_and_safety.md). FFTW plans are made and destroyed in the unnamed critical section, the one lock every FFTW planner call of SIMPLE takes (image takes it; FFTW's planner is not thread-safe, and a named section would be a second, independent lock; corrected in Phase 1, together with the scattering simulator, which used a named one). The scatter is the existing coloured scatter with its serial rim pass; losses and dot products are summed per particle in double precision and reduced in particle order. Results are therefore identical at any thread count, which the tests check at 1, 11, 16 and 24 threads, repeated.
    • Errors go through THROW_HARD, built in a string variable when long (the macro's argument is never continued across lines). The simulator's error stop calls go with the move.
    • Compile time. use ..., only: with the names used; no umbrella module in new code and nothing added to an umbrella; no new CMake target (the library globs its sources); testers declare large objects class(T), allocatable.
    • Release-4 rules. No compatibility shims: simulate_scattering's scattering_model key becomes wave_model and its absorption key is retired in favour of fraca, with no alias. No goto, no reliance on integer overflow, no short-circuit on an allocatable's element. Dead code found on the way is deleted.

    5.2 specter as the oracle¶

    specter has its own suite (111 test files, golden outputs, parity tests between its simulator and its reconstructor). On the Dell it serves as the oracle: installed outside SIMPLE, its tests for the parts used here run first, then an export script in the run's scratch writes reference cases.     Conventions cross the boundary through RELION star files. specter writes and reads them with its own code; on SIMPLE's side Phase 0 found that export_relion writes no poses and no origins at all (only image and micrograph names, optics, CTF and coordinates), and that SIMPLE's star import and its three star exports (the particle table of simple_starfile.f90, the star project of simple_starproject.f90 and the stream's export in simple_starproject_stream.f90) copy RELION's rlnOriginXAngst/YAngst, in angstroms, into the particle shift x, y, in pixels, and back, without the pixel size.     6. Risks¶

    • Accuracy of the KB lattice. About 1e-3 against specter's 1e-6. It sets the parity tolerance; it is far below the noise of any particle. Measured in Phase 2: the PCG lattice's three-tap window interpolates a section to 0.7 to 1% of the image contrast (1.3 to 1.8% of a gradient; corrected in Phase 5 to the maxima of section 8, which read 0.6 to 0.9% and 1.2 to 1.8% here before), ten times the estimate; a seven-tap window brings the operator to 4e-5 to 9e-4. Both are far below the noise of a particle; the wave operator keeps the PCG lattice's window, and new_lattice takes a window size for a caller that needs more.
    • Scale of real data. Dose-weighted sums do not have Poisson background noise, so g from the dose is approximate. It only scales the nonlinear terms, which are small for proteins; the fitted gain shows how far off it is.
    • Local minima for strong scatterers. specter reports that a specimen with 17 rad peak phase converged from a 15 A low-passed start and not from zero. The start here is a linear map; a low-pass schedule is the fallback.
    • Repeated particle passes. Four to five reads per half; the particle cache makes them cheap when cache=yes, otherwise they cost what a PCG accumulation costs each time.
    • Thread safety of the curved scatter. The colouring still separates windows; the rim prefilter changes. Covered by the repeated thread tests.
