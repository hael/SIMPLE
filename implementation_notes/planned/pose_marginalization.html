    Pose marginalization in the probabilistic 3D search¶

    Implementation note, 2026-10-21, revised 2026-10-22 twice: after the measurements (sections 8-9) and after the review (pose_marginalization_review.md, section 11). Design, not yet justified; the diagnostic is implemented (prob_post_diag=yes), the design is not, and the review gate of section 11 comes before it. Sections 2-3 describe what exists and the posterior; section 4 is the design; sections 5-7 are the diagnostic, the preconditions and the test plan; sections 8-9 the measurements, with section 9's inference withdrawn in section 11. Written after the aggressive-frequency-marching experiment (freq_march_aggressive, solve3D_policy.md section 4): HR-HAIR's high-resolution start works inside cryoSPARC's SGD with pose marginalization over mini-batches, and PfCRT latest10 showed it does not transfer to a hard-assignment stochastic hill climb from random orientations. This note asks what marginalization itself would look like in SIMPLE, and whether it is worth building.

    The answer so far (sections 8-9, as corrected in 11). Given the reference of the iteration, the posterior over the direction grid is sharp (n_eff near 1 for the median particle), so there is no broad posterior to spread; whether that sharpness is calibrated against the data (and not an artefact of the Gaussian model, the correlated polar samples or the nuisance search) is open until the known-pose test of section 11. The assignment's draw ignores that posterior: at T = 1 on the normalized loss it is near-uniform over the ~140 best frontier heads (a code fact, section 2), and the production row's mode is unstable between iterations where a deterministic re-scoring of the same reference is stable (section 9, corrected). The proposed fix, drawing the committed pose from the row posterior at its temperature (section 4), is therefore a hypothesis with a mechanism behind it, not a demonstrated remedy: the "cross-half" rows of section 9 scored the same merged reference, so the self-reinforcement chain written there is unproven.

    1. The question¶

    For a small membrane protein the pose likelihood is expected to be broad or multimodal: several views through the micelle fit nearly equally. A hard assignment then commits each particle to one of them and the reconstruction averages the commitments' errors; marginalization lets each particle enter the reconstruction at every plausible pose with its posterior weight, so a particle that cannot decide does not vote for one wrong answer. cryoSPARC offers it as an option ("adaptive marginalization") and reports it as a secondary help for small, low-SNR targets, behind non-uniform regularization and masking. Whether it helps here is an empirical question with a cheap answer: measure how broad the posterior actually is on PfCRT.

    2. What exists¶

    • The table holds the posterior's support. In the prob stages (3-5) each table worker fills, for its particles, a full row dists(iptcl, 1:nrefs) over every projection direction (and state) at a likelihood-sampled in-plane rotation, then shift-refines the projs_ns best into the per-particle refined list, whose refined distances overwrite the row (fill_tab_range, refine_best_refs, simple_eul_prob_tab.f90). The rows are written to the part file and read by the master into dists(nptcls, nrefs). The neighbourhood tables of the prob_neigh stages (6-8) hold the same over a neighbourhood.
    • The assignment is not a per-particle draw. ref_assign is a reference-driven balanced matching: every reference's particles sorted by distance, the frontier heads drawn by draw_likelihood, one (reference, particle) pair committed per step. strategy3D_prob carries that single pose. The draw's weights, exp(-(L - L_min)) on the normalized loss, are nearly flat (section 3), so the draw is in effect uniform over the prob_athres window of frontier heads: an exploration device, not a posterior.
    • The reconstruction already takes weighted multi-insertion. calc_3Drec(..., wset, wtab) lets a particle enter several states with weights; update_state_half_rec inserts fplanes(i) through insert_plane_oversamp(..., w=) per entry (simple_matcher_3Drec.f90). The PCG backend takes records (take_records, get_rec_observation).
    • sigma2 is estimated at the committed pose. calc_sigma2_pftc writes the residual at the assigned orientation into the particle's record (gen_sigma_contrib: the mean squared residual over two per ring, the variance per real or imaginary part).

    Marginalization is therefore not a new search. It is: keep the top of the row with weights instead of drawing one entry, and hand the weighted list to the two consumers that should see it.

    3. The posterior and its temperature¶

    The row holds the normalized Euclidean loss (gen_raw_euclid_vals_impl)

    L_r = sum_k (k/sigma2_k) sum_p |X_kp - CTF_kp R_kp,r|^2 / wsqsum(X),
wsqsum(X) = sum_k (k/sigma2_k) sum_p |X_kp|^2,

    summed over the pftsz polar samples of each ring k in the band. The Gaussian negative log-likelihood runs over the Cartesian half-plane components, pik of them in ring k, so each polar sample stands for pik/pftsz of them; with sigma2 the variance per real or imaginary part,

    NLL_r = sum_k (pi k/pftsz) sum_p |r_kp|^2 / (2 sigma2_k)
      = pi wsqsum(X) L_r / (2 pftsz).

    The posterior over the row is p_r proportional to exp(-T (L_r - L_min)) with

    T = pi wsqsum(X) / (2 pftsz)        (posterior_temperature).

    L lives in [0, ~2] and wsqsum is of order 2 pftsz sum_k k (1 + SNR_k), so T is of order pi sum_k k, about 1.5 K^2 for K shells in the band: roughly a thousand at the first prob stage. A loss difference of 0.01 between two directions is then ten nats; the posterior is sharp whenever the data can tell the directions apart and broad only where they fit within about 0.001 in L. Two consequences:

    1. The current draw at T = 1 is not a posterior and should not be read as one. This is a finding about the existing sampler, not a defect: it is doing exploration within an angular window.
    2. The posterior self-adapts: concentrated for a high-SNR particle, broad for a weak one, which is the behaviour marginalization needs.

    The constant pi/(2 pftsz) assumes the pft is taken at integer ring radius on the cropped grid (one Cartesian component per unit area) and that the particle-group sigma2 is per real or imaginary part. Both can be checked on the diagnostic output: at the mode, T L_min is the NLL, which for a calibrated sigma2 should be close to the number of components in the band, about pi (k2^2 - k1^2) / 2; and on bgal at the fine stages the effective number of references should sit near one.

    4. The design (revised 2026-10-22, Monte Carlo form)¶

    Draw the committed pose from the posterior; let the update scheme do the averaging. SIMPLE is already a Monte Carlo marginalizer by architecture: one pose per particle per iteration, drawn stochastically, averaged over particles within an iteration and, when the fractional update is on, over iterations by the trailing chain. Inserting k planes with posterior weights and inserting one plane drawn from the posterior give the same reconstruction accumulators in expectation, conditional on fixed weights; the restored map is a ratio of accumulators, so its expectation is not exactly the weighted map (review finding 4), and the weighted form removes the within-iteration variance of the draw. With n_eff 1.05-1.3 and 95% of the mass on the mode for most particles (sections 8-9) that variance is small. Two qualifications: under forced full sampling (nsample at or above 90% of the particles, the PfCRT benchmark's mode) set_refine3D_trailrec_policy turns the trailing chain off, so there is no averaging across iterations there; and the weights change with the reference between iterations, so the chain does not integrate one fixed posterior. The draw is also the cheaper estimator for sigma2: the residual at a pose drawn from the posterior is unbiased over the posterior, where the argmax residual is biased low.

    What is wrong today, in these terms, is the draw's distribution, not the number of planes: draw_likelihood draws with exp(-(L - L_min)) on the normalized loss, where the posterior is exp(-T (L - L_min)) with T of order 500-4000 (section 3; the constant checks to 1% on the mean residual scale, section 8, which is not yet a validation of the likelihood ratios, section 11). At T = 1 the draw is near-uniform over the ~140 best frontier heads, so a sizeable fraction of particles is committed far from the row's mode each iteration. That this harms the reconstruction is the hypothesis of this note, not a measurement (section 11): what the diagnostic showed is that the production row's mode is itself unstable between iterations. At the posterior temperature the far-draw rate would fall to the posterior's own far mass, about 5% on PfCRT, held by the particles whose row leaves two views open.

    1. The draw. On the worker, where the row and wsqsum are: the posterior over the row at the temperature of section 3, the candidates holding 99% of the mass (capped at projs_ns, the refined ones, each with its refined shift and in-plane), and one draw from them. The draw is written as the particle's assignment; strategy3D_prob commits it as today, the reconstruction inserts it once as today. The carried pose is the draw, which is the mode for 95% of particles and a grid neighbour for most of the rest, so the prob_neigh stages inherit the data's answer. Nothing changes in the tables, the reconstruction, the fractional update or the trailing chain.
    2. sigma2. Unchanged in code: the residual at the committed pose, now a posterior draw, is the right estimator in expectation.
    3. The balancing. ref_assign is reference-driven: every reference draws its best available particle, which fills every direction. The per-particle draw drops that guarantee. In 3D with a thousand directions an empty direction is arguably the right answer when no particle's data put it there (the reconstruction handles missing views through neighbouring slices, and filling the direction with rejected particles is exactly the wrong vote of section 9), but whether the stage-3 consolidation (overlap 0.23 to 0.50) needs the balancing is a hypothesis the test must settle. The middle paths, if it does: keep the frontier structure and temper its draw, or cap the per-reference population and draw within the cap.
    4. Tempering. The exact posterior temperature may be too cold in the first iterations of stage 3 (n_eff 2.9 at the first, 1.3 by the fifth): a factor on T rising to 1 over the first iterations of a stage is the annealing knob, in one constant.
    5. Unchanged. In-plane and shift as the production row leaves them (sampled for the unrefined entries, continuously refined for the top projs_ns: the draw is an approximate direction sampler on that row, not a joint pose posterior; section 11), the prob_neigh stages at first (their neighbourhood draw can follow the same rule as a second step), multi-state (the draw within the particle's assigned state).

    The weighted form (k planes per particle with their posterior weights, the sigma2 expectation computed explicitly, k insertions on gridding and k records on PCG) stays as the variance-reduction option, worth building only if the per-iteration noise of the draw shows in stages 3-4, where there is no trailing chain. A colder frontier draw (section 4.3's first middle path) is the smaller code change but keeps the reference-driven assignment; the per-particle draw is the one that follows from the data.

    5. The diagnostic (implemented)¶

    prob_post_diag=yes (internal key, default no, objfun=euclid only) makes every dense table worker compute, per particle, once its row is complete:

    column meaning
    iptcl particle index
    istate, iproj the mode (lowest distance)
    nvalid valid distances in the row (0: no mode, zeros follow)
    lmin the mode's normalized loss
    temp T = pi wsqsum / (2 pftsz)
    pmax the mode's posterior mass
    neff exp(entropy): the effective number of references
    k99 references holding 99% of the mass
    spread_deg posterior-weighted mean angular distance of those references from the mode, in the mode's state, across the symmetry operations (sym_dists)

    and, in the v3 of 2026-10-22 (after the review, section 11), the controls on the same particle, each answering one question:

    column meaning
    null_a_nats the identical-pose null test: the production mode's stored loss re-computed at its exact (reference, shift, grid rotation) with the kernel the stored value came from, minus the stored loss, in nats; expected zero up to precision. mode_src (last column) says which case: 1 the mode was shift-refined, the stored shift is the search's particle-frame value (shift_ref2ptcl of the kernel-frame minimizer) and null_a feeds shift_ptcl2ref of it to the shifted-reference kernel (gen_euclid_val_at); 2 the mode is an unrefined entry scored through the seed bank, null_a is the bank kernel at the stored rotation (gen_euclid_val_at_bank); 3 unshifted, the kernel at zero shift
    null_b_nats the contrast: case 1, the stored shift fed to the kernel as it is (the wrong frame; nonzero whenever the shift is); case 2, the shifted-reference kernel at the frame-converted seed, which differs from the bank kernel by the angular Nyquist bin the former drops (the size of that bin on real data); case 3, equal to null_a
    iproj_d, lmin_d, pmax_d, neff_d, k99_d, spread_d_deg the deterministic row on the matcher's references: best grid rotation for every direction at the particle-frame seed the production search scored at, through the seed bank when the particle has one (gen_best_euclid_val_bank; a reference shift is one frame per rotation, so the seed cannot be fed to the shifted-reference kernel for all rotations at once), unshifted otherwise (gen_best_objfun_val); no sampling, no refinement; and its posterior
    dist_d_deg, rank_d the search test: the angle between the production mode and the deterministic mode, and the production mode's rank in the deterministic row (1: the same mode)
    iproj_h ... spread_h_deg the same deterministic row on the particle's own real half (the diagnostic model below, the reference at iref + nrefs), and its posterior
    dist_h_deg, rank_h the angle between the matcher-row deterministic mode and the own-half mode, and the matcher mode's rank in the own-half row: what the merged reference and the own half disagree on
    iproj_x ... spread_x_deg the same deterministic row on the other real half (cross=.true., the bank or unshifted kernel at iref + nrefs), and its posterior
    dist_x_deg, rank_x the reference test: the angle between the own-half and other-half deterministic modes, and the own-half mode's rank in the other-half row (1: the two halves agree on the mode)
    iproj_p the pose the particle went into its half with: its previous direction on the current grid (find_closest_proj; exact within a stage), 0 when there is none (a virgin field, an invalid state)
    self_nats the self-reinforcement test: T (L_other - L_own) at that pose (previous direction, previous in-plane rotation, zero residual shift: the image is pre-shifted by its stored shift). Positive when the own half, which holds the particle, favours the pose it was inserted at. The pose was chosen on the previous merged reference, to which both halves contributed alike, so the selection bias is the same for both halves and the mean over particles isolates the particle's own contribution.
    ctrl_nats the same difference at the same direction a half turn away in-plane, where the particle's own contribution does not correlate with it (a half turn is the Friedel mate on the polar grid: the cross term is Re X^2, zero in expectation): the noise scale of self_nats, expected zero on average
    mode_src the null test's case, 1-3 as above (0: no valid mode)

    Two more header lines say which case the file is in: # slots_identical yes|no (polarft_calc%ref_slots_identical, the matcher's two slots; solve3D's LP-set stages give yes) and # halves yes|no (the real halves loaded beside the matcher's references; no leaves the _h, _x and self-test columns zero). The columns are written as prob_posterior_iterNNN_partNN.txt in the run directory, one file per part and iteration (write_posterior_diag).

    The diagnostic model of the real halves. The matcher's references in solve3D's LP-set stages are one merged map in both slots (section 11, finding 1), so the master prepares a second polar model for the diagnostic: after materialize_reprojection_model_from_volumes has written reprojection_model_{even,odd}.bin it calls read_mask_filter_reproject_refvols(..., map_shift=.false., diag_halves=.true.), which runs read_mask_filter_refvols with the independent vol_even/vol_odd maps of every state (the _nu_filt halves under NU filtering, the raw halves otherwise) and the same masking, centering and filtering as the matcher's references but with the merged-map copies bypassed (the LP-set average, the merged NU reference, the ICM average), and writes reprojection_model_diag_{even,odd}.bin. No shift mapping, no band-limit estimation, no Cartesian volumes on that pass; the files are removed with the model's by remove_ref_section_files. The dense table workers (exec_prob_tab, exec_prob_tab_neigh) call read_reprojection_model(..., diag_halves=params%l_prob_post_diag): when the pair is on disk the polar object is made with 2*nrefs references, the matcher's model loaded at 1 and the diagnostic pair at nrefs+1 (read_ref_pfts(..., iref_from=), the header's reference count checked against the range), and eul_prob_tab keeps nrefs = nspace*nstates for the table, so the production search never sees the extra references. The reference memory of a worker doubles in diagnostic mode (mirr_proj=yes is excluded: its memoization assumes nrefs/2). The deterministic rows cost three more scoring passes per particle, the self test four at-pose evaluations.

    test_cross_half_euclid (suite "polar correlations") pins that the slot switch flips the reference half alone, that gen_euclid_val_at reproduces the best value at its rotation, and that the slot detector tells distinct sections from a copy; test_ref_pfts_offset_io (same suite) that a model written by a two-reference object lands unchanged at iref_from 1 and 3 of a four-reference one, both halves, and that the plain read is the iref_from 1 case; test_bank_and_at_pose_kernels (same suite) that the at-pose loss at a nonzero reference shift is the shift search's objective (gen_objfun_vals) at that pose, that a seed-bank entry scores at its best rotation as the reference shifted by shift_ptcl2ref(u, rot) up to the Nyquist bin (and not as the unconverted seed), that the bank's best and at-pose losses agree, and that cross swaps the half for the bank kernels too. The frame conversions themselves are pinned by test_shift_frames (suite "in-plane search"). posterior_temperature and posterior_row_stats live in simple_eul_prob_tab_utils.f90 and are pinned by test_posterior_row_stats (unit_... suite "3D probability table").

    The cost is one exponential and a sort per row, negligible beside the scoring; the files are one line per sampled particle and iteration.

    How to read it. neff and k99 say how many references the data cannot tell apart; spread_deg says whether they are neighbours (a smooth peak sampled by several grid points: benign, the posterior is a kernel around one view) or far apart (true multimodality: the case marginalization is for). Per stage, on PfCRT against bgal:

    • neff near 1 and spread small: the data decide the pose; marginalization changes nothing, and the small-particle problem is elsewhere (the reference, the band, the shifts).
    • neff large with spread within a few degrees: finite grid sampling of a broad but unimodal likelihood; marginalization smooths, and a finer nspace or a continuous polish (refine=cont) does the same job.
    • neff large with spread of tens of degrees in a sizeable fraction of the particles: multimodal; build section 4.

    Also worth reading off: temp * lmin against the component count, the calibration check of section 3, and the trajectory of neff over the iterations of a stage (does the posterior sharpen as the reference improves, or does the alignment churn?).

    6. What has to be true first¶

    • The temperature constant and the sigma2 convention: the mean residual scale checks to 1% on PfCRT (section 8), so the algebra of section 3 is right; the likelihood ratios between poses are not validated by that (review finding 3), and the known-pose simulation of section 11 is the test.
    • Reference independence of the diagnostic: not established in solve3D, whose staged children score one merged map in both slots (section 11). The posterior's honesty against an independent reference is unmeasured.
    • Whether the stage-3 consolidation needs the balanced draw's exploration is not known. The balancing exists to keep the reference population from collapsing early; carrying the mode removes it from the pose. The test plan watches the stage-3 overlap trajectory and the cFAR for a preferred-orientation collapse, with the stage-3-only fallback of section 4.3.
    • A calibrated noise model: the sigma2 expectation of section 4.4 is the minimum. cryoSPARC leaves marginalization out of local refinement because a wrong sigma2 under micelles and crowding makes it worse; here the weights are near-hard for most particles, so the exposure is small.

    10.3 Decisions to take before coding¶

    1. Convergence under a cold draw. check_conv3D ends a stage on frac_srch > fracsrch .and. mi_proj > overlap, with overlap 0.9 in stages 4-6 and 0.95 in 7-8 (solve3D_controller). mi_proj is the fraction of particles keeping their direction between iterations; under the frontier draw it sits at 0.2-0.5 through the climb and the stages run to their budgets or to lpexit. Under the posterior draw it will sit near the posterior's own stability, 0.9+ from early in a stage, and the overlap rule would end stages at once. solve3D_policy.md already records that the early stop cannot tell a frozen assignment from convergence. Raising overlap does not help: mi_proj > 0.99 is still met by a frozen assignment at mi_proj = 1 (review finding 5). Under prob_assign=posterior the pose-overlap exit must be bypassed explicitly (a check_conv3D branch that converges on lpexit, the res05 gain guard, or MAXITS only), with a unit test of frozen assignments and a still-improving map. A map-based rule (res05 stalled) for every mode is the larger change and the better one.
    2. Multi-state. Keep the state label deterministic (argmin over all states, as assign_state_labels does) and draw the direction within the state; the state-level sampling of refine3D_states is a separate contract.
    3. Tempering. Start at the exact posterior temperature (section 4.4's factor = 1); add the schedule only if stage 3's first iterations collapse.

    13. Cross-half matching: an experimental search policy, parked (2026-10-23)¶

    The first draft of this section (2026-10-23 morning) called scoring each particle against the half it did not enter "gold-standard" and claimed "zero self-reinforcement at every shell". Both claims were wrong, as the review of the same day (pose_marginalization_review, finding 1) says. Gold-standard refinement is the opposite arrangement: each half-set is refined against its own map, so the particle's own insertion is fully in its reference, and what the scheme buys is the independence of the two refinement histories, hence an honest FSC. Cross-half scoring removes the particle's direct term from its reference but couples the two histories: even poses depend on the odd map, which depends on odd poses, which depend on the even map the particle is in. The indirect self-influence remains, one loop later, and the FSC between the halves is no longer that of two independent refinements. The three arrangements are distinct: the merged reference of solve3D's LP-set stages (half the direct term, coupled histories), gold standard (the full direct term, independent histories), cross-half (no direct term, coupled histories).

    Hans's constraint, 2026-10-23: full gold-standard matching cannot be used in solve3D's ab initio stages, where it does not converge on a good model from the start it has; re-introducing it when the NU machinery is on is conceivable but would need extensive validation of its own. Cross-half scoring, if ever tried, is an experimental search policy with its own validation, not a remedy the design relies on. Nothing in this note's plan depends on it; section 16 proceeds without it.

    What remains useful from the draft: the mechanics are a slot assignment (the kernels take the reference slot from the particle's half, so loading the halves swapped makes every score cross-half with no kernel change; the GPU gen_many2many_* kernels read REF_EVEN alone and would need the same rule), and the diagnostic's _x columns already measure what such a reference sees. The direct self term is 0.8-6.8 nats on the own half at stages 3-5 (sections 12, 14), half of that on the merged reference; what the coupled loop adds back is not measured.

    Terminology (Hans's question, 2026-10-23). At beta below one the draw is from the tempered posterior, p(theta|X)^beta normalized, the power posterior of annealing and parallel tempering; it is the posterior only at beta 1. Two readings of beta < 1 are both exact: annealing, the same function used as search dynamics rather than as weights; or the posterior under a noise model whose variance is inflated by 1/beta, since beta T is what a sigma2 larger by that factor gives. The second reading is why the known-pose calibration (gate item 3) still matters: if the physical T is overestimated by some factor, that part of beta is a correction and the ramp's end should be that factor, not 1.
