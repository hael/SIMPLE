    Dose-Series Accumulation¶

    Problem¶

    Every frame of a movie adds dose, and dose fades the signal: radiation damage first, and in practice also residual motion that the motion model does not remove. Grant & Grigorieff measured how fast on one specimen and summarised it as a critical exposure per spatial frequency; every dose weighting in common use applies that one curve to every dataset. dose_filter3D measures the fading of the data at hand instead, from half-map pairs reconstructed from consecutive windows of fractions, and accumulates the windows under it. reextract can then weight the movie fractions of every particle by the same measured curve.

    Data¶

    fractionate_model reconstructs state 1 from blocks of stepf fractions with the refined poses held fixed and writes dose_series.txt: per window its fractions, its physical dose bounds and its unfiltered, unmasked even and odd half maps (fractionate_model, dose-series export). The manifest also carries the box, sampling, voltage and dose per fraction, so the program needs only the manifest, smpd and mskdiam:

    simple_exec prg=dose_filter3D filetab=dose_series.txt smpd=<A> mskdiam=<A> \
    [hp=20] [lp=<A>] [bfac_model=line|free] [zerodose=no|yes] [outvol=dose_series.mrc] nthr=16

    An overlapping last window replaces its predecessor; at least two disjoint windows are required. Doses are in the scaled coordinate of calc_dose_weights: physical dose divided by 0.8 at 200 kV and by 0.64 at 100 kV.

    Model¶

    Window w sees the structure attenuated, relative to window 1, by

    a_w(k) = c_w exp(-B_w k^2 / 4),      c_1 = 1, B_1 = 0,

    a frequency-flat contrast c_w and a B-factor B_w. Across windows both follow straight lines in dose,

    B(D) = B0 + beta D,      ln c(D) = ln c0 - gamma D,

    fitted over windows 2 to W, with an excess scatter tau about each. The weights use each window's own values, so a fading that is not linear in dose is followed rather than forced onto the lines.

    Algorithm¶

    1. Statistics. Both halves of every window are masked by the soft sphere of mskdiam, Fourier transformed and rescaled to unit mean power. Per Fourier shell, one pass forms the 2W x 2W matrix of products of all halves, the noise variance of each half from the half difference, and the variance of the cross-half products.

    2. Band. From hp (20 Å by default) to lp, or when lp is not given to the last shell where any window's own even/odd FSC exceeds 0.1. At least six shells.

    3. Attenuation, model-free. Per shell, a rank-one weighted least-squares fit of the symmetrised cross-half products gives a_w(s) relative to window 1, with its full covariance between windows (a sandwich estimate: each window's noise projected on the structure is shared by every pair holding it). The independent count of a shell under the mask is the plane formula f = 3 (sum M^2)^2 / (N^2 sum_axes sum_plane (sum_perp M^2)^2).

    4. One B-factor per window. A Guinier fit of c_w exp(-B_w k^2/4) to a_w(s) over the band, started by a log-domain regression and refined in the linear domain by Levenberg-Marquardt with step acceptance. Errors come from the curvature, inflated by the correlation between shells under the mask:

    text F = (1/3) sum_axes n_a sum M^4 / sum_perp (sum_a M^2)^2,

    1 without a mask. A window whose fit fails or whose contrast does not clear five of its errors is rejected: it leaves the lines and the model's weights.

    1. The lines. Weighted lines of B_w and ln c_w in dose, in two passes: weights 1/se^2, then 1/(se^2 + tau^2) with tau^2 the DerSimonian-Laird estimate under the trace of a weighted line. With bfac_model=line each B_w is shrunk towards the line, B*_w = (B_w/se^2 + B(D_w)/tau^2) / (1/se^2 + 1/tau^2); with free, B*_w = B_w.

    2. Weights and accumulation. Per shell, the matched filter omega_w ∝ c_w exp(-B*_w k^2/4) / sigma_w^2, normalised to sum one, applied to the halves as given (unmasked). The even accumulation uses only even halves and the odd only odd, so they remain a gold-standard pair. Past the band the edge shells' weights continue. The same is done under Grant & Grigorieff's attenuation (the control) and with equal weights (the flat mean).

    3. Readouts.

    4. FSC 0.5 and 0.143 of the model, the control, the flat mean and window 1 alone, unmasked and under the soft sphere of mskdiam with zero background, as reconstruct3D masks before its FSC.
    5. The cross-half prediction error of each scheme: each window's odd half predicted from the even halves of all windows under the scheme's attenuation and vice versa, whitened by the window's noise (1 is a noise-free prediction, 2 the window alone). The model-minus-control difference is descriptive: the attenuations are fitted on both halves.
    6. The critical exposure per shell with no model, ln a_w(s) regressed through the origin on D_w - D_1, N_e = -1/(2 slope); beside it the lines' 1/(2 gamma + beta k^2/2), the per-window model through the same estimator, and Grant & Grigorieff's.
    7. The critical exposure between neighbouring windows at 20, 10, 8, 6 and 4 Å; flat when the fading is exponential in dose.
    8. The FSC of every pair of windows, the even half of one against the odd half of the other.
    9. With zerodose=yes, the accumulated map divided per shell by its residual attenuation and continued along the B-factor line to zero dose.

    Model values are reported against the sharpest fitted window (least B*): early beam-induced motion can leave window 1 blurrier than the next, whose B would otherwise read negative. The shift changes no ratio between windows, no weight and no map.

    Re-extraction under the measured curve¶

    simple_exec prg=reextract projfile=<project> extractfrommov=yes dose_weighting=measured \
    dose_curve=<outvol>_curve.txt nparts=<n> nthr=<n>

    Fraction f of a movie with physical dose d per fraction sits at the scaled centre dose x_f = (f - 1/2) d / s_kV. Between fitted windows ln c and B* are interpolated linearly in dose; before the first window the first segment continues; past the last the lines' slopes continue, clamped at zero so an unmeasured fraction never gains on the last measured one. Each frame's Fourier transform is weighted by

    omega_f(k) = c_f exp(-B_f k^2/4) / sqrt( sum_g c_g^2 exp(-B_g k^2/2) ),

    unit squared sum at every frequency, so the summed noise spectrum stays flat, as in the Grant & Grigorieff mode. The curve was measured on plain sums of fractions, so it also replaces the motion-correction frame weights. The run stops if a micrograph's voltage or dose per fraction differs from the curve's, or if it has no motion model (the integrated micrograph carries Grant & Grigorieff's weights).

    Outputs¶

    <outvol> is outvol without its extension.

    File Contents
    <outvol>_even.mrc, _odd.mrc, .mrc The model's accumulated halves and their mean, the delivered map: unfiltered, unsharpened, unmasked
    _fsc.bin, _fsc_masked.bin The FSC of the model's half pair, unmasked and masked
    _control_even.mrc, _control_odd.mrc, _control.mrc, _control_fsc.bin, _control_fsc_masked.bin The same under Grant & Grigorieff's weights
    _fsc.txt Per shell: the model's and the control's FSC, unmasked and masked
    _window_fsc.txt Per shell: the FSC of every pair of windows; fsc_w_w is window w's own
    _zerodose.mrc With zerodose=yes: the zero-dose extrapolation
    _model.txt Per window: dose, c_w, B_w with its error, the line's B(D_w), B*_w, residual z_w, Guinier rms, used; the B-factor and contrast lines
    _curve.txt The dose curve reextract reads
    _attenuation.txt Per shell: every window's model-free attenuation and its error
    _weights.txt, _weights_res.txt The model's weights per shell, and at 20, 10, 8, 6 and 4 Å beside Grant & Grigorieff's
    _cv.txt Per shell: the three cross-half prediction errors and the model-minus-control difference
    _decay.txt, _decay_steps.txt The critical exposures, per shell and between neighbouring windows

    Rationale¶

    The windows share particles and poses, so they differ only in dose: whatever separates them is the fading, measured on the specimen and the processing at hand. Ratios of cross-half products cancel the structure's power and keep the noise of each half independent of the other's. A B-factor per window is the fewest parameters that follow a fading of any shape in dose; the lines summarise it, extrapolate it past the last window and, under bfac_model=line, steady windows measured with large errors.

    Measured on the data, the fading need not follow Grant & Grigorieff. On β-galactosidase and apoferritin it accelerates with dose, mostly in B, as an uncorrected displacement growing linearly with dose would; the per-window weights follow it, so a curve measured before per-particle polishing does not hold after it. With equal-width windows, averaging over a window's fractions cancels against window 1 when the fading is exponential in dose, so window centres stand for fractions; under curvature the bias grows with the square of the window width.
