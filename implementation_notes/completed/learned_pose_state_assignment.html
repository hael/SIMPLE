    Learned pose assignment: a student distilled from the probabilistic search¶

    Proposal of 2026-11-05, revised on 2026-11-06 with Hans and after a methodology review. SIMPLE's probabilistic search supplies per-dataset soft labels over the 3D direction, and a small model trained on them proposes directions for particles the search has not seen, so that a local search and the local refinement can finish the alignment. The first delivery is single-state: a pose student, validated for poses before anything else. Whether it saves time is a measurement. Nothing is built. Sections 1 and 2 are the idea and what exists, sections 3 to 9 the design, section 10 the validation, section 11 the order of work, section 12 the open decisions for Hans, sections 13 to 15 the risks, the rules, the outlook and the sources.

    Changed after the run, 2026-11-09 (in the maintainer's tree): the student's classes are the 500 mirror pairs of the grid, not its 1,000 directions, and HHCNN is invariant to the mirror; the mirror is resolved by the search through the Euler relation (euler_mirror), as the opt-in mirrored reference extraction (mirr_proj=yes) pairs the spiral. Sections 4 and 6 and decision 16 describe it; the Progress entry "Mirror pairs (2026-11-09)" holds the change and its measurements. The phases' entries keep the design of their day.

    1. What is proposed¶

    A pose student: a small network, trained per dataset on the probabilistic posterior of the dense 3D table over a fixed grid of directions. The network is HHCNN, the Harmonic Hermitian convolutional neural network: Hermitian because every layer commutes with conjugation on the half ring with the conjugate wrap, so the network is a model of the Fourier transform of a real image; harmonic because the readout's class templates live in the angular harmonic domain of the polar transform and the rotation search is the cross-correlation over the cyclic shifts through the angular Fourier transform; convolutional on the polar Fourier representation (the name is Hans's ruling R5 of 2026-11-08, applied in phase 5). It reads a particle's CTF-multiplied polar Fourier transform in a low-resolution band and proposes a distribution over the grid. Hermitian circular convolutions on the half ring and pooling provide in-plane rotation invariance on the calculator's own rotation grid; shifts are learned as tolerance from the calculator's own phase ramps and found exactly by the shift map in the candidate search that follows. The student's direction and the width of its posterior are handed to refine3D's neighbourhood search, which finds the in-plane rotation, the shift and its own posterior with the machinery it has; a particle whose posterior is too broad for a neighbourhood takes the dense search. No new command-line key: the model file travels through infile and fname as the class-average quality model's does, and every threshold and constant that decided lives in the model file.

    What sets this apart from the published attempts, which learn in real space, is the polar implementation: the representation the search already uses, with the in-plane rotation a cyclic shift of the input, the translation handled exactly by the Fourier-Bessel shift map, the CTF applied as the search applies it, the sigma2 whitening of the objective, and the table's own posterior as the teacher. Everything the student sees and learns from is what the probabilistic search computes.

    Two questions decide the design's worth, and section 10 asks them first. On real data: split the dataset into two view-balanced halves, reconstruct with the standard machinery from one half, train the student on it, use the student on the other half, and compare the result with the standard machinery's alignment of that half. And how few training images suffice: if the cycle of making training rows, training, predicting and refining is fast enough on a small set sampled from classes as the balanced sampling samples, the student can run inside the iterations and stages of refine3D and solve3D, extending each iteration's alignment of its sampled fraction to the particles it did not search.

    Hans's rulings of 2026-11-06 are recorded in section 12; the open questions are the training-set size and, later, the production arrangement of the halves.

    2. Where the idea stands¶

    2.1 Prior work¶

    • Unsupervised amortized pose inference (cryoPoseNet, cryoAI, cryoFIRE): an encoder learns the poses jointly with the map, with no labels. Encoders converge slowly and get stuck; the semi-amortized method of Shekarforoush et al. (NeurIPS 2024) and DRGN-AI both retreat to per-particle optimization after an amortized warm start. That is evidence against learning poses from scratch, not against the supervised setting.
    • Supervised pose assignment, which is this setting: the CESPED benchmark (Sanchez-Garcia et al., arXiv 2311.06194; the package is checked out at ~/src/cesped, section 2.4) and cryoPARES (Sanchez-Garcia et al., bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.03.04.641536). cryoPARES trains on a refined apo dataset and infers on ligand-bound datasets of the same protein; a U-Net feeds an Image2Sphere head that outputs a distribution over an SO(3) grid of 294,912 rotations; the CTF enters as a phase-flipped input channel; in-plane rotation and shift are learned by augmentation. Its numbers: network inference above 30,000 particles per minute per GPU; the raw top-1 pose within 5° of the RELION pose for about 30% of the particles, above 60% after a local refinement; resolutions 0.1 to 0.6 Å worse than RELION on six datasets; a direction-normalised confidence rejects junk as well as 2D classification does; two RELION runs disagree by more than 5° on over 20% of the particles. Trained per protein, up to 100 epochs on GPUs, checkpoints of 40 GB.
    • CESPED's own numbers: eight EMPIAR entries of at least 200,000 particles at 1.5 Å per pixel with poses re-estimated by RELION auto-refine, each split at random into two halves, a model trained on one half and judged on the other; its Image2Sphere baseline (an ImageNet ResNet152 on 256-pixel real-space images, an orthographic projection onto a hemisphere, S2 and SO(3) convolutions at lmax 8, a softmax over 294,912 rotations, phase-flipped input, no shift prediction) reaches mean angular errors of 13° to 45° per entry (28.7° over the benchmark) and maps from its poses 1.3 Å worse than the truth at FSC 0.143 and 3.4 Å at 0.5; a local refinement within 6° of its poses brings one entry's map correlation from 0.969 to 0.997 at a threefold speed-up over auto-refine. The same entry simulated gives 6.0° against 13.3° real, and consensus labels 8.3°: the labels and the noise set the error as much as the model.
    • Lessons taken: the raw pose is coarse and a local refinement supplies the precision; the confidence is worth as much as the pose; the student is judged on soft targets and downstream recovery; the model is dataset-specific, so it is trained per dataset and retrained when the references change.
    • Related architectures, none of which combines this design's ingredients:
    • Polar images with cyclic convolution: CyCNN (Kim et al., 2020) maps images to polar coordinates and wraps its convolutions along the angle, so that a rotation is a translation, and beats plain CNNs on rotated benchmarks without augmentation; Polar Transformer Networks (Esteves et al., ICLR 2018) do the same in log-polar coordinates and first predict the polar origin with a small network, their answer to the translation. In cryo-EM, the Polar Transformers of Andén and Sagemüller (arXiv 2506.11283, a NeurIPS oral) use polar CNN feature extractors with attention over all relative angular shifts by FFT correlation, equivariant to the independent in-plane rotations of every image of a set, for joint denoising; up to a twofold MSE reduction at SNR 0.02 and about 20% better ab initio resolution on simulated data.
    • Equivariant pose encoders in cryo-EM: de Ruijter and Cesa (ICLR 2024 GEM workshop) build a D4-equivariant encoder on the fact that an in-plane rotation or mirror of the image transforms the pose predictably; it converges faster and more often than cryoAI's encoder, in real space and on simulated data.
    • Fourier-Bessel, rotation-invariant representations without a network: Zhao and Singer (2014) expand images in the Fourier-Bessel basis, where a rotation is a phase shift of the coefficients, and build a complete invariant from the bispectrum for viewing-direction classification; PolarGMM (2022) clusters noisy projections with a polar-coordinate Gaussian mixture fitted by EM. These are the ancestors of the representation here and of flex's polar E-step.
    • Complex-valued networks: Deep Complex Networks (Trabelsi et al., ICLR 2018) supply complex convolutions, complex batch normalisation and the modReLU activation of section 6, used in MRI reconstruction and radar; Harmonic Networks (Worrall et al., 2017) restrict filters to circular harmonics so that complex feature maps rotate by a phase under input rotation, the nearest thing to features that transform like Fourier coefficients.
    • Not found: a network that imposes the Hermitian symmetry of a real image's transform by conjugation-equivariant layers on the half plane (Fourier-domain operators keep the non-redundant half of a real FFT, without the equivariance), or one that keeps the CTF in the operator and learns from the search's own posterior. With the polar implementation itself, those are where this design stands apart (section 1).

    2.4 What the CESPED checkout teaches¶

    ~/src/cesped (oxpig/cesped) holds the benchmark's loader, the Image2Sphere baseline, its training configuration and its evaluation. It settles several things.

    • Held-out halves, and an evaluation through reconstruction. An entry's half 0 trains (with a 70/30 split inside it for validation and hyperparameters) and half 1 is judged, then the reverse. The evaluation reconstructs from the predicted poses and reports the FSC against the reference map beside the reference halves' own FSC, so that the method's loss is separated from the map's quality. That is the split-half protocol of section 10, with their halves random where ours are view-balanced, and tier 4 adopts the same two numbers.
    • Means hide the tail. CESPED reports mean angular errors, which a bimodal distribution (most particles within a few degrees, a fraction wildly wrong) turns into tens of degrees; cryoPARES later reported the fraction within 5°. The reads here are fractions of recoverable seeds and the size of the set that holds the posterior, never a mean angle.
    • Seed, then refine. The only result of theirs that reaches the truth's quality is the local refinement within 6° of the predicted pose, at a threefold speed-up; the student alone is not the product. That is this design's consumer: a seed, a search at the predicted directions, the local refinement.
    • Labels and confidence. Their label is the nearest grid rotation to one RELION pose, weighted in the loss by RELION's rlnMaxValueProbDistribution; a cleaner label on the same particles cut the error by a third. The teacher's soft row at the student's band (section 4) is the same idea carried through: the whole row, not one pose and one weight.
    • Translations were the stated gap. Image2Sphere predicts no shift, the evaluation used the truth shifts, and joint rotation and translation estimation heads the authors' list of improvements. The shift map in the candidate search at the predicted directions (sections 5 and 8) is this design's answer.
    • CTF. Their loader offers ctf_multiply beside phase_flip, and the paper used the flip; no one measured the difference, and neither keeps the CTF where flex keeps it, in the operator. The student takes flex's formalism (sections 2.5 and 3) and reads its effect by defocus bin.
    • Capacity. Their encoder is an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet152 on 65,000 real-space pixels, trained for up to 400 epochs on four A100s; this design's network has about a million weights on 10,000 band-limited polar values with the rotation built in and the shift found by the map. The lower capacity is plausible on the lower-dimensional input and is a risk the wider networks of section 6 bound.
    • The head. Their distribution over SO(3) is band-limited at lmax 8, so its peaks cannot be much sharper than 20° whatever the 3.75° readout grid; and it classifies the in-plane angle as a class dimension, which the authors name as not separable from the in-plane translation. The flat softmax over directions with the in-plane angle left to the search is the cheaper factoring.
    • The data. Eight public entries with RELION poses, two membrane proteins in a micelle or a nanodisc among them (the GPCR complexes 10786 and 11120 are the hardest, at 33° and 45°), two symmetric ones (C2, D2), and published baselines, all homogeneous refinements. Tier 4 takes two CESPED entries as its real data beside the Dell's own, so that the numbers compare with Image2Sphere's and cryoPARES's on the same particles. No entry is on this box yet; they come from Zenodo, with a small TEST entry for smoke tests.

    2.5 What the flex machinery teaches¶

    src/main/flex (the simple-main-flex skill; doc/algorithms/heterogeneity_analysis/flex_pca.md) models Fourier data with a learned low-rank basis, in flex_pca over projections and in the PPCA kernels of cls_expansion and trajectory_denoise over class members and frames. It has solved, in this code base, the questions a network over Fourier transforms raises.

    • Hermitian by the half plane, never by redundancy. Flex stores and computes on the native k <= 0 half plane; the k = 0 line is its own Friedel mate and is counted once (cov_herm_inner). Every inner product is the real half-plane product <u,v> = sum_j wq_j Re(conj(u_j) v_j) with the quadrature weight wq 2 for a coefficient whose mate lies off the stored half and 1 for a self-mate, which equals the full-plane real product by Parseval, and its polar sampler packs each sample as sqrt(wq) times (Re, Im) into a real vector (polar_sample_particle_fused), so that a Euclidean dot product on the packed reals is the Hermitian product. Taps that fall on k > 0 read the conjugate of the mirrored sample: the Friedel mate per tap. The student takes this whole: the half ring as the data, the shell weight packed as its square root, the conjugate wrap at the ring's ends, and layers that commute with conjugation, so that the network is a model of the Fourier transform of a real image and the full ring is never materialised (sections 3 and 6).
    • The CTF formalism. Flex's observation is the whitened, CTF-modulated central section, y_i = a_i C_i S_i P_i V + n_i, and the CTF never leaves the operator: the data enter through the cross term b_q = <T u_q, y_i> with T = C S P, which for a real CTF is the CTF-free basis correlated with the CTF-multiplied data, and the Gram G_qr = <T u_q, T u_r> carries the CTF squared, so that a latent A^-1 b is never biased by the envelope and nothing is deconvolved from an image. In the polar E-step the Gram factorises as sum_k w_i(k) C_qr(k) with w_i(k) the ring mean of the CTF squared and the azimuthal variation reported as tazim, the approximation's own diagnostic; the contrast a_i is fitted in closed form against the projected mean and clamped to [0.1, 5]. The student takes the formalism whole for its first layer (section 3, item 2): the cross term of each filter with the CTF-multiplied particle under the shell weights, normalised by that filter's energy under the particle's CTF squared, which is the Gram's diagonal. No CTF channel, no flip, no Wiener filter.
    • Two fits on disjoint halves, and the agreement as the reproducibility. Flex fits two bases on disjoint particle halves (the mod-4 rule of flex_pca_half_of), frame-aligns and merges them, trusts each component by its match cosine between the independent fits rather than by the requested rank, and feeds the cross-fit FSC back as the SSNR ridge of the joint solve. The student's two production halves and the diagnostic agreement of section 7 are the same discipline; whether an agreement statistic may regularise, as the ridge does, rather than only report, is not proposed here.
    • Coefficient-split cross-validation. The PPCA kernels choose rank and bandwidth without holding out a single particle: the fit band is split into two interleaved sets of 4 by 4 Fourier blocks in a checkerboard (coeff_sets), the model fitted on one set scores the other, and the pre-images use each set's weights from the other set's model, so there is no selection bias and no Stein term. It is noted as the way flex chooses what cannot be chosen on particles.
    • A noise spectrum from the data. flex_traj_noise_spectrum takes the per-shell noise power from the median half-squared difference of consecutive frames; flex_pca takes sigma2 from the high-frequency shells. The student keeps the calculator's sigma2 and its bootstrap, the same quantity the objective uses.
    • Data-free initialisation. Flex starts its subspace from geometry, the lowest lattice points realised as cosine and sine pairs, so that two runs are comparable and the EM never seeds inside a noise subspace. The student's weights start from a fixed seed recorded in the model, and the teacher's rows, not a data moment, are the first thing read.
    • A plane cache with a validated contract. Prepped Fourier planes are cached on disk under a run token with a provenance header (the project's path hash and mtime, the rows, box, crop, sampling, the selection), written last and renamed into place atomically, a quarter of the free space at most, fetched by copy, deleted once nothing after the embedding reads planes (simple_flex_pca_plane_cache, simple_flex_pca_planes). The student's input tensors are read once per epoch, width and band; they are cached under the same contract (section 9).
    • The OpenMP discipline. Per-thread scratch sized by omp_get_max_threads() at entry and indexed by the thread number; only the E-step body inside the region, bank builds and inserts outside; no FFTW plan in a region; the BLAS probed for the calling thread (probe_blas); reductions in chunks. The network's trainer follows the same rules (section 6).
    • Gates that are declared and that stand down. The two merge gates are a disattenuated FSC-type ratio at 0.98 under complete linkage and a chi-square view test at p = 0.001 with a robust effect-size cut, and the view gate stands down when more than a third of the states are flagged. The student's gates are declared the same way and decline the same way.
    • One project gateway. Every project read and write of flex goes through simple_flex_pca_project_gateway; workers never write the project; a cached embedding is reused only when particle identity and model provenance match. The student's pconf and route writes and its model's refusal of a changed project follow it.

    3. The student's input¶

    simple_polar_feats (src/main/learn/): one routine builds the input of a particle from the polar calculator, called by training and prediction alike so that the two never drift apart.

    1. The band. Rings kfromto(1) to box*smpd/lp with lp the student's low-pass. The band is one of the two hyperparameters (section 6), 15 Å first, then 10 and 8 Å; the number of rings depends on box*smpd/lp alone, about 25 at 15 Å for the benchmark's box 128 at 3 Å per pixel.
    2. The CTF, in flex's formalism. The tensor is built in the angular-sample domain from pfts_ptcls(:,k,i)*ctfmats(:,k,i), the CTF-multiplied particle, because that is what the cross term of a CTF-free filter with the observation is: the search's Q_k = REF CTF conj(X) and flex's <T u, y> alike correlate the CTF-free model with the CTF-multiplied data. The CTF stays in the operator: the first layer's filters are CTF-free, each filter's response is normalised by its energy under the particle's CTF squared and the shell weights, the Gram's diagonal, so that the envelope CTF^2 of the product never reaches the deeper layers as structure, and a shell near a zero carries no signal and enters at no weight. The normalisation uses the ring mean of the CTF squared, as flex's polar E-step factorises its Gram, with the azimuthal variation reported as flex reports tazim (decision 14 keeps the full azimuthal CTF squared as the alternative). The calculator's ft_ptcl_ctf is the angular FFT of this Friedel-extended ring: its index is the harmonic order, not the angle, so it is the shift map's input (section 5) and never an angle-axis CNN's. Nothing is restored: no phase flip, Wiener filter or deconvolution in any design, as nothing is in flex; the output is a class, not an image. The per-particle contrast flex fits against its model is here the band's scale by the particle's weighted power (item 3).
    3. The shell weights. The shells are weighted as the search weights them under objfun=euclid, the objective of the biomolecular workflows this is for: k/sigma2(k), where sigma2 is the global spectrum of the particle's half, never a per-particle one: the calculator's weights are two spectra, one per half, and a part's per-particle residual records exist only to be summed into them. An unseen particle therefore needs nothing beyond its half, assigned at import, and its own weighted power (wsqsum_ptcl_band), which scales the band. The weights belong to the model, not to the project: the student's record holds the weights it was trained with, and prediction applies them, never the project's current sigma2, so a later refinement that moves the spectrum does not move the student's input distribution and a retrain picks the new spectrum up. A project without a spectrum is given one by the sigma2 bootstrap (calc_pspec) before training or prediction, never scored under another objective. Tensor amplitudes use sqrt(w_k) so that a squared norm applies the shell weight once. The logarithm of the weighted power is one scalar side input whose usefulness for the confidence is measured; it does not authorise a particle-rejection decision.
    4. The half ring, with the conjugate wrap. The calculator's pftsz angles over the half turn are the data; the other half is their Friedel mate and is never materialised. The angular axis is the calculator's own rotation grid, so an in-plane rotation by one grid step is one cyclic shift, and what passes the ring's end returns conjugated: the convolution's padding at both ends is the conjugate of the opposite end, as flex's polar sampler takes the Friedel mate per tap. Rotation equivariance is then exact on the search's rotation grid, with no resampling and no interpolation (a resampled grid only if the cost demands it).
    5. The tensor. Two channels by the rings by the half ring's pftsz angles: the real and imaginary parts of the CTF-multiplied particle, each scaled by sqrt(w_k) so that a Euclidean product of the packed reals is the objective's Hermitian product on the full ring, as flex packs its polar samples by sqrt(wq). The particle's CTF squared, ring-averaged, travels beside the tensor for the first layer's normalisation, not as a channel. About 8,500 values at 15 Å for the benchmark's box. The phases are kept throughout; nothing is reduced to a power. Augmentation lives in the same module: the calculator's own phase ramp for shifts (memoize_shifted_ptcl), cyclic shifts with the conjugate wrap for rotations on the grid.

    4. The direction grid and the teacher¶

    • The grid. oris%spiral at NSPACE_STUDENT (1000) directions, about 6° apart, over the whole sphere in c1 and the asymmetric unit as build%eulspace is built for a symmetric point group. The in-plane angle is not a class: the student is invariant to it by construction and the search at the predicted directions recovers it (section 8). No rotation or shift is regressed, the candidate search supplies the in-plane angle and the shift, and Euler angles stay the project's interchange.
    • The classes are the mirror pairs (Hans, 2026-11-09; decision 16). The spiral of build_refspiral holds the unmirrored directions of the asymmetric unit in its first half and their mirrors in its second, paired with a frame angle (oris%get_mirror_pairs, the pairing of mirr_proj=yes). A direction and its mirror are related by the Euler angles (euler_mirror), and the projection of one is the reflected image of the other. The student classifies the 500 pairs; its output, a distribution over pairs, is placed on each pair's unmirrored direction of the grid, and the search tells the two members apart with a cross-correlation (the neighbourhood search scores the cone about the direction and about its mirror). The teacher still scores all 1,000 directions; its rows are folded onto the pairs before the loss. The fold of ruling R1, below, is now the class space itself rather than a step after the prediction.
    • The folded sphere (Hans, ruling R1, 2026-11-07, after phase 2's "What the cones are"). The student is Friedel-symmetric by construction: a view and its mirror view (the antipodal direction, whose projection is the reflected image; the spiral's mirror partner) are one class to it, and the network is not changed to separate them. The posterior is folded over the mirror pairs before the cone is read: the mass of a direction and of its partner are summed, the mode and the 99% set are taken on the folded sphere, and the cone's radius is the set's spread about that mode (a member's distance the smaller of its own and its partner's). The two possibilities are resolved by the search (section 8). Ruling R2 (the same day) asks whether the student is, in effect, a network of spectral powers without phases, which would make it shift-invariant too: predict_poses reports a shift check on its first particles (the posterior and the pooled features of each particle shifted through the calculator's phase ramps against its own), and the training's shift augmentation stands or falls by it (section 5; the finding in Progress).
    • The references. The teacher scores a particle against the reconstruction of the particles it is trained with: in the split-half protocol the training half's map, in the stream and the batch consumers the particle's own half's map as the matcher's parity references are prepared (prepare_refs_sigmas_and_pftc; refine3D reconstructs them from the project's halves at its first iteration). The model records the references' provenance (the project, the rows, the band, a hash of each volume).
    • The teacher is the posterior of the probabilistic machinery (Hans, decision 4): the table's tempered posterior rows at the student's grid and band, as fill_tab_range computes them against the training half's map, each reference scored at its drawn in-plane rotation and the best candidates polished in shift through the shift map, with no assignment draw taken. In the loop of refine3D and solve3D these rows exist for the sampled particles anyway. If the in-plane draw proves to leave too much noise in the labels, the same rows with the deterministic best over the in-plane grid (deterministic_best, the diagnostic posterior's path) are the alternative, at the cost of a fill.
    • The soft label is the row's posterior t_c = exp[-tau_i (L_c-L_min)] / sum_j exp[-tau_i (L_j-L_min)] with tau_i the recorded prob_beta times the particle's temperature (posterior_temperature), the factor the table draws at; objective, temperature and shell weights are recorded in the model.
    • What the earlier search contributes. The map of the training particles, the halves, which particles have a converged pose (the candidate training rows), and the initial shift support. The teacher and the prediction use the same image preparation and initial shift support; converged shifts or centring widths that a new particle will not have cannot restrict the training searches.
    • The targets retain uncertainty. The loss uses the full soft rows in bounded batches. There is no uniform label smoothing, no fixed top-k renormalisation and no inverse-frequency class weighting in the probability head: with 1,000 classes, 5% of uniform smoothing would cap the mass in 64 classes at about 95% even for a perfect fit, so the 99% coverage of section 8 could never be met; and reweighting the classes changes the meaning of the predicted probabilities. A later sparse encoding must preserve the omitted mass and its distribution, or bound its approximation error; a broad row stays broad. A class-balanced draw of the training rows may improve coverage, but a nonuniform draw is corrected by its known inclusion weights for the intended particle population. Rare-view performance is reported separately rather than changing the probabilities' meaning.
    • Discrete grid, continuous pose (Hans, decision 2). The teacher's rotations and shifts are continuous: the polish is continuous in shift and the local refinement in both. The student's output is discrete because the posterior over directions is multimodal at low SNR and the references are discrete: a softmax over a 6° grid holds competing directions with their masses, which a regressed rotation cannot. The grid is a sampling of a continuous posterior on the sphere: a continuous direction is the posterior-weighted mean of the directions in the mode's cone, with the cone's spread as its uncertainty, and the continuous in-plane rotation and shift come from the neighbourhood search, which has the references and is the better continuous estimator. Learning a continuous density on SO(3) directly, as Image2Sphere does with spherical harmonics at lmax 8, gives a smoother but blurrier posterior than the grid at 1,000 directions.
    • Training and evaluation targets have different roles. Training may use the band-specific soft scores. Every design is evaluated against one frozen full-search reference pose (the standard machinery's, at its band) and the actual downstream refinement, with the truth where it exists. A coarser band does not win by agreeing with its own easier teacher.
    • The teacher's resolvability is read before anything is trained. The deterministic rows give, per row and band, pmax, neff and k99; their distributions over the training particles say whether the band carries a concentrated assignment under that score model, before any training. These are conditional on the references, the objective and the temperature, not an information-theoretic bound. A band at which the median k99 spans a ring of directions is reported as such, and the student's claim for that dataset is assessed against the common reference and the truth (section 10).
    • The consumer's grid is decoupled from the student's by the local search: the student's directions seed an in-plane and shift search and then refine=cont within athres_cont, and refine3D's own grid (set_projs picks the nearest direction in the job's grid) can be 2,500 or 5,000 as the stage likes.
    • Cost. The rows cost what a table fill costs at the student grid and band, nothing when they are the iteration's own; the training-set size study of section 10 says how many particles they are needed for.

    5. Shifts and the Fourier-Bessel shift map¶

    On the half ring the shift matrix of s = rho (cos a, sin a) is exp(i z_k sin(theta_p - a)) with z_k = 2 pi k rho / box, and the Jacobi-Anger expansion turns the cross term of a reference/particle pair over every rotation and a whole disk of shifts into one Bessel-weighted sum over angular orders n:

    a_n(irot, rho) = (1/nrots) sum_m exp(-2 pi i m (irot-1)/nrots) sum_k w_k J_n(z_k(rho)) R^_k[m-n] conj(X^_k[m]),
C(irot, a, rho) = Re sum_n exp(-i n a) a_n(irot, rho),

    R^ and X^ the memoised full-ring spectra of the reference and of the CTF-multiplied particle. It is exact, the direction a is exact, only rho is gridded, and the orders kept follow the truncation rule: about 20 at a 30 Å radius in the 15 Å band, about 40 at 8 Å. Three things in it matter here.

    • The memoised spectra are the Fourier-Bessel domain (ring k, order m). A rotation is the phase exp(i m alpha) per order; a shift is a convolution along m with the kernel J_n(z_k) exp(-i n a), whose width grows with k and rho. No basis is diagonal in both, so an architecture absorbs one nuisance exactly and must search or learn the other. The circular convolution along the angle absorbs the rotation; a convolution along m wide enough to absorb a 30 Å shift at the band's outer ring would span about 40 orders, which is the map's work, not a network's.
    • The map is linear in the reference, so a reference may be a reprojection or a class average in the band.
    • The CTF enters as the search applies it, in the cross spectrum, per particle and exact.

    The shift is handled in two places, and in neither by the network's architecture.

    • Training: tolerance by augmentation. The network of section 6 is trained with shifts drawn uniformly in the disk through the calculator's shift matrices (memoize_shifted_ptcl), the exact phase ramps, and with rotations as cyclic shifts: it learns to put a shifted particle in the right class. Its accuracy against the shift magnitude is a declared read (section 10). As built, the disk is three times shift_sigma in pixels (SHIFT_AUG_NSIG), the keyed draw per row and epoch, and the measurements (phases 1 to 3) used a 2 px width: the plan's 10 px disk learns nothing. Ruling R2's shift check (section 4) found the student not shift-invariant, so the augmentation stays, and on uncentred particles the student seeds about one in a hundred (phase 3's Progress).
    • Prediction: refine3D's neighbourhood search (Hans, decision 7). predict_poses writes the student's direction as the particle's orientation and the width of its posterior as the cone; refine3D under refine=prob_neigh prob_neigh_mode=geom searches that cone with its own in-plane and shift search, the map and the polish, from the particle's prepared shift, and draws its pose from its own posterior. The student runs no search of its own.

    The centring record is the search's (Hans, decision 13): the neighbourhood search writes the shift, shincr and shsig from its own shift posterior, as it does for every particle it searches; the student writes no shift.

    Cost. The neighbourhood search over a cone of a few directions costs the map and the polish for those directions, ten to a hundred times below the dense search over thousands; it is measured in section 10, never assumed.

    6. The hyperparameters and the network¶

    Two hyperparameters. The network's width (16, 32 or 64 channels in the first block, doubling per block) and the band (15, 10 or 8 Å). The cheapest, 16 channels at 15 Å, is tried first; a wider network or a finer band replaces it only when held-out micrographs say it recovers clearly more seeds (section 7). A small membrane protein in a micelle is, at 15 Å, dominated by the micelle, whose outline gives the tilt of the membrane normal and little of the azimuth about it; the azimuth may sit at 8 to 12 Å, in the transmembrane helices, which is why the search's late stages run there, and why the band is a hyperparameter.

    The network (simple_hhcnn, src/main/learn/; simple_circ_cnn until ruling R5 renamed it in phase 5): HHCNN, the Harmonic Hermitian convolutional neural network: Hermitian because every layer commutes with conjugation on the half ring with the conjugate wrap, so the network is a model of the Fourier transform of a real image; harmonic because the readout's class templates live in the angular harmonic domain of the polar transform and the rotation search is the cross-correlation over the cyclic shifts through the angular Fourier transform; convolutional on the polar Fourier representation. An encapsulated class with private components, new/kill, forward, backward, one Adam step, read and write.

    • Hermitian layers on the half ring. The network is a model of the Fourier transform of a real image, so every layer commutes with conjugation, and the half ring with the conjugate wrap computes exactly what the full ring would. Three blocks of (a 3 by 3 convolution along the ring and the angle with real kernels applied alike to the real and imaginary channels, the complex signal's linear map with real weights, which is the only linear map that commutes with conjugation; conjugate-wrap padding along the angle and zero padding along the rings; a bias on the modulus; a modulus-gated nonlinearity z * relu(|z| + b) / |z|, which mixes the real and imaginary parts only through the conjugation-invariant modulus; average pooling along the rings only), with 16, 32 and 64 channels and angular stride one throughout. The first block's filters are CTF-free and their responses are divided by the filter's energy under the particle's ring-averaged CTF squared and the shell weights before the modulus gate, flex's Gram diagonal (section 3, item 2); the deeper blocks see structure, not the envelope. Since 2026-11-09 the kernels are symmetric along the angle (the two angle offsets of a kernel share one weight, their gradients summed so that Adam keeps the symmetry bit for bit), so a reflected input gives reflected feature maps; with the readout's reflected score (next item) the network is invariant to the mirror on the rotation grid, as the mirror-pair classes require. The head as first designed (phases 1 and 2) pooled rotation-invariant statistics of the last block (the angular mean over the full ring, which for a Hermitian signal is the mean of the real part over the half ring, the mean of the modulus, and the magnitudes of eight harmonics of each), then a dense layer to 256 with ReLU, the side scalar appended, and a dense layer to the classes with a softmax; it was replaced by the readout of the next item (ruling R3), because the pooling discarded the angular structure that tells views apart and made a view and its mirror one class. Rotation equivariance is exact on the calculator's rotation grid. The per-particle cost is counted on the retained layout before it is quoted: 0.5 to 0.9 ms for the features and the forward pass at 1,000 classes (phase 3).
    • The reflected score (2026-11-09). A class scores the readout map and its reflection, whose angular spectrum is the conjugate, each maximised over the rotation grid, and takes the larger; the gradient follows the winner, through the conjugate spectrum when the reflection won. A class is a mirror pair, so its template matches either member, and the search decides which.
    • The readout replaces the invariant head (Hans, ruling R3, 2026-11-07, after phase 3's folded cones and modulus-only variant showed the head to be the defect: it discards the angular structure that tells views apart, makes a view and its mirror one class, and without its phase-sensitive terms learns nothing). The three blocks stay. After them a pointwise complex-linear map with real weights reduces the channels to NREAD (8) and an average over ring bands reduces the rings to KREAD (8) bands (fewer when the last block has fewer rings); the result is Hermitian on the half ring and its full-ring angular spectrum is read to order MREAD (16), the even orders from the real part and the odd from the imaginary (the Hermitian extension's). One template per direction class lives in that domain (NREAD by KREAD by MREAD complex parameters, the order-0 term real), initialised from geometry as each class's reprojection through the network at the start and then trained with the rest. The class score is the maximum over the ring's rotation grid of the real Hermitian inner product of the feature map with the template (the spectra's product summed over channels and bands, the inverse transform of the ring's length, the real part), times a learned scale; the softmax over the classes is the posterior and the gradient flows through the maximising rotation. A template and its mirror differ, so the readout separates a view from its mirror by itself; ruling R1's fold and pair search stay as the backstop. The side scalar is no longer read (the interface keeps it). Shifts are unchanged (ruling R2: the features carry the phases). The model file's version is 2 and the weights file's 2: a model of the invariant head is refused.
    • The convolution as im2col plus sgemm, the backward pass as the two transposed products; weights, activations and gradients real(sp) and allocatable; one Adam state per weight array.
    • Bit-identical on any thread count. The mini-batch is cut into fixed chunks; the chunks are computed in parallel, each into its own gradient buffer indexed by chunk, not by thread, with per-thread activation buffers sized by omp_get_max_threads() read before the region and the BLAS on the calling thread inside it; the chunks are reduced serially in chunk order after the region. The same sum at one thread and at any team, bit for bit, and the tester asks for identity, not a tolerance.
    • The loss: the cross-entropy against the complete soft targets; Adam at 1e-3, batches of 256, weight decay 1e-5; at most 30 epochs, with early stopping on held-out micrographs of the training set.
    • The model file. A key=value text header (model_version=2 since the readout) and the weights as a binary file beside it (two when the stream's two students share a file, decision 7). The header records the design and the band, the grid with nspace and pgrp, the angular grid (pftsz, or the resampled count), the frozen weights, the reference and spectrum hashes and source cohorts, the teacher's definition and temperatures, the held-out micrographs, the confidence threshold, the timings and the reason a model was kept or declined; a reader refuses incompatible grids, objectives or references.

    7. Held-out micrographs, the choice of the design and the confidence¶

    • The halves. In the split-half protocol of section 10 they are the two view-balanced halves of the dataset as eo_rebalance assigns them by direction and score, one to train on and one to test on. In production each half of the even-odd partition would have its own student, trained and applied within it; whether that is required, or the cross arrangement of the protocol is admissible for the gold-standard FSC, is decided later on tier 4's evidence (section 12).
    • Held-out micrographs. Whole micrographs of the training half are held out before any fit, for early stopping, for the choice of width and band, and for the confidence threshold. The test half is never touched until the model is frozen; in the stream the next publication's new rows play that part.
    • The choice. The cheapest design is kept unless a wider or finer one recovers clearly more seeds on the held-out micrographs: a seed is recovered when the neighbourhood search from the student's direction reaches the pose the full search gives the same particle, and the comparison is the paired difference over micrographs, resampled by micrograph. As built, recovery is read on the student's own output without running the search: the teacher's direction, or its mirror partner, lies inside a folded cone within the cap and the route's bound (ruling R1); the paired difference is resampled 1,000 times by micrograph with a Lehmer generator seeded from the run seed and the wider design is kept when the 5% quantile of its mean gain is above zero; the held-out micrographs are every tenth stack, or every tenth selected particle of a single stack.
    • The confidence is the student's posterior mass inside the cone the neighbourhood search will cover. Its threshold is set on the held-out micrographs so that, above it, the full search's direction lies inside the cone in a declared fraction of the particles; below it the particle takes the dense search. The threshold and the fraction are recorded in the model. As built, the declared fraction is 95% and the threshold the confidence of the longest confidence-sorted prefix of the eligible held-out rows that keeps it (above every confidence when none does), with the bound it was calibrated against; a student that seeds no held-out row therefore seeds nobody.
    • Agreement is diagnostic. Where two halves have students, their agreement on untouched particles is reported and never used to choose or to gate.
    • Decline. A student that does not recover more seeds than the incumbent route on the held-out micrographs, or whose test-half result fails section 10's reads, is not used; the search runs as it would have.

    8. The two programs¶

    • learn_pose_student, a private program: the strategy routine run_learn_pose_student(cline) on the caller's (params, build) with a thin commander. Inputs: projfile, lp, nspace (the student grid), mskdiam, pgrp, fname, nthr; the references are the training particles' map (vol1 in the split-half protocol, the project's half reconstructions in production). Steps: prepare the references and the calculator as refine3D's matcher does; hold out micrographs; fill the rows of the training particles at the student grid and band, or take the iteration's own; build the features; train, choose width and band, set the confidence threshold on the held-out micrographs; write the model and its record.
    • predict_poses, a private program: run_predict_poses(cline) with a thin commander. Inputs: projfile, infile (the model), mskdiam, nthr. Per selected particle: the features under the model's band and weights; the forward pass; the mode direction, the cone holding 99% of the posterior, at most KMAX_STUDENT (64) directions, and the confidence; the mode written as the particle's orientation with the cone's width, the confidence to pconf and the route to its key, both numbered ori slots. A particle whose cone exceeds the limit or whose confidence is below the threshold is marked for the dense search. The program writes a part file the caller merges, never the caller's project; refine3D does the rest.
    • The folded cone's route and its search (Hans, ruling R1, 2026-11-07). The mode, the cone and the confidence are read on the folded sphere (section 4); the mode written as the orientation is the heavier of the pair. The route rule bounds the folded cone's radius against the dense search's cell, prob_athres or the grid's spacing, whichever is larger, recorded in the model as the bound the threshold was calibrated with (cone_bound_deg), beside the count's cap: a folded set wider than the bound goes to the dense search. The confidence threshold is calibrated with recovery on the folded sphere: the teacher's direction, or its mirror partner, inside a cone within the cap and the bound. The neighbourhood search (refine=prob_neigh prob_neigh_mode=geom), given a particle's cone, scores the references within the cone about the stored direction and within the cone about its antipode (euler_mirror), both at the same radius, and takes the better pose as it takes any; a project without the slot runs exactly as before.
    • One source per particle. Every particle's pose comes from the search that ran for it, the neighbourhood one seeded by the student or the dense one; the route is recorded per particle.

    10. Validation¶

    The split-half protocol, the real-data test, on every dataset of tier 4 and, with the truth beside it, on the sets of tiers 2 and 3:

    1. The dataset is split into two view-balanced halves as eo_rebalance splits them, by direction and score from the standard machinery's alignment of the whole set.
    2. The standard machinery reconstructs from the training half alone: its map, its spectrum and its rows are the teacher's.
    3. learn_pose_student trains on the training half, with micrographs held out inside it, and predict_poses seeds the test half, which no map, spectrum or fit has seen; refine3D's neighbourhood search aligns the seeded particles and the dense search the rest; one iteration of the local refinement follows and the test half is reconstructed.
    4. The reads: the fraction of the test half seeded; among the seeded, the fraction whose pose agrees with the standard machinery's independent alignment of the same particle within the local refinement's capture range; the cone size per particle; the FSC of the test half's map against the training half's map beside the FSC of the two halves under the standard machinery, CESPED's two numbers; and the time of every step against aligning the test half the standard way.
    5. The halves are swapped and the protocol repeated.

    Under ruling R3 (2026-11-07) the network's suite tests the readout: the gradient check through it, the rotation test (a rotated input gives the same class scores and moves the maximising rotation by the step), the mirror test (a class's tensor and its mirror image score differently against its chiral template), the thread identity and the toy problem at its floor; the identity of the existing routes is unaffected and not rerun. The order: build and test; the medium assembly at 15 Å, blank and at the truth shifts, with the size-curve points at 5,000 rows and the whole half, the held-out mode agreement and seed recovery reported against the invariant head's (5 to 7% and 0%); the real sets of phase 3 run only if the new readout seeds at least a tenth of the held-out rows with the teacher's direction inside the cone for 95% of them on the medium assembly, otherwise phase 3 ends after the medium assembly with the result recorded. The prediction's cost per particle is reported beside the coarse fill's own (2.2 ms per particle at the student's grid and band).

    The student-only read (Hans, ruling R4, 2026-11-08). The reads above judge the student's direction against one dense search at 15 Å from nothing, which on the real sets lands within 6° of the reference alignment for only 24 to 69% of the particles, so disagreement with it does not say the student is wrong; the student's orientations, drawn from the rows of the whole training set, may be better than that alignment, and the maps judge. For every test half with a trained student, blank and at the shifts: every selected row takes the student's folded mode as its direction regardless of the threshold, the cap and the bound; the in-plane rotation and the shift come from the neighbourhood search restricted to the cone about the mode and about its mirror at the larger of the folded 99% set's radius and the bound, with no dense fallback; the test half is reconstructed from those poses with no local refinement and again after a single refine=cont iteration; the reads are both maps' FSC against the training half's map and, where it exists, the truth map (the value at 12 Å, the 0.143 and 0.5 shells) beside the reference's reconstruction from the dense search's poses, the direction agreement of the modes, of the cone search's poses and of the refined poses with the reference alignment (the truth on the truth sets) for all rows beside the dense search's, and the cost per particle of the prediction and the cone search beside the dense search's. On the real sets the agreement with the reference alignment is a diagnostic, not the judge; the maps are. The read decides nothing by itself; the maintainer reads the maps. Its table is in Progress (scratch/keep/studentonly.sh).

    Under ruling R1 (2026-11-07) the reads of step 4 are taken with the folded cones, beside the sphere-wide ones phase 2 recorded, on the medium assembly first (blank and at the truth shifts, 15 and 10 Å) and then on the real sets; the agreement of a direction with the reference is read on the folded sphere where the student's class is meant (the cone), and on the full sphere for the final pose the search returns. Ruling R2's shift check (section 4) is read on every prediction; if the student is shift-invariant within noise, the blank and the truth-shift variants are one and the shift augmentation is dropped from the training.

    13. Risks and limits¶

    • Low SNR. The student cannot create missing pose information; a cone that does not hold the true direction sends the neighbourhood search astray, which is why the dense search stays behind it and the cone's coverage is read on the test half.
    • Small particles and small membrane proteins. At 15 Å a micelle gives the tilt and not the azimuth; the finer bands exist for that, and the small-particle datasets and the CESPED membrane protein of tier 4 say whether a useful seeded fraction remains.
    • Preferred orientation. Rare views are reported on their own, since a high overall seeded fraction can conceal missing directions; the view-balanced split and the class-balanced training draw are the protocol's answer.
    • Defocus as a confounder. The first layer's normalisation removes the CTF's envelope as flex's Gram does, but the information lost at the zeros still differs by defocus, and a training set that confounds defocus with the view (views that cluster by acquisition) can still teach it. The excess error and the calibration are compared within defocus bins against the teacher in the same bin, and later acquisition blocks are audited.
    • Drift. A student is tied to the map and the spectrum it was trained against; it is retrained when they change, never judged on its own seeds.
    • The in-loop consumer. Its teacher is the iteration's own rows, its map changes every iteration, and the next iteration's search is its check; whether the cycle is fast enough on a few hundred particles is the training-set size study's question, answered before anything is integrated.
    • Cost on the Dell (24 cores, no GPU): every timing is an estimate until tier 5, and a faster replacement for a dense particle search need not accelerate a whole stage.
    • Compile time. The three modules are small; no new CMake target; heavy arrays allocatable.

    16.3 The rules of the run¶

    • Nothing is committed; git is read-only; the base is a git archive export.
    • No new command-line key, public or internal: the band is lp, the grid nspace, the model infile and fname, the search's cone a per-particle record; if the work seems to need a key, stop and write it up.
    • The existing routes are untouched in behaviour: a project without student records runs byte for byte as the base, checked in phase 2 and kept through phases 3 and 4. No edits to the stream, the flex folder, the quality model or the sampling policy.
    • Single-state only; the multi-state extension and consumer 2's integration are out of scope, and the run measures what they would need.
    • No floor is set from the first run of the code that is judged; the run measures, Progress reports, and Hans rules on the numbers (decision 16 of section 12 is his). The thresholds the programs set on held-out micrographs are recorded with their evidence.
    • New or changed OpenMP code gets a tester at 16 threads, repeated, compared bit for bit with one thread; no string temporaries inside parallel regions; work arrays sized by the team in use; the BLAS on the calling thread inside a region.
    • No goto; THROW macros never continued with //&; no .and. short circuit on allocatable arrays; use ..., only:; no large type as a plain component; a tester that needs private entities is a submodule of the module it tests.
    • The datasets are read-only; copies live under scratch; bulky outputs are deleted once their numbers are in Progress; scratch/keep/ holds the base export and build, the models and tables of the measurements and their logs; the CESPED downloads stay under /data2/cesped.
    • Timed work runs with the load recorded; a measurement under load is repeated.
    • Write prose for people: Progress entries and the report read without other documents, with SIMPLE terms explained and acronyms spelled out on first use; no item IDs in the report.

    Mirror pairs (2026-11-09, in the maintainer's tree)¶

    Done by hand on the Dell in ~/src/SIMPLE, after the run's code was brought into the working tree on top of HEAD b2154da6f (Cyril's opt-in mirrored reference extraction, mirr_proj=yes), uncommitted.

    Why. Cyril found that generating the second half of the reference spiral by mirroring the first accelerates reference preparation (mirror_ref_bank, the frame angles of oris%get_mirror_pairs). The student was trained over all 1,000 directions, so the network had to tell a view from its mirror, a distinction the search makes exactly with a cross-correlation of the two candidates. Hans ruled that the mirror correspondence be used through the Euler relation in the search space: the student classifies the mirror pairs and leaves the pair to the search.

    A first attempt, not kept. Two classes per pair with the partner's template tied to its partner's mirror in the polar Fourier domain, T_m(n) = conj(T_i(n)) exp(-i n psi) (the angular-harmonic form of mirror_ref_bank), and symmetric kernels. Without the frame angle the tie blurred each template against a rotated copy of itself (the spiral's mirror partner is the reflection rotated by an off-grid angle), and the 200-particle student fell from 69% to 30% within 6°; with the frame angle it was level with the untied student at 200 and 5,000 particles and worse at 1,000. It was the wrong level for the relation and was replaced by the pair classes.

    • simple_strategy_pose_student.f90: the prediction's diagnostic rank of the reference direction is taken over its pair.
