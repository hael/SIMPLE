    One Levenberg-Marquardt for the library¶

    Status, 2026-11-18: Phases 0-4 done (run lm_optimizer; report lm_optimizer_report.md). The polar joint mode and quality_logistic default to 'lm'; the real-data reads (R8: Cyril, Hans) are open, so the note stays in planned/ (R5). Planned 2026-11-17; revised the same day to one engine for every LM, then after the design review (section 6). Base: aae467bc7 (numerical source unchanged from e94681175). Depends on: nothing for Phases 0-3; the PorF A/B of Phase 4 needs the batch-capacity floor (aae467bc7, validated: no). Needed by: polar_continuous_view.md (Phases 1-2 here).

    1. Why¶

    The polar searches refine shifts with L-BFGS-B. In PorF prob_neigh, iteration 8, refinement took 950 thread-s against 398 for scoring the bank: 10 shift searches per particle at 13-15 gradient evaluations each (8.6 in iteration 1). The Cartesian route's LM needs 3.3 calculator passes per five-parameter solve by iteration 8 (9.0 in iteration 1). The factory offers lbfgsb, simplex and de. The library has three LM loops, none in the factory: refine_joint and refine_shift in cartft_pose_opt, and the Guinier fit of simple_dose_series. Rulings (Hans, 2026-11-17):

    • both solvers everywhere a client can use them;
    • one LM in the library;
    • each client's measurement decides its default.

    Checked at the base.

    • Factory clients with derivatives: pftc_shsrch_grad (shift mode, 2 variables; joint mode, 3), ftexp_shsrch (motion correction), ctf_estimate_cost4Dcont, quality_logistic. ctf_estimate_cost2D (DE) and volpft_symsrch (simplex) use no derivatives.
    • Polar Euclidean score: exp(−L), and the shift prior multiplies it by exp(−w|d|²), so L + w|d|² has the minimiser of today's cost. The cc shift costs divide by norms that do not depend on the shift.
    • refine_joint is generic except for four pose-specific steps:
    • the SO(3) right increment;
    • the rotation and shift step caps;
    • the cumulative guard against the transaction seed;
    • the prior, added after the data-only identifiability test.
    • refine_shift (a 2×2 closed-form solve) serves only the internal cont_route=shift_then_joint (production runs joint) and the tester's shift-only cases.
    • The Guinier fit is classic Marquardt (λ ×10 on a failed try, ×0.1 after a success), with 2 unbounded parameters.
    • simple_linalg owns cholesky, chol_forward and chol_backward (users: flex and XD-GMM). cholesky subtracts each product in turn, while solve_pose_cholesky subtracts a dot_product. The two round differently (review 3), so swapping one for the other does not preserve identity.

    2. Contract¶

    • One LM algorithm. After Phase 3 no other LM loop exists in the library.
    • The engine owns the algorithm only. Each client supplies its terms, its step limits, its trial point and its constraints.
    • The Cartesian route is byte-identical at one thread after Phase 1. The exception is the shift-only path (decision 10).
    • Factory protocol unchanged. specify('lm', ...), set_fdfcurvfun_8, factory new, x and limits, minimize. The result is in x/x_8, the counts in nevals, ngevals, niter.
    • No default changes before Phase 4's read. The dose-series fit is the one result-changing migration, judged by its own suites.
    • One problem or spec per thread, as now.

    3. Decisions (2026-11-17)¶

    1. Two layers, following nlcg_solver.

    2. The engine, src/utils/math/simple_lm_solver.f90. lm_solve(problem, options, outcome) works on an abstract lm_problem whose deferred procedures belong to the client:

      • eval: f, gradient and curvature at the current trial point;
      • limit_step: may shorten a step, and reports whether it did;
      • propose: forms the trial point and reports whether it is feasible;
      • accept: makes the trial current, receives the ratio, and reports whether its objective changed.

      penalty (prior terms, added after the observability test) defaults to nothing. Two hooks default to "always true" (revised after review 1 and 2): - observable receives the scaled data system and may veto the solve; - free marks the coordinates the solve may move this iteration.

      The engine solves on active .and. free. The options are maxits, a coordinate scale(D) and active(D). Optional seed terms skip the first evaluation. The outcome holds the status and the counters. The engine lives in src/utils so that simple_dose_series can import it too. - The factory optimizer, opt/simple_opt_lm.f90. opt_lm extends optimizer, is selected by 'lm' and works in double precision. It runs a private lm_problem over opt_spec: - eval calls fdfcurvfun_8; - free freezes the coordinates that are active on a bound (decision 4); - limit_step clips to limits; - propose adds the step; - accept copies it and runs opt_callback. 2. Callback. opt_spec gains fdfcurvfun_8(fun_self, vec, f, grad, curv, D) and set_fdfcurvfun_8: f, the gradient and a symmetric positive semi-definite curvature. 'lm' stops at new without it. A client that sets both callbacks runs under either solver. 3. Step control: refine_joint's, extracted verbatim (revised after review 2 and 3). - Damped system. C + μ·diag(d), with d_i = max(C_ii, √ε·max|C|, ε²). It is solved by a new simple_linalg routine, spd_solve_rel: solve_pose_cholesky generalised to n, with its reduction order, its relative pivot floor √ε·max|·| and its finite checks. - Damping updates. μ₀ = 1e-3. A rejection multiplies μ by m, where m starts at 4 and doubles; an acceptance resets m. μ halves when the ratio exceeds 0.75. - Acceptance. A step is accepted when the actual reduction is positive and at least 0.25 of the predicted. - Unreliable solves. A damped solve that fails the pivot test counts as a rejection (μ grows) instead of ending the solve. Today refine_joint stops; PorF showed 0 no-reliable-update outcomes in about 300,000 solves. - Stops. 8 consecutive rejections; predicted reduction below 1e-6·|f|; accepted relative reduction below 1e-6; masked scaled |g| below 1e-8; maxits iterations. (Amended by R3: the three tolerances are lm_options, these values by default; the factory passes its spec's.) - Outcomes. Its six outcomes (accepted, no improvement, no reliable update, bound rejected, invalid numerics, iteration limit), to which CARTFT_* map one-to-one. (2026-11-19: a non-finite proposal is a rejection, and the status describes the returned point: accepted when a step was. 2026-11-20: the outcome also says whether a stopping test ended the solve, converged, which the factory reports.) 4. Bounds (factory): a solve on the free coordinates (revised after review 1). Componentwise clipping of a coupled step can point uphill: the review's counterexample stalls at the start. - Free coordinates. Each iteration, the adapter's free freezes a coordinate that sits on a bound with its gradient pointing outward. - Clipping. limit_step clips the free step to the box, and the predicted reduction is the clipped step's. - Convergence. The stationarity test on the masked gradient is then the projected-gradient test. As μ grows, the clipped step tends to scaled steepest descent on the free coordinates, which descends, so a minimum on a bound is reached. 5. Hook (factory). opt_callback runs in accept. When it changes the objective (the alternating search's discrete angle), the engine re-evaluates and counts the evaluation. 6. Curvature by objective (revised after review 2 and 4). - Euclidean. The whitened residual's Gauss-Newton matrix JᵀWJ. - Polar shift prior. In the shift mode (a fixed rotation) it is a penalty with curvature 2w·I. In the joint mode the centre c(a) = R(a)u0 turns with the angle, so the curvature is 2w·[I, −c′(a)]ᵀ[I, −c′(a)], in rotation-index units (prior_centre_angle's dc0). That is also the exact Hessian at zero prior residual. - cc with shift-invariant norms (polar shift search at a fixed rotation, ftexp_shsrch). The Gauss-Newton matrix of the equivalent least-squares problem. It is constant over the shift, so it is computed once per solve. - cc whose norms move (the polar joint search under cc, the CTF fit).     4. Acceptance and stops¶

    The LM tests go in simple_opt_tester.f90 (unit_numerics suite=optimisers), through the factory:

    • L1. Rosenbrock in residual form, r = (10(y − x²), 1 − x), from the tester's start, reaches (1, 1) within 1e-6 (guessed). Evaluations against lbfgsb are reported, not gated.
    • L2, coupled bound (review 1). f = ((x + 2y + 1)² + (y − 1.9)²)/2 on [0, 10] × [−10, 10], starting at (0, 0) on the bound, reaches (0, −0.02) within 1e-6 (guessed), and its projected gradient falls below 1e-8. Plain clipping must fail this case: that is the negative control.
    • L3, negative control. With the gradient's sign flipped, LM accepts no step and returns the start after 8 rejections.
    • L4. opt_callback runs once per accepted step, and nevals counts the re-evaluation.
    • L5, rank deficiency (review 2). r = x + y − 1 with a singular curvature reaches f < 1e-12, and x − y keeps its start value: the null direction gets no step.
    • L6, null coordinate (R4). r = x − 1 in two variables reaches f < 1e-12, y keeps its start value, and the status is not "no improvement".

    Curvature oracle, per client. At zero residual the curvature equals the central-difference Hessian of the analytic gradient, within the client's gradient tolerance, and it is positive semi-definite. The logistic Fisher matrix must equal the Hessian at any point. The polar joint oracle runs with a nonzero prior centre and the angle active. The CTF client also runs from an isotropic start (dfx = dfy): the defocus improves and the angle does not move.

    Stops.

    • The Cartesian identity fails: diagnose it, and never accept a near-identity.
    • A client's oracle fails: exclude that client and report it.
    • An LM test passes only with a constant other than refine_joint's (since R3: on the Cartesian route only; the factory's tests set factr and pgtol).
    • A file outside the table.

    Gates no ruling waives. The fast gate; the optimisers suite with L3; the Cartesian identity (Phase 1); the polar identity (Phase 3); polar_matching_bench suite=fills.

    6. Design review (2026-11-17)¶

    Reviewed against the current source at aae467bc7; the relevant numerical source is unchanged from e94681175. The engine/factory split is appropriate, but the following contracts need revision before implementation. These findings do not change the decisions above.

    1. P1 — Box clipping is not sufficient for the promised bound convergence (decision 4). A coupled unconstrained step can point uphill after componentwise clipping. Independent double-precision Python emulation of the stated controls gives a concrete counterexample: f(x,y) = ((x + 2y + 1)^2 + (y - 1.9)^2)/2, box [0,10] x [-10,10], start (0,0). Its curvature is positive definite, [[1,2],[2,5]], but the solve stops at iteration 6 on predicted reduction with no accepted step. The exact constrained minimum is (0,-0.02); the feasible y-gradient at the returned start is 0.1. Specify a solve on the free coordinates or a projected descent fallback and a projected-gradient convergence check. L2 must include a coupled quadratic starting on a bound, not just a diagonal quadratic.

    2. P1 — Data identifiability is a Cartesian policy, not a generic LM prerequisite (decisions 1–3). prepare_pose_data_system Cholesky-tests the undamped data matrix and rejects a singular active block before adding either a prior or damping. Extracting that rule verbatim conflicts with the callback's positive-semidefinite contract. In the supported 3/4-variable CTF fit, dfx=dfy makes the astigmatism-angle Jacobian identically zero (simple_ctf_estimate_cost::fdf), although the defocus coordinates can still improve. Keep the Cartesian observability veto in its adapter, and define how the generic engine regularizes or freezes null directions, including its relative pivot threshold. Add a rank-deficient least-squares check and an isotropic-CTF start; preserve the existing Cartesian test that forbids a prior from rescuing unobservable pose data.

    3. P2 — The Cholesky swap does not preserve the stated arithmetic order (premise and decision 3). solve_pose_cholesky traverses rows and computes A(i,j) - dot_product(...); simple_linalg::cholesky traverses columns and repeatedly subtracts each product from A(i,j). They also differ in finite-value checks. A fixed-seed 5x5 SPD Python comparison of those arithmetic forms produced a differing factor entry (5.55e-17 absolute). This does not establish a compiled workflow failure, but it disproves the premise used to justify byte identity. Preserve the Cartesian reduction order and checks in the shared numerical owner, with the necessary file-table change, or separate this substitution from the identity-preserving extraction. Do not weaken the identity gate.

    4. P2 — The polar joint prior needs angle and cross curvature (decision 6). joint_grad_at_angle uses P = w |s - c(a)|^2, where c(a)=R(a)u0 follows the stored seed. For a nonzero seed, its PSD Gauss-Newton curvature is 2w [I,-c'(a)]^T [I,-c'(a)], including shift-angle and angle-angle entries; 2w I describes only fixed-angle shifts. At zero prior residual this full matrix is also the exact Hessian. Specify it in the client contract, using the existing rotation-index units, and extend the curvature oracle to a nonzero prior centre with angle active. Otherwise the proposed matrix cannot pass the stated zero-residual Hessian oracle for that client.

    Validation: source inspection and independent standard-library Python numerical checks only. No Fortran implementation, build, fast gate, workflow identity, real-data or Linux/BOX run was performed for this design review. The revised contracts and their compiled regressions remain open.

    Disposition (Hans, 2026-11-17: "update the plan accordingly"). All four findings were accepted.

    1. Decision 4 now solves on the free coordinates (the free hook); L2 is the review's coupled case.
    2. The observability veto moves into the Cartesian problem (observable, decisions 1 and 9). The generic engine handles null directions by the damping floor, and an unreliable solve raises μ (decisions 3 and 6). L5, the isotropic CTF start and the two Cartesian prior/unobservable tests check this.
    3. The Cartesian factorization moves to simple_linalg as spd_solve_rel, with its reduction order and checks, and the identity gate is unchanged (decision 3; premise corrected). Merging it with cholesky is out of scope.
    4. Decision 6 gives the joint-mode prior curvature 2w·[I, −c′]ᵀ[I, −c′], and the polar joint oracle runs with a nonzero centre and the angle active.
